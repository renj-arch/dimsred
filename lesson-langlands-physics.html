Math of QFT & Strings — S-Duality, Geometric Langlands & Topological Phases

Deep dive into the profound mathematics underlying quantum field theory and string theory — the Feynman path integral (a rigorous integration theory on the space of fields, the stationary phase approximation and perturbative expansion in Feynman diagrams), gauge theories and the Yang–Mills equations (connections on principal bundles, the moduli space of instantons, the Atiyah–Singer index theorem for the Dirac operator in the background of a gauge field), anomalies (the chiral anomaly as the index of a Dirac operator, the 't Hooft anomaly matching conditions, the Wess–Zumino–Witten term), the renormalization group (the beta function, asymptotic freedom in non-abelian gauge theories, the Callan–Symanzik equation), the string theory (the Polyakov path integral on Riemann surfaces, the BRST quantization, the Virasoro algebra and conformal field theory, the moduli space of algebraic curves, mirror symmetry and the homological mirror symmetry conjecture of Kontsevich), the S-duality (the Montonen–Olive duality for N = 4 super Yang–Mills, the electromagnetic duality exchanging electric and magnetic charges), and the geometric Langlands correspondence (the Kapustin–Witten interpretation as S-duality of a topologically twisted N = 4 super Yang–Mills theory, the Hitchin moduli space and the Langlands dual group LG).

Part 1

⚛️ Quantum Field Theory and the Path Integral

QFT Foundations
Feynman Path Integral, Gauge Theories, and the Yang–Mills Equations
The Feynman path integral Z = ∫ Dφ exp(iS[φ]/ħ) is a heuristic integral over the infinite-dimensional space of field configurations. The partition function Z generates the correlation functions ⟨φ(x₁)⋯φ(x_n)⟩ via functional derivatives. In gauge theories, the fields are connections ∇ = d + A on a principal G-bundle P → M, where A is a g-valued 1-form (the gauge potential). The Yang–Mills action S_YM(A) = (1/2e²) ∫_M tr(F_A ∧ *F_A) where F_A = dA + A ∧ A is the curvature (the field strength). The Yang–Mills equations d_A *F_A = 0 are the Euler–Lagrange equations; solutions are gauge fields or connections. The instanton equation F_A = ±*F_A (the (anti-)self-dual Yang–Mills equations) gives absolute minima of the action on 4-manifolds. The moduli space of instantons M_k (with instanton number k) has dimension 8k − 3(1 − b₁ + b₂⁺) (for gauge group SU(2)). The Atiyah–Singer index theorem computes the index of the Dirac operator D̸ = γ^μ(∂_μ + A_μ) in a gauge field background: index(D̸) = (1/8π²) ∫_M tr(F_A ∧ F_A) — the Atiyah–Singer index theorem for the Dirac operator gives the chiral anomaly. The renormalization group flow (Wilson) integrates out high-energy degrees of freedom, producing an effective action with running coupling constants governed by the beta function β(g) = μ dg/dμ. For non-abelian gauge theories, β(g) = −(11N/3 − 2n_f/3) g³/16π² + O(g⁵) — asymptotic freedom (Gross–Politzer–Wilczek, 1973) explains why quarks are nearly free at high energies.
The path integral formalizes quantum dynamics; gauge theories describe fundamental forces via connections on principal bundles; the Yang–Mills equations govern the dynamics of gauge fields.
Part 2

🌀 Anomalies, Renormalization, and Conformal Field Theory

Symmetries
Chiral Anomalies, the Renormalization Group, and CFT
An anomaly is a classical symmetry that is broken by quantization. The most famous is the chiral anomaly (Adler–Bell–Jackiw, 1969): in a theory with massless fermions, the classically conserved axial current J⁵_μ satisfies ∂^μ J⁵_μ = (1/16π²) tr(F_μν *F^{μν}) — a non-zero divergence due to the quantum effects. The 't Hooft anomaly matching condition: if a symmetry is spontaneously broken, the low-energy theory must reproduce the same anomaly. The Wess–Zumino–Witten (WZW) term is a topological term (an integral over a 5-manifold bounding spacetime) that cancels anomalies. The renormalization group (Wilson, 1974): a QFT is described by an effective action S_Λ at scale Λ; integrating out modes between Λ and Λ' gives the Wilsonian effective action. The beta function β(g) determines how coupling constants change with scale. Fixed points of the RG flow define conformal field theories (CFTs) — quantum field theories invariant under scale and conformal transformations. In 2 dimensions, the Virasoro algebra [L_n, L_m] = (n−m)L_{n+m} + (c/12)n(n²−1)δ_{n+m,0} is the infinite-dimensional symmetry algebra; c is the central charge. The operator product expansion (OPE) encodes the singular part of the product of two local operators. The conformal bootstrap program (Belavin–Polyakov–Zamolodchikov, 1984) solves 2d CFTs entirely from symmetry constraints: the minimal models correspond to c < 1 and the unitary series c = 1 − 6/m(m+1). The modular invariance of the torus partition function imposes strong constraints on the spectrum of conformal dimensions.
Anomalies reveal the quantum breaking of classical symmetries; the renormalization group governs the flow of couplings; conformal field theory captures scale-invariant fixed points.
Part 3

🪢 String Theory, D-Branes, and Mirror Symmetry

Strings
The Polyakov Path Integral, D-Branes, and Homological Mirror Symmetry
String theory replaces point particles with 1-dimensional extended objects (strings). The Polyakov action S = (1/4πα') ∫_Σ √h h^{ab} g_{μν}(X) ∂_a X^μ ∂_b X^ν defines a 2d QFT on the worldsheet Σ coupled to the spacetime metric g. The BRST quantization (Becchi–Rouet–Stora–Tyutin) introduces ghost fields to remove unphysical degrees of freedom. The critical dimension for the bosonic string is D = 26; for the superstring, D = 10. D-branes (Dirichlet branes) are extended objects on which open strings end; they are classified by their dimension p (Dp-brane). A D-brane carries a Chan–Paton gauge group U(N) on its worldvolume. The type II string theories (IIA, IIB) have D-branes of even/odd dimensions respectively. T-duality relates type IIA on a circle of radius R to type IIB on a circle of radius α'/R. Mirror symmetry (Candelas–de la Ossa–Green–Parkes, 1991) states that Calabi–Yau 3-folds come in mirror pairs (X, X̌) such that type IIA on X is equivalent to type IIB on X̌. The homological mirror symmetry conjecture (Kontsevich, 1994) states an equivalence of derived categories: D^b(Coh(X)) ≅ D^b(Fuk(X̌)) — the derived category of coherent sheaves on X is equivalent to the Fukaya category of Lagrangian submanifolds on X̌. The enumeration of rational curves on Calabi–Yau manifolds (Gromov–Witten invariants) is computed by the mirror map via period integrals of the mirror family. Kontsevich's formality theorem and the deformation quantization program relate the Poisson geometry of the classical limit to the associative algebra structure of quantum observables.
String theory unifies gravity and gauge interactions via extended objects; mirror symmetry reveals a deep equivalence between symplectic and complex geometries on Calabi–Yau manifolds.
Part 4

🔋 S-Duality, Geometric Langlands, and Topological Phases

Duality
Montonen–Olive Duality, Kapustin–Witten, and Topological Quantum Field Theory
S-duality (Montonen–Olive, 1977) conjectures that N = 4 super Yang–Mills theory with gauge group G at coupling τ = θ/2π + 4πi/e² is equivalent to the same theory with the Langlands dual group LG at coupling −1/(n_g τ). Here n_g is the ratio of the lengths of long and short roots of G. The spectrum of 't Hooft–Polyakov monopoles (solitonic particles carrying magnetic charge) is exchanged with the perturbative spectrum of W-bosons (electric charge) under S-duality. Kapustin–Witten (2006) reinterpreted the geometric Langlands correspondence as S-duality of a topologically twisted N = 4 super Yang–Mills theory on the product of a Riemann surface C and a surface ℝ². The Hitchin moduli space M_H(G) of Higgs bundles on C appears as the moduli space of solutions to the Nahm equations. The A-branes and B-branes on M_H(G) correspond to Hecke eigensheaves and automorphic sheaves on the moduli space of G-bundles. The geometric Langlands correspondence states an equivalence of derived categories: D^b(Coh(Loc_{^LG}(C))) ≅ D^b(Mod_{D}(Bun_G(C))) — coherent sheaves on the moduli space of LG-local systems correspond to D-modules on the moduli space of G-bundles. The topological quantum field theory (TQFT) (Atiyah, Witten) assigns vector spaces to codimension-1 submanifolds and linear maps to cobordisms. The Chern–Simons theory (Witten, 1989) is a TQFT whose partition function gives the Jones polynomial and its generalizations. The triangulated category of topological phases (Kitaev, Freed–Hopkins–Teleman) classifies gapped phases of matter using unitary TQFTs, tensor categories, and the cobordism hypothesis (Lurie, Baez–Dolan).
S-duality exchanges electric and magnetic degrees of freedom; the geometric Langlands program emerges as the S-duality of topologically twisted gauge theory, bridging representation theory and quantum field theory.
