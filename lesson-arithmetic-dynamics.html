Arithmetic Dynamics — Height Functions, the Mordell Conjecture & Dynamical Systems over Number Fields

Arithmetic dynamics studies dynamical systems over number fields and finite fields, blending algebraic number theory with complex dynamics. A central tool is the height function h(P) measuring the arithmetic complexity of points — the Weil height on projective space, and the Néron-Tate canonical height on abelian varieties. The Mordell conjecture (proved by Faltings, 1983) states that a smooth projective curve of genus g ≥ 2 over a number field has finitely many rational points — a landmark result proved via arithmetic geometry. The Morton-Silverman uniform boundedness conjecture and the dynamical Mordell-Lang conjecture extend these ideas to dynamical systems. The equidistribution of preperiodic points (Bilu, Duke, Szpiro-Ullmo-Zhang) and the dynamical Manin-Mumford conjecture form the modern frontier, connecting heights, Diophantine geometry, and complex dynamics.

"Arithmetic dynamics is diophantine geometry modeled on complex dynamics."
— Joseph Silverman
Part 1

📏 Heights and the Northcott Property

Heights
Weil Height, Northcott's Theorem, and Canonical Heights
The Weil height on ℙⁿ(̄ℚ) is defined by h([x₀:...:xₙ]) = (1/[K:ℚ]) Σ_{v∈M_K} log max_i |x_i|_v, where K is a number field containing all coordinates and M_K is the set of places. This gives an absolute logarithmic height independent of the choice of field and coordinates up to a bounded error. Northcott's theorem (1950): for any B, D > 0, the set of algebraic points of degree ≤ D and height ≤ B is finite — a foundational finiteness result. The canonical height for abelian varieties: ȟ(P) = lim_{n→∞} h([n]P)/n², known as the Néron-Tate height, is quadratic on the Mordell-Weil group and non-degenerate modulo torsion. Tate's limit: for a morphism f of degree d, the canonical height is ȟ_f = lim_{n→∞} h(fⁿ(P))/dⁿ, giving a powerful tool for studying dynamical systems.
Height functions measure arithmetic complexity; Northcott's theorem shows finiteness of points with bounded height and degree — the bedrock of arithmetic dynamics.
Part 2

🌍 The Mordell Conjecture and Faltings' Theorem

Faltings
Mordell's Conjecture, the Parshin Trick, and the Proof via Arithmetic Geometry
Mordell's conjecture (1922): a smooth projective curve C of genus g ≥ 2 over a number field K has finitely many K-rational points. Proved by Faltings (1983) using profound methods from arithmetic geometry: Shimura varieties, the moduli space of abelian varieties A_g, the theory of Néron models, and a height function on the moduli space. The proof crucially uses the Parshin trick — associating to each rational point a family of curves (a covering of C ramified over that point) and studying the corresponding abelian varieties via the de Franchis-Severi theorem (geometric finiteness for abelian varieties). The Manin-Mumford conjecture (proved by Raynaud): the torsion points on an abelian variety are Zariski dense in the group of all torsion points, but the intersection of a curve of genus ≥ 2 with the torsion points is finite — a deep result about torsion points on curves.
Faltings' proof of the Mordell conjecture revolutionized arithmetic geometry, establishing finiteness of rational points on high-genus curves.
Part 3

🧮 The Dynamical Mordell-Lang and the Uniform Boundedness Conjecture

Dynamics
Mordell-Lang, Dynamical Mordell-Lang, and Uniform Boundedness
The Mordell-Lang conjecture (proved by Faltings, Vojta, McQuillan): if A is a semiabelian variety and X ⊂ A is a subvariety, then X(K) is a finite union of cosets of subgroups of A — a sweeping generalization. The dynamical Mordell-Lang (Derksen-Masser, Bell-Ghioca-Tucker): for a self-map f: X → X, the intersection of an orbit with a subvariety Y is "sparse" — a finite union of arithmetic progressions. The Morton-Silverman uniform boundedness conjecture: the number of rational preperiodic points for a rational function over a number field is bounded by a constant depending only on the degree and the field. Silverman's height bound: for a non-isotrivial elliptic curve, the canonical height of preperiodic points is bounded below by a positive constant. The Szpiro-Ullmo-Zhang equidistribution: the preperiodic points of the multiplication-by-2 map on a CM elliptic curve equidistribute with respect to the canonical measure on the curve.
The dynamical Mordell-Lang and uniform boundedness conjectures extend the classical Diophantine finiteness paradigm to the setting of dynamical systems.
Part 4

🔥 Equidistribution and the Dynamical Manin-Mumford

Equidistribution
Bilu's Theorem, Dynamical Manin-Mumford, and Applications
Bilu's theorem (1997): the Galois orbit of a sequence of algebraic numbers of height → 0 is equidistributed on the unit circle with respect to the uniform measure — a striking link between height and distribution. The dynamical Manin-Mumford conjecture (Zhang, 1990s): for a polarized dynamical system (X, f, L), a subvariety Y ⊂ X contains a Zariski dense set of preperiodic points iff Y itself is preperiodic — a dynamical analog of the classical Manin-Mumford and Mordell-Lang conjectures. The equidistribution of small points on abelian varieties (Szpiro-Ullmo-Zhang, 1997): points of small Néron-Tate height equidistribute with respect to the Arakelov-Calabi measure. The Yuan-Zhang theorem on equidistribution of points of small height on algebraic tori generalizes Bilu's result. Applications include the Bogomolov conjecture (proved by Ullmo, 1998): on a curve of genus ≥ 2, the set of points with canonical height < ε is finite — a refinement of the Mordell conjecture.
Equidistribution theorems connect height functions to analytic measures, while the dynamical Manin-Mumford conjecture seeks to characterize preperiodic subvarieties.
