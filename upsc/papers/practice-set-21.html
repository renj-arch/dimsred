Q3. Current Affairs
With reference to the Anusandhan National Research Foundation (ANRF) Act, 2023, which of the following statements is correct?
A. It subsumes the Science and Engineering Research Board (SERB) established in 2008.
B. The Governing Board is chaired by the Union Minister of Science and Technology.
C. The Executive Council is chaired by the Secretary, Department of Science and Technology.
D. It provides for a financial corpus entirely funded by private sector contributions.
Correct Answer: A
Shortcut: Eliminate options with incorrect chairpersons. The ANRF Act mandates the Prime Minister as ex-officio President of the Governing Board (not Science Minister) and the Principal Scientific Adviser as Chairperson of the Executive Council (not Secretary, DST). The funding is primarily governmental (Rs 50,000 crore over 5 years, 28% govt share), not entirely private. Option A is the only factually accurate statement: the Act repeals the SERB Act, 2008, subsuming SERB into ANRF. Full Solution: The ANRF Act, 2023 establishes the Anusandhan National Research Foundation to seed, grow and promote R&D. Section 24 repeals the Science and Engineering Research Board Act, 2008, dissolving SERB and transferring its assets/liabilities to ANRF. The Governing Board is headed by the PM (President) and Union Ministers of S&T and Education (Vice-Presidents). The Executive Council is chaired by the Principal Scientific Adviser. The total estimated cost is Rs 50,000 crore (2023-28), with Rs 14,000 crore (28%) from Govt of India and the rest from non-government sources.

Q13. Current Affairs
With reference to the three new Criminal Laws enacted in 2023 (Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, and Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam), which of the following statements is correct?
A. They came into force on 26th January 2024.
B. The offence of 'Sedition' under Section 124A of the IPC has been retained verbatim in the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita.
C. Community Service has been introduced as a form of punishment for the first time in the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita.
D. The new laws are not applicable to the Union Territory of Jammu and Kashmir.
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut: Eliminate options using key factual anchors. Option A is wrong because the notified date of commencement is July 1, 2024, not Republic Day. Option B is wrong because 'Sedition' (Sec 124A IPC) has been repealed and replaced by a new offence 'Acts endangering sovereignty, unity and integrity of India' (Sec 150 BNS). Option D is wrong because post-Article 370 abrogation, Central laws apply directly to J&K. Option C remains as the only plausible major reform highlight. Worked Solution: The three Bills were passed in Dec 2023, received Presidential Assent on Dec 25, 2023, and were notified to come into force on July 1, 2024. The BNS introduces 'Community Service' (Section 4(f)) as a new punishment for petty offences (e.g., attempt to commit suicide, public servant unlawfully engaging in trade, non-appearance in response to proclamation). Sedition is replaced, not retained. The laws extend to the whole of India including J&K.

Q15. General Studies
Under Article 368 of the Constitution of India, which one of the following categories of Constitutional Amendment Bills requires ratification by the Legislatures of not less than one-half of the States before being presented to the President for assent?
A. A Bill seeking to alter the boundaries of an existing State
B. A Bill seeking to amend the provisions relating to the election of the President of India
C. A Bill seeking to amend the Directive Principles of State Policy
D. A Bill seeking to abolish the Legislative Council of a State
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: Only amendments affecting the 'Federal Structure' (List in Art 368 proviso: Art 54, 55, 73, 162, 241, Ch IV Part V, Ch V Part VI, Ch I Part XI, Seventh Schedule, Representation of States in Parliament, Art 368 itself) require State ratification. Step-by-step: 1. Option A (Art 3/4): Requires Simple Majority of Parliament only. 2. Option B (Art 54/55 - Election of President): Explicitly listed in Art 368 proviso. Requires Special Majority of Parliament + Ratification by 1/2 States. 3. Option C (DPSP): Requires Special Majority of Parliament only (Art 368 general). 4. Option D (Art 169): Requires Special Majority of Parliament + Resolution by concerned State Legislative Assembly (not ratification by 1/2 States).
