Key Concepts

Nepali Optional — Syllabus Map & Strategy

Nepali is Indo-Aryan (Pahari group) and is written in Devanagari script. It is spoken mainly in Sikkim, Darjeeling and parts of Assam. It was added to the Eighth Schedule by the 71st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992. The optional has two papers of 250 marks each.

Paper I — History of the Language and Literature

  • History of the language: origin, stages of development, dialect variation and standardisation.
  • Literary movements: Bhanubhakta tradition, Modern Nepali movement, Sahitya Akademi phase.
  • Major authors and works: period-wise study of representative writers and texts.

Paper II — Prescribed Texts, Criticism and Answer Writing

  • Prescribed texts: close reading of canonical texts and prescribed extracts.
  • Literary criticism: traditional rasa criticism, romantic and nationalist criticism, modernist and diaspora approaches
  • Explanation and essays: paraphrase, context, theme, form and critical evaluation.

Periods at a Glance

  • Early period (18th–19th c.): Bhanubhakta Acharya and the beginnings of modern Nepali literature.
  • Early twentieth century: Laxmi Prasad Devkota, Balkrishna Sama and drama.
  • Modern period (mid-20th c.–present): Poetry, fiction and diaspora writing.
Solved Example: Period-wise Answer
Question: Trace the main stages of Nepali literature.
Answer: Early period (18th–19th c.) — Bhanubhakta Acharya and the beginnings of modern Nepali literature.; Early twentieth century — Laxmi Prasad Devkota, Balkrishna Sama and drama.; Modern period (mid-20th c.–present) — Poetry, fiction and diaspora writing..

Standard Sources

  • Sahitya Akademi histories of Nepali literature
  • Encyclopaedia of Indian Literature
  • University syllabi and prescribed texts
  • Standard Nepali literary histories

Common Traps

  • Writing a general Indian-literature answer without naming Nepali authors and texts.
  • Confusing periods and movements; give approximate centuries, not exact years you are unsure of.
  • Quoting a translation as if it were the original; mention the translator when you quote.
  • Ignoring the script and dialect question in Paper I.
  • Writing plot summaries instead of critical analysis in Paper II.
Mains strategy
Build a one-page timeline of Nepali literature with period, movement, authors and works. For Paper I, answer in three layers: origin and development → movement → representative authors. For Paper II, use the structure: context → theme → form and language → critical evaluation. Quote short phrases with the original term and an English gloss.

The Interplay of Language, Politics, and Literary Evolution

Nepali literature is not merely a sequence of texts but a dynamic reflection of Nepal's geopolitical shifts and linguistic evolution. A serious aspirant must understand that the transition from Newar to Bhausaah (Bhasa) in the 19th century was not just a literary choice but a political assertion of identity against Sanskrit dominance. The standardisation of the language, often attributed solely to Bhanubhakta Acharya, is actually a cumulative process involving the 'Vidya Shiksha' movement and later state interventions under Rana rule and the Panchayat era. Understanding this nuance allows you to answer questions on linguistic purity versus inclusivity with depth.

Furthermore, the distinction between 'Desi' (local) and 'Arya' (Sanskritic) influences is crucial for Paper I analysis. While early poetry like the Ramayana translations leaned heavily on Sanskrit metres and vocabulary, the 20th-century modernist movement consciously broke these chains to embrace colloquial speech. This shift is evident in the works of Laxmi Prasad Devkota and Balkrishna Sama, who introduced psychological realism and social critique into a medium previously reserved for mythological narratives. Recognizing this tension between tradition and modernity is key to scoring high marks.

Another critical layer involves the dialectal variations often ignored in standard textbooks. While Standard Nepali (Khas-Kurukh) is the norm, literature from the Terai region (Bhojpuri influence) and the Himalayan regions (Tamang, Gurung influences) offers rich comparative material. Exam questions sometimes probe this by asking for a comparison of poetic sensibilities across dialects. A sophisticated answer would cite specific verses or stylistic choices that reflect regional phonology and vocabulary without compromising the standard script's integrity.

Technique: Comparative Analysis of Literary Movements
Question: Compare the 'Bhanubhakta Tradition' with the 'Modern Nepali Movement' in terms of thematic focus and linguistic style. How did the latter challenge the former?
Solution:
The Bhanubhakta tradition, initiated by Acharya Bhanubhakta in the 19th century, focused on religious devotion (Bhakti) and used a Sanskritised vocabulary with strict adherence to classical metres like Chanda. The themes were predominantly mythological, drawing from the Ramayana and Bhagavad Gita.
In contrast, the Modern Nepali Movement, spearheaded by Laxmi Prasad Devkota and Balkrishna Sama in the early 20th century, shifted focus to social realism, human psychology, and national identity. Linguistically, they abandoned archaic Sanskritisms for colloquial Khas-Kurukh speech, making literature accessible to the common man.
The challenge lay in breaking the monopoly of religious texts as literary subjects. Devkota's 'Ekayana' (One Way) is a seminal example where he used simple language to critique social hypocrisy, directly challenging the devotional purity of the Bhanubhakta era.

Critical Evaluation: Beyond Plot Summaries in Paper II

Paper II demands more than just summarizing prescribed texts; it requires critical evaluation using specific theoretical frameworks. For instance, when analyzing a poem by Sano Bhandari or a play by Balkrishna Sama, one must apply the lens of 'Rasa' theory alongside modern psychological criticism. The examiner often asks for an analysis of how a specific character represents a socio-political archetype.

To excel here, students should master the concept of 'Intertextuality'. Many Nepali texts are dialogues with each other. For example, Devkota's work is a constant dialogue with Bhanubhakta's legacy—sometimes honoring it, sometimes subverting it. Identifying these echoes in your answer demonstrates a high level of textual awareness. Additionally, understanding the 'Diaspora' phase is essential for modern questions, where themes of displacement and identity crisis are prevalent.

The structure of your answer must move from close reading (identifying imagery, tone, syntax) to thematic analysis (what does this say about society?) and finally to critical evaluation (how does this fit into the broader literary canon?). Avoid generic statements; always anchor your critique in specific lines or scenes from the prescribed texts.

Technique: Contextual Criticism
Question: Analyze the role of 'Nature' as a metaphor in the poetry of Bhupi Sherchan, considering his historical context.
Solution:
In Bhupi Sherchan's poetry, nature is not merely a backdrop but an active participant reflecting human emotion and societal turmoil. During the turbulent 1960s-70s in Nepal, Sherchan often used the imagery of storms, dark skies, and withered plants to mirror political instability and personal angst.
Critically, his use of nature differs from earlier pastoral poetry which idealized rural life. Here, nature is often hostile or indifferent, reflecting a modernist disillusionment. For instance, in his famous lines about the wind and the mountain, the mountains are not serene but looming presences. This shift indicates a move from Romanticism to Existentialism in Nepali literature.
Exam-Day Strategy: The 'Twist' Question
When you encounter a question that seems to ask for a standard fact (e.g., "Who wrote X?"), do not stop there. Immediately pivot to the implications. Why was this work written when it was? How does it challenge previous norms? If the question asks about a period, mention one exception or anomaly within that period to show depth. Always conclude with a forward-looking statement on how this literature influences contemporary Nepali writing.

Advanced Sources and Cross-Disciplinary Connections

While standard textbooks provide the foundation, advanced aspirants must consult secondary sources like 'The History of Nepali Literature' by various scholars to understand historiographical debates. For instance, there is a debate on whether Bhanubhakta was the sole founder or if he was part of a larger movement. Acknowledging such debates adds weight to your answers.

Cross-disciplinary connections are also vital. Nepali literature intersects with history (e.g., the impact of the Anglo-Nepal War on literary themes), sociology (caste dynamics in texts), and even geography (the influence of the Himalayan landscape on poetic meter). A strong answer might briefly reference a historical event, such as the unification of Nepal under Prithvi Narayan Shah, and explain how it altered the linguistic landscape from regional dialects to a unified national tongue.

  • Over-generalizing: Avoid stating that all modern poets wrote about social issues; some focused on metaphysics or pure aesthetics. Be specific about authors.
  • Ignoring Script Evolution: Do not forget to mention the shift from Devanagari to the inclusion of other scripts in diaspora literature if relevant to the question.
  • Misinterpreting 'Modernism': In Nepali context, modernism started earlier than in India; ensure you date it correctly relative to the 1950s political changes.
  • Plot vs. Critique: Never write a story summary for Paper II unless explicitly asked. Always prioritize thematic and stylistic analysis.
  • Dating Errors: Be precise with dates; confusing the 1950 revolution with the 1960 cultural awakening can lead to significant mark deductions.
