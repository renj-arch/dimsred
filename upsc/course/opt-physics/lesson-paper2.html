Key Concepts

Physics Paper II — Electrodynamics, Quantum Mechanics, Statistical Mechanics

Electrodynamics

  • Electrostatics: Coulomb's law, Gauss's law, electric field & potential, Poisson's equation (∇²φ = -ρ/ε₀), Laplace's equation (∇²φ = 0), boundary value problems (method of images), capacitance & capacitance of isolated conductors, energy of electrostatic field, dielectrics (polarization, electric displacement D = ε₀E + P, dielectric constant), boundary conditions at dielectric interfaces.
  • Magnetostatics: Biot-Savart law, Ampère's law (∮ B·dl = μ₀I_enclosed), magnetic vector potential (B = ∇ × A), magnetic vector potential, magnetic dipole, magnetic materials (diamagnetism, paramagnetism, ferromagnetism), magnetic susceptibility & permeability, hysteresis.
  • Electrodynamics: Faraday's law of induction (∇ × E = -∂B/∂t), displacement current (Maxwell's correction), Maxwell's equations (differential & integral forms), electromagnetic waves (plane wave solutions, dispersion relation ω = ck, speed of light c = 1/√(μ₀ε₀)), Poynting vector (S = E × H), Poynting theorem (energy conservation), radiation pressure.
  • Electromagnetic Waves: Plane wave solutions, polarization (linear, circular, elliptical), reflection & transmission at boundaries (Fresnel equations), Brewster's angle, total internal reflection, skin depth in conductors, waveguides (rectangular waveguide, cutoff frequency, TE & TM modes), cavity resonators.
  • Radiation: Retarded potentials (Liénard-Wiechert potentials), radiation from oscillating electric dipole, radiation from oscillating magnetic dipole, radiation from accelerating charges (Larmor formula: P = q²a²/(6πε₀c³)), radiation from oscillating electric dipole (power radiated P = μ₀p₀²ω⁴/(12πc)), antenna theory.

Quantum Mechanics

  • Foundations: Wave-particle duality, de Broglie hypothesis (λ = h/p), Heisenberg uncertainty principle (ΔxΔp ≥ ℏ/2, ΔEΔt ≥ ℏ/2), Schrödinger equation (time-dependent: iℏ∂ψ/∂t = Ĥψ; time-independent: Ĥψ = Eψ), wave function & probability interpretation (|ψ|²dx = probability), normalization, superposition principle.
  • 1D Problems: Infinite square well (infinite square well: E_n = n²π²ℏ²/(2mL²), ψ_n(x) = √(2/L) sin(nπx/L)), finite square well, harmonic oscillator (H = p²/(2m) + (1/2)mω²x², E_n = ℏω(n + 1/2), Hermite polynomials), free particle (plane waves), potential step, potential barrier (tunneling, transmission coefficient), delta function potential.
  • 3D Problems: Spherical coordinates, spherical harmonics (Y_l^m(θ,φ)), hydrogen atom (energy levels E_n = -13.6 eV/n², degeneracy n²), angular momentum (L², L_z operators, [L_i, L_j] = iℏε_ijk L_k), spin (Pauli matrices, spin-1/2), addition of angular momenta (Clebsch-Gordan coefficients, total angular momentum J = L + S).
  • Approximation Methods: Time-independent perturbation theory (non-degenerate: E_n^(1) = ⟨n|H'|n⟩, E_n^(2) = Σ_{m≠n} |⟨m|H'|n⟩|²/(E_n - E_m); degenerate perturbation theory), variational method (Rayleigh-Ritz), WKB approximation, time-dependent perturbation theory (Fermi's golden rule: Γ = (2π/ℏ)|⟨f|H'|i⟩|²ρ(E)), sudden & adiabatic approximations.
  • Identical Particles: Symmetric & antisymmetric wave functions, bosons & fermions, exchange symmetry, Pauli exclusion principle (no two fermions in same quantum state), Bose-Einstein statistics & Fermi-Dirac statistics.

Thermodynamics & Statistical Mechanics

  • Thermodynamics: Zeroth law (temperature), First law (energy conservation: dU = δQ - dW), Second law (entropy: dS ≥ δQ/T, Clausius inequality), Third law (Nernst theorem: S → 0 as T → 0), thermodynamic potentials (internal energy U, enthalpy H = U + pV, Helmholtz free energy F = U - TS, Gibbs free energy G = H - TS), Maxwell relations (∂S/∂V|_T = (∂P/∂T)_V, etc.), phase transitions (first order: latent heat, discontinuous order parameter; second order: continuous order parameter, critical exponents), Clausius-Clapeyron equation (dP/dT = L/(TΔv)).
  • Statistical Mechanics: Microcanonical ensemble (isolated system, all microstates equally probable, S = k_B ln Ω), canonical ensemble (canonical distribution: P_i = e^(-βE_i)/Z, Z = partition function = Σ e^(-βE_i)), grand canonical ensemble (μ, V, T fixed: P_i = e^(-β(E_i - μN_i))/Z, grand partition function Z = Σ e^(-β(E_i - μN_i))), Maxwell-Boltzmann statistics, Bose-Einstein statistics, Fermi-Dirac statistics, ideal gas (classical & quantum), blackbody radiation (Planck's law: u(ν) = (8πhν³/c³)/(e^(hν/kT) - 1)), Bose-Einstein condensation.
  • Statistical Mechanics Applications: Ideal gas (classical & quantum), blackbody radiation (Planck's law), specific heat of solids (Dulong-Petit law: C_V = 3R, Einstein model: C_V = 3R(θ_E/T)² e^(θ_E/T)/(e^(θ_E/T) - 1)², Debye model: C_V = 9Nk_B(T/θ_D)³ ∫₀^(θ_D/T) x⁴ e^x/(e^x - 1)² dx, low T: C_V ∝ T³), paramagnetism (Curie's law: M = C/T·H, Curie's law), Pauli paramagnetism (free electron gas), ferromagnetism (Ising model - mean field theory: M = m tanh(βJqM + βh), Curie temperature T_c = zJq/k_B).
  • Phase Transitions: First order vs second order phase transitions, order parameter, critical exponents (α, β, γ, δ, ν, η), scaling relations (α + 2β + γ = 2, Rushbrooke; γ = ν(2 - η), Fisher), universality classes (Ising model: 2D Ising model exact solution by Onsager), mean field theory (Ising model - mean field approximation, critical exponents: β = 1/2, γ = 1, δ = 3, α = 0).
Technique: Schrödinger Equation - Infinite Square Well
Question: Solve the time-independent Schrödinger equation for a particle in a 1D infinite square well of width L.
Answer: V(x) = 0 for 0 < x < L, V(x) = ∞ elsewhere. TISE: -ℏ²/2m d²ψ/dx² = Eψ. General solution: ψ(x) = A sin(kx) + B cos(kx). Boundary conditions: ψ(0) = 0 → B = 0, ψ(L) = 0 → kL = nπ, k = nπ/L. Energy: E_n = n²π²ℏ²/(2mL²) = n²π²ℏ²/(2mL²) = n²π²ℏ²/(2mL²) = n²π²ℏ²/(2mL²) = n²π²ℏ²/(2mL²) = n²π²ℏ²/(2mL²). Normalized wavefunction: ψ_n(x) = √(2/L) sin(nπx/L), n = 1,2,3,...

Memory Tricks

  • Schrödinger Equation: iℏ∂ψ/∂t = Ĥψ (time-dependent), Ĥψ = Eψ (time-independent).
  • Thermodynamics Laws: 0th law (temperature), 1st law (energy conservation), 2nd law (entropy increases), 3rd law (S → 0 as T → 0).
  • Quantum numbers: n (principal), l (azimuthal), m_l (magnetic), m_s (spin).
Mains strategy
Physics answers: derive equations step by step, show all steps clearly, verify assumptions before applying theorems, draw diagrams (graphs, surfaces, curves), quote constants (ħ, c, G, k_B, e, m_e, m_p), quote experiments (Michelson-Morley, photoelectric effect, Stern-Gerlach, double-slit experiment).
