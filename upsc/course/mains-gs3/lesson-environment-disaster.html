1. Environment and Ecology

GS III environment covers conservation, environmental pollution and degradation, environmental impact assessment, climate change, and disaster management. The constitutional foundation: Article 48A (Directive Principle — State shall endeavour to protect and improve environment), Article 51A(g) (Fundamental Duty — every citizen to protect and improve natural environment). The Supreme Court (M.C. Mehta v. UoI, 1987; Vellore Citizens Forum v. UoI, 1996) has read Article 21 (Right to Life) to include right to clean environment. (Source: Constitution of India, Articles 48A, 51A(g); SC Judgments: M.C. Mehta v. UoI 1987; Vellore Citizens Forum v. UoI 1996)

1.1 Environmental Pollution: Air, Water, Soil, Plastic, E-Waste

1.1.1 Air Pollution

Key Pollutants: PM2.5 (fine particulate matter ≤2.5 μm), PM10, NOx, SO2, CO, O3 (ground-level ozone), NH3, Pb. Sources: Transport (vehicular emissions), Industry (thermal power, cement, steel, brick kilns), Agriculture (stubble burning — Punjab/Haryana/UP, ~15–20 Mt/yr), Construction/Demolition dust, Residential (biomass cooking), Waste burning. (Source: CPCB National Air Quality Monitoring Programme; SAFAR-IITM; Economic Survey 2023–24)

National Clean Air Programme (NCAP, 2019): Target: 20–30% reduction in PM2.5/PM10 by 2024 (base 2017) in 131 non-attainment cities. Revised target (2024): 40% reduction by 2026. Components: city-specific action plans, source apportionment studies, GRAP (Graded Response Action Plan) for Delhi-NCR, public grievance redressal (SAMEER app), PRANA portal for monitoring. (Source: MoEFCC NCAP Document 2019; Revised Guidelines 2024; PRANA Dashboard)

1.1.2 Water Pollution

Sources: Industrial effluents (textiles, tanneries, chemicals, pharma — 30% of BOD load), Domestic sewage (70% of BOD; ~72,000 MLD generated, ~28,000 MLD treated — 39% treatment capacity), Agricultural runoff (fertilisers, pesticides — eutrophication), Groundwater contamination (arsenic — West Bengal, Bihar, UP, Punjab, Assam; fluoride — Rajasthan, AP, Telangana, Karnataka; nitrate — agricultural areas). (Source: CPCB Water Quality Status Reports; CGWB Groundwater Quality; NITI Aayog Composite Water Management Index)

Namami Gange (2014): ₹20,000 cr (revised ₹40,000 cr); 400+ projects (STPs, riverfront, ghats, biodiversity, afforestation, industrial effluent monitoring). STP capacity created: 2,600+ MLD (target 3,300 MLD). Challenges: O&M of STPs, industrial non-compliance, faecal coliform persistence, tributary pollution (Yamuna, Gomti, Damodar). (Source: NMCG Annual Reports; CAG Report on Namami Gange 2022)

1.1.3 Plastic Waste and E-Waste

Plastic Waste Management Rules (2016, amended 2018, 2021, 2022): Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) — producers/importers/brand owners (PIBOs) responsible for collection/recycling; single-use plastic (SUP) ban (19 items, effective 1 July 2022) — earbuds, cutlery, straws, stirrers, wrapping films, PVC banners <100 µm; recycled content targets (2025–26 onwards); centralised EPR portal (CPCB). (Source: Plastic Waste Management Rules 2016, Amendments 2021/2022; CPCB EPR Portal)

E-Waste Management Rules (2016, amended 2018, 2022): EPR for producers (collection targets by weight — 30% of sales in FY23, 40% FY24, 50% FY25, 60% FY26, 70% FY27); authorised dismantlers/recyclers; reduction of hazardous substances (RoHS); EPR certificates tradable. India generates ~1.6 Mt e-waste/yr (3rd globally); formal recycling <20%. (Source: E-Waste Management Rules 2016, Amendments 2022; CPCB Annual Report; ASSOCHAM-EY Study)

1.2 Environmental Degradation: Land, Forests, Wetlands

Land Degradation: 97.8 Mha (29.7% of TGA) degraded (ISRO/NRSC 2018–19) — water erosion (37%), wind erosion (17%), salinity/alkalinity (4%), waterlogging (3%), others. Desertification: 82.6 Mha (25% TGA) undergoing desertification (UNCCD). (Source: ISRO/NRSC, Desertification and Land Degradation Atlas 2019; UNCCD National Report)

Forest Cover (ISFR 2021): 7,13,789 km² (21.71% GA); Very Dense Forest (VDF) 99,779 km², Moderately Dense (MDF) 3,06,890 km², Open Forest (OF) 3,07,120 km². Tree cover outside forests: 95,748 km². Target: 33% GA under forest/tree cover (National Forest Policy 1988). (Source: FSI, India State of Forest Report 2021)

Compensatory Afforestation (CAMPA): Forest (Conservation) Act 1980 — non-forest use requires compensatory afforestation + NPV (Net Present Value). Compensatory Afforestation Fund Act 2016 — National Fund (10%) + State Funds (90%); accumulated corpus ~₹54,000 cr (2023). Issues: monoculture plantations, low survival, diversion of funds, forest rights violations. (Source: Compensatory Afforestation Fund Act 2016; CAG Report on CAMPA 2020)

1.3 Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA)

EIA Notification 2006 (under EPA 1986): Prior Environmental Clearance (EC) for 39 categories (mining, thermal power, infrastructure, industry). Categories: A (MoEFCC appraisal) — large projects, sensitive areas; B1/B2 (State EIA Authority/SEAC) — smaller projects. Process: Screening → Scoping → Public Consultation (30 days notice) → Appraisal → EC (with conditions) → Post-EC monitoring (six-monthly compliance reports). (Source: EIA Notification 2006; MoEFCC)

Draft EIA 2020 Controversy: Post-facto clearance (regularisation of violations), reduced public notice (20 days), exemptions for "strategic" projects, longer validity, reduced compliance reporting. Not finalised; Supreme Court (2022) directed MoEFCC to address concerns. (Source: Draft EIA Notification 2020; SC Order in Writ Petition 2022)

PYQ Integration: EIA and Post-Facto Clearance (GS III 2020)
"The draft EIA Notification 2020 dilutes environmental safeguards. Critically examine."
Structure: (1) Key changes — post-facto clearance (violators can apply), reduced public consultation (20→30 days), exemption categories expanded (linear projects in border areas, "strategic" projects), compliance reporting annual→half-yearly, validity extended. (2) Critique — undermines precautionary principle (Vellore Citizens Forum 1996), weakens public participation (Aarhus Convention spirit), rewards violations, reduces monitoring. (3) Counter — streamlines process, reduces delays for strategic projects, digital platform. (4) SC view — post-facto clearance cannot be routine; polluter pays must apply. (5) Way forward — balance speed with safeguards; independent EIA authorities; cumulative impact assessment; grievance redressal. (Source: PYQ 2020 GS III; Draft EIA 2020; SC Orders; Vellore Citizens Forum Judgment)

2.2 Species Conservation Flagship Programmes

  • Project Tiger (1973): 55 reserves, 78,735 km². Tiger population: 3,682 (2022, range 3,167–3,925) — up from 2,967 (2018) and 1,411 (2006). NTCA (2006) — statutory body; tiger translocation (e.g., Sariska, Panna reintroduction). (Source: NTCA, All India Tiger Estimation 2022; WII)
  • Project Elephant (1992): 33 reserves; population ~30,000 (2017 census); human-elephant conflict (HEC) — 500+ human deaths/yr; corridors (88 identified), early warning, ex-gratia. (Source: Project Elephant Division, MoEFCC; WII)
  • Project Rhino (Assam): Indian Rhino Vision 2020 — population 3,000+ (Kaziranga 2,613, 2022); translocation to Manas, Pobitora. (Source: Assam Forest Dept; IRV 2020)
  • Other: Project Snow Leopard (2009), Project Hangul (Kashmir stag), Vulture Conservation (Action Plan 2020–25 — NSAID ban: diclofenac, ketoprofen, aceclofenac; breeding centres), Crocodile Breeding (gharial, mugger, saltwater), Sea Turtle (Olive Ridley arribada — Odisha, Gahirmatha/Rushikulya; leatherback — Andaman), Dugong (Gulf of Mannar, Palk Bay). (Source: MoEFCC Species Conservation Plans; WII)

2.3 Forest Rights Act (FRA) 2006 and Human-Wildlife Interface

FRA 2006: Recognises individual forest rights (IFR — cultivation, habitation) and community forest rights (CFR — minor forest produce, grazing, water, biodiversity, intellectual property) for Forest-Dwelling Scheduled Tribes (FDSTs) and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (OTFDs — 3 generations/75 years pre-2005). Gram Sabha is authority for vesting. (Source: FRA 2006; MoTA Guidelines)

Status (Mar 2024): 44 lakh claims filed; 23 lakh titles distributed (52%); CFR recognition abysmal (<10% of potential). Issues: Rejection without Gram Sabha consent, evidence demands (satellite imagery pre-2005), forest department resistance, compensation for relocation not paid, critical wildlife habitats (CWH) not notified. (Source: MoTA FRA Monthly Progress Report, Mar 2024; CFR-LA Reports)

Human-Wildlife Conflict (HWC): 500+ human deaths/yr (elephant, tiger, leopard, snake); crop/livestock loss ~₹1,000 cr/yr. Mitigation: Early warning (radio collars, SMS alerts), barriers (electric fences, trenches), compensation (state schemes — delays), corridor protection, community stewards (e.g., "Hathi Mitras" in Assam/Chhattisgarh). (Source: MoEFCC HWC Advisory 2021; State Forest Depts; WII)

2.4 Biodiversity Hotspots, International Conventions, and Access & Benefit Sharing

  • Biodiversity Hotspots in India (Conservation International): (1) Himalaya (Eastern + Western) — high endemism, glacial ecosystems; (2) Western Ghats — 5,000+ flowering plants, 325 globally threatened species; (3) Indo-Burma (NE India) — 7,000+ endemics; (4) Sundaland (Andaman & Nicobar) — marine/terrestrial endemism. (Source: Conservation International; WII; ZSI/BSI)
  • Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD, 1992): Objectives — conservation, sustainable use, fair and equitable benefit sharing. Nagoya Protocol (2010): Access to genetic resources and benefit sharing (ABS). India: Biological Diversity Act 2002 (amended 2023) — NBA, State Biodiversity Boards (SBBs), Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs) at local level. People's Biodiversity Registers (PBRs) — 2.7 lakh+ prepared. (Source: CBD; Nagoya Protocol; Biological Diversity Act 2002/2023; NBA Annual Reports)
  • CITES (1975): India party since 1976; WCCB (Wildlife Crime Control Bureau, 2007) — enforcement; TRAFFIC India partner. Appendix I species (tiger, elephant, rhino, pangolin, great Indian bustard) — trade prohibited. (Source: CITES; WCCB Annual Reports)

PYQ Integration: Climate Finance and Equity (GS III 2021)
"Climate finance commitments by developed countries remain unmet. Discuss implications for India's climate action."
Structure: (1) Commitment — $100 bn/yr by 2020 (Copenhagen 2009, reaffirmed Paris 2015); actual ~$83 bn (OECD 2020), mostly loans not grants; mitigation-biased (70%), adaptation <20%. (2) Implications for India — NDC conditional on finance/technology; solar/wind need $500+ bn by 2030 (IEA); adaptation (coastal, agriculture, Himalaya) underfunded. (3) India's stance — CBDR-RC; climate justice; historical emitters must lead; L&D fund (COP27) operationalisation; new collective quantified goal (NCQG) post-2025. (4) Domestic mobilisation — Green bonds (₹36,000 cr FY23–24), NABARD climate fund, perform-achieve-trade (PAT), green taxonomy (RBI), sovereign green bonds. (5) Way forward — grant-based finance, technology transfer (IPR waivers), capacity building, reform MDBs (World Bank/ADB) for climate lending. (Source: PYQ 2021 GS III; OECD Climate Finance Reports; COP Decisions; India's NDC; Budget 2024–25)

3.4 Adaptation: Key Sectors and Initiatives

  • Agriculture: Climate-resilient varieties (ICAR — 1,900+ varieties, 2014–24: drought/flood/heat tolerant); PKVY (organic, 12 lakh ha); rainfed area development (RAD); crop insurance (PMFBY — adaptation instrument); agroforestry (SAMRIDH). (Source: ICAR Climate-Resilient Varieties; NMSA; DAC&FW)
  • Water: Jal Shakti Abhiyan (water conservation, rainwater harvesting); Atal Bhujal Yojana (groundwater management); river rejuvenation; aquifer mapping (NAQUIM). (Source: DoWR; CGWB)
  • Coastal: Coastal Zone Management Plans (CZMPs); mangrove plantation (MISHTI — 540 km² by 2030); cyclone shelters; saline embankments. (Source: MoEFCC CZMPs; MISHTI Guidelines)
  • Urban: Heat Action Plans (Ahmedabad model — 30+ cities); urban flooding (Sponge City concept); green buildings (GRIHA, ECBC); public transport (metro, BRTS). (Source: NDMA Heat Wave Guidelines; Smart Cities Mission)
  • Himalaya: Glacier monitoring (GSI, WII, ISRO); glacial lake outburst flood (GLOF) early warning (NDMA-SDMA); springshed revival; community-based adaptation. (Source: NMSHE; NDMA GLOF Guidelines; ICIMOD)

4.3 Disaster Management Cycle: From Response to Resilience

Sendai Framework for DRR (2015–2030): 4 Priorities — (1) Understanding disaster risk; (2) Strengthening governance; (3) Investing in DRR (structural + non-structural); (4) Enhancing preparedness for effective response and "Build Back Better" in recovery. India aligned National Plan (2016, revised 2019) with Sendai. (Source: Sendai Framework 2015; NDMA National Disaster Management Plan 2019)

4.4 Technological and Community Innovations

  • Early Warning Systems: IMD — Doppler Weather Radars (35+), INSAT-3D/3DR, Nowcasting (3-hr), Impact-based Forecasting (cyclone/flood/heat). INCOIS — Indian Tsunami Early Warning Centre (ITEWC), Storm Surge Guidance. CWC — Flood Forecasting Network (Level + Inflow). ISRO/NRSC — Satellite imagery (Cartosat, RISAT, Oceansat) for damage assessment, landslide mapping, crop loss. (Source: IMD/INCOIS/CWC/ISRO Annual Reports)
  • Common Alerting Protocol (CAP): Standardised alert format (XML) disseminated via SMS, TV, radio, sirens, mobile apps (SAMEER, UMANG, NDMA Alert). (Source: NDMA CAP Guidelines; TRAI)
  • Aapda Mitra: Community volunteers (1 lakh+ trained across 350 districts); first responders; equipped with kits; honorarium during deployment. (Source: NDMA Aapda Mitra Guidelines; MHA)
  • India Disaster Resource Network (IDRN): Online inventory of equipment, manpower, critical supplies (boats, pumps, generators, medical) across districts. (Source: NDMA IDRN Portal)
  • Post-Disaster Needs Assessment (PDNA): Standardised methodology (World Bank/UN/EU); used for Kerala 2018, Odisha 2019, Assam 2022 — links recovery financing to resilient reconstruction. (Source: NDMA PDNA Reports; World Bank)
PYQ Integration: Disaster Preparedness (GS III 2022)
"India's disaster management has shifted from response-centric to preparedness-centric. Evaluate with examples."
Structure: (1) DM Act 2005 — institutionalised NDMA/SDMA/DDMA; NDRF (16 battalions). (2) Preparedness gains — IMD cyclone forecasting (5-day track, landfall accuracy <50 km); Cyclone Phailin 2013 (1.2 Mn evacuated, ~44 deaths) vs 1999 Odisha Super Cyclone (~10,000 deaths); heatwave action plans (Ahmedabad model — 30% mortality reduction); tsunami warning (INCOIS — <10 min). (3) Gaps — urban flooding (Chennai 2015, Bengaluru 2022, Delhi 2023 — drainage, encroachment); GLOF monitoring (Chamoli 2021, Sikkim 2023 — sensors installed late); landslide early warning (Wayanad 2024 — no warning); drought declaration (manual → satellite, still delayed); post-disaster livelihood restoration (weak). (4) Emerging — CAP alerts, Aapda Mitra, IDRN, PDNA, Build Back Better (Kerala housing). (5) Way forward — impact-based forecasting, urban resilience (sponge cities), glacial lake monitoring network, community-led DRR, climate-resilient infrastructure standards. (Source: PYQ 2022 GS III; NDMA Reports; IMD Cyclone Reports; Kerala/ Odisha Post-Disaster Assessments)

4.5 Biological Disasters and Pandemic Preparedness

COVID-19 (2020–2022): First major biological disaster under DM Act 2005 (notified as "notified disaster"). Response: National lockdown (Mar–May 2020), NDRF deployment, PM CARES Fund, vaccine development (Covaxin, Covishield) — 220+ cr doses administered (Mar 2024), CoWIN platform. Lessons: Need for pandemic-specific plan (National Disaster Management Plan 2019 had limited bio-hazard detail); surge capacity (oxygen, ICU, ventilators); genomic surveillance (INSACOG); risk communication; migrant worker crisis; federal coordination challenges. (Source: MHA COVID Orders; ICMR/INSACOG; MoHFW; PM CARES Audit)

Other Biological Hazards: Nipah (Kerala 2018, 2021, 2023), Zika, Dengue/Chikungunya (vector-borne), Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR — National Action Plan 2017–21, revised 2023), Food safety (FSSAI), Animal disease (Lumpy Skin Disease 2022 — 2 lakh cattle deaths; FMD control). (Source: ICMR; DAHD; NCDC; FSSAI; NAP-AMR)

Common Traps

  • Confusing mitigation (reducing emissions) with adaptation (coping with impacts).
  • Missing that EIA 2006 is the operative notification; draft EIA 2020 was controversial and not finalised as proposed.
  • Thinking all forests are protected — only notified national parks/sanctuaries have strict protection; reserve/production forests allow regulated use.
  • Assuming India is bound by Kyoto targets — India had no binding targets (developing country, no binding commitments under first period).
  • Overlooking that NDMA is chaired by the PM, SDMA by the CM — political heads, not bureaucrats.
  • Confusing NDRF (Central force, 16 battalions) with SDRF (State force, funded 75:25).
  • Thinking "Build Back Better" only means stronger houses — includes livelihoods, governance, environment, social protection.
  • Missing that FRA 2006 recognises community forest rights (CFR) not just individual rights — CFR recognition is the bigger gap.
  • Assuming Ramsar sites get automatic legal protection — they need state notification under Wetlands Rules 2017.
  • Confusing emissions intensity (emissions/GDP) with absolute emissions — India's intensity falls but absolute emissions rise.

Memory Tricks

  • Climate agreements: UNFCCC (framework), Kyoto (targets for developed), Paris (NDCs for all), Glasgow (net zero 2070 for India).
  • Disaster cycle: Prevent → Mitigate → Prepare → Respond → Recover → Rebuild (Build Back Better).
  • India's climate pledges (Panchamrit): 500 GW non-fossil by 2030; 50% renewable energy; 45% emissions intensity reduction; 1 bn tonne CO2 reduction; Net zero 2070.
  • Protected Area hierarchy: National Park (strictest) > Sanctuary > Conservation Reserve > Community Reserve.
  • Ramsar: Wetlands of international importance (82 sites in India).
  • EIA Categories: A (Centre), B1/B2 (State); Public consultation mandatory for A & B1.
  • DM Funds: NDRF (Centre), SDRF (75:25 Centre:State), NDMF (mitigation).
  • Seismic Zones: II (low) → V (very high); Zone V — NE, J&K, HP, Uttarakhand, Gujarat, Andaman.
Mains Exam-Day Strategy
For environment, link global agreements (Paris, Kyoto, CBD, Ramsar) to India's domestic actions (NAPCC missions, solar mission, EV policy, CAMPA, FRA, WLPA). For disasters, structure by disaster cycle — don't just list events; discuss mitigation/preparedness (early warning, building codes, NDMA guidelines, Aapda Mitra). Cite recent disasters (cyclones Fani/Amphan/Biparjoy, floods Kerala/Himachal/Assam, heatwaves, Chamoli/Sikkim GLOF, Wayanad landslide) and institutional response (NDRF, IMD forecasts, CAP alerts). Conclude with community participation, technology (satellite early warning, CAP), climate-resilient infrastructure, and mainstreaming DRR in development planning (Sendai alignment).
