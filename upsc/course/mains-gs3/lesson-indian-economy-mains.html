Key Concepts

Economic Growth and Development

Indian economy is the world's fifth-largest by nominal GDP and third-largest by PPP. GS III economy covers growth, development, planning, mobilisation of resources, and inclusive growth challenges.

  • GDP vs GVA: GDP = gross value added + product taxes − subsidies. GVA = value of output − intermediate consumption. GVA by sector shows sectoral performance; GDP includes taxes.
  • Growth vs development: Growth = increase in output/income; development = growth + improvement in health, education, equity, freedom (HDI, multidimensional poverty).
  • HDI: Human Development Index (UNDP) — health (life expectancy), education (schooling), standard of living (GNI per capita). India ranks ~134/193 (2022).
  • Planning in India: Planning Commission (1950–2014) replaced by NITI Aayog (2015) — think tank, cooperative federalism, Aspirational Districts Programme, no five-year plans since 12th Plan (2012–17).
  • Mobilisation of resources: Tax revenue (direct — income, corporate; indirect — GST), non-tax (dividends, disinvestment, spectrum), borrowing (market stabilisation scheme, ways and means), external (World Bank, ADB, bilateral, sovereign bonds).

Fiscal Policy and Budgeting

  • Union Budget: Article 112 — Annual Financial Statement. Revenue account (current income/expenditure) and capital account (assets/liabilities). Presented Feb 1 (since 2017), merged Railway Budget (2017).
  • Fiscal deficit: Total expenditure − total receipts (excluding borrowings). FRBM Act 2003 targets — eliminate revenue deficit, reduce fiscal deficit to 3% of GDP (targets relaxed post-pandemic).
  • Revenue deficit: Revenue expenditure exceeds revenue receipts — indicates government is borrowing for consumption, not investment.
  • Primary deficit: Fiscal deficit minus interest payments — shows current-year borrowing excluding past debt burden.
  • Deficit financing: Borrowing from RBI (monetised deficit — now limited), market borrowing, external debt.
  • GST: 101st Amendment 2016, launched July 1, 2017 — one nation one tax, subsumed ~17 central/state taxes, dual GST (CGST/SGST/IGST), GST Council (constitutional body, Article 279A), compensation cess (ends 2026).

Monetary Policy and Banking

  • RBI: Established 1935, nationalised 1949; monetary authority, banker to banks/government, currency issuer, regulator, manager of forex.
  • Monetary Policy Committee (MPC): 2016 (Finance Act), 6 members (3 RBI + 3 external), inflation targeting — CPI inflation 4% ± 2% band; repo rate is policy rate.
  • Policy rates: Repo (RBI lends to banks), reverse repo (RBI borrows), MSF (marginal standing facility — emergency overnight), bank rate (same as MSF), CRR (cash reserve ratio), SLR (statutory liquidity ratio).
  • Inflation: CPI (retail, headline), WPI (wholesale), core inflation (excluding food and fuel). Causes — demand-pull, cost-push, imported (oil).
  • Banking: Public sector banks (PSBs), private banks, small finance banks, payment banks, regional rural banks (RRBs), local area banks. NPAs (bad loans), Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code 2016, NCLT, SARFAESI Act, one-time settlement, bad bank (NARCL).
  • Financial inclusion: PMJDY (zero-balance accounts), MUDRA, Stand-Up India, SHG-bank linkage, BC (business correspondent) model, Jan Dhan-Aadhaar-Mobile (JAM) trinity, DBT.
  • Digital payments: UPI (NPCI), RTGS, NEFT, IMPS, BHIM, RuPay, AEPS, e-RUPI; RBI's regulatory sandbox.

External Sector

  • Balance of Payments (BoP): Current account (trade in goods/services, income, transfers) and capital account (FDI, FPI, ECB, NRI deposits, banking capital). BoP = current + capital + errors/omissions + reserve changes.
  • FDI vs FPI: FDI — long-term, controlling interest, brings technology/management (automatic route + government route sectors); FPI — portfolio investment, short-term, volatile (hot money).
  • Forex reserves: Foreign currency assets, gold, SDR, IMF reserve tranche; managed by RBI; import cover ~9–10 months.
  • Exchange rate: Managed float (RBI intervenes to curb volatility); real effective exchange rate (REER); rupee depreciation/appreciation effects on trade and inflation.
  • Trade agreements: FTAs (ASEAN, Japan, South Korea, UAE, Australia — ECTA/ECTA), bilateral investment treaties, RCEP (India opted out, 2019), WTO.

Inclusive Growth and Employment

  • Employment: PLFS (Periodic Labour Force Survey) — unemployment rate, labour force participation rate (LFPR), worker-population ratio; informal sector ~90%; gig/platform workers; Code on Wages 2019, occupational safety codes.
  • Poverty: NITI Aayog MPI, Tendulkar/Rangarajan lines; schemes — MGNREGA, NFSA, PMJAY, PMUY, PMAY, NSAP.
  • MSMEs: Definition (investment and turnover criteria, revised 2020), Udyam registration, Credit Guarantee Fund (CGTMSE), PMEGP, SFURTI, CHAMPIONS portal; contribution to GDP (~30%), exports (~45%), employment (~11 crore).
  • Startups: Startup India (2016), Fund of Funds (SIDBI), tax holidays, DPIIT recognition, unicorns; India is 3rd largest startup ecosystem.
  • Ease of Doing Business: India improved ranking (World Bank, discontinued 2021); reforms — single window, labour codes, GST, insolvency.

Investment Models

  • PPP (Public-Private Partnership): BOT (build-operate-transfer), BOOT, BOO, BLT, O&M; viability gap funding (VGF), Indian Infrastructure Finance Company (IIFCL), National Investment and Infrastructure Fund (NIIF).
  • Disinvestment/Privatisation: Strategic disinvestment (Air India, BPCL, Shipping Corporation), CPSE (central public sector enterprises), NIP (National Infrastructure Pipeline, ₹111 lakh crore).
Technique: Fiscal deficit framing
Question: “Fiscal deficit targets under FRBM are often breached — is the Act ineffective?”
Answer: FRBM Act 2003 mandates fiscal consolidation but escape clauses allow deviation (national security, calamity, structural reforms). Deviations occurred post-2008 crisis and during COVID (fiscal deficit ~9–10% in FY21). The Act institutionalised transparency (medium-term fiscal policy statement) but lacks enforcement teeth — targets are aspirational, and off-budget borrowings (FCI, NSSF) mask true deficit.
Technique: Monetary policy transmission
Question: “Repo rate cuts by RBI do not always reach borrowers.” Explain.
Answer: Transmission depends on banks' cost of funds, deposit rates, liquidity (surplus/deficit), NPA burden, and credit demand. If banks have excess liquidity or high NPAs, they may not pass on rate cuts. MPC's external benchmark linkage (since Oct 2019 — loans linked to repo/T-bill) improved transmission for new floating-rate loans.

Common Traps

  • Confusing fiscal deficit (total borrowing) with revenue deficit (borrowing for consumption).
  • Assuming FDI and FPI are similar — FDI is stable and long-term; FPI is volatile.
  • Missing that GST is a destination-based consumption tax, not origin-based.
  • Thinking RBI directly controls inflation — it influences via policy rates; supply-side inflation (food, oil) is harder to control.
  • Overlooking that NPA recognition and resolution (IBC) are different steps.

Memory Tricks

  • Deficits: Revenue < Fiscal < Primary (narrowest to widest concept).
  • RBI rates: Repo = RBI lends; Reverse repo = RBI borrows; MSF = emergency.
  • GST: One nation, one tax, one market.
  • External: Current account = trade + transfers; Capital account = investments + loans.
Mains exam-day strategy
For economy questions, always cite recent data (Budget, Economic Survey, RBI bulletin, PLFS, CPI/WPI). Use correct terminology (GVA, fiscal deficit, repo, CRR, FDI/FPI distinction). For policy questions, discuss objectives, achievements, gaps, and reforms. Conclude with a way forward — fiscal consolidation, financial inclusion, MSME support, infrastructure investment.
