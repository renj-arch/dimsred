1.2 Nuclear Technology — Three-Stage Programme, Civil Cooperation, and Regulation

India's nuclear power programme, conceptualised by Homi Bhabha (1950s), leverages vast thorium reserves (~25% world share, monazite sands in Kerala, Odisha, Tamil Nadu) against limited domestic uranium. DAE Annual Report 2023

Stage 2FBR (Fast Breeder Reactor)Plutonium (from Stage 1) + U-238 → breeds more Pu; Th-232 → U-233PFBR (500 MW, Kalpakkam) — criticality achieved 2024; more FBRs planned
Stage 3Thorium-based (AHWR, ABWR)U-233 (bred from Th) + Th-232; sustainable, long-termAHWR-300 design ready; deployment post FBR commercialisation

Civil Nuclear Cooperation: NSG waiver (2008) — India not NPT signatory; bilateral agreements with US (123 Agreement 2008), France, Russia, Japan, Canada, Australia, Kazakhstan, Mongolia. NSG 2008; MEA 123 Agreement 2008 Uranium imports (Kazakhstan, Canada, Australia, Uzbekistan) fuel safeguarded reactors.

Regulation: AERB (Atomic Energy Regulatory Board) — safety oversight; NPCIL (operator); BHAVINI (FBR operator). Weapons Doctrine: No First Use (NFU, 1998 declared policy); voluntary moratorium on testing; Credible Minimum Deterrence. Draft Nuclear Doctrine 1999; CCS 2003

Critique: Nuclear Programme Gaps
(1) Stage 2 delay — PFBR criticality 2024 vs original 2010 target; commercial FBR fleet 2030s+. (2) Uranium supply vulnerability — NSG membership blocked by China (NPT condition); import dependence persists. (3) Liability law (Civil Liability for Nuclear Damage Act 2010) — supplier liability clause deters foreign vendors (Westinghouse, GE-Hitachi, Areva). (4) Waste management — no deep geological repository; interim storage only. (5) AERB independence — statutory backing pending (Nuclear Safety Regulatory Authority Bill lapsed).

1.3 Defence Technology — Indigenisation and Strategic Capabilities

DRDO (1958) leads defence R&D. Key systems: Missiles — Agni (I–V, IRBM/ICBM up to 5,000+ km), Prithvi (SRBM), Akash (SAM, Akash-NG), BrahMos (supersonic cruise, Indo-Russia, 290 km → 450 km extended, export to Philippines), Nirbhay (subsonic cruise), Astra (BVR AAM), Shaurya (hypersonic tech demonstrator). DRDO Annual Report 2023 Platforms — LCA Tejas (Mk1A 83 ordered), Arjun Mk1A MBT, Pinaka MLRS (guided), BrahMos coastal/ship/air variants. Strategic Triad — Land (Agni), Air (Rafale, Jaguar, Mirage nuclear-capable), Sea (INS Arihant SSBN, K-4 SLBM 3,500 km). Indian Defence Review 2023

PYQ 2022 (15 marks): "Discuss the achievements of DRDO in the field of missile technology and their significance for India's strategic deterrence."
Model: Classify by range/type (strategic Agni series, tactical Prithvi/Prahar, air defence Akash, cruise BrahMos/Nirbhay, AAM Astra). For each: range, propulsion, guidance, induction status. Strategic significance — credible minimum deterrence, NFU posture, second-strike capability (K-4, Arihant). Indigenisation metrics — % indigenous content (Agni-V ~80%, BrahMos ~65%+), positive indigenisation lists (DAP 2020), export success (BrahMos Philippines $375M). Gaps — long-range cruise, hypersonic (HSTDV tested 2020, 2022), MIRV (Agni-P test 2024).

2.2 Semiconductor Mission and Electronics Manufacturing

India Semiconductor Mission (ISM, 2021) — ₹76,000 crore incentive scheme for chip fabrication, display fabs, compound semiconductors, OSAT, design. MeitY ISM 2021 Approved projects: Tata-PSMC (Dholera, 50,000 wafers/mo, 28nm), CG Power-Renesas (Sanand, OSAT), Micron (Sanand, ATMP), Tata Semiconductor Assembly (Morigaon). Critique: Semiconductor Ambitions
(1) Technology gap — 28nm (Tata-PSMC) vs global 3nm/2nm; no leading-edge fab. (2) Talent — 85,000 semiconductor professionals targeted by 2032; current ~20,000. (3) Ecosystem — EDA tools, IP cores, packaging materials largely imported. (4) Water/power — fabs need ultra-pure water, uninterrupted power; Gujarat/Assam infrastructure untested at scale. (5) Geopolitics — US CHIPS Act, EU Chips Act, Taiwan/TSMC dominance; India late entrant.

3.4 R&D Ecosystem, Innovation Indices, and IPR Regime

R&D Spend: 0.64% GDP (2022–23) — target 2% (STIP 2013, reiterated STIP 2020 draft); Centre:State ~60:40; business enterprise R&D (BERD) only ~37% (vs 70%+ in US, China, Korea). DST R&D Statistics 2023 Researchers: 255 per million (2020) vs 4,300 (Korea), 1,200 (China). Global Innovation Index (GII): 40th (2023) from 81st (2015). WIPO GII 2023

Institutional Architecture: CSIR (38 labs), ICAR (100+ institutes), ICMR (27 institutes), DST, DBT, DAE, DRDO, ISRO, MeitY, MoES. Anusandhan National Research Foundation (ANRF) Act 2023 — replaces SERB; ₹50,000 cr (2023–28), 72% private sector; strategic, translational, basic research. ANRF Act 2023

IPR Regime: Patents Act 1970 (amended 2002, 2005 — TRIPS compliance, product patents); Compulsory Licensing (Sec 84 — Natco/Bayer Nexavar 2012 first grant); Section 3(d) — enhanced efficacy bar (Novartis Glivec SC 2013 rejection); National IPR Policy 2016 — 7 objectives (awareness, generation, legal framework, administration, commercialisation, enforcement, adjudication). Patents Act 1970; SC 2013; DIPP 2016 Patent Filing: 90,300 (2022–23); residents 45% (up from 30% 2015); grant rate ~30%; pendency ~42 months. IPO Annual Report 2023

Critique: R&D and IPR Gaps
(1) Low BERD — PLI schemes target manufacturing, not R&D; tax incentives (weighted deduction 150% withdrawn 2020, partially restored). (2) University-industry disconnect — <1% industry-funded research in IITs/IISc. (3) Patent quality — low grant rate, high abandonment; Section 3(d) deters pharma innovation (industry view) vs prevents evergreening (public health view). (4) ANRF — private sector 72% target ambitious; governance (Governing Board chaired by PM) centralised. (5) STIP 2020 draft not finalised — policy vacuum.
Answer-Writing Framework for S&T Questions (10/15 marks)
Deconstruct: Identify theme (space/nuclear/biotech/AI/cyber/R&D/IPR) and directive (examine/discuss/analyse/critically evaluate).
Thesis: One-sentence position — e.g., "India's space programme demonstrates application-first philosophy with emerging strategic signalling."
Evidence Matrix: Scheme/Mission → Achievement → Gap → Reform. Use 3–4 concrete data points (budget, timeline, indigenous %, global rank).
Institutional/Legal Layer: Cite Act, Policy, Body (e.g., DPDP Act 2023, ANRF Act 2023, IN-SPACe Act 2020, DAP 2020).
Critique: 2–3 structural gaps (funding, talent, regulatory, geopolitical) with source.
Way Forward: Specific, implementable — not generic "more funding." E.g., "Extend weighted deduction to 200% for deep-tech R&D; mandate 1% turnover R&D spend for PLI beneficiaries."
Conclusion: Tie to national goal (Atmanirbhar, $5T economy, strategic autonomy, SDGs).

Common Traps

  • Confusing PSLV (polar, remote sensing, 4 stages, solid/liquid) with GSLV (geosynchronous, heavier, cryogenic upper stage) and SSLV (small sat, on-demand, 3 stages all solid). ISRO 2024
  • Thinking Bt brinjal is commercially grown — moratorium since 2010; only Bt cotton approved. GEAC 2010
  • Assuming India is NSG member — 2008 waiver only; membership blocked by China (NPT non-signatory condition). MEA 2024
  • Overlooking NFU is declared policy, not treaty obligation; "credible minimum deterrence" allows arsenal expansion. CCS 2003
  • Confusing CERT-IN (incident response) with NCIIPC (CII protection) with I4C (cyber crime coordination) — distinct mandates, overlapping in practice. MHA/MeitY 2024
  • Thinking ANRF replaces all science agencies — it co-exists with CSIR, DST, DBT, DAE, DRDO, ISRO; only replaces SERB. ANRF Act 2023

4.2 Genomics, Precision Health, and Biobanking

Genome India Project (2020): 10,000 whole genomes from 99 ethnic groups — India's first population-scale reference genome. DBT 2024 INSACOG (2020): 60+ labs for SARS-CoV-2 genomic surveillance; detected Delta (B.1.617.2), Omicron sub-lineages; model for pathogen genomics. IndiGen (CSIR, 2019): 1,008 whole genomes; pharmacogenomic variants (CYP2C19, SLCO1B1) for drug dosing. Biobanking: ICMR-NIB (National Institute of Biomedical Genomics) — 50,000+ samples; NIBMG biobank; ethical framework (ICMR National Ethical Guidelines 2017, DPDP Act 2023 for genomic data).

Critique: Genomics Policy Gaps
(1) No dedicated genomics law — genomic data falls under DPDP Act 2023 (sensitive personal data) but lacks specific provisions for familial implications, re-identification risk, insurance/employment discrimination. (2) Clinical translation lag — pharmacogenomic guidelines not integrated into standard treatment protocols (e.g., clopidogrel/CYP2C19, warfarin/VKORC1). (3) Infrastructure — long-read sequencing (PacBio, ONT) concentrated in 3-4 centres; compute/storage for petabyte-scale data inadequate. (4) Workforce — <500 trained clinical geneticists; genetic counselling not a recognised profession. (5) Industry — diagnostic labs export samples for sequencing; domestic reagent/kits manufacturing nascent.

4.4 GM Crops, Gene Editing, and Regulatory Architecture

Current Status: Bt Cotton (2002) — only commercial GM crop (Cry1Ac, Cry2Ab); ~95% cotton area. Bt Brinjal (2010) — GEAC approval overturned by MoEFCC moratorium (indefinite); SC 2023 referred to larger bench. GM Mustard (DHM-11, 2022) — GEAC recommended environmental release; SC hearing pending (barstar-barnase system, herbicide tolerance). GEAC 2022; SC 2023-24

Gene Editing (SDN-1/SDN-2): DBT 2022 guidelines — SDN-1 (site-directed nuclease, no foreign DNA) and SDN-2 (template-guided repair) exempt from GMO regulation if no foreign DNA; SDN-3 (transgenic) regulated as GMO. DBT 2022 Applications: ICAR — CRISPR for bacterial blight resistance in rice, drought tolerance in wheat, high-oleic acid in mustard, low-acrylamide potato. Regulatory Bodies: GEAC (MoEFCC, EPA 1986 Rules 1989), RCGM (DBT), IBSC (institutional), SBCC (state), DLC (district).

Critique: GM Crop Regulatory Paralysis
(1) Decision-making vacuum — GEAC recommends, MoEFCC decides, SC adjudicates; no finality (Bt brinjal 14 years, GM mustard 2+ years). (2) Federal friction — agriculture State List; states can block field trials (Kerala, Tamil Nadu, West Bengal opposed GM mustard trials). (3) Evidence vs precaution — 20+ years of Bt cotton data (yield +24%, insecticide -40%, farmer income +68% — ICAR 2018) vs ideological opposition. (4) Gene editing regulatory clarity — DBT 2022 guidelines progressive but not legislated; EPA Rules 1989 amendment pending. (5) Trade implications — non-GM certification demand (EU, Japan); identity preservation infrastructure absent.
Answer-Writing Framework: Health & Biotech Policy (10/15 marks)
Deconstruct: Theme (vaccines/genomics/bioeconomy/GM crops) + Directive (examine/discuss/analyse).
Thesis: One sentence — e.g., "India's COVID vaccine success demonstrates PPP potential but structural gaps in mRNA platforms, genomic law, and GM regulatory closure limit pandemic preparedness."
Institutional-Scheme Layer: Name bodies (DBT, ICMR, CDSCO, BIRAC, GEAC, NIBMG) and schemes (Mission COVID Suraksha, Genome India, BioE3, PLI Pharma, NBM).
Data Anchors: 2–3 verifiable numbers — $300B bioeconomy target, 2.2B COVID doses, 10,000 genomes, 95% Bt cotton coverage, 1,500 BIRAC startups.
Critique: 2–3 structural gaps — regulatory (GM paralysis, genomic law absent), capacity (mRNA, manufacturing, talent), federalism (State List agriculture).
Way Forward: Specific — e.g., "Enact Genomic Data Protection Act; codify EUA pathway in NDCT Rules; create sovereign mRNA platform fund ₹2,000 cr; amend EPA Rules 1989 for gene editing; establish National Biomanufacturing Institute."
Conclusion: Link to health security (Ayushman Bharat, NHP 2017), economic sovereignty (Atmanirbhar, $5T economy), and global leadership (Vaccine Maitri, Quad vaccine partnership).

8.3.3 Concerns & Governance Gaps

Critique: Blockchain — Hype vs Reality in Governance
(1) Garbage in, gospel out — blockchain ensures tamper-evidence after writing; it cannot verify truth of input data (oracle problem). Land records on blockchain still need ground-truth survey. (2) Scalability vs decentralisation — permissioned solves throughput but centralises trust in validator consortium; who governs the governors? (3) Legal recognition — IT Act 2000 Sec 65B (electronic evidence) applies but no specific blockchain evidence law; smart contract enforceability untested in Indian courts. (4) Privacy — DPDP Act 2023 requires data minimisation, purpose limitation, right to erasure; immutable ledger conflicts with erasure. Solutions: off-chain personal data + on-chain hashes, zero-knowledge proofs (nascent). (5) Key management — citizen wallet custody (seed phrases) is UX barrier; account abstraction, social recovery needed. (6) Energy — permissioned is low-energy but CBDC/retail scale (1B+ users) needs rigorous efficiency benchmarking.
