Key Concepts

Science and Technology — Developments and Applications

GS III S&T covers Indian achievements in science and technology, indigenisation of technology, developing new technology, and applications in everyday life.

  • Space: ISRO (1969), INSAT (communication), IRS (remote sensing), PSLV (workhorse), GSLV (cryogenic, heavy lifts), Chandrayaan-1 (2008, water on moon), Chandrayaan-2 (2019, orbiter + lander attempt), Chandrayaan-3 (2023, soft landing near south pole — first country), Mangalyaan/Mars Orbiter Mission (2013, first Asian nation to Mars orbit, first in first attempt), Aditya-L1 (solar mission, 2023), Gaganyaan (human spaceflight, planned), NavIC (regional navigation satellite system), GSAT series, earth observation (EOS), small satellite launch vehicle (SSLV), commercial arm NSIL, IN-SPACe (private sector promotion).
  • Nuclear: Three-stage programme (Homi Bhabha) — Stage 1: PHWR (natural uranium, heavy water), Stage 2: FBR (plutonium, thorium breeding), Stage 3: thorium-based reactors (ABWR, AHWR); India has large thorium reserves (monazite sands), limited uranium. Civil nuclear cooperation — NSG waiver 2008 (India not NPT signatory), deals with US, France, Russia, Japan; Kudankulam (Russia), Tarapur, Rawatbhata, Kalpakkam, Kakrapar. Nuclear regulation — AERB; weapons — No First Use doctrine, voluntary moratorium on testing.
  • Defence: DRDO (1958), missiles — Agni (IRBM/ICBM), Prithvi (SRBM), Akash (SAM), BrahMos (supersonic cruise, India-Russia), Nirbhay (subsonic cruise), Astra (BVR AAM), Shaurya; LCA Tejas (fighter), Arjun (tank), Pinaka (MLRS), BrahMos exports; indigenous sonar, radar (Uttam AESA), electronic warfare; STRATEGIC — nuclear triad (land, air, sea — SINS, Arihant-class SSBN).
  • Biotechnology: Bt cotton (only GM crop approved commercially), Bt brinjal (moratorium), GM mustard (DHM-11, approval contested), Genome India Council, biofuels (ethanol blending — E20 target 2025), biopharma (vaccines — Covishield, Covaxin, India as vaccine hub), biotechnology parks, BIRAC, DBT.
  • Nanotechnology: National Mission on Nano Science and Technology (Nano Mission, 2007) — applications in medicine (drug delivery), electronics, energy, textiles, water purification; toxicity and ethical concerns.
  • IT and electronics: ISRO, MeitY, Digital India, Semiconductor Mission (India Semiconductor Mission, 2021, chip fabrication), supercomputers (Param series, Pratyush, Mihir), AI (National AI Strategy, NITI Aayog; AI for All), quantum computing (National Mission on Quantum Technologies and Applications — ₹8000 crore), blockchain, 5G (rollout 2022), IoT, cybersecurity (CERT-IN, Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre — I4C), data protection (Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023).
  • Health S&T: Telemedicine (e-Sanjeevani), genome sequencing (INSACOG — COVID variants), vaccines, diagnostics, medical devices (Ayushman Bharat digital health mission).

Awareness in IT, Space, Computers, Robotics

  • Robotics: Industrial automation, healthcare robots, defense robots (Daksh — bomb disposal), space robotics (rover Pragyan on Chandrayaan-3), humanoid (Vyommitra for Gaganyaan).
  • Computers: Supercomputing mission, quantum computing, edge computing, cloud, AI ethics (bias, transparency, job displacement).
  • Space applications: GPS-aided navigation (NavIC), telemedicine, tele-education, disaster management (flood/cyclone monitoring), agriculture (crop insurance, soil mapping), urban planning, climate monitoring.

Indigenisation of Technology

  • Policy: Defence Acquisition Procedure (DAP 2020) — Buy Indian-IDDM priority, indigenously designed developed manufactured (IDDM); positive indigenisation lists (embargo on import of weapon systems); Atmanirbhar Bharat; Make in India; defence FDI limit raised (74% automatic, 100% government route).
  • Examples: Tejas, Arjun, Akash, BrahMos, INS Arihant, Tejas Mk1A, UAVs (Rustom, Heron), naval vessels (Mazagon Dock, GRSE), missiles.
Technique: ISRO achievements
Question: “India's space programme is application-oriented rather than prestige-driven.” Examine.
Answer: ISRO focuses on societal applications — remote sensing (agriculture, water, urban planning), communication (telemedicine, tele-education, DTH), navigation (NavIC), meteorology, disaster management (flood/cyclone forecasting), satellite-aided search and rescue (SAS&R), and space science (Chandrayaan, Mangalyaan, Aditya-L1). Cost-effective (Mangalyaan ~$74 million, Chandrayaan-3 ~$75 million). Commercial arm NSIL and IN-SPACe open space to private players.
Technique: Nuclear three-stage programme
Question: Why does India's nuclear power programme focus on thorium?
Answer: India has ~25% of world's thorium reserves (monazite sands in Kerala, Odisha) but limited uranium. Three-stage programme: Stage 1 PHWRs produce plutonium; Stage 2 FBRs breed more plutonium and convert thorium to U-233; Stage 3 uses U-233 and thorium for sustainable, long-term nuclear energy. NSG waiver 2008 enabled uranium imports for Stage 1 reactors under safeguards.

Common Traps

  • Confusing ISRO's launch vehicles — PSLV (polar, remote sensing), GSLV (geosynchronous, heavier, cryogenic), SSLV (small satellites, on-demand).
  • Missing that Chandrayaan-3's south pole landing (2023) made India the first country to soft-land near the lunar south pole.
  • Thinking Bt brinjal is commercially grown — it's under moratorium; only Bt cotton is approved.
  • Assuming India is an NSG member — waiver obtained (2008) but membership blocked by China (NPT condition).
  • Overlooking that India's No First Use nuclear doctrine is a declared policy, not a treaty obligation.

Memory Tricks

  • ISRO missions: Moon—Chandrayaan, Mars—Mangalyaan, Sun—Aditya-L1, Human—Gaganyaan, Navigation—NavIC.
  • Nuclear stages: Stage 1—uranium/PHWR; Stage 2—plutonium/FBR; Stage 3—thorium.
  • Defence missiles: Agni—surface-to-surface; Prithvi—short-range; Akash—surface-to-air; BrahMos—cruise.
Mains exam-day strategy
For S&T, connect technology to application (space → disaster management; biotech → vaccines; IT → governance). Mention Indian institutions (ISRO, DRDO, DBT, CSIR, ICMR, MeitY, NPCIL) and recent missions. For policy questions, discuss objectives, achievements, gaps, and the indigenisation angle (Atmanirbhar Bharat, Make in India, positive indigenisation lists). Conclude with ethical/safety concerns where relevant (AI bias, GM crops, nuclear safety, cybersecurity).
