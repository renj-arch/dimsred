Key Concepts

Indian Agriculture — Overview

Agriculture employs ~45% of India's workforce and contributes ~18% to GDP. GS III covers cropping patterns, irrigation, storage, transport, marketing, e-technology, and farmer welfare.

  • Cropping seasons: Kharif (monsoon, June–October — rice, cotton, sugarcane, jowar, bajra), Rabi (winter, October–March — wheat, gram, mustard, barley), Zaid (summer — vegetables, fodder, watermelon).
  • Major crop regions: Rice — West Bengal, UP, Andhra Pradesh, Punjab, Tamil Nadu; Wheat — UP, Punjab, Haryana, MP; Cotton — Maharashtra, Gujarat, Punjab, Haryana; Sugarcane — UP, Maharashtra, Karnataka; Tea — Assam, WB, Kerala; Coffee — Karnataka, Kerala, TN; Spices — Kerala, Karnataka; Jute — WB, Assam.
  • Agricultural revolutions: Green (wheat/rice, 1960s, M.S. Swaminathan), White (milk, Verghese Kurien, Operation Flood), Blue (fish), Yellow (oilseeds), Golden (fibres/horticulture), Silver (wool/cotton).
  • Land holdings: 86% small and marginal (<2 hectares), average holding size declining (~1.08 ha), fragmentation a constraint to mechanisation.
  • Land reforms: Abolition of zamindari, tenancy reforms, ceiling on holdings, consolidation of holdings, Bhoodan movement (Vinoba Bhave), computerisation of land records (Digital India Land Records Modernisation Programme).

Irrigation and Water Management

  • Sources: Wells and tube wells (largest share), tanks (south/east), canals (Indira Gandhi, Bhakra, Damodar, Upper Ganga), micro-irrigation (drip, sprinkler — PMKSY-PDMC).
  • Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana (PMKSY): "Har Khet ko Pani" — end-to-end solutions in source creation, distribution, management, field application; per drop more crop.
  • Issues: Waterlogging and salinity (canal areas), groundwater over-exploitation (Punjab, Haryana, Tamil Nadu), inequitable distribution, evaporation losses.

Agricultural Marketing and Reforms

  • APMC Acts: State-regulated mandis; issues — commission agents (arhatiyas), market fees, restricted competition, farmer exploitation.
  • e-NAM (National Agriculture Market, 2016): Online trading platform connecting APMCs; transparent price discovery; quality assaying.
  • Model APLM Act 2017: Allows direct marketing, private markets, farmer-consumer markets, e-trading; adopted by many states.
  • Farm laws 2020 (repealed 2021): Farmers' Produce Trade and Commerce (Promotion and Facilitation) Act, Farmers (Empowerment and Protection) Agreement on Price Assurance and Farm Services Act, Essential Commodities (Amendment) Act — repealed after farmer protests (2020–21).
  • Minimum Support Price (MSP): Announced for 23 crops (CACP recommends, Cabinet decides); procurement mainly rice/wheat (PDS); issue — MSP not legally guaranteed, limited reach beyond wheat-rice and select states.
  • Warehousing and storage: FCI, CWC, SWCs, private warehouses, cold storage (insufficient, ~10% of perishables), post-harvest losses (~10–15%, higher for fruits/vegetables).

Food Security and Public Distribution

  • National Food Security Act (NFSA), 2013: Right to food; covers up to 75% rural and 50% urban population; 5 kg foodgrains/person/month at ₹3/₹2/₹1 (rice/wheat/coarse grains); Antyodaya Anna Yojana (AAY) — 35 kg/household/month for poorest.
  • Public Distribution System (PDS): Fair price shops (FPS), issues — leakages, diversion, ghost beneficiaries, electronic PoS (e-PoS), Aadhaar-linked biometric authentication, direct benefit transfer in food grains (cash transfer experiments).
  • Mid-Day Meal / PM POSHAN: School meals, nutrition, enrolment; governed by NFSA Schedule II.
  • ICDS and Anganwadi: Supplementary nutrition, pre-school, health check-ups for children under 6 and pregnant/lactating mothers; Saksham Anganwadi and Poshan 2.0.
  • Buffer stock: FCI maintains buffer norms for food security; procurement (rice/wheat), storage, distribution; issue — high economic cost, rotting, regional concentration of procurement (Punjab-Haryana).

Agriculture Credit and Insurance

  • Credit: Kisan Credit Card (KCC, 1998), short-term and long-term credit, NABARD (refinancing), RRBs, PSBs, cooperatives, SHG-bank linkage; interest subvention.
  • Insurance: Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY, 2016) — crop insurance against yield loss, premium 2% (kharif)/1.5% (rabi)/5% (commercial), rest by Centre/States; issues — delayed claims, low coverage, area approach vs individual assessment.

Technology and Allied Sectors

  • E-technology: Kisan Suvidha, mKisan, soil health cards, e-choupal (private), drone use (nutrient/pesticide spraying), satellite imagery (crop yield estimation, Crop Insurance), e-marketplaces.
  • Animal husbandry: White revolution, National Livestock Mission, Rashtriya Gokul Mission (indigenous breeds), artificial insemination, vaccination (foot-and-mouth, lumpy skin disease), dairy cooperatives (Amul model — NDDB).
  • Fisheries: Blue Revolution, Pradhan Mantri Matsya Sampada Yojana (PMMSY), inland and marine fisheries, aquaculture, harbours, cold chain.
  • Horticulture: Mission for Integrated Development of Horticulture (MIDH), fruits, vegetables, flowers, spices; India is second-largest producer of fruits and vegetables.
  • Sericulture, apiculture, floriculture: Allied income sources for farmers.
Technique: MSP vs market price
Question: “MSP is not a legal right but a policy instrument.” Comment.
Answer: MSP is announced by the Cabinet on CACP recommendations for 23 crops; it is not backed by statute. Procurement is largely limited to wheat and rice (for PDS), mainly in Punjab-Haryana and select states. Farmers in most regions sell below MSP in open markets. Swaminathan Commission recommended C2+50% MSP; not implemented. A legal guarantee would raise fiscal burden and market distortion concerns but could address income instability.
Technique: PMFBY assessment
Question: “Crop insurance has not reached small farmers effectively.” Examine.
Answer: PMFBY covers yield losses (not post-harvest fully), premium is subsidised, but issues — area approach means individual farmers with low yield but high village average get nothing; delayed claim settlement (states must pay premium share); low awareness among smallholders; insurance companies' profitability focus. Reforms — technology (smart sampling, drones), individual assessment, faster claims, wider crop coverage.

Common Traps

  • Confusing MSP (minimum support price, govt announced) with market price (demand-supply determined).
  • Assuming all farmers benefit from PDS procurement — mostly wheat-rice growers in select states.
  • Missing that agriculture is a State subject (List II), Centre supports via Centrally Sponsored Schemes.
  • Overlooking post-harvest losses vs production losses — storage/transport gaps.
  • Thinking PMFBY covers all risks — it primarily covers yield loss, not price risk fully.

Memory Tricks

  • Revolutions: Green—grain, White—milk, Blue—fish, Yellow—oilseeds.
  • Seasons: Kharif—rain, Rabi—winter, Zaid—summer.
  • Food security trinity: Availability (production), Access (purchasing power), Absorption (nutrition/health).
  • Credit: KCC = short-term, NABARD = refinance institution.
Mains exam-day strategy
For agriculture, link production → marketing → credit → insurance → food security. Cite specific schemes (PMFBY, e-NAM, PMKSY, NFSA) and data (agriculture's GDP share, workforce %, small/marginal holding %). For water questions, discuss both supply (irrigation) and demand (water-guzzling crops like sugarcane/paddy in dry areas). Conclude with diversification, sustainable practices, and farmer income focus (doubling farmers' income framework).
