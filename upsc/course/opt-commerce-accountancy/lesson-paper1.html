Key Concepts

Commerce Paper I — Accounting & Company Law (Detailed)

Company Accounts

  • Issue of shares: Issue at par, premium, discount (prohibited except sweat equity), forfeiture & reissue of shares, surrender of shares, calls in arrears & calls in advance, interest on calls in arrears & calls in advance (Table F — 10% & 12% respectively), issue of debentures (at par/premium/discount, for cash, for consideration other than cash, as collateral security), interest on debentures, redemption of debentures (out of profits, out of capital, DMDR — Debenture Redemption Reserve 25% for listed, 10% for unlisted unless exempt), redemption out of proceeds of fresh issue/sinking fund/investment method.
  • Final accounts of companies: Schedule III format — balance sheet (equity & liabilities: share capital, reserves & surplus, money received against share warrants, share application money pending allotment, non-current liabilities: long-term borrowings, deferred tax liabilities, other long-term liabilities, long-term provisions; current liabilities: short-term borrowings, trade payables, other current liabilities, short-term provisions; assets: non-current assets — tangible/intangible/Capital WIP/intangible assets under development, non-current investments, deferred tax assets (net), long-term loans & advances, other non-current assets; current assets — current investments, inventories, trade receivables, cash & cash equivalents, short-term loans & advances, other current assets), statement of profit & loss (revenue from operations, other income, total revenue, expenses — cost of materials consumed, purchase of stock-in-trade, changes in inventories, employee benefits expense, finance costs, depreciation & amortisation, other expenses, total expenses, profit before tax, tax expense — current tax & deferred tax, profit for the period, other comprehensive income, total comprehensive income, EPS), cash flow statement (operating, investing, financing — direct & indirect method), statement of changes in equity.
  • Branch accounting: Debtors system, debtors & stock control system, stock & debtors system, final accounts system (wholesale & retail price), independent branches (foreign branches — conversion of foreign currency, translation of foreign branches — monetary items at closing rate, non-monetary at historical, income items at average rate, exchange difference to P&L).
  • Departmental accounting: Departmental trading & P&L account, allocation of common expenses (sales value, floor area, number of employees, time, direct allocation), inter-departmental transfers (at cost or at market price), unrealised profit elimination (1/3rd or 1/(1+margin%) of unsold stock).
  • Consignment accounts: Consignor & consignee, commission (ordinary, del-credere, del-credere & over-riding), valuation of unsold stock (cost or market price whichever lower, less proportionate expenses), abnormal loss (normal loss estimated & spread over good units; abnormal loss valued at cost and transferred to P&L), goods sent on consignment at cost or invoice price (with loading & reserve for unsold stock).
  • Joint venture accounts: Joint venture vs partnership vs consignment, methods — (1) separate set of books, (2) no separate set of books (each venturer records own transactions), (3) memorandum joint venture account method, settlement of accounts.
  • Royalty accounts: Minimum rent (dead rent), short-workings, recoupment of short-workings, strike & lock-out clause, sub-lease, accounting treatment in books of lessee & lessor.

Company Law in Detail

  • Company formation: Promoters (duties, liabilities, pre-incorporation contracts), incorporation (application to ROC, MoA — clauses: name, situation, object, liability, capital, subscription; AoA — contents, alteration, entrenchment), certificate of incorporation, commencement of business (declaration under Section 10A for companies with share capital).
  • Share capital: Authorised, issued, subscribed, called-up, paid-up capital; issue at par/premium/discount; share premium account (uses per Section 52), sweat equity, employee stock option scheme (ESOS), buyback (Section 68 — conditions: 25% of paid-up capital & free reserves, debt-equity ratio not exceeding 2:1 after buyback, declaration of solvency), redemption of preference shares (Section 55 — out of profits or fresh issue, DRR, premium out of premium account/profits), transfer & transmission of shares, forfeiture & reissue, lien on shares, calls on shares, surrender of shares.
  • Debentures: Types (secured/unsecured, registered/bearer, convertible/non-convertible, redeemable/irredeemable, first/second mortgage), issue at discount/premium, issue for consideration other than cash, as collateral security, interest on debentures (payable periodically, income tax deduction), redemption (out of profits — DRM/DRR, out of proceeds of fresh issue, purchase in open market), redemption methods (lump sum, instalments, purchase in open market, conversion into shares/debentures), DRR (25% for listed companies, 10% for unlisted — exemptions: infrastructure companies, banks, housing finance companies, etc.).
  • Company management: Board of directors (appointment, retirement by rotation, removal, remuneration, duties — fiduciary duties, duty of care, duty to act within powers, disclosure of interest, restriction on related party transactions — Section 188), key managerial personnel (KMD — MD, CEO, CFO, CS, whole-time director), meetings (board meetings — notice, quorum, agenda, minutes, resolutions — ordinary & special; general meetings — AGM & EGM, notice period 21 days, quorum, proxy, poll, e-voting, postal ballot).
  • Company Secretary: Definition, appointment, duties (filing returns, maintaining registers & records, signing annual return, convening meetings), liabilities.
  • Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR): Section 135 — applicability (net worth ₹500 crore or turnover ₹1000 crore or net profit ₹5 crore), CSR committee, 2% of average net profits of preceding 3 years, activities (Schedule VII — eradicating hunger, education, gender equality, environmental sustainability, etc.), CSR report in board's report.
  • Winding up: Modes — voluntary (members' voluntary & creditors' voluntary), by tribunal (compulsory — grounds: inability to pay debts, just & equitable, default in filing RoC, acting against sovereignty/integrity/security of India), official liquidator, contributories, preferential payments (workmen's dues, government dues, secured creditors), declaration of solvency for voluntary winding up.
  • Insolvency & Bankruptcy Code 2016: Insolvency resolution process (corporate insolvency resolution process — CIRP, initiation by financial/operational creditor/corporate debtor, declaration of moratorium, appointment of interim resolution professional (IRP), constitution of committee of creditors (CoC), appointment of resolution professional (RP), invitation of resolution plans, approval by CoC (75% voting share), implementation, liquidation (liquidation order, liquidator, realisation of assets, distribution — waterfall: secured creditors, workmen & employees, central/state government debts, unsecured creditors, preference shareholders, equity shareholders), fast track insolvency resolution process (for small companies), cross-border insolvency (UNCITRAL Model Law adoption pending), personal insolvency (individuals & partnership firms), insolvency professionals & IPAs, information utilities (National e-Governance Services Ltd. — NeSL), adjudicating authority (NCLT for companies, NCLAT for appeals, DRT for individuals/Draft Appellate Tribunal for individuals).
  • SEBI regulations: SEBI (Listing Obligations & Disclosure Requirements) Regulations 2015 (LODR — listing agreement, continuous disclosure, related party transactions, corporate governance — composition of board, woman director requirement, independent directors — 50% for listed companies with non-executive chairman, 1/3 otherwise, audit committee, nomination & remuneration committee, stakeholders relationship committee), SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations 2015 (insider trading — definition, prohibited communications, trading window closure, code of conduct for designated persons, disclosures — initial disclosure, continual disclosure, annual disclosure), SEBI (Substantial Acquisition of Shares & Takeovers) Regulations 2011 (open trigger 25%, creeping acquisition 5% per year, open offer 26% for listed companies, offer price — highest of negotiated price, volume weighted average price of last 26 weeks, highest price paid by acquirer in last 26 weeks, open offer timeline), SEBI (Prohibition of Fraudulent & Unfair Trade Practices) Regulations, SEBI (Issue of Capital & Disclosure Requirements) Regulations 2018 (ICDR — IPO & FPO eligibility, pricing, promoter contribution, lock-in, prospectus requirements), SEBI (Listing Obligations & Disclosure Requirements) — quarterly & annual disclosures, related party transactions policy, vigil mechanism/whistle blower, corporate social responsibility reporting.
  • Competition Law: Competition Act 2002 — anti-competitive agreements (horizontal — cartels: price fixing, market allocation, bid rigging, output restriction; vertical — tie-in arrangements, exclusive supply/distribution, refusal to deal, resale price maintenance), abuse of dominance (predatory pricing, barriers to entry, leverage in one market to another), combinations (mergers & acquisitions above thresholds — assets ₹1000 crore/turnover ₹3000 crore domestic, assets USD 1 billion with ₹500 crore in India / turnover USD 3 billion with ₹1500 crore in India cross-border), CCI powers, penalties.
Technique: Companies Act 2013 answer
Question: 'Discuss the provisions related to CSR under Companies Act 2013.'
Answer: Section 135 — applies to companies with net worth ₹500 crore or turnover ₹1000 crore or net profit ₹5 crore in any preceding financial year. Board must constitute CSR committee (3+ directors, at least 1 independent), formulate CSR policy, ensure spending 2% of average net profits of 3 preceding financial years on activities in Schedule VII (education, healthcare, rural development, environment, etc.), disclose in board report, disclose reasons if unspent (transfer to Unspent CSR Account & transfer to specified fund within 6 months), penalties for non-compliance (fine ₹50,000-₹25 lakh on company, imprisonment up to 3 years or fine ₹50,000-₹25 lakh or both for officer in default). Significance: India first country to mandate CSR; aligns business with social welfare.

Common Traps

  • Confusing provisional allotment with firm allotment in IPO.
  • Missing that buyback max 25% of paid-up capital & free reserves, debt-equity ≤ 2:1 post-buyback.
  • Assuming all companies need CSR — applies only to specified thresholds.
  • Confusing private company (max 200 members) with public company (no max).
  • Missing that LIFO is not permitted under Ind AS 2/AS 2 for inventory valuation.

Memory Tricks

  • Company types: Pvt Ltd (2-200 members), Public (7+ members, no max), OPC (1 member).
  • CSR: 2% rule.
  • Buyback: 25% cap, debt-equity 2:1 post-buyback.
  • Directors: rotation — 1/3rd retire every AGM.
Mains strategy
Company law answers: cite section numbers precisely, cite case laws (Salomon v. Salomon & Co — separate legal entity, Foss v. Harbottle — rule in Foss v Harbottle, majority rule & exceptions, Ashbury Railway Carriage & Iron Co v Riche — ultra vires, Guinness v Land Corporation of Ireland — prospectus misstatement), quote section numbers accurately, discuss recent amendments (Companies Amendment Act 2020 — decriminalisation of certain offences, CSR unspent account). Draw formats: balance sheet Schedule III format, cash flow statement direct & indirect methods, share capital structure, debenture redemption process flowchart.
