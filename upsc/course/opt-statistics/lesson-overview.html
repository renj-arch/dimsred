Key Concepts

Statistics Optional — Overview

Statistics is a popular optional for mathematics/statistics graduates with good overlap with GS III (economy, data analysis) and essay. Paper I covers probability, probability distributions, sampling, statistical inference. Paper II covers linear models, design of experiments, sampling theory, quality control, operations research.

Paper I — Probability & Statistical Inference

  • Probability: Sample space, events, axioms of probability, conditional probability, Bayes' theorem, independence, random variables (discrete & continuous), probability mass function (pmf), probability density function (pdf), cumulative distribution function (cdf), expectation, variance, moments, moment generating function (mgf), characteristic function, probability generating function.
  • Distributions: Discrete: Bernoulli, binomial, Poisson, geometric, negative binomial, hypergeometric; Continuous: uniform, exponential, gamma, beta, normal (Gaussian), exponential, Cauchy, Laplace, Cauchy, logistic, Student's t, chi-square, F distribution.
  • Joint Distributions: Joint pmf/pdf, marginal distributions, conditional distributions, independence, covariance, correlation, conditional expectation, regression of Y on X, best linear predictor.
  • Convergence: Convergence in probability, almost sure convergence, convergence in distribution, law of large numbers (weak & strong), central limit theorem (CLT), Lindeberg-Lévy CLT, Lyapunov CLT.
  • Sampling Distributions: Sampling distribution of sample mean, sample variance, sample proportion, t-distribution, chi-square distribution, F-distribution, relationships between them.
  • Estimation: Point estimation (method of moments, maximum likelihood estimation - MLE, properties of MLE: consistency, efficiency, sufficiency, sufficiency via factorization theorem, completeness, completeness & sufficiency, UMVUE - uniformly minimum variance unbiased estimator, Rao-Blackwell theorem, Lehmann-Scheffe theorem), confidence intervals (for mean, proportion, difference of means, difference of proportions, ratio of variances).
  • Testing of Hypotheses: Null & alternative hypotheses, Type I & Type II errors, size & power of a test, most powerful test (MP), uniformly most powerful (UMP) test, Neyman-Pearson lemma, likelihood ratio test (LRT), likelihood ratio test (LRT) for various tests, p-value.
  • Non-parametric Tests: Sign test, Wilcoxon signed-rank test, Wilcoxon rank-sum test (Mann-Whitney U test), Kruskal-Wallis test, runs test, Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, chi-square test for goodness of fit, independence test (chi-square test of independence), Fisher's exact test.
  • Order Statistics: Distribution of order statistics, joint distribution of order statistics, distribution of sample range, sample median, sample quantiles, asymptotic distribution of sample quantiles.
Technique: MLE derivation
Question: 'Find the MLE of parameter θ for a random sample from Poisson(θ) distribution.'
Answer: Likelihood function: L(θ) = ∏(e^(-λ) λ^(x_i) / x_i!) = e^(-nλ) λ^(Σx_i) / ∏(x_i!). Log-likelihood: ℓ(λ) = -nλ + (Σx_i) log(λ) - Σ log(x_i!). Differentiate: ℓ'(λ) = -n + (Σx_i)/λ = 0 → λ̂ = (Σx_i)/n = x̄. Second derivative: ℓ''(λ) = -(Σx_i)/λ² < 0, so maximum. MLE of λ = x̄ (sample mean).

Memory Tricks

  • Distributions: Binomial (n, p) - fixed n trials, p success prob; Poisson (λ) - rare events, λ = mean; Geometric (p) - trials until first success; Negative binomial (r, p) - trials until r successes; Hypergeometric (N, K, n) - sampling without replacement.
  • Continuous: Uniform (a, b) - uniform density; Exponential (λ) - memoryless, memoryless property; Normal (μ, σ²) - bell curve, CLT; t (df) - small sample, unknown σ; Chi-square (df) - sum of squares of standard normals; F (df1, df2) - ratio of chi-squares.
  • Hypothesis Testing: H₀: null hypothesis (status quo), H₁: alternative (research hypothesis), Type I error (α = P(reject H₀ | H₀ true)), Type II error (β = P(fail to reject H₀ | H₁ false)), Power = 1 - β.
  • Estimators: Unbiased (E(θ̂) = θ), Consistent (θ̂ →p θ), Efficient (minimum variance), Sufficient (contains all info about θ), Complete (no non-zero function has expectation 0).
Mains strategy
