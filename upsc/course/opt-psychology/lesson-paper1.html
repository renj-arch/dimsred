Psychology Paper I — Foundations of Psychology

Foundations of Psychology

  • Definition & scope: Psychology is the scientific study of mind & behavior. Roots in philosophy (Descartes, Locke, Berkeley, Hume) & physiology (Helmholtz, Fechner, Helmholtz). Wundt founded first psychology lab (1879, Leipzig). Structuralism (Wundt, Titchener) vs Functionalism (James).
  • Schools of psychology: Structuralism (Wundt), Functionalism (James), Psychoanalysis (Freud), Behaviorism (Watson, Skinner), Gestalt (Wertheimer, Köhler, Koffka), Humanistic (Maslow, Rogers), Cognitive (Neisser, Chomsky), Evolutionary, Positive (Seligman).
  • Research methods: Experimental (independent, dependent, control variables), Correlational, Survey, Case study, Naturalistic observation, Experiment (independent & dependent variables, control group, experimental group, random assignment), Ethics (informed consent, deception, debriefing, confidentiality, right to withdraw, protection from harm).
  • Biological basis of behavior: Neuron structure (dendrite, cell body, axon, myelin sheath, axon terminal, synapse), Action potential (resting potential, action potential, refractory period), Synapse (synaptic cleft, neurotransmitters: acetylcholine, dopamine, serotonin, norepinephrine, GABA, glutamate, endorphins), Nervous system (Central: brain & spinal cord; Peripheral: somatic & autonomic), Brain structures (brainstem: medulla, pons, midbrain; limbic system: thalamus, hypothalamus, hippocampus, amygdala; cerebral cortex: frontal, parietal, temporal, occipital lobes; corpus callosum), Neuroplasticity, Neurogenesis.
  • Sensation & Perception: Sensation vs Perception, Absolute threshold, Difference threshold (Weber's law), Signal detection theory, Sensory adaptation. Vision: Eye structure (cornea, pupil, iris, lens, retina, rods & cones, fovea), Color vision (trichromatic theory, opponent-process theory), Hearing (ear structure: outer, middle, inner ear; cochlea, organ of Corti, basilar membrane, hair cells), Place theory & Frequency theory of pitch, Somatosensation (touch, pain, temperature, proprioception, kinesthesis, vestibular sense), Chemical senses (olfaction - olfaction, gustation - taste), Pain (gate control theory), Perceptual organization (Gestalt principles: figure-ground, proximity, similarity, continuity, closure, common fate), Depth perception (binocular: retinal disparity, convergence; monocular: linear perspective, relative size, interposition, texture gradient, height in plane, motion parallax), Perceptual constancy (size, shape, color, brightness, shape constancy), Illusions (Müller-Lyer, Ponzo, Moon illusion, Ames room), Extrasensory perception (ESP: telepathy, clairvoyance, precognition, psychokinesis - controversial, lack of evidence).
  • Learning: Classical conditioning (Pavlov: UCS, UCR, CS, CR, acquisition, extinction, spontaneous recovery, generalization, discrimination), Operant conditioning (Skinner: operant behavior, reinforcement - positive & negative, punishment - positive & negative, primary & secondary reinforcers, schedules of reinforcement: fixed-ratio, variable-ratio, fixed-interval, variable-interval; shaping, chaining, biological constraints), Observational learning (Bandura: observational learning, mirror neurons, Bobo doll experiment), Cognitive learning (Tolman: cognitive maps, latent learning, insight learning - Köhler), Biological constraints on learning (Garcia & Koopman: taste aversion - taste aversion learning, preparedness).
  • Memory: Memory processes (encoding, storage, retrieval), Memory models (Atkinson-Shiffrin: sensory memory, short-term/working memory, long-term memory; Baddeley's working memory model: phonological loop, visuospatial sketchpad, central executive, episodic buffer), Sensory memory (iconic - iconic memory, echoic - auditory), Short-term memory (George Miller: 7±2 items, chunking), Long-term memory (explicit/declarative: episodic, semantic; implicit/non-declarative: procedural, classical conditioning, priming), Forgetting (encoding failure, storage decay, retrieval failure - tip-of-the-tongue, retroactive & proactive interference, motivated forgetting - repression, decay theory), False memories (Loftus - misinformation effect, imagination inflation), Improving memory: retrieval cues, encoding specificity, state-dependent & mood-congruent memory, overlearning, spaced rehearsal, mnemonic devices (method of loci, pegword, acronyms, keywords, chunking).
  • Thinking & Language: Thinking (mental images, concepts - formal concepts, natural concepts, prototypes, prototypes vs exemplars), Problem solving (algorithms, heuristics - availability heuristic, representativeness heuristic, anchoring & adjustment heuristic, confirmation bias, belief persistence, framing effect, framing effect, availability heuristic, representativeness heuristic, anchoring heuristic), Problem solving strategies (means-ends analysis, working backward, means-end analysis, algorithm vs heuristic, insight - sudden insight, functional fixedness, mental set), Language (phonemes, morphemes, syntax, semantics, pragmatics), Language development (babbling, one-word stage, two-word stage, telegraphic speech), Language acquisition theories (Chomsky's language acquisition device - LAD, universal grammar, Skinner's operant conditioning, interactionist perspective - Vygotsky), Bilingualism, Animal communication (bee dance, Vervet monkey alarm calls, Kanzi the bonobo, Washoe chimpanzee, Alex the parrot), Thinking in images, Language & thought (Whorfian hypothesis - linguistic relativity vs linguistic determinism).

Memory

  • Memory models: Atkinson-Shiffrin model (sensory, short-term/working, long-term), Working memory model (Baddeley & Hitch: phonological loop, visuospatial sketchpad, central executive, episodic buffer), Levels of processing (Craik & Lockhart: shallow - structural/phonemic, deep - semantic), Dual-store model, Parallel distributed processing (connectionism).
  • Encoding: Automatic processing (space, time, frequency, well-learned skills), Effortful processing (rehearsal, chunking, mnemonics, hierarchies), Visual encoding, Acoustic encoding, Semantic encoding (deepest level, best for retention), Mnemonics (method of loci, peg-word method, acronyms, keyword method, chunking, spacing effect, retrieval practice, testing effect), Serial position effect (primacy effect - primacy effect, recency effect), Self-reference effect, Generation effect, Levels of processing theory (Craik & Lockhart: shallow processing - structural & phonemic encoding, deep processing - semantic processing leads to better retention).
  • Storage: Sensory memory (iconic - visual, echoic - auditory, haptic - touch), Short-term/working memory (duration ~20 seconds, capacity 7±2 items), Long-term memory (essentially unlimited capacity & duration), Consolidation (consolidation - process of transferring STM to LTM, sleep & memory consolidation), Flashbulb memories (highly detailed, vivid memories of emotionally significant events - 9/11, JFK assassination), Reconsolidation (reconsolidation - retrieving & re-storing memories, malleability of memory), False memories (Loftus - misinformation effect, imagination inflation, source amnesia, source monitoring error).
  • Retrieval: Retrieval cues (context-dependent memory, state-dependent mood-dependent memory), Serial position effect (primacy effect - primacy effect, recency effect - recency effect), Retrieval failure (tip-of-the-tongue phenomenon), Context-dependent memory (environmental context, state-dependent memory - internal state), Encoding specificity principle (retrieval is enhanced when retrieval conditions match encoding conditions), Recognition vs Recall (recognition easier than recall, multiple choice easier than fill-in-the-blank, essay), Misinformation effect (Loftus & Palmer: leading questions alter memory), Reconstructive memory (Bartlett's schema theory - memory is reconstructive, not photographic), Source monitoring error (confusing source of information), Imagination inflation (imagining an event increases belief it occurred), Prospective memory (remembering to do something in the future).
  • Forgetting: Forgetting curve (Ebbinghaus - rapid forgetting initially, then levels off), Encoding failure (never encoded), Storage decay (memories fade over time), Retrieval failure (proactive interference - old info interferes with new, proactive interference; retroactive interference - new info interferes with old), Motivated forgetting (repression - Freud's concept, controversial), Decay theory, Interference theory (proactive & retroactive interference), Amnesia (retrograde amnesia - loss of past memories, anterograde amnesia - inability to form new memories - H.M. case study by Milner), Infantile amnesia (cannot recall events before age 3-4).

Memory Strategies

  • Mnemonics: Method of loci (memory palace), Peg-word method, Chunking, Acronyms (HOMES for Great Lakes), Acrostic (Every Good Boy Does Fine - lines of treble clef), Rhymes, Keyword method.
  • Retrieval practice: Testing effect (retrieval practice improves retention more than restudying), Spaced repetition (distributed practice better than massed practice/cramming), Interleaved practice (mixing different topics), Retrieval cues (context-dependent memory - Godden & Baddeley diving study, State-dependent memory - mood-dependent memory), Elaborative rehearsal (connecting new info to existing knowledge) vs Maintenance rehearsal (simple repetition - less effective).
  • Forgetting curves: Ebbinghaus forgetting curve - rapid forgetting in first hour/day, then levels off. Overlearning reduces forgetting. Spaced repetition counteracts forgetting curve.
  • Eyewitness testimony: Loftus & Palmer's studies on leading questions ("smashed" vs "contacted"), Misinformation effect (misinformation incorporated into memory), Reconstructive nature of memory, Coached witnesses, Children's suggestibility, Recovered memories controversy (false memories - recovered memories of abuse may be false), Scott v. State & Daubert standard for expert testimony.
Technique: Classical Conditioning Example
Question: "In Pavlov's experiment, what is the conditioned response?"
Answer: Salivation in response to the bell (after pairing bell with food). The bell (CS) triggers salivation (CR) after pairing with food (UCS) which naturally causes salivation (UCR). Acquisition: pairing NS (bell) with US (food) → CS (bell) → CR (salivation). Extinction: presenting CS without US leads to extinction of CR. Spontaneous recovery: CR reappears after rest period. Stimulus generalization: responding to similar stimuli. Stimulus discrimination: distinguishing between similar stimuli.

Memory Tricks

  • Memory stages: Encoding → Storage → Retrieval
  • Memory types: Sensory (iconic & echoic), Short-term/Working, Long-term (explicit/declarative: episodic & semantic; implicit/non-declarative: procedural & classical conditioning)
  • Encoding types: Acoustic (sound), Visual (visual), Semantic (meaning) - semantic deepest & best remembered
  • Forgetting curves: Ebbinghaus - rapid forgetting first hour/day, then levels off. Overlearning reduces forgetting. Spaced repetition counteracts forgetting curve.
  • Encoding specificity: Retrieval cues matching encoding context improve retrieval (Godden & Baddeley diving study - context-dependent memory)
