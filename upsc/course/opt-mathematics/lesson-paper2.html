Key Concepts

Mathematics Paper II — Algebra, Complex Analysis, ODE & PDE, Mechanics

Algebra

  • Groups: Groups (definition, examples), subgroups, Lagrange's theorem, cyclic groups, permutation groups (S_n), homomorphisms & isomorphisms, normal subgroups, quotient groups, first/second/third isomorphism theorems, automorphisms, inner automorphisms, Cayley's theorem, Cayley table, center of group, center of GL(n,R), center of SL(n,R).
  • Rings: Rings (definition, examples), ideals, quotient rings, prime ideals, maximal ideals, Euclidean domains (ED), principal ideal domains (PID), unique factorization domains (UFD), Euclidean algorithm, polynomial rings over fields, irreducibility criteria (Eisenstein's criterion, Gauss's lemma).
  • Fields: Fields (definition, examples), field extensions (algebraic & transcendental), algebraic extensions, finite fields (Galois fields GF(p^n)), splitting fields, normal extensions, separable extensions, Galois theory (Galois correspondence, fundamental theorem of Galois theory), solvability by radicals, Galois groups of polynomials (x^n - 1, x^p - p, etc.).
  • Matrices: Matrices (definition, operations), rank, inverse (if exists), elementary operations, row echelon form, canonical forms (Smith normal form, Jordan canonical form), diagonalization, eigenvalues & eigenvectors, characteristic polynomial, minimal polynomial, characteristic polynomial, trace, determinant, properties of determinants, Cayley-Hamilton theorem, bilinear forms, quadratic forms, orthogonal diagonalization, spectral theorem for symmetric matrices.

Complex Analysis

  • Complex Numbers: Complex numbers (z = x + iy), complex plane, modulus & argument, Euler's formula (e^(iθ) = cos θ + i sin θ), De Moivre's theorem, roots of unity, complex functions, limits, continuity, differentiability.
  • Complex Functions: Complex functions (f(z) = u(x,y) + i v(x,y)), Cauchy-Riemann equations (∂u/∂x = ∂v/∂y, ∂u/∂y = -∂v/∂x), analyticity (holomorphic/analytic functions), harmonic functions (∇²u = 0, ∇²v = 0), conformal mappings (conformal maps preserve angles locally).
  • Contour Integration: Contour integration (line integrals in complex plane), Cauchy's theorem (∮_C f(z) dz = 0 for analytic f in simply connected domain), Cauchy's integral formula (f(a) = (1/2πi) ∮_C f(z)/(z-a) dz), Cauchy's integral formula for derivatives (f^(n)(a) = n!/(2πi) ∮_C f(z)/(z-a)^(n+1) dz), Cauchy's estimate, Liouville's theorem (bounded entire function is constant), maximum modulus principle, minimum modulus principle.
  • Series & Residues: Taylor series (Taylor's theorem for complex functions), Laurent series (Laurent series expansion around isolated singularity), residue theorem (∮_C f(z) dz = 2πi Σ Res(f, a_k)), residue calculation (residue at simple pole: Res(f, a) = lim_{z→a} (z-a)f(z); residue at pole of order m: Res(f, a) = (1/(m-1)!) lim_{z→a} d^(m-1)/dz^(m-1)[(z-a)^m f(z)]), evaluation of real integrals using residue theorem (trigonometric integrals, rational function integrals, Fourier transforms).
  • Singularities: Singularities (isolated singularities: removable singularity, pole, essential singularity; non-isolated singularities: branch points, natural boundaries), Casorati-Weierstrass theorem (image of punctured neighborhood of essential singularity is dense in C), Picard's great theorem, Riemann sphere (extended complex plane), stereographic projection.
  • Conformal Mapping: Conformal mappings (angle-preserving maps), examples (z^n, e^z, log z, sin z, cos z, tan z, Möbius transformations (z → (az+b)/(cz+d))), mapping regions (upper half-plane to unit disk via Möbius transformation), applications to boundary value problems (Laplace's equation in simply connected domains).

Ordinary Differential Equations

  • First Order ODE: First order ODE (dy/dx = f(x,y)), separable equations, homogeneous equations, exact equations (exactness condition: ∂M/∂y = ∂N/∂x), integrating factors, linear first-order ODE (y' + P(x)y = Q(x), integrating factor μ(x) = e^(∫P(x)dx)), Bernoulli equation (y' + P(x)y = Q(x)y^n), Riccati equation, Clairaut's equation, Lagrange's equation.
  • Higher Order Linear ODE: Higher order linear ODE (homogeneous & non-homogeneous), linear independence (Wronskian), fundamental set of solutions, reduction of order, variation of parameters, method of undetermined coefficients, Cauchy-Euler equation (x^n y^(n) + ... + a_1 x y' + a_0 y = f(x)), exact equations, integrating factors.
  • Systems of Linear ODE: Systems of linear ODE (X' = A(t)X + f(t)), fundamental matrix, Wronskian, Abel's formula, eigenvalue method for constant coefficient systems, complex eigenvalues, repeated eigenvalues, defective matrices.
  • Power Series Solutions: Power series solutions (ordinary points, regular singular points), Frobenius method (indicial equation, recurrence relations), special functions (Bessel functions J_v(x), Y_v(x); Legendre polynomials P_n(x); Hermite polynomials H_n(x); Laguerre polynomials L_n(x); Chebyshev polynomials T_n(x)), properties of special functions (orthogonality, recurrence relations, generating functions).
  • Sturm-Liouville Theory: Sturm-Liouville problem ((p(x)y')' + (q(x) + λw(x))y = 0), eigenvalues & eigenfunctions, orthogonality of eigenfunctions, expansion in eigenfunctions (Fourier-Bessel series, Fourier-Legendre series), Rayleigh quotient, Rayleigh-Ritz method.

Partial Differential Equations

  • First Order PDE: First order PDE (a(x,y,u) u_x + b(x,y,u) u_y = c(x,y,u)), Charpit's method, Charpit's equations, complete integral, singular integral, general integral, Clairaut's equation, singular solution vs general solution, Envelope method.
  • Second Order Linear PDE: Second order linear PDE (classification: elliptic, parabolic, hyperbolic based on discriminant B² - 4AC), canonical forms, wave equation (u_tt = c² ∇²u), heat equation (u_t = k ∇²u), Laplace equation (∇²u = 0), Poisson equation (∇²u = f(x,y,z)), separation of variables.
  • Wave Equation: Wave equation (∂²u/∂t² = c² ∇²u), D'Alembert's solution (u(x,t) = (f(x+ct) + f(x-ct))/2 + (1/(2c)) ∫_{x-ct}^{x+ct} g(s) ds for 1D wave equation), d'Alembert's solution for semi-infinite string, reflections, energy conservation.
  • Heat Equation: Heat equation (∂u/∂t = k ∇²u), separation of variables, Fourier series solutions (Fourier sine series, Fourier cosine series), steady-state solutions, maximum principle, uniqueness, Duhamel's principle (for non-homogeneous heat equation), Green's function for heat equation.
  • Laplace Equation: Laplace equation (∇²u = 0), separation of variables, Fourier series solutions, Dirichlet problem (Dirichlet problem for disk: Poisson integral formula), Neumann problem (Neumann boundary conditions), maximum principle (strong maximum principle, weak maximum principle), mean value property, Harnack's inequality, Poisson integral formula for disk.
  • Conformal Mapping: Conformal mappings (angle-preserving maps), examples (z^n, e^z, log z, sin z, cos z, tan z, Möbius transformations (z → (az+b)/(cz+d))), mapping regions (upper half-plane to unit disk via Möbius transformation w = (z - z_0)/(z - conj(z_0))), applications to boundary value problems (Laplace's equation in simply connected domains via conformal mapping).
Technique: Cauchy-Riemann equations
Question: Show that f(z) = z² is analytic everywhere.
Answer: f(z) = z² = (x+iy)² = x² - y² + 2ixy. So u(x,y) = x² - y², v(x,y) = 2xy. Compute partial derivatives: u_x = 2x, u_y = -2y, v_x = 2y, v_y = 2x. Check Cauchy-Riemann: u_x = v_y (2x = 2x) ✓, u_y = -v_x (-2y = -2y) ✓. Since u_x, u_y, v_x, v_y are continuous and C-R equations hold everywhere, f(z) = z² is analytic everywhere (entire function).

Memory Tricks

  • Cauchy-Riemann equations: u_x = v_y, u_y = -v_x (u_x = v_y, u_y = -v_x — "x with x, y with -y").
  • Classification of PDEs: Elliptic (B² - 4AC < 0, e.g., Laplace), Parabolic (B² - 4AC = 0, e.g., heat equation), Hyperbolic (B² - 4AC > 0, e.g., wave equation).
  • Series convergence: p-series (∑1/n^p converges iff p > 1), geometric series (∑ar^n converges if |r| < 1), ratio test, root test, comparison test, integral test.
  • Laplace transforms: L{f'(t)} = sF(s) - f(0), L{f''(t)} = s²F(s) - sf(0) - f'(0).
