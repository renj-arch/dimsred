Key Concepts

Medieval India (750-1857 CE)

Early Medieval India (750-1200 CE)

  • Regional kingdoms: Palas (Bengal), Pratiharas (Kannauj), Rashtrakutas (Deccan), Cholas (Tamil Nadu), Cheras (Kerala), Pandyas (Tamil Nadu).
  • Chola administration: Local self-government (ur, sabha, nagaram), village administration, naval power, overseas trade (Sri Lanka, Southeast Asia), temple administration, land revenue system.
  • Arab conquest of Sindh (712 CE): Muhammad bin Qasim's conquest, impact on India-Islam contact.
  • Turko-Afghan invasions: Mahmud of Ghazni (17 invasions, 1001-1027 CE, Somnath temple 1026), Muhammad Ghori (1175-1206, Battles of Tarain 1191 & 1192), establishment of Turkish rule in India.

Delhi Sultanate (1206-1526 CE)

  • Mamluk/Slave Dynasty (1206-1290): Qutb-ud-din Aibak (founder, Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque, Qutb Minar), Iltutmish (consolidation, Iqta system, Silver tanka & copper jital coins), Razia (first woman ruler), Balban (blood & iron policy, espionage).
  • Khalji Dynasty (1290-1320): Jalaluddin Khalji (founder), Alauddin Khalji (market reforms, price control, market reforms, Mongol invasions repelled, South India expedition - Malik Kafur, Chittor conquest 1303), administrative reforms.
  • Tughlaq Dynasty (1320-1414): Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq (founder), Muhammad bin Tughlaq (capital shift to Daulatabad, token currency experiment, agricultural experiments, Doab famine), Firoz Shah Tughlaq (irrigation, public works, slavery, jaziya on non-Muslims, establishment of new towns).
  • Sayyid Dynasty (1414-1451): Khizr Khan (founder), weak rulers, decline of Delhi Sultanate.
  • Lodi Dynasty (1451-1526): Bahlul Lodi (founder), Sikandar Lodi (aggressive expansion, iconoclasm), Ibrahim Lodi (last Lodi ruler, defeated by Babur at First Battle of Panipat 1526).

Deccan & South India

  • Vijayanagara Empire (1336-1646): Harihara & Bukka (founders), Krishnadevaraya (greatest ruler, Amuktamalyada, foreign travelers - Abdur Razzak, Domingo Paes, Nicolo Conti), administration, art & architecture (Hampi, Hazara Rama Temple, Vitthala Temple), decline after Battle of Talikota (1565).
  • Bahmani Kingdom (1347-1527): Alauddin Hasan Bahman Shah (founder), Muhammad Shah I, Muhammad Shah III, Mahmud Gawan (prime minister, Mahmud Gawan Madrasa), disintegration into five Deccan Sultanates (Ahmadnagar, Bijapur, Golconda, Berar, Bidar).
  • Bhakti & Sufi Movements: Bhakti saints (Ramanuja, Kabir, Kabir, Guru Nanak, Chaitanya, Mirabai, Tulsidas, Eknath, Tukaram, Namdev), Sufi saints (Moinduddin Chishti - Ajmer, Nizamuddin Auliya - Delhi, Fariduddin Ganjshakar - Pakpattan, Qutbuddin Bakhtiar Kaki - Delhi), Sufi orders (Chishti, Suhrawardi, Qadiri, Naqshbandi, Suhrawardi).

Common Traps

  • Confusing different dynasties with similar names (e.g., Khalji vs Tughlaq, Rashtrakuta vs Chalukya).
  • Mixing up dates of important battles (Tarain 1191 & 1192, Panipat 1526, Khanwa 1527, Ghaghra 1529, Haldighati 1576).
  • Confusing different Sufi orders and their saints.
  • Assuming all Delhi Sultans were equally powerful - many were weak figureheads controlled by nobles.
  • Missing the regional diversity of medieval India - not all regions were under Delhi Sultanate control.

Memory Tricks

  • Delhi Sultanate dynasties: Mamluk (1206-1290), Khalji (1290-1320), Tughlaq (1320-1414), Sayyid (1414-1451), Lodi (1451-1526) - MKTSL.
  • Vijayanagara founders: Harihara & Bukka (founded 1336).
  • Sufi saints: Moinuddin Chishti (Ajmer), Nizamuddin Auliya (Delhi), Fariduddin Ganjshakar (Pakpattan), Qutbuddin Bakhtiar Kaki (Delhi).
  • Bhakti saints: Kabir (Bhakti-Nirguna), Tulsidas (Ramcharitmanas), Kabir (Bijak), Guru Nanak (Guru Granth Sahib).
Mains strategy
Medieval India answers: dynasty → founder → important rulers → administration → art & architecture → decline. Draw maps (extent of Delhi Sultanate, Vijayanagara empire, Bahmani kingdom). Quote foreign travelers (Ibn Battuta, Marco Polo, Abdur Razzak, Domingo Paez, Nicolo Conti, Alberuni). Quote contemporary chronicles (Tarikh-i-Firoz Shahi by Barani, Tarikh-i-Firuz Shahi, Baburnama, Akbarnama).
