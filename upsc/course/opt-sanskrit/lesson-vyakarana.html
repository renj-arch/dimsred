1. Varna-parichaya — the sounds of Sanskrit

Sanskrit phonology is the foundation — every sandhi and grammar rule assumes it. The UPSC syllabus asks direct questions on sound classification.

3. Yogavahikas — 4

अं (anusvāra), ः (visarga), plus two others in extended counts. These are "yoga-joining" sounds that modify preceding vowels in sandhi.

Worked example
Classify: य, श, म, ए
Solution: य = antastha (semivowel); श = ūṣman (dental sibilant); म = sparśa (labial, fifth varga); ए = guṇa vowel.

2. Sandhi — the five rules

Sandhi = phonetic combination at word boundaries. UPSC asks both directions: apply the rule AND split a sandhi word.

Sandhi types with examples
TypeRuleExample
SavarnaSame vowel + same vowel → long vowel (अ+अ→आ, इ+इ→ई)राम + अत्र → रामात्र
Guṇaअ/आ + इ/ई/उ/ऊ/ऋ → ए/ओ/अर्हर + इंद्र → हरेन्द्र; महा + ऋषि → महर्षि
Vṛddhiअ/आ + ए/ऐ/ओ/औ → ऐ/औ/अर्महा + एक → महैक; देव + औषधि → दैवौषधि
Yanइ/ई/उ/ऊ + different vowel → य/व/र before vowelइति + आदि → इत्यादि; सु + अस्तु → स्वस्तु
Āyādiआ + vowel → special diphthong/doublingदेवा + अत्र → देवात्र (ā + a → ā)

Vyanjana sandhi: final consonant + initial consonant — e.g., त् + ज → ज्ज (वाक् + जी_arguments → वाग्जी); ष् + त → स्त. Also purva-rūpa (preceding form retained) and para- yoga (following form retained).

Worked example
Split: महोत्सव
Solution: महा + उत्सव — ā + u → au (vṛddhi/āyādi). The word means "great festival".

3. Samasa — compound types

Samāsa joins two+ words into one. UPSC asks to identify type and split.

Samasa types
TypeStructureExampleMeaning
Avyayībhāvaavyaya + noun → avyayaयथाशक्ति (as per strength)According to capacity
Tatpuruṣasubordinate — one word modifies other (6 vibhakti subtypes)राजपुत्र (son of king), दिनचर (day-walker)King's son, one who walks by day
Karmadhārayaadjective + noun, or appositionनीलकंठ (blue-throat), नीलोत्पलNeelkanth, blue lotus
Dvandvaco-ordinate equalsरामसीता, माता-पिताRāma and Sītā
Bahuvrīhi"possessing" — neither word is the headनीलकंठ (having blue throat = Śiva), चतुर्भुजHe who has blue throat
Dvigunumeral + noun → collectiveत्रिलोकी (three worlds), पञ्चरत्नThe three worlds
Alukvibhakti retained in compoundआत्मनेपद (ātman + ending retained)Special case

Tatpuruṣa subtypes (by vibhakti): ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa (genitive — राजपुत्र), pañcamī (fifth — ग्रामात् आगतः → ग्रामागत), caturthī (fourth — purpose — student for knowledge), tṛtīyā (third — instrument —(done)by hand), dvandva-type. UPSC asks "which vibhakti is hidden in this compound" — identify the case relation.

Karmadhāraya vs Bahuvrīhi trap
नीलकंठ: is it "blue throat" (karmadhāraya, describing the throat) or "he who has a blue throat" (bahuvrīhi, describing Śiva)? Test: does the compound name the thing itself or something else that possesses it? Nīlakaṇṭha = Śiva → bahuvrīhi. Nīlotpala = a blue lotus → karmadhāraya.

4. Vibhakti and Karaka

The most-tested link in Sanskrit grammar: 8 cases ↔ 6 karakas.

The 8 vibhaktis and 6 karakas
#VibhaktiSanskrit nameKarakaFunction
1Prathamā nominativeKartāDoer/agent
2DvitīyāaccusativeKarmaObject/goal
3TṛtīyāinstrumentalKaraṇaInstrument/means
4CaturthīdativeSampradānaRecipient/purpose
5PañcamīablativeApādānaSeparation/source
6Ṣaṣṭhīgenitive— (no karaka)Possession/relation
7SaptamīlocativeAdhikaraṇaLocation/base
8Sambodhanavocative— (address)Calling/addressing

Key points:

  • Sixth case (ṣaṣṭhī) has NO karaka — it shows possession, not a semantic role.
  • Eighth (sambodhana) is vocative — used only for addressing.
  • Kartā → prathamā is the most-asked single mapping.
  • In "gacchati pathā" (goes by path), patha is tṛtīyā = karaṇa.

5. Linga, Vachana and Samkhya

4. Linga (gender) — 3

  • Pumliṅga: masculine — राम, सूर्य, बालक
  • Strīliṅga: feminine — सीता, गंगा, बालिका
  • Napumsakaliṅga: neuter — फलम्, जलम्, ् (jyotiḥ)

Neuter nouns have identical nominative/accusative singular (e.g., फलम् both subject and object form).

5. Vachana (number) — 3

  • Ekavachana: singular — बालकः
  • Dvivachana: dual — बालकौ
  • Bahuvachana: plural — बालकाः

8. Examiner traps

Traps that cost marks
  • Guṇa vs vṛddhi: guṇa raises to ए/ओ; vṛddhi raises further to ऐ/औ. अ+इ=ए (guṇa) but अ+ए=ऐ (vṛddhi) — test the second vowel's height.
  • Karmadhāraya vs bahuvrīhi: same phonetic form, different meaning — check whether the compound denotes the thing or its possessor.
  • Sixth case has no karaka: ṣaṣṭhī is genitive/possession only — don't force a karaka onto it.
  • Sambodhana: vocative is the 8th vibhakti but has no karaka — it's for addressing only.
  • Neuter singular: nominative = accusative for neuter nouns — "phalám" is both "fruit (subject)" and "fruit (object)".
  • Dhatu classes: "gam" is 1st gaṇa but becomes "gacchati" (ch insertion) — memorize the present stem, not just the root.
  • Samkhya vs vibhakti endings: tri (3) takes different endings by gender — त्रयः (m.pl), त्रीणि (n.pl), तिस्रः (f.pl) — gender agreement traps.

9. Practice plan

Phase 1 — classification drill: 20 random Sanskrit words daily — classify each sound (vowel/consonant type), each compound (samasa type), each ending (vibhakti).

Phase 2 — sandhi practice: 10 sandhi splits + 10 sandhi joins daily. Write both directions — examiners test both.

Phase 3 — karaka-vibhakti mapping: the 8×6 table must be instant recall. Drill until you can fill the table from memory in under 60 seconds.

Phase 4 — mixed mock: previous-year Sanskrit Paper I grammar questions under time pressure. Most candidates lose 15–20 marks here from weak grammar — this section is where an optional topper is made.

Pro Tip
Sanskrit grammar is the highest-ROI section of the optional: it's pure rule-based knowledge, not interpretation. A student who masters varna→vibhakti can score 60–70% of Paper I's grammar marks reliably — the literature papers are where interpretation and textual knowledge matter more.
