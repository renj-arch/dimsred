Key Concepts

Human Geography

Population & Settlement

  • Population distribution: Uneven distribution globally and in India. Factors: physical (climate, terrain, water, soil), economic (mineral resources, industrialization, urbanization), social/cultural (historical, cultural, religious factors), political (government policies, security).
  • Population growth: Demographic transition theory (4 stages: high stationary, early expanding, late expanding, low stationary, declining), Malthusian theory (population grows geometrically, food supply arithmetically), optimum theory of population (Carr-Saunders).
  • Migration: Types (internal - rural-rural, rural-urban, urban-urban; international - immigration & emigration), causes (push factors - unemployment, poverty, natural disasters, conflict; pull factors - employment, better living standards, education, healthcare), consequences (demographic, economic, social, environmental on origin & destination areas).
  • Rural settlements: Types (compact, dispersed, nucleated, linear, radial, star-shaped), factors affecting location (water, defense, resources, soil, climate), functions of rural settlements.
  • Urban settlements: Classification (towns, cities, million-plus cities, megacities, megaregions), urban morphology (CBD, CBD transition zone, residential zones, industrial zones, slums, suburbs), urban morphology models (concentric zone model - Burgess, sector model - Hoyt, multiple nuclei model - Harris & Ullman), urban problems (slums, housing, transportation, pollution, waste management, crime), urbanization trends, counter-urbanization, smart cities, urban planning (master plans, zoning, land use planning), urban morphology theories.

Technique: Demographic transition theory
Question: 'Explain the demographic transition theory with reference to India.'
Answer: Demographic transition theory describes the transition from high birth and death rates to low birth and death rates as a country develops from a pre-industrial to an industrialized economic system. Four stages: (1) High stationary - high birth rate, high death rate, slow population growth (pre-1920 India); (2) Early expanding - high birth rate, falling death rate, rapid population growth (1920-1950 India); (3) Late expanding - falling birth rate, low death rate, slowing population growth (1950-1970 India); (4) Low stationary - low birth rate, low death rate, stable population (current developed countries, India approaching). India is currently in the late expanding/low stationary stage - fertility rate at 2.0 (below replacement level - NFHS-5), population still growing due to population momentum.

Memory Tricks

  • Demographic transition stages: High stationary (high birth, high death) → Early expanding (high birth, falling death) → Late expanding (falling birth, low death) → Low stationary (low birth, low death).
  • Population density: Arithmetic density = total population / total land area; Physiological density = population / arable land; Agricultural density = farmers / arable land.
  • Migration: Push factors (unemployment, poverty, natural disasters, conflict) → Pull factors (employment, better living standards, education, healthcare).
  • Settlement types: Compact (clustered), dispersed (scattered), linear (along roads/rivers), radial (around central point), star-shaped (along multiple radial roads).
  • Urbanization models: Concentric zone (Burgess - CBD at center), Sector (Hoyt - wedge-shaped), Multiple nuclei (Harris & Ullman - multiple centers).
