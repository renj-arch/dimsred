Economics Optional — Syllabus Map & Strategy

Economics has two papers of 250 marks each: Paper I is economic theory, Paper II is the Indian economy. The optional rewards candidates who can combine diagrams, models and current data — a rare edge in Mains answer writing.

Paper I — Economic Theory

  • Advanced Microeconomics: consumer theory (utility, indifference curves, Slutsky equation), production and cost, market structures (perfect competition, monopoly, monopolistic competition, oligopoly), factor pricing, general equilibrium, welfare economics.
  • Advanced Macroeconomics: national income accounting, classical and Keynesian models, IS–LM, aggregate demand and supply, inflation and unemployment (Phillips curve), growth models (Harrod–Domar, Solow), business cycles.
  • Money and Banking: money supply and demand, money multiplier, commercial banking, central banking, monetary policy transmission, inflation targeting.
  • Public Economics: public goods, externalities, market failure, taxation (incidence, deadweight loss, optimal taxation), public expenditure, public debt, fiscal federalism.

Paper II — Indian Economy

  • Pre-Independence: land systems, commercialisation of agriculture, drain theory, deindustrialisation, railways, public finance.
  • Post-Independence: planning, poverty and inequality, agriculture, industry, services, foreign trade, balance of payments, fiscal and monetary policy, liberalisation and reforms.
Solved Example: Money Multiplier
Question: If the reserve–deposit ratio is 0.10 and the currency–deposit ratio is 0.25, find the money multiplier (M = (1 + c)/(c + r)).
Answer: M = (1 + 0.25)/(0.25 + 0.10) = 1.25/0.35 ≈ 3.57. A monetary base of ₹100 would support money supply of about ₹357.

Standard Sources

  • Microeconomics: Koutsoyiannis; H.L. Ahuja; Hal Varian (for rigour)
  • Macroeconomics: Dornbusch & Fischer; Oliver Blanchard; H.L. Ahuja
  • Public Economics: R.A. Musgrave; Bhatia
  • Money and Banking: S.B. Gupta; RBI publications
  • Indian Economy: Uma Kapila; Mishra & Puri; Ramesh Singh; Economic Survey; NCERT

Common Traps

  • Describing a model without a diagram — micro and macro answers score with correctly labelled curves.
  • Quoting outdated data. Use the latest Economic Survey and RBI Annual Report figures.
  • Confusing fiscal deficit with revenue deficit, or primary deficit with fiscal deficit.
  • Treating the Phillips curve as stable; mention the expectations-augmented version.
  • Giving policy opinion without theory — link every Indian-economy answer to a model or concept.
Mains strategy
For theory answers: define → state assumptions → diagram → derivation or explanation → limitation. For Indian-economy answers: data point → trend → cause → policy response → evaluation. Keep a one-page data sheet (GDP growth, inflation, fiscal deficit, CAD, unemployment, poverty) updated from the Economic Survey and Budget.
