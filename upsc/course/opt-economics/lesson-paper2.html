Key Concepts

Macroeconomics & International Economics

National Income & Aggregates

  • National Income Accounting: GDP, GNP, NNP, Personal Income, Disposable Income. Methods: Product, Income, Expenditure.
  • GDP vs GNP: GDP = domestic production; GNP = GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad.
  • Real vs Nominal GDP: GDP Deflator = (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) × 100.
  • Green GDP: GDP adjusted for environmental degradation.
  • Limitations of GDP: Does not measure welfare, excludes non-market transactions, ignores income distribution, environmental degradation.

National Income Determination

  • Classical Theory: Say's Law - supply creates its own demand; full employment equilibrium.
  • Keynesian Theory: Effective demand (aggregate demand = aggregate supply), equilibrium level of output & employment.
  • Consumption Function: C = a + bY (autonomous consumption + MPC × income), APC & MPC.
  • Investment Function: Investment depends on interest rate & Marginal Efficiency of Capital (MEC).
  • Investment Multiplier: k = 1/(1-MPC) = 1/MPS.
  • Foreign Trade Multiplier: k_f = 1/(1 - MPC + MPM) where MPM = marginal propensity to import.
  • Foreign Trade Multiplier: k_f = 1/(1 - MPC + MPM) where MPM = marginal propensity to import.

Money & Banking

  • Money Demand: Transactions motive, precautionary motive, speculative demand (Keynes's liquidity preference theory).
  • Money Supply: M1, M2, M3, M4 in India. Money multiplier = 1/(crr + cr + er) where crr = cash reserve ratio, cr = currency-deposit ratio, er = excess reserves ratio.
  • Money Supply & Price Level: Quantity Theory of Money (MV = PY), Fisher's equation of exchange, Cambridge equation (M = kPY).
  • Demand for Money: Transactions motive, precautionary motive, speculative demand for money (Keynes's liquidity preference theory).

Inflation & Unemployment

  • Inflation: Inflation (sustained increase in general price level), types: demand-pull inflation, cost-push inflation, built-in inflation/wage-price spiral.
  • Unemployment: Types: frictional unemployment (search unemployment), structural unemployment (mismatch between skills & jobs), cyclical unemployment (due to business cycle), seasonal unemployment, frictional unemployment, natural rate of unemployment = frictional + structural, NAIRU - Non-Accelerating Inflation Rate of Unemployment), Okun's law (relationship between unemployment gap & output gap: every 1% increase in unemployment above natural rate reduces output by ~2% below potential), Phillips curve (short-run Phillips curve: inverse relationship between inflation & unemployment, long-run Phillips curve: vertical at natural rate of unemployment), expectations-augmented Phillips curve (adaptive expectations & rational expectations), sacrifice ratio (cost of disinflation in terms of lost output), sacrifice ratio measurement.

Technique: Investment Multiplier
Question: 'If MPC = 0.8, what is the investment multiplier?'
Answer: Investment multiplier k = 1/(1-MPC) = 1/(1-0.8) = 1/0.2 = 5. A ₹1 crore increase in investment increases national income by ₹5 crore through the multiplier process.

Memory Tricks

  • GDP Deflator: (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) × 100
  • Money Multiplier: 1 / (crr + cr + er)
  • Investment Multiplier: 1 / (1 - MPC)
  • Fiscal Deficit: Total expenditure - Total receipts (excluding borrowings)
  • Primary Deficit: Fiscal deficit - Interest payments
  • Revenue Deficit: Revenue expenditure - Revenue receipts
