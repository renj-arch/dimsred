Economics Paper II — Indian Economy, Development & International Economics

Indian Economic Development

  • Economic planning in India: Planning Commission (1950-2014) — replaced by NITI Aayog (2015) — think tank, cooperative federalism, Aspirational Districts Programme; Five Year Plans (1st (1951-56) to 12th (2012-17)) — Mahalanobis model (heavy industry focus, import substitution), Green Revolution (1960s — HYV seeds, fertilizers, irrigation — M.S. Swaminathan), White Revolution (Operation Flood — Verghese Kurien, dairy), Blue Revolution (fisheries), Yellow Revolution (oilseeds), Golden Revolution (horticulture), Silver Revolution (egg/poultry); shift from planning to NITI Aayog — NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India) — governing council (PM chairperson, all CMs + Lt. Governors), regional councils, Atal Innovation Mission (AIM), Aspirational Districts Programme (112 districts — health, nutrition, education, agriculture, infrastructure, financial inclusion, skill development), NITI Aayog reports (Strategy for New India @ 2022, India@2047 vision).
  • Economic reforms 1991 (LPG): Background — Balance of Payments crisis 1991 (forex reserves ~$1 billion, ~3 weeks of imports), fiscal deficit ~8.4% of GDP, inflation ~15-17%, Gulf War 1990-91 (oil price shock), dissolution of Soviet Union (major trading partner); reforms — Liberalisation (industrial deregulation — abolition of industrial licensing (except 18 industries), MRTP Act restrictions relaxed (MRTP Act 1969 replaced by Competition Act 2002), delicensing, decontrol of prices, reduction in number of reserved industries for public sector (from 17 to few — atomic energy, railway transport, atomic minerals), Privatisation (disinvestment — strategic disinvestment (BPCL, Air India, Shipping Corporation, BPCL, IDBI Bank, Pawan Hans, etc.), minority stake sales, strategic sale policy 2021 (non-strategic sectors to be privatised), Globalisation (liberalisation of foreign investment (FDI — automatic route & government route sectors, FDI caps sector-wise), foreign institutional investors (FPI) — portfolio investment, foreign technology agreements, external commercial borrowings (ECB), exchange rate liberalisation (LERMS 1992 → unified exchange rate 1993 → current account convertibility 1994), tariff reduction (peak customs duty reduced from >100% to lower levels, peak customs duty now ~10% (non-agriculture)), WTO membership 1995.
  • Impact of LPG reforms: GDP growth accelerated (Hindu rate of growth ~3.5% (1950-80) → ~6-7% (post-1991, with variations)), foreign exchange reserves increased (from ~$1 billion 1991 to ~$580 billion (2023) — 4th largest), FDI inflows increased, IT & software services boom (Bangalore — Silicon Valley of India), telecom revolution (New Telecom Policy 1999, TRAI 1997), mobile revolution, GDP composition — services sector dominance (~50%+ of GDP), agriculture share declining (~18% GDP but ~45% employment), manufacturing stagnant (~16-17% of GDP), poverty reduction (poverty ratio declined — Tendulkar line: 37.2% (2004-05) → 21.9% (2011-12); NITI Aayog MPI — 24.85% (2015-16) → 14.96% (2019-21) — ~135 million people exited multidimensional poverty), inequality increased (Gini coefficient rising, top 1% holds large share of wealth — Oxfam reports), regional disparities (southern & western states outperforming northern & eastern states), jobless growth concerns, informal sector dominance (~90% of workforce), farmer distress (farmer suicides, agrarian distress — small & marginal landholdings (<2 ha) ~86% of farmers), MSP procurement bias (rice-wheat in Punjab-Haryana), water table depletion (Punjab-Haryana rice cultivation), climate change vulnerability (monsoon dependence).
  • Financial sector reforms: Banking sector reforms (Narasimham Committee 1991 & 1998 — prudential norms (income recognition, asset classification, provisioning — NPA norms), capital adequacy (Basel I → Basel II → Basel III implementation), banking technology (core banking system, RTGS, NEFT, IMPS, UPI — Unified Payments Interface (NPCI, 2016) — real-time inter-bank transactions, RuPay card, BHIM app, BCSBI (Banking Codes & Standards Board of India), financial inclusion (PMJDY — zero-balance accounts (~48 crore accounts by 2023), RuPay debit card, overdraft facility, accident insurance (₹2 lakh), life insurance (₹30,000 under PMJBY), Pradhan Mantri Jeevan Jyoti Bima Yojana (PMJJBY — life insurance ₹2 lakh, premium ₹330/year), Pradhan Mantri Suraksha Bima Yojana (PMSBY — accident insurance ₹2 lakh, premium ₹12/year), Atal Pension Yojana (APY — guaranteed pension ₹1000-5000/month after 60), Stand Up India (loans ₹10 lakh-₹1 crore to SC/ST & women entrepreneurs), MUDRA (Pradhan Mantri Mudra Yojana — micro units development & refinance agency — loans up to ₹10 lakh (Shishu up to ₹50,000, Kishor ₹50,000-₹5 lakh, Tarun ₹5 lakh-₹10 lakh) to micro-enterprises), credit guarantee schemes (CGTMSE — Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro & Small Enterprises), NSIC (National Small Industries Corporation), NSIC - credit guarantee), financial inclusion index (RBI — Financial Inclusion Index (FI Index) — 2021 — 53.9), banking consolidation (merger of public sector banks — 27 PSBs → 12 PSBs (2019-20), Dena Bank & Vijaya Bank → Bank of Baroda, United Bank of India & Oriental Bank of Commerce → Punjab National Bank, Oriental Bank of Commerce & United Bank → Punjab National Bank, Syndicate Bank → Canara Bank, Allahabad Bank → Indian Bank, Andhra Bank & Corporation Bank → Union Bank of India, Indian Overseas Bank — remains separate), NPA crisis (bad loans peaked ~₹10 lakh crore gross NPAs (March 2018)), Insolvency & Bankruptcy Code (IBC) 2016 — NCLT/NCLAT, insolvency professionals, insolvency resolution process (CIRP), liquidation, Insolvency & Bankruptcy Board of India (IBBI), strategic debt resolution (SDR) (discontinued), Scheme for Sustainable Structuring of Stressed Assets (S4A) (discontinued), asset reconstruction companies (ARCs), National Asset Reconstruction Company Ltd (NARCL) — "bad bank" (2021) with India Debt Resolution Company Ltd (IDRCL).
  • Fiscal sector reforms: FRBM Act 2003 — targets (eliminate revenue deficit, reduce fiscal deficit to 3% of GDP), NK Singh Committee (2017) — debt-to-GDP targets (40% centre + 20% states = 60% combined by 2024-25), fiscal deficit targets (flexible — deviation up to 0.5% of GDP for specified reasons), revenue deficit elimination target, GST (101st Amendment 2016 — Article 246A, 269A, 279A — GST Council (chair: Union Finance Minister, members: all state finance ministers), GST rates (5%, 12%, 18%, 28% slabs + cess on sin/luxury goods), compensation to states (revenue loss compensation for 5 years (2017-2022) — funded by compensation cess (sin & luxury goods)), GST composition scheme (composition levy for small businesses (turnover up to ₹1.5 crore)), e-way bill, GSTN (Goods & Services Tax Network — IT backbone), direct taxes (income tax — slabs (old & new regime — Budget 2023 new regime is default), corporate tax (22% for existing companies (Section 115BAA) (September 2019), 15% for new manufacturing companies (Section 115BAB) (no exemptions), MAT (Minimum Alternate Tax) — 15% of book profits (Section 115JB), Dividend Distribution Tax (DDT) abolished 2020 (dividend taxed in hands of shareholders at applicable rates), Securities Transaction Tax (STT), Goods & Services Tax (GST) — subsumed central excise duty, service tax, central sales tax (CST), state VAT, octroi, entry tax, luxury tax, entertainment tax (except local bodies), advertisement tax, purchase tax, etc. GST structure — CGST (Central), SGST (State), IGST (Inter-state — collected by Centre), UTGST (Union Territories), GST compensation cess (sin & luxury goods — for compensation to states for revenue loss for 5 years (July 2017-June 2022) — extended beyond June 2022 to repay GST compensation borrowing), GST Council decisions (majority voting — centre 1/3rd weight, states 2/3rd weight, 3/4th majority required), e-way bill (electronic way bill for movement of goods >₹50,000), anti-profiteering authority (National Anti-profiteering Authority (NAA) — ensure benefit of GST rate reduction passed to consumers), GST Appellate Tribunal (pending constitution), GST rate rationalization (ongoing), e-invoicing (for turnover >₹5 crore (from Aug 2023) & ₹5 crore threshold extended), QR code on B2B invoices.
  • External sector: Balance of payments (current account — trade balance (exports - imports), invisibles (services, transfers, investment income), net errors & omissions; capital account (FDI, FPI, ECB, NRI deposits, banking capital, external assistance), BoP crisis 1991 — forex reserves ~$1 billion, devaluation of rupee (July 1991 — rupee devalued ~18-19% in two steps), current account convertibility achieved 1994, capital account convertibility — partial (Tarapore Committee roadmap), forex reserves (~$580 billion (2023) — 4th largest globally (after China, Japan, Switzerland)), exchange rate management (managed float — market determined with RBI intervention), REER (Real Effective Exchange Rate) — RBI computes NEER & REER, foreign trade policy (FTP) 2015-20 (extended to 2023-24 — Merchandise Exports from India Scheme (MEIS) replaced by RoDTEP (Remission of Duties and Taxes on Exported Products) (2021), Services Exports from India Scheme (SEIS), Export Promotion Zones (EPZs) — converted to SEZs, Agricultural Export Policy 2018, agricultural exports (rice, marine products, spices, cotton, etc.), trade facilitation agreement (WTO), Trade Facilitation Agreement (TFA) — WTO Bali Ministerial Conference 2013 (implementation — single window, pre-arrival processing, risk management, etc.), trade disputes (WTO Dispute Settlement Body (DSB)), India-US trade issues (poultry, solar panels, steel, aluminium (Section 232 tariffs)), India-EU FTA negotiations (restarted 2022), India-UK FTA negotiations (ongoing), India-UAE CEPA (2022), India-Australia ECTA (2022), India-Israel FTA (negotiations), IPEF (Indo-Pacific Economic Framework) — India joined (2022) — 4 pillars (trade, supply chains, clean energy, fair economy) — India did not join trade pillar initially. Export promotion councils (EPCs — APEDA (agricultural & processed marine products), MPEDA (marine products), EPCH (export promotion council of handicrafts), FIEO (Federation of Indian Export Organisations), EEPC (engineering exports), Pharmaceuticals Export Promotion Council (Pharmexcil), Cotton Corporation of India (CCI), Jute Corporation of India (JCI), National Agricultural Cooperative Marketing Federation of India Ltd (NAFED) — nodal agency for price support scheme (PSS) — procurement of pulses & oilseeds at MPS, Market Intervention Scheme (MIS) — perishable horticultural produce), agricultural export policy (2018) — target $100 billion agri-exports (from ~$30 billion (2021-22)), export of organic products, GI (Geographical Indication) — GI tag (Darjeeling tea, Basmati rice, Alphonso mango, Kanchipuram silk, etc.), GI tag benefits (GI tag registration under GI Act 1999).
  • External debt: External debt composition (multilateral (World Bank, IMF, ADB), bilateral (Japan, Germany, France, Russia, USA), commercial borrowings (ECB, FCCB, masala bonds (rupee-denominated Technique: Monetary policy transmission
Question: "Repo rate cut by RBI does not always reach borrowers." Explain.
Answer: Transmission depends on banks' cost of funds (deposit rates), liquidity (surplus liquidity → banks don't pass on rate cuts), NPA burden (high NPAs → banks reluctant to lend), credit demand, credit deposit ratio (CD ratio), external benchmark linkage (external benchmark lending rate (EBLR) — repo rate linked — from Oct 2019 — all new floating rate personal & retail loans & MSME loans linked to external benchmark (repo rate, T-bill yield, etc.) — improved transmission for new loans), existing borrowers on MCLR (marginal cost of funds based lending rate) — reset periods (monthly/quarterly/half-yearly/annual) — transmission with lag. Competition among banks, operating costs, risk premium, capital adequacy requirements also matter.

Memory Tricks

  • Inflation targeting: 4% ± 2% (RBI-MPC).
  • FRBM: Fiscal deficit 3% of GDP (centre), revenue deficit elimination.
  • GST slabs: 0%, 5%, 12%, 18%, 28%.
  • WPI vs CPI: WPI = wholesale (goods only, no services), CPI = retail (goods & services).
