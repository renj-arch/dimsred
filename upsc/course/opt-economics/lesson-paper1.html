Key Concepts

Microeconomics

  • Consumer Theory: Consumer preferences (preference axioms: completeness, transitivity, continuity), utility function, ordinal utility, cardinal utility, indifference curve analysis (properties of indifference curves: downward sloping, convex to origin, convexity, higher indifference curve = higher utility), budget constraint (budget line: p1x1 + p2x2 = m), consumer equilibrium (tangency condition: MRS = price ratio), income effect, substitution effect, Slutsky equation (total effect = substitution effect + income effect), compensating variation, equivalent variation, consumer surplus, compensated demand curve (Hicksian demand), ordinary demand curve (Marshallian demand), Giffen goods (inferior good where income effect dominates substitution effect), inferior goods, normal goods, luxury goods, necessity goods.
  • Producer Theory: Production function (Q = f(L, K)), short run vs long run, total product, marginal product, average product, law of diminishing marginal returns, isoquants (properties: downward sloping, convex to origin, higher isoquant = higher output), marginal rate of technical substitution (MRTS = MPL/MPK), isocost line (wL + rK = C), optimal input combination (MRTS = w/r), cost minimization, expansion path, long run vs short run production.
  • Costs: Total cost (TC = FC + VC), average cost (AC = TC/q), marginal cost (MC = dTC/dq), average variable cost (AVC), average fixed cost (AFC), relationship between AC, MC, AVC, AFC, U-shaped AC curve, economies of scale (increasing returns to scale), diseconomies of scale (decreasing returns to scale), constant returns to scale, economies of scope, learning by doing.
  • Profit Maximization: Profit = TR - TC, profit maximization condition (MR = MC), shutdown rule (P < AVC, shut down in short run), profit maximization under perfect competition (P = MR = MC), profit maximization under monopoly (MR = MC, P > MC), markup pricing (markup = (P - MC)/P = -1/elasticity of demand), Lerner index (L = (P - MC)/P = -1/|e|), degree of monopoly power.
  • Market Structures: Perfect competition (perfectly competitive market: many buyers & sellers, homogeneous product, free entry & exit, perfect information), profit maximization under perfect competition (P = MR = MC), short run equilibrium, long run equilibrium (zero economic profit in long run), entry & exit, supply curve (marginal cost curve above AVC), changes in supply & demand, taxes & subsidies, tax incidence, tax incidence depends on elasticities, tax incidence & elasticity, tax incidence & market structure.
  • Monopoly: Monopoly (single seller, no close substitutes, barriers to entry), monopoly power (sources: economies of scale, control of scarce resources, patents & copyrights, government franchises), monopoly profit maximization (MR = MC, P > MC, markup rule: P = MC/(1 + 1/|e|)), deadweight loss from monopoly, natural monopoly (economies of scale over entire range of output), regulation of monopoly (average cost pricing, marginal cost pricing, rate of return regulation), price discrimination (first degree: perfect price discrimination, second degree: quantity discounts, third degree: different markets have different elasticities), conditions for price discrimination (market power, ability to separate markets, no resale), welfare effects of price discrimination.
  • Monopolistic Competition: Monopolistic competition (many firms, differentiated products, free entry & exit), product differentiation, advertising, excess capacity (excess capacity theorem: monopolistically competitive firms operate below minimum efficient scale), advertising, product differentiation, non-price competition.
  • Oligopoly: Oligopoly (few firms, interdependence, strategic behavior), Cournot model (quantity competition, reaction functions, Nash equilibrium), Bertrand model (price competition, Bertrand paradox: P = MC with identical products), Stackelberg model (Stackelberg leader-follower model), collusion (cartels), prisoner's dilemma, game theory (dominant strategy, Nash equilibrium, dominant strategy equilibrium, subgame perfect equilibrium), cartels (OPEC), tacit collusion, price wars.

Market Failure & Welfare Economics

  • Market Failure: Market failure (market failure occurs when markets fail to allocate resources efficiently), externalities (negative externalities: pollution, positive externalities: education, vaccination), Coase theorem (if property rights are well-defined & transaction costs are zero, bargaining leads to efficient outcome regardless of initial allocation of rights), Pigouvian taxes (Pigouvian tax = marginal external damage at efficient quantity), subsidies (subsidies for positive externalities), tradable permits (cap & trade), public goods (non-rivalrous & non-excludable), free rider problem, private goods, common pool resources (tragedy of the commons), club goods.
  • Public Goods: Public goods (non-rivalrous & non-excludable), free rider problem, provision of public goods (government provision, voluntary contributions, matching grants), Samuelson condition for optimal provision of public goods (sum of MRS = MRT), Lindahl equilibrium, Tiebout model (voting with feet), local public goods.
  • General Equilibrium: General equilibrium (all markets in equilibrium simultaneously), Edgeworth box (exchange economy), contract curve (Pareto efficient allocations), core of the economy, Walras' law (excess demand in one market implies excess demand/supply in other markets), Walrasian equilibrium (competitive equilibrium: all markets clear simultaneously), existence of competitive equilibrium (existence theorem under convexity, convex preferences, convex production sets, local non-satiation), stability of general equilibrium (tâtonnement process, Walrasian adjustment process), comparative statics (comparative statics analysis: how equilibrium changes when parameters change), tariffs & quotas (effects of tariffs on domestic price, quantity, welfare: consumer surplus, producer surplus, government revenue, deadweight loss), optimum tariff (optimal tariff for large country), non-tariff barriers (quotas, voluntary export restraints, technical barriers), domestic content requirements, anti-dumping duties, countervailing duties.
  • Factor Markets: Factor markets (labor market, capital market, land market), derived demand for labor (MRPL = MPL × MR), backward bending labor supply curve, efficiency wages (efficiency wage theory: firms pay above-market wages to increase productivity, reduce shirking, reduce turnover), efficiency wage theory, unemployment (frictional unemployment, structural unemployment, cyclical unemployment, natural rate of unemployment), efficiency wages & involuntary unemployment, insider-outsider model, search theory (job search, reservation wage), human capital (education as investment, signaling model - Spence, screening model), discriminatory wages (taste discrimination, statistical discrimination), compensating differentials (compensating wage differentials for risky/disagreeable jobs).

Welfare Economics

  • Welfare Economics: Pareto efficiency (Pareto optimal: no one can be made better off without making someone else worse off), Pareto improvement, contract curve (set of Pareto efficient allocations in Edgeworth box), first fundamental theorem of welfare economics (competitive equilibrium is Pareto efficient under assumptions: complete markets, complete information, no externalities, perfect competition, no increasing returns), second fundamental theorem of welfare economics (any Pareto efficient allocation can be achieved as a competitive equilibrium with appropriate lump-sum transfers), social welfare function (utilitarian: sum of individual utilities, Rawlsian: maximize welfare of worst-off, utilitarian: sum of utilities, Bergson-Samuelson social welfare function), equity vs efficiency trade-off, Kaldor-Hicks criterion (potential Pareto improvement: gainers could compensate losers), Scitovsky paradox (circularity in compensation criteria), social welfare function (Bergson-Samuelson social welfare function: W = W(U1, U2, ..., Un)), Bergson-Samuelson social welfare function, utilitarian social welfare function (sum of individual utilities), Rawlsian social welfare function (maximize welfare of worst-off), Nozick's entitlement theory, Rawls' theory of justice (difference principle: inequalities justified only if they benefit the least advantaged), Amartya Sen's capability approach (capabilities & functionings), human development index (HDI: health, education, standard of living), multidimensional poverty index (MPI: health, education, living standards), Gini coefficient (measure of income inequality: 0 = perfect equality, 1 = perfect inequality), Lorenz curve (cumulative share of income vs cumulative share of population), Kuznets curve (inverted U-shape: inequality first rises then falls with development), environmental Kuznets curve (pollution increases then falls with income).
  • General Equilibrium & Welfare: General equilibrium (Walrasian equilibrium: all markets clear simultaneously), existence of competitive equilibrium (existence theorem under convexity, convex preferences, convex production sets, local non-satiation), stability of general equilibrium (tâtonnement process, Walrasian adjustment process: price adjustment process: excess demand raises price, excess supply lowers price), comparative statics (comparative statics analysis: how equilibrium changes when parameters change), Edgeworth box (exchange economy: two consumers, two goods, endowment point, contract curve: set of Pareto efficient allocations, core: set of allocations that cannot be improved upon by any coalition), core (core of the economy: set of allocations that cannot be improved upon by any coalition), core convergence theorem (as number of replicas increases, core shrinks to Walrasian equilibrium), Aumann's theorem (core = competitive equilibrium in limit economy with many agents).
  • Social Choice Theory: Social choice theory (voting rules, voting paradoxes: Condorcet paradox, Condorcet winner, Condorcet loser), Arrow's impossibility theorem (no social welfare function satisfies all: unanimity, independence of irrelevant alternatives, non-dictatorship, unrestricted domain), strategic voting, voting cycles, median voter theorem (single-peaked preferences: outcome is median voter's most preferred outcome), agenda setting, strategic voting, agenda manipulation, voting cycles (Condorcet cycle: A beats B, B beats C, C beats A), multi-dimensional voting (no general equilibrium in multi-dimensional voting: McKelvey-Schofield theorem: any agenda can be manipulated to yield any outcome).
Technique: Consumer Equilibrium
Question: 'Derive the demand function for a consumer with utility U(x,y) = x^0.5 y^0.5 and income M, facing prices px and py.'
Answer: Max U = x^0.5 y^0.5 subject to px·x + py·y = m. Lagrangian: L = x^0.5 y^0.5 + λ(m - px·x - py·y). FOCs: ∂L/∂x = 0.5x^(-0.5)y^0.5 - λpx = 0, ∂L/∂y = 0.5x^0.5y^(-0.5) - λpy = 0. Divide: (y/x) = (px/py) → y = (px/py)x. Substitute in budget: px·x + py·(px/py)x = m → 2px·x = m → x̄ = m/(2px), ȳ = m/(2py). Demand functions: x̄ = m/(2px), ȳ = m/(2py).

Memory Tricks

  • Consumer Theory: MRS = -MUx/MUy = -px/py (tangency condition: MRS = price ratio).
  • Producer Theory: Profit max: MR = MC; shutdown rule: P < AVC; break-even: P = ATC.
  • Market Structures: Perfect competition: P = MR = MC; Monopoly: MR = MC, P > MC; Monopolistic competition: MR = MC, P > MC, excess capacity; Oligopoly: strategic interdependence, game theory.
  • Welfare Economics: First welfare theorem: competitive equilibrium is Pareto efficient (under assumptions). Second welfare theorem: any Pareto efficient allocation can be achieved as competitive equilibrium with appropriate lump-sum transfers (separation of efficiency & equity).
