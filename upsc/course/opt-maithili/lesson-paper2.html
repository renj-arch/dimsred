2. How Paper II Questions Are Framed

  • Explanation (Vyakhya): a passage from a prescribed text is given; you explain its meaning, context and literary quality in Maithili.
  • Critical appreciation: you analyse a prescribed poem or prose extract for theme, imagery, structure and aesthetic effect.
  • Essay: a topic on Maithili literature or language; answered in Maithili with textual references.
  • Prescribed-text questions: character, theme, narrative technique or social context of a named work.

The examiner is testing first-hand reading. Generic commentary that could apply to any literature paper scores poorly; answers anchored in the specific text — its characters, stanzas, narrative choices — score well.

3. Literary Criticism Frameworks Used in Paper II

Indian poetics provides the vocabulary examiners expect. Learn each term as a distinct concept, not interchangeable labels:

  • Rasa: the aesthetic flavour or emotional essence a work produces in the sensitive reader (e.g., Shringara in Vidyapati's Geet-Shati).
  • Dhvani: suggestive meaning — what a text implies beyond its literal statement. Requires identifying what is suggested, not just describing sound.
  • Alankara: figures of speech — simile, metaphor, hyperbole and their Maithili equivalents.
  • Vakrokti: indirect or figurative speech — saying one thing to mean another.
  • Chandas: prosody — metre, line structure, sound patterning of the verse forms.
Critical method, not a quotation
When analysing a Vidyapati lyric: (1) identify the literal situation, (2) state the emotion it carries, (3) name the Rasa it evokes, (4) point to the word or image that carries the suggestion (Dhvani), (5) note any Alankara or sound pattern. This sequence works for any prescribed poem — apply it to Govind Das's Bhajanavali and Manbodh's Krishnajanm the same way.

4. Author Profiles You Must Build

For each prescribed author, prepare a one-paragraph profile in your notes: period, genre, major works, characteristic themes, and one representative extract you can analyse from memory of the text (not invented verse). Prioritise Vidyapati, Govind Das, Manbodh, Chanda Jha, Lal Das, Tantra Nath Jha, Surendra Jah 'Suman', Jyotirishwar, Hari Mohan Jha, Manipadma and the modern prose writers — the anthology entries (8 and 9) depend on the official list.

5. Vyakhya and Explanation Strategy

  1. Read the passage twice; mark unfamiliar words and resolve them from context first.
  2. State the literal meaning in straightforward Maithili.
  3. Add the contextual layer: who speaks, to whom, in what situation.
  4. Comment on literary quality: imagery, emotion, sound, structure.
  5. Conclude with the passage's place in the work — how it advances theme or character.
Language discipline

Paper II answers must be in Maithili. Study notes may be in English, but practise writing answers in Maithili — examiners award little for translated or English-medium answers. If a passage's meaning is uncertain, say so honestly rather than guessing at a quotation.

6. Essay Writing

  • Choose the question you can anchor in at least two prescribed texts.
  • Structure: introduction defining the theme → textual evidence with references → comparative point → conclusion.
  • Avoid generic claims ("Maithili literature is devotional") — support every claim with a named work and, where possible, a specific detail.

Common Traps

  • Treating Rasa, Dhvani, Alankara and Vakrokti as interchangeable synonyms — they are distinct critical concepts.
  • Writing answers in English or Hindi — Paper II requires Maithili.
  • Memorising portions from unofficial lists — verify against the notification.
  • Inventing or half-remembering quotations — if you cannot cite the exact text, analyse the idea without fake verbatim claims.
  • Ignoring the prescribed-portion limits (e.g., reading all of Varnaratnakar instead of the Second Kallol).
