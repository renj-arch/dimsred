Key Concepts

Maithili Optional — Syllabus Map & Strategy

Maithili is Indo-Aryan (Bihari group) and is written in Devanagari script. It is spoken mainly in the Mithila region of Bihar and Nepal. It was added to the Eighth Schedule by the 92nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003. The optional has two papers of 250 marks each.

Paper I — History of the Language and Literature

  • History of the language: origin, stages of development, dialect variation and standardisation.
  • Literary movements: Vidyapati tradition, Modern Maithili movement, Sahitya Akademi phase.
  • Major authors and works: period-wise study of representative writers and texts.

Paper II — Prescribed Texts, Criticism and Answer Writing

  • Prescribed texts: close reading of canonical texts and prescribed extracts.
  • Literary criticism: traditional rasa and dhvani criticism, historical criticism and modern stylistic approaches
  • Explanation and essays: paraphrase, context, theme, form and critical evaluation.

Periods at a Glance

  • Early period (14th–16th c.): Vidyapati’s padavali and devotional poetry.
  • Medieval period (17th–19th c.): Courtly and devotional writing; Maithili drama and song.
  • Modern period (20th c.–present): Prose, fiction, drama and literary criticism.
Solved Example: Period-wise Answer
Question: Trace the main stages of Maithili literature.
Answer: Early period (14th–16th c.) — Vidyapati’s padavali and devotional poetry.; Medieval period (17th–19th c.) — Courtly and devotional writing; Maithili drama and song.; Modern period (20th c.–present) — Prose, fiction, drama and literary criticism..

Standard Sources

  • Sahitya Akademi histories of Maithili literature
  • Encyclopaedia of Indian Literature
  • University syllabi and prescribed texts
  • Standard Maithili literary histories

Common Traps

  • Writing a general Indian-literature answer without naming Maithili authors and texts.
  • Confusing periods and movements; give approximate centuries, not exact years you are unsure of.
  • Quoting a translation as if it were the original; mention the translator when you quote.
  • Ignoring the script and dialect question in Paper I.
  • Writing plot summaries instead of critical analysis in Paper II.
Mains strategy
Build a one-page timeline of Maithili literature with period, movement, authors and works. For Paper I, answer in three layers: origin and development → movement → representative authors. For Paper II, use the structure: context → theme → form and language → critical evaluation. Quote short phrases with the original term and an English gloss.

Advanced Comparative Dynamics: The Vidyapati Canon vs. Modernist Deconstruction

While the standard syllabus asks you to list authors, the examiner at the advanced level tests your ability to distinguish between canonical continuity and radical rupture. A critical distinction in Maithili literature is the transition from the Vidyapati-centric aesthetic, which prioritized shringara rasa (erotic sentiment) and metaphysical devotion, to the Modernist deconstruction seen in the works of Ghanshyam Das or Kishori Lal Mishra. The examiner often twists this by asking for a comparative analysis of how the concept of 'love' shifts from divine union in Vidyapati's Madhav-Madhavi to social rebellion in modern prose.

Mechanism of Analysis: Do not merely describe the texts. You must analyze the linguistic shift from chamtiya (classical) vocabulary to bol-bhasha (colloquial) usage in modern fiction. A landmark case for study is the evolution of the padavali form; while Vidyapati used it for devotion, later poets like Bahadur Shah adapted it for courtly satire.

Technique: Comparative Periodisation
Question: Compare the treatment of 'Nature' in Vidyapati's poetry versus the modern Maithili short story. How does this reflect a shift from metaphysical to sociological consciousness?
Solution Structure:
1. Vidyapati Era (Metaphysical Nature): Nature is personified and acts as a mirror for divine love. The seasons (vasanta, varsha) are not just settings but emotional triggers linked to the beloved's beauty.
2. Modern Era (Sociological Nature): In modern fiction (e.g., works by Sashi Kumar), nature often reflects rural decay, drought, or political unrest. The landscape is no longer a sanctuary but a site of struggle.
3. Critical Synthesis: This shift mirrors the broader Indian literary movement from Rasa-based aesthetics to Vishleshan (realism) and social critique.

The Dialectology of Mithila: Standardisation vs. Regional Variation

Paper I often contains questions on dialect variation that go beyond simple definitions. The examiner expects you to understand the mechanisms of standardisation. Maithili has distinct dialects like Bhojpuri-influenced Mithili, Nepali-influenced Mithili, and the pure Mithila Bhasha. The key trap is confusing dialect with language.

Advanced Concept: Study the role of the Sahitya Akademi in codifying the script and vocabulary. A crucial exception to note is how certain words borrowed from Sanskrit are retained in high literature but replaced by Hindi or Urdu loanwords in spoken dialects. For instance, the word for 'love' varies from prema (Sanskrit) to josh or specific local terms depending on the region.

Technique: Lexical Mapping
Question: Trace the influence of Hindi and Nepali on Maithili vocabulary during the colonial period. Provide three specific examples where a standard Maithili word was replaced or adopted from these languages.
Solution Structure:
1. Colonial Context: During the British Raj, administrative needs led to increased borrowing from Hindi (for bureaucratic terms) and Nepali (due to proximity across the border).
2. Specific Examples:
- Term A: Standard Maithili uses X, but colloquial speech adopted Y from Hindi.
- Term B: Religious terms often retained Sanskrit roots, while secular terms borrowed heavily from Hindi.
3. Critical Evaluation: This linguistic borrowing reflects the socio-political integration of Mithila into the broader Bihar-Nepal cultural sphere.

Answer Writing Strategy: The 'Twist' in Literary Criticism

The examiner frequently twists standard literary criticism questions by asking for an analysis of a prescribed text's reception history. For example, instead of just analyzing Vidyapati's poem, you might be asked to analyze how modern critics reinterpret his work through feminist or post-colonial lenses. This requires knowledge of contemporary Maithili literary criticism.

Exam-Day Strategy: When facing a text-based question in Paper II, do not just paraphrase the extract. Identify the underlying theme (e.g., dharma, kama, or social justice) and contrast it with the original context. Always mention the script (Devanagari) and the specific dialect nuances in your introduction to show technical command.

Technique: Contextual Paraphrase
Question: Explain the theme of 'Suffering' in the provided extract from a modern Maithili novel. How does this differ from the suffering depicted in Vidyapati's poetry?
Solution Structure:
1. Contextual Paraphrase: Briefly summarize the extract without plot summary, focusing on the emotional core.
2. Thematic Contrast: Vidyapati's suffering is often spiritual or romantic (separation from the beloved). Modern novels depict material suffering (poverty, caste oppression).
3. Critical Evaluation: Conclude by stating that this shift represents a move from individual existential angst to collective social consciousness.
Exam-Day Strategy: The 'Script' Check
Before writing your answer in Paper II, always write a one-line note on the script and dialect of the text you are analyzing. If the text is from a specific region (e.g., West Mithila), mention how that regional flavor influences the vocabulary. This small addition often secures extra marks for 'technical accuracy' in the examiner's eyes.
  • Ignoring Reception History: Analyzing a text only through its original context without mentioning how modern critics view it is a common mistake that limits your score to average levels.
  • Confusing Dialects with Languages: Do not treat Bhojpuri-influenced speech as a separate language; classify it strictly under dialect variation within Maithili unless the syllabus explicitly separates them.
  • Over-Generalizing 'Nature': Avoid saying nature is romantic in all periods. Be specific: Vidyapati = personified nature; Modern = industrialized or decaying nature.
  • Neglecting the Script Question: Always mention the Devanagari script and its standardization history in Paper I answers, as this is a frequent source of marks.
