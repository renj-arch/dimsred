Key Concepts

Geology Paper I — Geomorphology, Structural Geology, Petrology and Stratigraphy

Geomorphology

  • Weathering: physical (frost, exfoliation, salt), chemical (solution, oxidation, hydrolysis), biological; produces regolith and soils.
  • Fluvial landforms: V-shaped valleys, waterfalls, meanders, oxbow lakes, floodplains, deltas, alluvial fans; erosion and deposition processes.
  • Glacial landforms: cirques, arêtes, horns, U-shaped valleys, moraines, drumlins, eskers, outwash plains.
  • Aeolian and coastal: barchans, seif dunes, loess; cliffs, wave-cut platforms, spits, bars, lagoons.
  • Karst: limestone solution — sinkholes, caves, stalactites, stalagmites, karst topography.
  • Geomorphic cycles: Davis’s cycle of erosion (youth, maturity, old age) and Penck’s alternative; polycyclic landscapes.

Structural Geology

  • Folds: anticline, syncline, monocline, recumbent, isoclinal; hinge, limb, axial plane, plunge.
  • Faults: normal, reverse, thrust, strike-slip, oblique; hanging wall and footwall; fault breccia and gouge.
  • Joints and fractures: systematic and non-systematic; joint sets and their relation to stress.
  • Unconformities: angular, non-conformity, disconformity, paraconformity.
  • Cleavage and foliation: slaty cleavage, schistosity, gneissosity; relation to folding.

Petrology

  • Igneous: classification by composition (felsic–mafic) and texture (phaneritic, aphanitic, porphyritic, glassy, vesicular); Bowen’s reaction series; magmatic differentiation, assimilation, fractional crystallisation.
  • Sedimentary: clastic, chemical and organic; textures, sorting, roundness, sedimentary structures (cross-bedding, graded bedding, ripples); diagenesis.
  • Metamorphic: contact and regional metamorphism; facies series; index minerals; P–T paths; migmatites.

Stratigraphy

  • Principles: superposition, original horizontality, lateral continuity, cross-cutting relations, faunal succession.
  • Geological time scale: Archaean, Proterozoic, Palaeozoic, Mesozoic, Cenozoic; key boundaries and events.
  • Indian stratigraphy: Dharwar Supergroup, Cuddapah, Vindhyan, Palaeozoic Tethyan sequences, Gondwana coal measures, Deccan Traps, Siwaliks.
Solved Example: Normal vs Reverse Fault
Question: Distinguish a normal fault from a reverse fault.
Answer: In a normal fault the hanging wall moves down relative to the footwall, caused by extension; the fault plane dips steeply. In a reverse fault the hanging wall moves up relative to the footwall, caused by compression; a low-angle reverse fault is a thrust. Normal faults produce grabens and horsts; reverse faults produce thrust sheets and fold-thrust belts.

Common Traps

  • Reversing hanging wall and footwall in fault diagrams.
  • Confusing cleavage with bedding.
  • Naming a rock by colour instead of mineral composition and texture.
  • Mixing Gondwana and Vindhyan ages.
  • Forgetting to label the geological time scale correctly.
Mains strategy
Draw block diagrams for faults and folds, labelled stratigraphic columns and P–T diagrams for metamorphism. Use Indian examples in every answer (Deccan Traps, Aravalli, Himalaya, Gondwana basins). Quote field evidence and textures rather than only definitions.
