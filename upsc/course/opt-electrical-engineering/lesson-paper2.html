Electrical Engineering Paper II — Control Systems, Electrical Machines & Power Systems

Control Systems

  • Control System Components: Open loop vs closed loop control systems, feedback and feedforward control, servo mechanisms, synchros, synchro transmitters and receivers, synchro torque differential systems, synchro capacitor, synchro applications.
  • Block Diagrams & Signal Flow Graphs: Block diagram reduction rules, block diagram algebra, Mason's gain formula, signal flow graphs, Mason's gain formula, Mason's gain formula applications.
  • Time Domain Analysis: Standard test signals (step, step, ramp, parabolic, impulse), transient response of first order and second order systems, rise time, peak time, peak overshoot, settling time, steady state error, steady state error for step, ramp, parabolic inputs, static error constants (Kp, Kv, Kv).
  • Stability Analysis: Routh-Hurwitz criterion, necessary and sufficient conditions for stability, special cases (zero row in Routh array, zero row in auxiliary polynomial), relative stability, root locus (root locus construction rules, angle condition, magnitude condition, angle of departure, angle of arrival, centroid, asymptotes, breakaway points, intersection with imaginary axis), root locus applications.
  • Frequency Response Analysis: Bode plots (magnitude plot, phase plot), corner frequencies, phase margin, gain margin, gain crossover frequency, phase crossover frequency, gain margin, phase margin, Bode plot stability criteria, Nyquist plot (Nyquist stability criterion, Nyquist stability criterion, encirclements of -1 point, Nyquist criterion), Nyquist plot construction.
  • Compensators: Lead compensator, lag compensator, lag-lead compensator, PID controller, PID tuning (Ziegler-Nichols rules), cascade control, feedforward control, cascade control.
  • State Space Analysis: State space representation, state space models (controllable canonical form, observable canonical form, diagonal form, Jordan canonical form), state transition matrix, properties of state transition matrix, solution of state equation, controllability (Kalman's test), observability (duality principle), pole placement (pole placement by state feedback), Ackermann's formula.

Electrical Machines

  • DC Machines: DC machine construction (stator, rotor, armature, field poles, interpoles, compensating windings), EMF equation (E = PNZ/(60A) × φN/60), torque equation (T = (PZ/2πa) × φIa), DC generator characteristics (open circuit characteristic, external characteristics of shunt, series, compound generators), DC motor characteristics (torque-speed characteristics, speed-torque characteristics), speed control of DC motors (armature control, field control, Ward-Leonard system), DC motor starters (3-point starter, 4-point starter).
  • Transformers: Transformer construction (core type, shell type), ideal transformer equations, practical transformer (equivalent circuit, equivalent circuit parameters - R1, X1, R2', X2', R0, X0), equivalent circuit parameters determination (open circuit test, short circuit test), voltage regulation, efficiency, all day efficiency, parallel operation of transformers (conditions for parallel operation, load sharing), autotransformer (advantages, disadvantages, applications), Scott connection (3-phase to 2-phase conversion), Scott connection applications.
  • Induction Motors: 3-phase induction motor construction (stator, rotor - squirrel cage, wound rotor), rotating magnetic field, principle of operation, slip (s = (Ns - N)/Ns), rotor EMF, rotor current, rotor frequency, torque equation (T ∝ φI₂cosφ₂), maximum torque condition (R₂ = X₂), maximum torque at starting (R₂ = X₂), torque-slip characteristics, speed control methods (stator voltage control, rotor resistance control, frequency control, pole changing, cascade control), induction generator, induction generator operation (self-excited induction generator, grid-connected induction generator).
  • Synchronous Machines: Synchronous machine construction (salient pole, cylindrical pole), EMF equation (E₄ = 4.44fNΦK_w), synchronous impedance method (open circuit test, short circuit test), voltage regulation (EMF method, MMF method, ZPF method, Potier method), synchronous motor (starting methods, V-curves, inverted V-curves, power factor control), synchronous condenser, hunting (hunting oscillations, damping windings), synchronous condenser applications.
Technique: Thevenin's Theorem
Question: Find Thevenin equivalent across terminals A-B of a circuit with 12V source, 4Ω resistor in series with parallel combination of 4Ω and 8Ω resistors.
Answer: V_th = open circuit voltage across A-B = voltage across 4Ω resistor = (4/(4+4))×12 = 6V. R_th = 4Ω || 4Ω = 2Ω. Thevenin equivalent: 6V source in series with 2Ω resistor.

Memory Tricks

  • Kirchhoff's Laws: KCL - algebraic sum of currents at node = 0 (charge conservation); KVL - algebraic sum of voltages around loop = 0 (energy conservation).
  • Thevenin's Theorem: V_th = V_oc (open circuit voltage), R_th = V_oc/I_sc (short circuit current), R_th = V_oc/I_sc.
  • Maximum Power Transfer: Maximum power transferred when load resistance equals Thevenin resistance (R_L = R_th), maximum power P_max = V_th²/(4R_th).
  • Resonance: Series resonance - impedance minimum, current maximum, power factor = 1; Parallel resonance - impedance maximum, current minimum, power factor = 1.
Mains strategy
Electrical Engineering answers: draw circuit diagrams, show step-by-step derivation of circuit equations, state assumptions clearly, quote IS codes (IS 456 for RCC, IS 800 for steel, IS 1893 for seismic design, IS 10262 for mix design), quote standard values (electrical resistivity of copper 1.72×10⁻⁸ Ω·m, copper density 8.96 g/cm³, electrical resistivity of aluminium 2.83×10⁻⁸ Ω·m, aluminium density 2.7 g/cm³).
