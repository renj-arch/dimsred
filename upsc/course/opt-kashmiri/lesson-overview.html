Key Concepts

Kashmiri Optional — Syllabus Map & Strategy

Kashmiri is Indo-Aryan (Dardic group) and is written in Perso-Arabic script (Sharada historically). It is spoken mainly in Kashmir Valley, Jammu and Kashmir. It is one of the original Eighth Schedule languages (1950). The optional has two papers of 250 marks each.

Paper I — History of the Language and Literature

  • History of the language: origin, stages of development, dialect variation and standardisation.
  • Literary movements: Shaiva mystic movement, Rishi order, Sufi poetry, Modern Kashmiri movement.
  • Major authors and works: period-wise study of representative writers and texts.

Paper II — Prescribed Texts, Criticism and Answer Writing

  • Prescribed texts: close reading of canonical texts and prescribed extracts.
  • Literary criticism: mystic and Sufi interpretation, traditional rasa criticism, modern stylistic and postcolonial approaches
  • Explanation and essays: paraphrase, context, theme, form and critical evaluation.

Periods at a Glance

  • Mystic period (14th–16th c.): Lal Ded’s vakhs and Nund Rishi’s shruks.
  • Medieval and Sufi period (16th–18th c.): Habba Khatoon, Mahmud Gami and Rasul Mir.
  • Modern period (19th–20th c.): Mahjoor, Zinda Kaul and Dina Nath Nadim; modern poetry and prose.
  • Contemporary period: Diaspora writing, conflict literature and new media.
Solved Example: Period-wise Answer
Question: Trace the main stages of Kashmiri literature.
Answer: Mystic period (14th–16th c.) — Lal Ded’s vakhs and Nund Rishi’s shruks.; Medieval and Sufi period (16th–18th c.) — Habba Khatoon, Mahmud Gami and Rasul Mir.; Modern period (19th–20th c.) — Mahjoor, Zinda Kaul and Dina Nath Nadim; modern poetry and prose.; Contemporary period — Diaspora writing, conflict literature and new media..

Standard Sources

  • Sahitya Akademi histories of Kashmiri literature
  • Encyclopaedia of Indian Literature
  • University syllabi and prescribed texts
  • Standard Kashmiri literary histories

Common Traps

  • Writing a general Indian-literature answer without naming Kashmiri authors and texts.
  • Confusing periods and movements; give approximate centuries, not exact years you are unsure of.
  • Quoting a translation as if it were the original; mention the translator when you quote.
  • Ignoring the script and dialect question in Paper I.
  • Writing plot summaries instead of critical analysis in Paper II.
Mains strategy
Build a one-page timeline of Kashmiri literature with period, movement, authors and works. For Paper I, answer in three layers: origin and development → movement → representative authors. For Paper II, use the structure: context → theme → form and language → critical evaluation. Quote short phrases with the original term and an English gloss.
