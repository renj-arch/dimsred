Social Change & Social Movements

  • Social Change: Social change in India (sanskritization, westernization, modernization, globalization, liberalization), Social movements (dalit movement, women's movement, environmental movement, farmers' movement, tribal movement, students' movement, workers' movement)
  • Sanskritization: M.N. Srinivas - lower castes/tribes emulate customs, rituals, lifestyle of upper castes (vegetarianism, sanskrit names, rituals, endogamy) to claim higher ritual status. Examples: yadavs, kurmis, gonds adopting vegetarianism, sanskrit names, sanskrit rituals. Critique: doesn't alter structural hierarchy; reinforces sanskritic norms; ignores regional variations & dalit assertion (ambedkarite politics).
  • Westernization: M.N. Srinivas - impact of british rule on indian society (education, law, science & technology, dress, food habits, language, values), impact on caste, family, marriage, religion, education
  • Modernization: Modernization - industrialization, urbanization, secularization, democratization, social mobility, geographic mobility, occupational mobility
  • Globalization: Globalization - economic globalization, cultural globalization, political globalization, impact on indian society (consumer culture, mnc's, outsourcing, it industry, media, fashion, food habits, lifestyle changes), impact on culture (westernization of culture, consumer culture, fast food, malls, multiplexes)
  • Social Movements: Dalit movement (dr. b.r. ambedkar, dalit panthers, dalit panthers movement), Women's movement (sati abolition, widow remarriage, women's education, women's suffrage, dowry prohibition, domestic violence prevention), Environmental movement (chipko movement, narmada bachao andolan, silent valley movement, chipko movement), Farmers' movement (shekhar sangathan, bharatiya kisan union), Students' movement, Workers' movement

Technique: Sanskritization
Question: 'Explain Sanskritization with examples.'
Answer: Sanskritization (m.n. srinivas) - process by which lower castes/tribes emulate customs, rituals, lifestyle of upper castes (vegetarianism, sanskrit names, rituals, endogamy) to claim higher ritual status. Examples: yadavs, kurmis, gonds adopting vegetarianism, sanskrit names, sanskrit rituals. Critique: doesn't alter structural hierarchy; reinforces sanskritic norms; ignores regional variations & dalit assertion (ambedkarite politics). Examples: yadavs claiming kshatriya status, kurmis claiming kshatriya status, gonds claiming rajput status.

Memory Tricks

  • Four pillars of sociology: Society, culture, social structure, social institutions - SSCI.
  • Major sociological thinkers: Auguste comte (father of sociology), karl marx (conflict theory), emile durkheim (social facts, division of labor, suicide), max weber (interpretive sociology, ideal types, bureaucracy), herbert spencer (social darwinism), herbert blumer (symbolic interactionism).
  • Social stratification: Caste (ascribed, closed, india), class (achieved, open, usa/uk), race (biological, ascribed), ethnicity (cultural, ethnic).
  • Social institutions: Family, marriage, kinship, religion, education, economy, polity - six basic institutions.
  • Social change: Sanskritization (caste emulation), Westernization (western influence), Modernization (industrialization, urbanization), Globalization (global integration).
