Key Concepts

Enlightenment and Revolutions (18th–19th Century)

The Enlightenment (Age of Reason) emphasised reason, science, individual rights, and secularism, challenging monarchical and church authority. It shaped modern political revolutions.

  • Key thinkers: John Locke (natural rights, social contract), Montesquieu (separation of powers), Voltaire (freedom of speech, religious tolerance), Rousseau (general will, popular sovereignty), Adam Smith (capitalism, invisible hand), Immanuel Kant (reason, enlightenment).
  • American Revolution (1775–83): "No taxation without representation"; Declaration of Independence (1776); Articles of Confederation; US Constitution (1787) — federalism, checks and balances, Bill of Rights (1791). First anti-colonial revolution.
  • French Revolution (1789–99): Causes — financial crisis, inequality (Estates-General), bread shortages, Enlightenment ideas. Events — storming of Bastille (1789), Declaration of the Rights of Man (1789), Reign of Terror (Robespierre), rise of Napoleon. Ideals — Liberty, Equality, Fraternity. Spread nationalism and republicanism across Europe.
  • Industrial Revolution (late 18th–19th c.): Began in Britain; steam engine (James Watt), textile machinery, railways, coal, iron. Shift from agrarian to industrial economy; urbanisation; factory system; child labour; rise of capitalism and working class; colonial markets for raw materials and finished goods.

Ideologies and Unifications (19th Century)

  • Nationalism: Loyalty to nation over dynasty/religion; language, shared history, territory as unifying factors; led to unification and independence movements.
  • Liberalism: Constitutional government, individual rights, free markets, limited state.
  • Socialism: Utopian (Owen, Saint-Simon) and scientific (Marx, Engels); critique of capitalism; class struggle; Communist Manifesto (1848).
  • Italian unification (Risorgimento, 1861): Mazzini (Young Italy), Cavour (diplomacy), Garibaldi (Redshirts), Victor Emmanuel II (first king). Role of realpolitik.
  • German unification (1871): Otto von Bismarck's "blood and iron"; Danish War (1864), Austro-Prussian War (1866), Franco-Prussian War (1870–71); proclamation of German Empire at Versailles.
  • Russian emancipation (1861): Serfdom abolished by Alexander II; limited land for peasants; revolutionary ferment continued.

Imperialism and Colonialism (19th–Early 20th Century)

  • Old vs new imperialism: Old (trade, settlements) vs new (political control, economic exploitation, "civilising mission," social Darwinism).
  • Scramble for Africa (1880s–1900): Berlin Conference (1884–85) divided Africa among European powers without African consent; colonisation of Congo, South Africa, Egypt, etc.
  • Asia: British in India, Burma, Malaya; French in Indochina; Dutch in Indonesia; US in Philippines; Japan's Meiji Restoration (1868) — modernisation and imperial expansion (Korea, Taiwan, Manchuria).
  • Consequences: Economic drain, cultural imposition, plantation economies, famines, resistance movements, and seeds of 20th-century decolonisation.

World Wars and the Interwar Period

  • WWI (1914–18): Causes — militarism, alliances, imperialism, nationalism (M-A-I-N); assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand (Sarajevo, 1914); Central Powers vs Allies; trench warfare; Treaty of Versailles (1919) — war guilt clause, reparations, League of Nations (weakened by US non-membership).
  • Russian Revolution (1917): February Revolution (Tsar abdicated, Provisional Government); October Revolution (Bolsheviks, Lenin); Treaty of Brest-Litovsk; Russian Civil War; USSR formed (1922). First communist state.
  • Interwar (1919–39): Weimar Germany (hyperinflation, 1923); Great Depression (1929, US stock market crash, global impact); rise of fascism (Mussolini, Italy, 1922) and nazism (Hitler, Germany, 1933); appeasement; Spanish Civil War (1936–39).
  • WWII (1939–45): Invasion of Poland (1939); Axis (Germany, Italy, Japan) vs Allies (UK, USSR, US); Pearl Harbor (1941); Stalingrad (turning point, 1942–43); D-Day (1944); atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki (1945); Holocaust (6 million Jews); UN formed (1945); Nuremberg trials.

Cold War and Decolonisation (1945–1991)

  • Cold War: US-led capitalist bloc vs USSR-led communist bloc; Truman Doctrine (1947), Marshall Plan (1948), NATO (1949), Warsaw Pact (1955); Korean War (1950–53), Vietnam War, Cuban Missile Crisis (1962), Space Race; détente; fall of Berlin Wall (1989); USSR dissolved (1991).
  • Decolonisation: India (1947), Indonesia (1949), Algeria (1962), African nations (1950s–70s); Non-Aligned Movement (1961, Belgrade; Nehru, Nasser, Tito, Sukarno, Nkrumah); struggles against apartheid (South Africa, Nelson Mandela).
  • Middle East: Israel-Palestine conflict (1948 creation of Israel, wars 1956, 1967, 1973); oil politics; Iranian Revolution (1979); Gulf Wars.

Post-Cold War World

  • Unipolar moment: US as sole superpower; Gulf War (1991); intervention in Balkans.
  • Globalisation: WTO (1995), regional blocs (EU, NAFTA/USMCA, ASEAN, SAARC), information technology revolution, outsourcing, rise of China (economic reforms from 1978, WTO entry 2001), resurgence of Russia.
  • 21st century challenges: 9/11 and War on Terror (Afghanistan 2001, Iraq 2003), climate change, pandemics, rise of multipolarity, great-power competition (US-China), Brexit, regional conflicts.
Technique: Comparing revolutions
Question: Compare the American and French Revolutions.
Answer: Both challenged monarchy and drew on Enlightenment ideas. American Revolution was anti-colonial (independence from Britain), produced a stable federal republic, and preserved property relations. French Revolution was internal (against the ancien régime), more radical (regicide, Reign of Terror), abolished feudal privileges, and exported revolutionary ideals across Europe through Napoleonic wars. Both inspired later nationalist and democratic movements.
Technique: Cold War blocs
Question: What led to the end of the Cold War?
Answer: Economic stagnation in USSR, costly arms race, Afghanistan war (1979–89), Mikhail Gorbachev's reforms (glasnost — openness, perestroika — restructuring), rise of nationalist movements in Soviet republics, fall of Berlin Wall (1989), and dissolution of USSR (1991) into 15 independent states.

Common Traps

  • Confusing the February and October Revolutions of 1917.
  • Thinking the Treaty of Versailles punished Germany alone — Austria-Hungary and Ottoman Empire were also dismantled (Treaties of Saint-Germain, Sèvres/Lausanne).
  • Assuming decolonisation was peaceful everywhere — Algeria, Vietnam, Kenya (Mau Mau), and Congo saw violent struggles.
  • Missing that NAM was not neutrality but active independence from both blocs.

Memory Tricks

  • M-A-I-N: Militarism, Alliances, Imperialism, Nationalism — causes of WWI.
  • Three unifications: Italy (1861), Germany (1871) — both used realpolitik and wars.
  • Cold War acronyms: NATO (West), Warsaw Pact (East), NAM (independent).
  • UN = 1945: Successor to the failed League of Nations.
Mains exam-day strategy
For world history, connect events to ideas (Enlightenment → revolutions → ideologies). Use maps mentally — locate events geographically. For comparative questions, use a framework: causes, course, leadership, outcome, global impact. Mention treaties and their terms precisely (Versailles, Vienna, Berlin). Avoid Eurocentrism — include Asian, African, and Latin American perspectives where relevant.
