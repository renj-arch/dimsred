Key Concepts

Physiographic Divisions of India

India's physical geography is divided into six major physiographic divisions, each with distinct geological history, relief, drainage, and resource base.

  • The Himalayas: Young fold mountains (formed by Indian-Eurasian plate collision, ~50 million years ago); three parallel ranges — Greater Himalaya (Himadri), Lesser Himalaya (Himachal), Shiwaliks; eastern and western syntaxes; important passes (Nathu La, Shipki La, Rohtang, Zoji La, Banihal, Bum La); rivers — Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra systems originate here.
  • The Northern Plains: Formed by alluvial deposition of Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra and tributaries; Punjab-Haryana-Delhi, Ganga plain, Brahmaputra plain; Bhabar, Terai, Bhangar, Khadar zones; fertile, densely populated.
  • The Peninsular Plateau: Oldest landmass (Gondwana land); triangular shape; bordered by Western and Eastern Ghats; subdivided into Central Highlands (Malwa, Bundelkhand, Baghelkhand, Chotanagpur) and Deccan Plateau; rich in minerals (coal, iron ore, manganese, mica); black cotton soil (regur) in Deccan.
  • The Indian Desert: Thar Desert (Rajasthan) — arid, sandy, sparse vegetation, Luni river, saline lakes (Sambhar), cold desert in Ladakh.
  • The Coastal Plains: Western (Konkan, Kanara, Malabar — narrow, rocky) and Eastern (Coromandel, Northern Circars — wider, deltaic, formed by Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri); lagoons (Chilika, Pulicat, Vembanad).
  • The Islands: Andaman and Nicobar (volcanic origin, Barren Island — only active volcano in India, Saddle Peak — highest), Lakshadweep (coral atolls, 36 islands, Minicoy).

Drainage System

  • Himalayan rivers (perennial): Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra — snow-fed, large basins, form deltas (Ganga-Brahmaputra — world's largest delta, Sundarbans), youthful topography (gorges, V-shaped valleys, waterfalls).
  • Peninsular rivers (seasonal): Narmada, Tapi (west-flowing, rift valleys), Godavari (Dakshin Ganga), Krishna, Kaveri, Mahanadi, Subarnarekha — older, graded, smaller basins, form estuaries (Narmada, Tapi) or deltas (others).
  • Drainage patterns: Dendritic (Indo-Gangetic), radial (Amarkantak — Narmada, Son), trellis (Himalayas), rectangular (Vindhyas).
  • River linking and disputes: Inter-state water disputes — Cauvery (Karnataka-Tamil Nadu), Krishna, Godavari, Mahanadi, Mahanadi, Ravi-Beas (Punjab-Haryana); River Boards Act 1956, Inter-State River Water Disputes Act 1956, tribunals.

Climate of India

  • Controls: Latitude (Tropic of Cancer passes through middle), altitude (Himalayas shield cold winds), pressure and wind systems (NE and SW monsoons), distance from sea, ocean currents, relief.
  • Seasons: Cold weather (Dec–Feb), hot weather (Mar–May), advancing monsoon (June–Sept), retreating monsoon (Oct–Nov).
  • Monsoon mechanism: Differential heating, ITCZ shift, Tibetan plateau heating, Somali jet (Findlater jet), easterly jet (Tropical Easterly Jet), El Niño effect.
  • Rainfall distribution: Very high (>200 cm) — Western Ghats, Northeast, Andaman; high (100–200) — Odisha, West Bengal, Bihar, eastern UP; moderate (50–100) — Gujarat, MP, AP, TN; low (<50) — Rajasthan, Ladakh, parts of Gujarat and Punjab.
  • Climate variability: Droughts, floods, cyclones (Bay of Bengal and Arabian Sea), heat waves, western disturbances (winter rain in northwest).

Soils and Vegetation

  • Soil types (ICAR classification): Alluvial (Indo-Gangetic, most fertile), black/regur (Deccan, cotton), red (TN, Karnataka, Odisha, iron-rich), laterite (Kerala, Karnataka, NE hills, acidic), arid/desert (Rajasthan, saline), forest/mountain (Himalayas, acidic), peaty/marshy (Kerala, Sundarbans, Orissa).
  • Natural vegetation: Tropical evergreen (Western Ghats, Andaman, NE — rainforest), tropical deciduous (monsoon forests — most widespread, teak, sal, shisham), thorny/scrub (Rajasthan, Gujarat — acacia, babul), montane (Himalayas — pine, deodar, oak, rhododendron, alpine), mangroves (Sundarbans — sundari, Andaman, Pichavaram).
  • Conservation: National parks, wildlife sanctuaries, biosphere reserves (Nilgiri, Gulf of Mannar, Sundarbans, Nanda Devi, etc.), Project Tiger, Project Elephant, Project Rhino, Crocodile conservation.

Natural Resources and Agriculture

  • Mineral distribution: Iron ore (Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Karnataka, Goa, Jharkhand), coal (Jharia, Raniganj, Singrauli, Talcher — Gondwana and Tertiary), petroleum (Assam — Digboi, Mumbai High, KG basin, Rajasthan), natural gas, bauxite (Odisha, Gujarat), mica (Andhra, Rajasthan, Bihar), gold (Kolar, Hutti), copper (Khetri, Rajasthan), manganese (Odisha, Madhya Pradesh), limestone (cement).
  • Power resources: Conventional — coal (thermal), hydro (Bhakra, Hirakud, Tehri, Koyna), nuclear (Tarapur, Rawatbhata, Kudankulam, Kalpakkam, Kakrapar), natural gas; Non-conventional — solar (Rajasthan, Gujarat), wind (Tamil Nadu, Gujarat, Maharashtra), biogas, tidal (Gulf of Kutch), geothermal (Ladakh, Himachal).
  • Agriculture: Types — subsistence, intensive, commercial, plantation, mixed; cropping seasons — kharif (rice, cotton, sugarcane), rabi (wheat, gram, mustard), zaid (vegetables, fodder); major crops distribution — rice (WB, UP, AP, TN), wheat (UP, Punjab, Haryana), cotton (Maharashtra, Gujarat, Punjab), tea (Assam, WB, Kerala), coffee (Karnataka, Kerala, TN), spices (Kerala), jute (WB, Assam).
  • Irrigation: Wells, tanks, canals (Indira Gandhi, Bhakra, Damodar), tube wells, drip and sprinkler (micro-irrigation); command areas and waterlogging/salinity issues.

Human Geography

  • Population distribution: Uneven — high density in Indo-Gangetic plains, coastal plains, deltas; low in mountains, deserts, NE hills; density ~464/km² (2011).
  • Migration: Rural-to-urban (employment), inter-state (Hindi belt to Maharashtra, Delhi, Gujarat), international (diaspora, Gulf, US, UK); internal displacement (dams, mining, disasters).
  • Settlements: Rural (compact, semi-compact, hamleted, dispersed) and urban (classified by size — census towns, statutory towns, urban agglomerations, million-plus cities, megacities).
  • Urban issues: Slums, traffic, pollution, waste management, water scarcity, urban floods, informal economy.
Technique: River dispute framing
Question: “Inter-state river water disputes are a recurring challenge in Indian federalism.” Examine with examples.
Answer: Water is a state subject (Entry 17, List II) but Centre can legislate for inter-state rivers (Entry 56, List I). Examples — Cauvery (Karnataka-TN, Supreme Court 2018 verdict), Krishna (AP-Telangana-Maharashtra), Mahanadi (Odisha-Chhattisgarh), Ravi-Beas (Punjab-Haryana-Rajasthan). Mechanisms — Inter-State River Water Disputes Act 1956, tribunals (Cauvery, Krishna, Godavari, Mahanadi, Vansadhara), but delays and non-compliance persist. Need — data sharing, basin-level planning, negotiation culture.
Technique: Monsoon variability
Question: How does El Niño affect Indian agriculture?
Answer: El Niño warms the eastern Pacific, often weakening the Indian summer monsoon (reduced rainfall, delayed onset, uneven distribution). Impact — kharif crop stress (rice, pulses), reservoir depletion, food inflation, rural distress. Not always deterministic — Indian Ocean Dipole (positive IOD can offset El Niño), and intra-seasonal variability matter more for local impacts.

Common Traps

  • Assuming all peninsular rivers form deltas — Narmada and Tapi form estuaries (flow west into rift valleys).
  • Missing that the Tropic of Cancer passes through 8 Indian states (Gujarat, Rajasthan, MP, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, WB, Tripura, Mizoram).
  • Confusing Western Ghats (continuous, higher, orographic rain) with Eastern Ghats (discontinuous, lower).
  • Thinking Andaman and Nicobar are coral islands — they are volcanic/tectonic; Lakshadweep are coral atolls.
  • Overlooking that soil erosion is worse in Shiwaliks, Chambal ravines, and NE hills (jhum cultivation).

Memory Tricks

  • Six physiographic divisions: Himalayas, Northern Plains, Peninsular Plateau, Indian Desert, Coastal Plains, Islands.
  • Perennial vs seasonal: Himalayan = snow-fed (perennial); Peninsular = rain-fed (seasonal).
  • Alluvial sub-divisions: Bhabar (pebble belt, streams disappear), Terai (marshy, stream re-emerges), Bhangar (older alluvium, kankar), Khadar (newer alluvium, fertile).
  • Black soil states: Maharashtra, MP, Gujarat, Andhra, Karnataka, TN — cotton belt.
Mains exam-day strategy
For Indian geography, always support descriptions with a locational map reference (state, river, mountain range). Use sketch maps where permitted. For resource questions, link distribution to geology (coal–Gondwana, iron–Odisha-Jharkhand, petroleum–Assam-Mumbai High). For climate questions, explain mechanism not just pattern. Conclude with sustainable management — conservation, micro-irrigation, disaster-resilient infrastructure.
