Key Concepts

Indian Society — Salient Features

Indian society is characterised by "unity in diversity" — immense regional, linguistic, religious, and cultural variation within a shared civilisational framework. Understanding its structure, dynamics, and challenges is central to GS I.

  • Diversity: 22 scheduled languages, hundreds of dialects, six major religions (Hinduism, Islam, Christianity, Sikhism, Buddhism, Jainism), numerous tribal communities, varied cuisines, attire, and customs across states.
  • Unity: Constitutional framework (single citizenship, federal structure), shared festivals, epics (Ramayana, Mahabharata), pilgrimage sites, and the idea of Bharatvarsha.
  • Caste system: Varna (four-fold classical division) and jati (thousands of birth-based groups); historically rigid, occupation-linked, with untouchability; constitutionally abolished (Article 17) but persists in social practice and politics.
  • Family and marriage: Joint family (traditional), nuclear family (growing, urban), arranged marriages, endogamy, changing gender roles, rising divorce and live-in relationships, LGBTQ+ recognition (Navtej Singh Johar, 2018).
  • Tribal societies: ~705 recognised STs (8.6% of population, 2011 Census); distinct languages, customs, autonomous councils (Sixth Schedule), forest-dependent livelihoods, vulnerable to displacement.

Role of Women and Women's Organisations

  • Constitutional provisions: Article 14 (equality), Article 15(3) (protective discrimination), Article 15(4), Article 16 (equal opportunity), Article 39(a),(d) (equal pay, livelihood), Article 42 (maternity relief), Article 243D/T (33% reservation in Panchayats and Municipalities).
  • Legislations: Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act 2005 (equal coparcenary rights), Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act 2005, Sexual Harassment at Workplace Act 2013, Dowry Prohibition Act 1961, MTP Act 1971 (amended 2021), PCPNDT Act 1994 (sex selection ban), Prohibition of Child Marriage Act 2006.
  • Women's organisations: AIWC (All India Women's Conference, 1927), SEWA (Self-Employed Women's Association, Ela Bhatt), National Federation of Indian Women, Mahila Mandals, SHGs (Self-Help Groups), NGO-led movements (Narmada Bachao — Medha Patkar, Chipko — women's participation).
  • Issues: Declining child sex ratio, domestic violence, workplace harassment, low female labour force participation (~25–30%), political underrepresentation (Women's Reservation Bill 2023 — 33% in Lok Sabha and Assemblies, not yet implemented), trafficking, honour killings, witch-hunting.
  • Schemes: Beti Bachao Beti Padhao, Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana, Pradhan Mantri Matru Vandana Yojana, Ujjwala (LPG for women), Stand-Up India (women entrepreneurs), Mission Shakti.

Population and Associated Issues

  • Demographic profile: ~1.4 billion (2023, India surpassed China), median age ~28 (young), fertility rate ~2.0 (below replacement), life expectancy ~70, urbanisation ~35% (rising).
  • Population explosion consequences: Pressure on resources (land, water, food), unemployment, urban slums, environmental degradation, strain on health and education infrastructure.
  • Demographic dividend: Large working-age population — opportunity if skilled and employed; risk if not (demographic burden).
  • Ageing: Rising elderly population (60+), erosion of joint family support, need for pension, healthcare, and elder-friendly infrastructure (National Policy for Older Persons, Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act 2007).
  • Migration: Rural-to-urban (distress and opportunity-driven), inter-state (linguistic and cultural friction), international (diaspora, remittances ~$100 billion), internal displacement (dams, disasters, conflict).
  • Census: Decennial exercise; Census 2021 delayed; SECC 2011 (socio-economic data); NFHS (health and nutrition).

Poverty and Developmental Issues

  • Poverty measurement: Tendulkar line, Rangarajan committee, NITI Aayog MPI (multidimensional — health, education, living standards); ~22% below poverty line (varies by estimate).
  • Vulnerable groups: SCs, STs, OBCs, minorities, landless labourers, informal workers (~90% of workforce), differently-abled, elderly.
  • Development vs growth: GDP growth without equitable distribution; HDI ranking (India ~134/193, 2022); inequality (Gini coefficient rising); regional disparities (south/west vs north/east).
  • Inclusive growth: Sabka Saath Sabka Vikas, Aspirational Districts Programme, Direct Benefit Transfer, financial inclusion (PMJDY), skill development.

Urbanisation and Its Problems

  • Trends: India urbanising fast (~35% now, projected ~50% by 2050); smart cities, AMRUT, PMAY-Urban.
  • Problems: Slums (Dharavi), housing shortage, traffic congestion, pollution (air, water, waste), inadequate water supply and sanitation, informal economy, urban heat islands, disaster vulnerability (floods — Chennai, Mumbai, Bengaluru).
  • Solutions: RERA (real estate), metro rail, waste-to-energy, rainwater harvesting, pedestrianisation, public transport, mixed-use development, participatory planning (74th Amendment — municipalities, ward committees).

Globalisation's Impact on Indian Society

  • Positive: IT and service sector growth, employment, consumer access, exposure to global ideas, women's workforce entry (BPOs, MNCs), remittances, medical tourism.
  • Negative: Consumerism, erosion of traditional values, nuclear families, loneliness, mental health issues, cultural homogenisation, job insecurity (gig economy), agricultural distress (WTO pressures, farmer suicides), digital divide.
  • Social movements: Anti-corruption (Anna Hazare, 2011), environmental (Chipko, Narmada, Save Aarey), gender (Nirbhaya protests, 2012; #MeToo India), caste (Rohith Vemula, 2016), farmers' protest (2020–21).
Technique: Social issue analysis
Question: “Declining child sex ratio reflects deeper societal issues.” Examine.
Answer: Child sex ratio (0–6 years) declined from 927 (2001) to 918 (2011 Census). Causes — son preference, dowry, patriarchy, sex-selective abortion (despite PCPNDT Act), small-family norm with gender bias. Consequences — bride trafficking, polyandry in some regions, skewed marriage markets. Solutions — Beti Bachao Beti Padhao, strict PCPNDT enforcement, women's education and employment, changing social norms through media and role models.
Technique: Demographic dividend
Question: Is India's young population a dividend or a burden?
Answer: It is a potential dividend — 65% of Indians are under 35. Realisation depends on education (NEP 2020), skill (PMKVY), health (Ayushman Bharat), and job creation (manufacturing, services, MSMEs). Without these, it becomes a burden — unemployment, social unrest, wasted potential. East Asia (South Korea, Taiwan) used this window in the 1970s–90s; India must replicate with Indian characteristics.

Common Traps

  • Assuming caste is only a Hindu phenomenon — it exists among Christians, Muslims, and Sikhs too.
  • Thinking urbanisation is only about cities — it includes peri-urban and rural-to-urban migration dynamics.
  • Missing that the Women's Reservation Bill (2023) is linked to delimitation and Census — not yet implemented.
  • Confusing demographic dividend with population explosion — both coexist (large population + young age structure).
  • Overlooking that tribal societies are not homogeneous — distinct languages, economies, and integration levels across regions.

Memory Tricks

  • Women's articles: 14, 15(3), 16, 39, 42, 243D/T.
  • Social evils → laws: Dowry → 1961; Sati → 1829 (Bentinck); Child marriage → 2006; Domestic violence → 2005.
  • Population: Young (median 28), fertility ~2.0, urban ~35%, elderly rising.
Mains exam-day strategy
For society questions, blend constitutional provisions, data (Census, NFHS, NITI Aayog MPI), schemes, and critical analysis. Avoid generalisations — regional, caste, and class differences matter. Use Supreme Court judgments (Navtej Johar, Joseph Shine, Puttaswamy) where relevant. Conclude with a way forward — behavioural change, law enforcement, education, and economic opportunity.
