Key Concepts

Translation is meaning, not word order

Paper A asks for translation in both directions: English to your language and your language to English. The examiner is checking whether the meaning survives and whether the target sentence reads naturally. A literal sentence that preserves word order but breaks the target language loses more than a freer sentence that reads well.

The first rule
Translate the sentence, not the words. If a literal version is unnatural, rewrite it in the target language and check that the meaning has not changed.

English to your language

  • Split long sentences. English uses long clauses; many Indian languages prefer shorter clauses with clear connectives.
  • Choose natural word order. Do not preserve English subject-verb-object order if the target language normally does something else.
  • Keep technical terms consistent. If you translate a term once, use the same term throughout the passage.
  • Watch register. A newspaper editorial is formal; a quoted conversation may not be. Match the passage, not your personal style.

Your language to English

  • Avoid literal translation. A sentence that is grammatical in your language may sound wrong in English.
  • Use standard English collocations. "Take a decision" and "make a decision" are both understandable, but one is the natural choice in the register.
  • Keep tense and modality. Do not turn "may" into "will" or "should" into "must".
  • Do not add explanation. If the source is ambiguous, keep the ambiguity rather than inventing a detail.

Grammar traps that cost marks

  • Subject-verb agreement in long sentences.
  • Tense consistency across a paragraph.
  • Articles and prepositions in English (a, an, the, in, on, at).
  • Reported speech: backshift the tense when the reporting verb is in the past.
  • Modifiers: put the modifier next to the word it describes.

Back-translation

Translate your answer back into the source language without looking at the original. If the back-translation says something different, the first translation lost or added meaning. This is the fastest self-check you can do without a teacher.

Practice drill
Take one 100-word English editorial paragraph. Translate it into your language. Wait ten minutes. Translate it back into English. Compare the two English versions and mark every difference in meaning.
