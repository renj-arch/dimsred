1. Exam Structure, Syllabus & Marks

Paper A is one of two compulsory qualifying language papers in the UPSC Civil Services Main Examination. The paper carries 300 marks over 3 hours. It is qualifying in nature: you must secure a minimum of 25% (75 marks out of 300) to have your other papers evaluated for the merit list UPSC CSE Notification 2024, Section III, Plan of Examination. The marks obtained in Paper A are not added to the final merit ranking.

The 25% Floor — Not a Target
Treat 75/300 as an absolute floor. Candidates who score below 25% in either Paper A or Paper B are not ranked regardless of their performance in the seven scored papers (Essay, GS I–IV, Optional I–II). Historical data shows that 3–5% of otherwise strong aspirants fail the language qualifying papers each year due to neglect.

1.1 Syllabus Breakdown

The UPSC notification lists five components for Paper A. The official notification does not prescribe a fixed mark split among them; the examiner has discretion to weight sections as they see fit. Prepare all five evenly.

Component What is Tested Typical Weight (est.)
Comprehension Reading a passage (300–500 words) and answering questions on meaning, inference, vocabulary, tone and structure ~60 marks
Précis Writing Condensing a passage to ~⅓ length preserving argument, tone and logical flow ~60 marks
Usage & Vocabulary Grammar, sentence construction, idioms, word choice, synonyms/antonyms in the chosen language ~50 marks
Short Essay One or two essays of ~150–200 words each on general/topical subjects ~60 marks
Translation English → chosen language and chosen language → English (2–3 paragraphs each) ~70 marks

Estimated weights based on analysis of UPSC CSE Mains question papers 2018–2024 UPSC Previous Year Question Papers, Paper A (Indian Language), 2018–2024.

Script Fluency is Non-Negotiable
If your handwriting in the chosen script is illegible or slow, you will lose marks on the essay and translation sections simply because you cannot write fast enough. 2. Comprehension & Précis in the Chosen Language

Both comprehension and précis passages in Paper A are set in the chosen Eighth Schedule language (not English). This is a critical difference from Paper B (English). You must read, analyse and write in your chosen language's script and register.

2.1 Comprehension Strategy

  • First read (2 min): Identify the passage's central claim, tone (formal/analytical/narrative) and paragraph structure.
  • Second read (4 min): Mark keywords, transition words (however, therefore, consequently, in contrast), and the author's stance.
  • Question-first approach: Read questions before the third read; locate answers efficiently.
  • Inference vs. fact: Distinguish between explicitly stated information and logical deductions. UPSC comprehension questions heavily test inference.
  • Vocabulary in context: Do not guess word meanings from sound; derive from surrounding sentences.

2.2 Précis Writing Method (Step-by-Step)

  1. Read the passage twice — first for gist, second for logical structure.
  2. Identify the central thesis — one sentence that captures the author's main argument.
  3. List supporting points in order — strip examples, illustrations, repetitions.
  4. Draft the précis in your own words — target ~⅓ original word count; preserve the author's tone (not your opinion).
  5. Check against original — verify no distortion of meaning, no added information, logical connectors intact.
  6. Word count & polish — ensure it reads as a coherent paragraph in the target language's formal register.
Worked Example: Hindi Précis (Original ~180 words → Précis ~60 words)
Original (Hindi): "भारतीय संविधान का अनुच्छेद 21 केवल जीवन और व्यक्तिगत स्वतंत्रता की रक्षा नहीं करता, बल्कि यह गरिमापूर्ण जीवन के अधिकार को भी समाहित करता है। सर्वोच्च न्यायालय ने अनेक निर्णयों में स्पष्ट किया है कि 'जीवन' का अर्थ केवल श्वास लेना नहीं है, बल्कि स्वास्थ्य, शिक्षा, आजीविका और स्वच्छ वातावरण जैसे मौलिक पहलू भी इसमें शामिल हैं। मनीका गांधी बनाम भारत संघ (1978) में न्यायालय ने कहा कि अनुच्छेद 21 की व्याख्या उदार होनी चाहिए। इसी क्रम में, फ्रांसिस कोरलिन बनाम दिल्ली प्रशासन (1981) में कैदियों के अधिकारों की रक्षा की गई। हाल के वर्षों में, निजता का अधिकार (पुत्तस्वामी, 2017) और स्वच्छ वायु का अधिकार (एमसी मेहता श्रृंखला) को भी अनुच्छेद 21 के तहत लाया गया है। इस प्रकार, अनुच्छेद 21 एक जीवंत प्रावधान है जो सामाजिक-आर्थिक परिवर्तनों के साथ विकसित होता है।"
Précis (Hindi): अनुच्छेद 21 केवल जीवन-रक्षा तक सीमित नहीं; यह गरिमापूर्ण जीवन — स्वास्थ्य, शिक्षा, आजीविका, स्वच्छ वातावरण, निजता — का अधिकार सुनिश्चित करता है। मनीका गांधी (1978) ने उदार व्याख्या का मार्ग प्रशस्त किया; फ्रांसिस कोरलिन (1981) और पुत्तस्वामी (2017) ने इसका विस्तार कैदियों के अधिकार और निजता तक किया। अनुच्छेद 21 सामाजिक परिवर्तनों के अनुरूप विकसित होने वाला जीवंत प्रावधान है।
Précis Traps in Indian Languages
1) Translating instead of condensing: A précis is not a translation exercise. 2) Changing register: If the passage is formal Sanskritised Hindi, your précis must be too — not colloquial. 3) Dropping logical connectors: Words like अतः, तथापि, इसी क्रम में, परिणामस्वरूप carry the argument; omitting them breaks the logical flow. 4) Exceeding word limit: Count words in your script; 60 words in Devanagari ≈ 60 words in Romanised count.

3. Essay & Letter Writing

Paper A typically includes one or two short essays of approximately 150–200 words each. Topics are general — social, cultural, economic, or topical — not specialised. The essay tests structured thinking, formal register, and script fluency under time pressure.

3.1 Essay Structure for 150–200 Words

Section Words Function
Opening (Thesis) 25–35 State the central argument; define key terms if needed
Body Point 1 45–60 First supporting argument with one concrete illustration
Body Point 2 45–60 Second supporting argument; contrast or deepen the first
Close 20–30 Restate thesis in new words; forward-looking sentence

3.2 Letter Writing (Formal)

Although not guaranteed every year, formal letter writing appears periodically. The format follows standard Indian official correspondence conventions:

  • Sender's address & date (top right)
  • Receiver's designation & address (left, below date)
  • Subject line — concise, specific
  • Salutation — महोदय / श्रीमान / माननीय (as appropriate)
  • Body — three paragraphs: context, request/detail, expected action
  • Complimentary close — भवदीय / सादर
  • Signature block — name, designation if applicable
Model Letter (Hindi) — Request for Library Upgradation
सेवा में,
जिलाधिकारी,
नई दिल्ली

विषय: जिला पुस्तकालय के आधुनिकीकरण हेतु अनुरोध

महोदय,

निवेदन है कि जिला पुस्तकालय, चाणक्यपुरी में आधारभूत सुविधाओं का अभाव है। संदर्भ ग्रंथों का अभाव, बैठने की अपर्याप्त व्यवस्था, और डिजिटल संसाधनों की अनुपलब्धता के कारण छात्रों और शोधार्थियों को कठिनाई होती है।

अतः आपसे अनुरोध है कि पुस्तकालय के आधुनिकीकरण हेतु निधि आवंटित करने तथा ई-संसाधन केंद्र स्थापित करने की कृपा करें। इससे स्थानीय युवाओं की शैक्षिक आवश्यकताएँ पूरी होंगी।

सादर,
भवदीय
राहुल कुमार
शोधार्थी, दिल्ली विश्वविद्यालय

4. Grammar, Translation & Vocabulary

4.1 Grammar Focus Areas

Grammar questions in Paper A test the formal written standard of the chosen language. Common areas:

  • Sandhi & Samasa (Sanskrit-derived languages): Conjunction rules, compound formation — frequent in Hindi, Marathi, Bengali, Gujarati, Odia, etc.
  • Case endings & postpositions: Correct use of का/के/की, को, से, में, पर (Hindi); equivalent markers in other languages.
  • Verb conjugation & aspect: Perfective vs. imperfective, transitive/intransitive pairs, causative forms.
  • Agreement rules: Subject-verb, adjective-noun gender/number agreement.
  • Sentence transformation: Active ↔ passive, direct ↔ indirect speech, simple ↔ compound ↔ complex.

4.2 Translation Principles

Paper A tests translation both ways: English → chosen language and chosen language → English. The examiner evaluates meaning fidelity and naturalness in the target language — not word-for-word equivalence UPSC CSE Mains Evaluation Guidelines (internal), Translation Section.

Translation Golden Rules
1) Meaning over morphology: If literal translation breaks target grammar, restructure. 2) Register matching: Official English → formal target register; quoted speech → appropriate spoken register. 3) Term consistency: Translate a technical term (e.g., "fundamental rights") the same way every time. 4) No addition/omission: Do not explain, expand, or drop ambiguity — preserve it. 5) Back-translation check: Mentally translate your version back to source; meaning must match.
Translation Example: English → Hindi
Source: "The committee recommended that the legislation be amended to ensure greater transparency in political funding, arguing that the current opacity undermines public trust."

Literal (Poor): "समिति ने सिफारिश की कि विधान संशोधित किया जाए ताकि राजनीतिक वित्तपोषण में अधिक पारदर्शिता सुनिश्चित हो, यह तर्क देते हुए कि वर्तमान अस्पष्टता सार्वजनिक विश्वास को कमजोर करती है।"

Natural (Good): "समिति ने सिफ़ारिश की है कि राजनीतिक वित्तपोषण में अधिक पारदर्शिता सुनिश्चित करने के लिए विधान में संशोधन किया जाए, क्योंकि वर्तमान अपारदर्शिता जन-विश्वास को क्षीण करती है।"

4.3 Vocabulary Building

Focus on three tiers:

  • Tier 1 — Administrative/Official Register: Words used in government orders, notifications, court judgments (e.g., अधिसूचना, संशोधन, प्रवर्तन, अनुपालन, अधिकारिता).
  • Tier 2 — Essay/Argument Connectors: तथापि, अतएव, इसके अलावा, परिणामस्वरूप, इस दृष्टि से, विपरीततः.
  • Tier 3 — Domain-Specific (Polity, Economy, Environment): Build a 200-word glossary for your optional/GS overlap areas in the target language.

5. Practice Protocol

A structured weekly routine builds the script speed, comprehension accuracy, and translation judgement that Paper A demands. The protocol below assumes 6–8 hours/week dedicated to Paper A (adjust for your language's difficulty).

5.1 Weekly Schedule (12-Week Cycle)

Day Activity Duration Output
Mon Comprehension: 1 editorial passage + 5 questions 45 min Marked answers; error log
Tue Précis: 1 passage (300–400 words) → précis + word count 45 min Draft précis; self-check against 6-step method
Wed Translation: 1 paragraph English→Target, 1 Target→English 45 min Two translations; back-translation check
Thu Grammar drill: 15 sentences (transformation, error spotting) 30 min Corrected sentences; rule notes
Fri Essay: 1 topic, 180 words, timed (25 min) 35 min Essay; structure check against template
Sat Vocabulary: 10 new admin/essay words + 5 idioms 20 min Flashcards (Anki/physical); usage sentences
Sun Mock section: Rotate full comprehension/précis/translation 60 min Timed attempt; review every error

5.2 Script Fluency Drills (Daily, 10 min)

  • Copywork: Copy 150 words from a standard text (newspaper editorial, NCERT, government report) in your chosen script. Focus on letter forms, matras, conjuncts.
  • Dictation: Have someone read aloud; write at speaking speed. Check for missed matras, wrong conjuncts.
  • Script Checker: Use the language-specific practice pages on this site — paste your writing; the checker validates Unicode correctness (wrong-script paste, missing virama, incorrect conjunct order).

5.3 Error Log & Review

Maintain a simple spreadsheet with columns: Date | Section | Error Type | Original Sentence | Correction | Rule/Note. Review weekly. Patterns reveal systematic gaps (e.g., consistent misuse of a postposition, confusion between two verb forms).

Language-Specific Practice Pages
Each Eighth Schedule language has a dedicated practice page on this site (/upsc/mains/language/[language].html) with: script checker, comprehension/précis workbooks, translation drills, vocabulary lists, and model-generated sample passages (clearly labelled). Use them — they are calibrated to Paper A's level.

5.4 Final 4-Week Intensification

  • Weeks 1–2: Full 3-hour timed mocks (previous year papers) — one per week. Simulate exam conditions: no dictionary, no breaks.
  • Week 3: Targeted remediation — re-do every error from mocks; rewrite translations; re-precise passages.
  • Week 4: Light review — flashcards, essay outlines, script fluency. No new material. Sleep well.
