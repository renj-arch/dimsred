Key Concepts

Animal Husbandry Paper II — Production, Veterinary Public Health & Allied Sectors

Dairy Production & Management

  • Cattle breeds: Milch: Gir, Sahiwal, Red Sindhi, Tharparkar, Kankrej, Deoni; Dual-purpose: Hariana, Ongole, Hallikar, Krishna Valley; Exotic dairy: Holstein Friesian, Jersey, Brown Swiss.
  • Buffalo breeds: Murrah (Haryana — highest milk), Nili-Ravi (Pakistan/Punjab), Jaffarabadi (Gujarat), Mehsana (Gujarat), Surti (Gujarat), Pandharpuri (Maharashtra), Bhadawari (UP).
  • Clean milk production: Clean udder, clean hands, clean utensils, clean environment, cooling (chilling to 4°C within 2 hours), testing (platform tests: clot-on-boiling, alcohol, acidity; laboratory: SNF, fat, microbial count).
  • Mastitis: Subclinical (no visible signs, detected by SCC/CMT) vs clinical (swollen, hot, painful udder, abnormal milk); pathogens: Staph. aureus, Strep. agalactiae, E. coli; prevention: teat dipping (pre/post), dry cow therapy, culling chronics, clean milking; treatment: intramammary antibiotics.
  • Milk composition: Water ~87%, fat 3-6% (buffalo higher), protein 3-3.5% (casein, whey), lactose 4.5-5%, minerals 0.7%; factors affecting: breed, stage of lactation, parity, season, nutrition, disease.
  • Milk processing: Chilling, filtration, clarification, pasteurization (HTST: 72°C/15s; LTLT: 63°C/30min; UHT: 135°C/1-2s), homogenization (prevents cream separation), packaging, cold chain.
  • Milk products: Butter, ghee, cheese (cheddar, paneer), skimmed milk powder (SMP), whole milk powder (WMP), condensed milk, ice cream, curd/yogurt, butter oil (ghee).
  • Dairy economics: Milk procurement by cooperatives (Amul model — Anand pattern: village society → district union → state federation → GCMMF), Operation Flood (White Revolution — Dr. Verghese Kurien), dairy plant economics, clean milk production incentives.

Poultry Production

  • Layers vs broilers: Layers = egg production (white leghorn, improved indigenous: Vanaraja, Gramapriya, Kalinga Brown); Broilers = meat (Cornish cross, Cobb, Ross).
  • Brooding (0-8 weeks): Temperature (95°F first week, reduce 5°F/week), ventilation, litter (wood shavings, rice husk), space (0.5 sq ft/chick), feed (starter crumbs), water.
  • Housing: Deep litter (floor litter system), cage system (battery cages for layers), free range; ventilation, temperature, humidity (50-65%), light.
  • Feeding phases: Starter (0-4 weeks), grower (5-8 weeks for broilers; up to 18 weeks for layers), finisher (broilers), layer mash (calcium for shell).
  • Vaccination: Day 1: Marek's; Day 7: RD/ND (Ranikhet/Newcastle); 2-3 weeks: IBD (Gumboro); 6-8 weeks: IB (Infectious Bronchitis); 8-10 weeks: RD booster; 12-14 weeks: fowl pox; layers: RD & IBD boosters, egg drop syndrome (EDS) at 14-16 weeks.
  • Economics: Feed cost ~65-70% of broiler cost; FCR (feed conversion ratio) ~1.6-1.8 for broilers (lower = better); egg production economics.

Sheep, Goat, Pig & Other Species

  • Sheep breeds: Wool: Merino, Deccani, Magra, Chokla, Jaisalmeri, Nellore; Meat: Muzaffarnagri, Avdehi, Bellary, Hassan, Mandya; Pashmina: Changthangi (Ladakh), Chegu.
  • Goat breeds: Meat: Boer, Beetal, Jamnapari, Barbari, Sirohi, Osmanabadi; Milk: Jamnapari, Beetal, Surti, Zalawadi; Fiber: Pashmina (Changthangi), Angora.
  • Pig breeds: Yorkshire, Landrace, Duroc, Hampshire (exotic); indigenous: Ghungroo, Niang Megha, Agonda Goan, T & W; pork production: fast growth, high FCR (~3:1), high prolificacy (litter size 8-12).
  • Rabbit, equine, camel, yak, mithun: Rabbit (meat/fur: Soviet Chinchilla, New Zealand White); Equine (horse: Marwari, Kathiawari, Marwari; mules/donkeys); Camel (Bikaneri, Jaisalmeri, Kutchi, Kalachiani); Yak (hilly); Mithun (NE India - mithun farming); Rabbit (meat & wool: Angora).

Veterinary Public Health & Epidemiology

  • Zoonoses: Rabies (dogs), brucellosis (abortion, undulant fever), tuberculosis (M. bovis), anthrax (Bacillus anthracis), leptospirosis, salmonellosis, campylobacteriosis, avian influenza (bird flu), COVID-19 (zoonotic origin discussions), Japanese encephalitis (pigs as amplifying host), nipah (bats/pigs).
  • Food-borne diseases: Salmonella, E. coli O157:H7, Listeria, Campylobacter, Staphylococcus aureus (enterotoxin), Clostridium botulinum (botulism) — from meat, milk, eggs.
  • Food safety systems: HACCP (Hazard Analysis Critical Control Points) in slaughterhouses & dairy plants; FSSAI standards; Agmark; BIS standards; milk & milk products order; meat food products order.
  • Abattoir & slaughter: Humane slaughter (stunning: captive bolt, electrical), halal/kosher considerations, dressing, chilling, inspection (ante-mortem & post-mortem), by-products (blood meal, meat meal, bone meal, hooves, horns, hides).
  • Epidemiology: Measures: incidence (new cases/population at risk), prevalence (existing cases), morbidity (illness), mortality (death), case fatality rate (deaths/cases), attack rate; outbreak investigation (descriptive: person, place, time; analytical: case-control, cohort); surveillance: passive (routine reporting) & active (active search); serosurveillance (antibodies in serum).
  • Vaccination programs (India): FMD (quadrivalent vaccine under NADCP), PPR (sheep/goat), HS (hemorrhagic septicemia - cattle/buffalo), BQ (black quarter - cattle), anthrax (cattle/sheep), rabies (dogs), ranikhet/Newcastle (poultry), fowl pox (poultry), IB (poultry).

Livestock Products Technology

  • Meat processing: Slaughter (stunning: captive bolt/electrical), bleeding (exsanguination), dressing (skinning, evisceration), chilling (0-4°C), aging/ripening (beef), cutting, packaging (vacuum, MAP), freezing (-18°C).
  • Egg processing: Collection, cleaning, grading (by weight & quality), candling (candling = examining egg interior by light), storage (cool), packaging.
  • Wool grading: Based on fiber diameter (micron), length, crimp, color, clean yield; breeds: Merino (fine), Deccani (coarse), Pashmina (ultra-fine, cashmere).
  • Leather: Curing (salting/drying), tanning (chrome/vegetable), finishing; from cattle, buffalo, sheep, goat skins/hides.
  • By-products: Blood meal, meat meal, bone meal, hoof/horn meal, gelatin, tallow, soap, pharmaceuticals (heparin, insulin - historical), albumin.
Technique: Mastitis economics
Question: 'Subclinical mastitis causes greater economic loss than clinical mastitis.' Comment.
Answer: Subclinical mastitis (no visible signs, detected by SCC/CMT) causes higher economic loss because: (1) undetected → continues lactation after lactation, (2) permanent udder damage, (3) milk discarded only when clinical, (4) spreads to herd via milkers, (5) reduces fat% & SNF. Clinical mastitis is detected early & treated. Losses: reduced yield (~15-30% in subclinical), discarded milk, treatment, culling, premature drying. Control: hygiene, teat dipping, dry cow therapy, regular SCC monitoring.

Memory Tricks

  • Milk hormones: Prolactin = makes milk; Oxytocin = lets milk out.
  • Ruminant stomach: Rumen (fermentation), Reticulum (hardware trap), Omasum (water absorption), Abomasum (acid digestion) = RROA.
  • Vaccines: FMD + Brucellosis under NADCP; HS/BQ for cattle; PPR for sheep/goat; RD/ND for poultry.
  • Broiler vs layer: Broiler = meat (fast growth, high FCR); Layer = eggs (longer life, layer mash with calcium).
Mains strategy
For AH&VS Paper II, link to schemes: Rashtriya Gokul Mission (indigenous cattle), National Livestock Mission (NLM), NADCP (FMD & Brucellosis), LIDCP (livestock health & disease control), Dairy processing & infrastructure development fund (DIDF). Quote figures: India largest milk producer (~230 MT), bovine population ~190-300 million, per capita milk availability ~450 g/day, livestock ~30% of agri-GDP, ~50% of income for landless households.
