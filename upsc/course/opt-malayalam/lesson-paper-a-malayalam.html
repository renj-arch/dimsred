Malayalam Paper A (Language): what is actually tested

Paper A is the compulsory Indian-language paper (300 marks, qualifying at 25%). You may answer it in Malayalam. It is not a literature paper: it tests whether you can read, understand, summarise, translate and write correct Malayalam. The nine components are comprehension, precis writing, usage and vocabulary, short essays, translation (English → Malayalam and Malayalam → English), grammar, sentence correction and letter/paragraph writing. Marks here do not count in the merit list, but a fail disqualifies the entire Mains — so this paper is a gate, not a formality.

The script you must be able to write by hand

Vowels (സ്വരം), 14: അ ആ ഇ ഈ ഉ ഊ ഋ എ ഏ ഐ ഒ ഓ ഔ. Consonants (വ്യഞ്ജനം): ക ഖ ഗ ഘ ങ · ച ഛ ജ ഝ ഞ · ട ഠ ഡ ഢ ണ · ത ഥ ദ ധ ന · പ ഫ ബ ഭ മ · യ ര ല വ ശ ഷ സ ഹ ള ഴ റ. Chillu (ചില്ല്), 6: ൻ ർ ൽ ൾ ൺ ൿ. Conjuncts are written with the virama marker ് — ക് + ഷ = ക്ഷ, ജ് + ഞ = ജ്ഞ. The exam is handwritten: practise the stroke order until the conjuncts and chillu are automatic, because a misread letter costs a mark in comprehension and translation.

Sandhi: how words join

  • Savarna-dirgha (സവർണ്ണദീർഘം): അ + അ = ആ; ഇ + ഇ = ഈ — e.g. കവി + ഇന്ദ്രൻ = കവീന്ദ്രൻ.
  • Guna sandhi (ഗുണസന്ധി): അ + ഇ = ഏ; അ + ഉ = ഓ — e.g. സൂര്യ + ഉദയം = സൂര്യോദയം.
  • Vriddhi (വൃദ്ധി): അ + ഏ = ഐ; അ + ഓ = ഔ — e.g. ഏക + ഏകം = ഐകൈകം (recognise the pattern rather than memorise every word).
  • Consonant sandhi: ൽ, ൾ, ൻ before a vowel often become ല, ള, ന — e.g. കാൽ + ഇൽ = കാലിൽ, മകൻ + ആണ് = മകനാണ്.

In Paper A you are not asked to recite sandhi rules; you are asked to write the correct joined form. Practise by joining ten pairs a day and reading the result aloud.

Tense: past -ഇ/-ഉ (എഴുതി, ചെയ്തു), present -ഉന്നു (എഴുതുന്നു), future -ഉം (എഴുതും). Negation: -ഇല്ല / -ആതെ (എഴുതിയില്ല, എഴുതാതെ). Honorifics: അവൻ / അവൾ (informal), അദ്ദേഹം / അവർ (respectful); in official letters use അങ്ങ് / താങ്കൾ. Sentence correction in the paper usually turns on subject–verb agreement, case suffix, or a wrong sandhi — read the sentence aloud and the error is usually audible.

Prosody (ഛന്ദസ്സ്): enough for Paper A

A syllable (അക്ഷരം) is short (ലഘു) or long (ഗുരു); a mātrā (മാത്ര) is one short unit. Dravidian metres are built on mātrā counts — കാകളി and വഞ്ചിപ്പാട്ട് are the two you will meet in appreciation questions. Sanskrit-derived വൃത്തങ്ങൾ (ശ്ലോകം, ആര്യ) are identified by gaṇa patterns. Paper A only asks you to identify the metre or explain a line, not to compose; learn one example of each and the terms ലഘു / ഗുരു / ഗണം / വൃത്തം.

Translation and precis: the scoring sections

English → Malayalam: translate meaning, not word order. Keep official terms in their accepted Malayalam form (ഭരണം, നിയമം, സാമ്പത്തികം) and do not invent a new coinage in the exam hall. Malayalam → English: use simple, correct English; a plain sentence that is grammatical beats a complex one that is not. Precis: one-third of the passage, in your own words, no examples, no direct quotation. Practise with a 150-word newspaper editorial: write a 50-word precis, then check whether it keeps the main claim and drops the illustrations.

Eight-week drill for a qualifying paper

  1. Weeks 1–2: write the alphabet and conjuncts daily for 10 minutes; copy one Malayalam editorial by hand.
  2. Weeks 3–4: ten sandhi joins and ten case-suffix sentences a day; one comprehension passage.
  3. Weeks 5–6: one translation each way and one precis every alternate day; keep a personal error list.
  4. Weeks 7–8: full Paper A under 3 hours, handwritten, once a week. Get it looked at for grammar, not content.

Common traps: writing the essay in literary Malayalam when the paper wants simple standard Malayalam; transliterating English words instead of using the accepted Malayalam term; losing marks in precis by copying whole sentences; and misreading a chillu in comprehension. The paper is qualifying — write plainly, correctly and to length.
