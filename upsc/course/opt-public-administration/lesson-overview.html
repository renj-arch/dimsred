Key Concepts

Public Administration Optional — Overview

Public Administration is a popular optional with significant overlap with GS II (governance, constitution, polity, social justice) and essay. Paper I covers administrative theory, theories of organization, administrative behavior, personnel administration, financial administration. Paper II covers Indian administration, constitutional framework, union government, state government, district administration, local government, financial administration, public sector undertaking, development administration, administrative law, administrative reforms, e-governance.

Paper I — Administrative Theory & Organization

  • Meaning, Nature & Scope of Public Administration: Public administration as art & science, evolution of public administration (classical, human relations, behavioral, systems, ecological, new public administration, new public management), public administration vs private administration, principles of public administration, role of public administration in modern state (welfare state, development administration, good governance).
  • Theories of Organization: Classical theory (Fayol's 14 principles, Taylor's scientific management), Human relations theory (Mayo's Hawthorne studies), Behavioral approach (Chester Barnard - zone of indifference, informal organization), Systems theory (Ludwig von Bertalanffy, Katz & Kahn, general system theory), Ecological approach (Fred Riggs - prismatic society, fused-prismatic-prismatic society, agrarian, industrial, prismatic societies), Public choice theory (Buchanan & Tullock, economic analysis of bureaucracy), New Public Administration (dwight waldo - relevance, values, client focus, change, equity), New Public Management (Hood, Osborne & Gaebler - entrepreneurial government, market-based management, results orientation).
  • Principles of Organization: Division of work, unity of command, unity of direction, span of control, delegation, decentralization, centralization, hierarchy, unity of command, discipline, unity of direction, subordination of individual interest, remuneration, stability of tenure, initiative, esprit de corps.
  • Administrative Behaviour: Motivation (Maslow's hierarchy, Herzberg's two-factor, McGregor's Theory X & Y, Vroom's expectancy theory, Adams' equity theory), leadership (trait theory, behavioral theory, situational/contingency theory, transformational & transactional leadership), communication (communication process, barriers to communication, upward/downward/horizontal communication), motivation & morale, leadership styles (autocratic, democratic, laissez-faire, transformational, transactional, charismatic, servant leadership).
  • Administrative Behaviour (Herbert Simon): Bounded rationality, satisficing vs maximizing, decision-making process (intelligence, design, choice, implementation), programmed vs non-programmed decisions, administrative behavior, organizational theory.
  • Public Personnel Administration (HRM in Public Sector): Recruitment (merit system, patronage system), training (pre-service & in-service), performance appraisal, promotion, discipline & discipline, morale, motivation, collective bargaining, civil service neutrality, civil service neutrality, political neutrality, civil service neutrality.
  • Financial Administration: Budget (budget cycle: preparation, enactment, execution, audit), budget types (line-item, performance, program, zero-based, zero-based budgeting, zero-based budgeting), budgetary process (budget formulation, legislative approval, execution, accounting & auditing), fiscal policy, monetary policy, public debt, fiscal federalism (Centre-State financial relations, Finance Commission, GST Council).
  • Administrative Law: Rule of law (Dicey's three principles), administrative law (delegated legislation, administrative tribunals, ombudsman - Lokpal & Lokayukta, administrative adjudication, natural justice rules - rule against bias, audi alteram partem, right to be heard), public interest litigation (PIL), judicial review, writs (habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, certiorari, quo warranto).
  • Development Administration: Development administration (F.W. Riggs - prismatic society model), development administration vs traditional administration, sustainable development, sustainable development goals (SDGs), good governance (World Bank indicators), citizen's charter, e-governance (National e-Governance Plan - NeGP, Digital India, MyGov, DigiLocker, UMANG), transparency & accountability (RTI Act 2005, social audit, citizen's charter, Lokpal & Lokayukta, Central Vigilance Commission, Central Information Commission).

Paper II — Indian Administration

  • Evolution of Indian Administration: Ancient India (Kautilya's Arthashastra - Mandala theory, Saptanga theory - seven elements of state: Swami, Amatya, Janapada, Durga, Kosha, Danda, Mitra), Medieval India (Mughal administration - Mansabdari system, Jagirdari system, central & provincial administration), British rule (Company rule - Regulating Act 1773, Pitt's India Act 1784, Charter Acts 1813/1833/1853, Government of India Act 1858, Government of India Act 1858 - Crown rule, Indian Councils Act 1861, Indian Councils Act 1892, Indian Councils Act 1909 - Morley-Minto, Government of India Act 1919 - Montagu-Chelmsford - dyarchy, Government of India Act 1935 - federal structure, provincial autonomy), Indian Independence Act 1947, Constitution of India 1950.
  • Constitutional Framework: Preamble (sovereign, socialist, secular, democratic, republic), Fundamental Rights (Articles 12-35), Directive Principles (Articles 36-51), Fundamental Duties (Article 51A), Centre-State relations (legislative - Seventh Schedule - Union, State, Concurrent Lists; administrative - Articles 256-263; financial - Articles 268-281, Finance Commission), Emergency provisions (Articles 352, 356, 360), Centre-State relations (Inter-State Council - Article 263, Zonal Councils, Inter-State Council), Governor's role (Article 153 - appointment, powers, discretion, controversies), Governor's role in state administration, Governor's role in President's Rule (Article 356), President's Rule (Article 356 - grounds, procedure, judicial review - S.R. Bommai case), President's Rule implications.
  • Union Government: President (election, powers & functions, emergency powers, veto powers, pardoning powers), Vice-President, Prime Minister (appointment, powers & functions, Council of Ministers - collective responsibility, individual responsibility, doctrine of collective responsibility), Cabinet (composition, functions, Cabinet committees), Parliament (Lok Sabha - composition, election, speaker, speaker's role, Lok Sabha procedures; Rajya Sabha - composition, election, powers, special powers - Article 249 - Parliament can legislate on State List subjects, Article 312 - All India Services), Parliament procedures (budget procedure, demand for grants, voting of demands, guillotine, Finance Bill, Appropriation Bill, Finance Bill), Parliamentary committees (standing committees, ad-hoc committees, financial committees - Estimates Committee, Public Accounts Committee, Committee on Public Undertakings).
  • State Government: Governor (appointment, powers & functions, discretionary powers), Chief Minister (appointment, powers & functions), Council of Ministers (collective responsibility, individual responsibility), State Legislature (Legislative Assembly - directly elected, Legislative Council - indirectly elected where exists), High Court (jurisdiction, powers, judicial review, writ jurisdiction), subordinate courts (district courts, sessions courts, magistrate courts), Lok Adalats, Gram Nyayalayas, tribunals (administrative tribunals - CAT, income tax appellate tribunal, etc.).
  • District Administration: District administration (District Collector/Magistrate - roles & functions, revenue administration, law & order, development administration, coordination of departments), Sub-division (Sub-divisional Magistrate - SDM), Tehsil/Taluka (Tehsildar/Talukdar), Village (Gram Panchayat - Gram Panchayat, Gram Sabha, Gram Sabha powers & functions), Panchayati Raj (73rd Constitutional Amendment 1992 - three-tier system - Gram Panchayat, Panchayat Samiti, Zila Parishad, reservation for women - 33%, SC/ST seats proportional to population, State Election Commission, State Finance Commission), Urban local bodies (74th Constitutional Amendment 1992 - Municipal Corporation, Municipal Council, Nagar Panchayat, functions & functions of urban local bodies, urban local body finances, urban planning & development - Town and Country Planning, Master Plan, zoning regulations, building bye-laws).

Financial Administration

  • Union Budget: Budget preparation (Ministry of Finance, Department of Economic Affairs, Department of Expenditure, Department of Revenue, Department of Financial Services, Department of Investment & Public Asset Management - DIPAM), budget speech (Finance Minister's budget speech), budget passage in Parliament (general discussion, discussion on demands for grants, voting of demands, guillotine, Appropriation Bill, Finance Bill), implementation of budget, monitoring & evaluation (Parliamentary Committees - Public Accounts Committee, Estimates Committee, Committee on Public Undertakings), CAG (Comptroller & Auditor General - functions, powers, reports), PAC (Public Accounts Committee), Estimates Committee, Committee on Public Undertakings.
  • State Budget: State budget preparation, State Finance Commission, state budget process, state debt, state borrowing limits (Fiscal Responsibility & Budget Management - FRBM Act provisions for states), state share of central taxes (Finance Commission distribution), grants-in-aid (Article 275, Article 282), special category states (special category status - 11 states - North Eastern states, Jammu & Kashmir (before 2019), Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Sikkim), special category status criteria (hilly terrain, low population density, strategic location, economic backwardness, non-viability of state finances).

Common Traps

  • Confusing Articles 352 (National Emergency), 356 (President's Rule), 360 (Financial Emergency).
  • Missing the difference between discretionary powers of Governor (constitutional discretion) and discretion of President (constitutional discretion vs situational discretion).
  • Overlooking that Article 356 (President's Rule) requires both houses of Parliament approval within 2 months (initially 2 months, extendable up to 3 years with parliamentary approval every 6 months).
  • Confusing CAG's role (audit) with PAC's role (examination of CAG reports).
  • Overlooking that Governor's discretionary powers include: reservation of bills for President's consideration (Article 200), sending report to President about failure of constitutional machinery (Article 356), appointment of Chief Minister when no clear majority (discretionary), dismissal of ministry (when it loses majority but refuses to resign - discretion).

Memory Tricks

  • 7th Schedule Lists: Union List (defence, foreign affairs, banking, currency, communications), State List (police, public health, agriculture, local government), Concurrent List (education, forests, wildlife protection, population control, adoption, marriage & divorce, bankruptcy, contempt of court, drugs & poisons).
  • 3-tier Panchayati Raj: Gram Panchayat (village level), Panchayat Samiti (block level), Zila Parishad (district level).
  • 3-tier Urban Local Bodies: Municipal Corporation (population >1 million), Municipal Council (population 1 lakh to 1 million), Nagar Panchayat (population 10,000 to 1 lakh).
  • Financial Articles: Article 268-281 (financial provisions), Article 280 (Finance Commission), Article 280(3)(a) & (b) (devolution of taxes & duties, grants-in-aid), Article 280(3)(b) (grants-in-aid for special needs), Article 275(1) (grants-in-aid for scheduled tribes & scheduled areas), Article 275(2) (grants for development of tribal areas).
