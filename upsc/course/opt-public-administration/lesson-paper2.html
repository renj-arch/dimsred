Public Administration Paper II — Indian Administration & Public Policy

Indian Administration: Constitutional Framework

  • President of India: Article 52-62 - Election, qualifications, term, powers (executive, legislative, financial, emergency, diplomatic, military, pardon), impeachment (Article 61), impeachment process (Article 61), relations with Council of Ministers (Article 74), discretionary powers.
  • Prime Minister & Council of Ministers: Article 74 - aid and advice of Council of Ministers, Article 75 - appointment, tenure, removal, collective responsibility, individual responsibility, cabinet vs council of ministers, kitchen cabinet, role of PM in policy-making, appointment of ministers, distribution of portfolios.
  • Parliament: Lok Sabha (composition, election, powers - legislative, financial, control over executive, constitutional amendment, electoral, judicial), Rajya Sabha (composition, election, special powers under Article 249 - Parliament can legislate on State List subjects in national interest, Article 312 - creation of All India Services), Speaker of Lok Sabha (election, powers, functions, role in money bills, certification of money bills, disciplinary powers), Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha, Leader of Opposition, Leader of the House.
  • State Government: Governor (appointment, powers - executive, legislative, financial, discretionary powers, discretionary powers under Article 356 - President's Rule), Chief Minister (appointment, powers & functions, collective responsibility), Council of Ministers (collective responsibility, individual responsibility), State Legislature (Legislative Assembly - directly elected, Legislative Council - indirectly elected where exists), High Court (jurisdiction, powers, judicial review, writ jurisdiction under Article 226), subordinate courts (district courts, sessions courts, magistrate courts), Lok Adalats, Gram Nyayalayas, tribunals (administrative tribunals - CAT, income tax appellate tribunal, etc.).
  • District Administration: District Collector/Magistrate (DM) - roles & functions (revenue administration, law & order, development administration, coordination of departments, election officer, disaster management), Sub-divisional Magistrate (SDM), Tehsildar/Talukdar, Gram Panchayat (village level - Gram Sabha powers & functions), Panchayat Samiti (block level), Zila Parishad (district level), urban local bodies (Municipal Corporation, Municipal Council, Nagar Panchayat), urban local body finances, urban planning & development (Town and Country Planning, Master Plan, zoning regulations, building bye-laws).

Financial Administration

  • Union Budget: Budget preparation (Ministry of Finance, Department of Economic Affairs, Department of Expenditure, Department of Revenue, Department of Financial Services, Department of Investment & Public Asset Management - DIPAM), budget speech (Finance Minister's budget speech), budget passage in Parliament (general discussion, discussion on demands for grants, voting of demands, guillotine, Appropriation Bill, Finance Bill), implementation of budget, monitoring & evaluation (Parliamentary Committees - Public Accounts Committee, Estimates Committee, Committee on Public Undertakings), CAG (Comptroller & Auditor General - functions, powers, reports), PAC (Public Accounts Committee), Estimates Committee, Committee on Public Undertakings.
  • State Budget:State budget preparation, State Finance Commission, state budget process, state debt, state borrowing limits (Fiscal Responsibility & Budget Management - FRBM Act provisions for states), state share of central taxes (Finance Commission distribution), grants-in-aid (Article 275, Article 282), special category states (special category status - 11 states - North Eastern states, Jammu & Kashmir (before 2019), Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Sikkim), special category states criteria (hilly terrain, low population density, strategic location, economic backwardness, non-viability of state finances).
  • Financial Administration: Budget preparation, budget execution, budgetary control, legislative control over finance (Parliamentary control over finance - demand for grants, voting of demands, guillotine, Appropriation Bill, Finance Bill), legislative control over executive (through questions, adjournment motions, calling attention, short duration discussions), parliamentary committees (standing committees, ad-hoc committees, financial committees - Public Accounts Committee, Estimates Committee, Committee on Public Undertakings), CAG (Comptroller & Auditor General - functions, powers, reports), PAC (Public Accounts Committee), Estimates Committee, Committee on Public Undertakings.
  • Local Government Finance: Local body finances (property tax, water tax, profession tax, entertainment tax, octroi - abolished, service charges), State Finance Commission (recommendations on devolution of taxes & grants to local bodies), 73rd & 74th Constitutional Amendments (devolution of powers & responsibilities to PRIs & ULBs, State Finance Commission, election of local bodies, reservation for women - 33%, SC/ST seats proportional to population), untied funds vs tied funds, untied funds (untied funds - funds that can be used at local discretion), tied funds (funds for specific purposes).

Administrative Law

  • Rule of Law: Dicey's three principles - absence of arbitrary power, equality before law, equality before law, supremacy of law (law is supreme & no one is above law), equality before law (equal treatment before law & courts), rule of law (law is supreme & no one is above law).
  • Administrative Law: Delegated legislation (types - positive & negative delegation, sub-delegation, conditions precedent, commencement clauses, Henry VIII clauses - delegated legislation that amends primary legislation), safeguards against delegated legislation (parliamentary scrutiny - laying of rules, selective & negative resolution, affirmative resolution, consultative committees), adjudication (administrative tribunals - CAT, income tax appellate tribunal, etc.), ombudsman (Lokpal & Lokayukta - functions, powers, composition, appointment, tenure, removal, jurisdiction, investigation of complaints), administrative adjudication (administrative tribunals - CAT, income tax appellate tribunal, etc.), natural justice rules (rule against bias - nemo judex in causa sua, audi alteram partem - right to be heard, reasoned decisions), public interest litigation (PIL), PIL - features (relaxation of locus standi, public interest litigation for public interest, relaxation of locus standi requirements, Supreme Court & High Courts under Articles 32 & 226), judicial review (power of courts to review executive & legislative actions for constitutionality & legality), judicial activism vs judicial restraint.

Development Administration

  • Development Administration: Development administration (F.W. Riggs - prismatic society model), development administration vs traditional administration, development administration characteristics (change-oriented, goal-oriented, client-focused, flexible, innovative), development administration characteristics (change-oriented, goal-oriented, client-focused, flexible, innovative), sustainable development (SDGs - 17 goals, 169 targets, 232 indicators), environmental administration (environmental laws - Environment Protection Act 1986, Water (Prevention & Control of Pollution) Act 1974, Air (Prevention & Control of Pollution) Act 1981, National Clean Air Programme (NCAP) 2019 - target 20-30% reduction in PM2.5 & PM10 by 2024 in 132 non-attainment cities).
  • Public Policy: Policy cycle (agenda setting, policy formulation, policy adoption, policy implementation, policy evaluation), policy implementation (top-down vs bottom-up approach, implementation gap, implementation deficit), policy evaluation (formative evaluation - during implementation, summative evaluation - after implementation), policy transfer & lesson drawing, evidence-based policy making, evidence-based policy making (evidence-based policy making - EBPM).
  • E-Governance: E-governance (National e-Governance Plan - NeGP, Digital India, MyGov, DigiLocker, UMANG - Unified Mobile Application for New-age Governance), e-governance models (G2G - government to government, G2C - government to citizen, G2B - government to business, G2E - government to employee), e-governance challenges (digital divide, digital literacy, infrastructure, privacy & data security, interoperability), e-governance benefits (transparency, accountability, efficiency, citizen participation, reduced corruption, faster service delivery).
  • Transparency & Accountability: RTI Act 2005 (Right to Information Act - provisions, exemptions under Section 8, public information officers, first appellate authority, Central Information Commission - CIC, State Information Commissions - SICs), social audit (community-based monitoring of public programs & services), citizen's charter (citizen's charter - standards of service delivery, time limits, grievance redressal mechanisms), Right to Information Act 2005 (RTI Act - provisions, exemptions under Section 8, public information officers, first appellate authority, Central Information Commission - CIC, State Information Commissions - SICs), citizen's charter (citizen's charter - standards of service delivery, time limits, grievance redressal mechanisms).
Technique: RTI Act answer
Question: 'Discuss the salient features of the Right to Information Act, 2005.'
Answer: RTI Act 2005 - right to information as a fundamental right under Article 19(1)(a), public authorities covered (all public authorities - government departments, PSUs, NGOs substantially funded by government), information definition (any material in any form including records, documents, memos, emails, opinions, advice, press releases, circulars, orders, logbooks, contracts, reports, papers, samples, models, data material held in any electronic form & information relating to any private body which can be accessed by a public authority under any other law for the time being in force), time limits (information within 30 days, 48 hours for life & liberty concerns), fees & charges (application fee ₹10, copying charges ₹2 per page), exemptions (Section 8 - information affecting sovereignty & integrity, security, strategic, scientific or economic interests of India, contempt of court, breach of privilege of Parliament/State Legislature, commercial confidence, trade secrets, intellectual property, fiduciary relationship, information received in confidence from foreign government, information which would impede process of investigation or prosecution, cabinet papers (records of deliberations of Council of Ministers, Secretaries & other officers), information related to personal information the disclosure of which has no relationship to any public activity or interest, or which would cause unwarranted invasion of privacy of the individual), Public Information Officers (PIO - Public Information Officer, Assistant Public Information Officer), First Appellate Authority (within 30 days of receipt of decision), Second Appeal - State Information Commission/Central Information Commission (within 90 days), penalties (₹250 per day up to ₹25,000 for delay or refusal), penalties for frivolous complaints, role of CIC & SICs (Central Information Commission - CIC at national level, State Information Commissions at state level), CIC & SICs powers (summoning persons, requiring production of documents, receiving evidence on affidavit, requiring discovery & inspection of documents, examining witnesses on oath, etc.), penalty for non-compliance (₹250 per day up to ₹25,000), transparency & accountability (RTI promotes transparency & accountability in governance, empowers citizens to seek information from public authorities).
