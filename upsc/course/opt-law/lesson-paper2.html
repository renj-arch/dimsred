Indian Penal Code & Criminal Procedure Code

Indian Penal Code (IPC), 1860

  • General Exceptions (Sections 76-106): Mistake of fact (s.76), act of judge (s.77), accident (s.78), absence of criminal intention (s.79), necessity (s.81), infancy (s.82-83), unsoundness of mind (s.84), intoxication (s.85-86), consent (s.87-89), necessity (s.81), communication (s.93), compulsion (s.94), slight harm (s.95), right of private defence (s.96-106).
  • Criminal Conspiracy (Section 120A-120B): Agreement between two or more persons to do an illegal act or an act by illegal means; punishment for criminal conspiracy.
  • Offences against the State: Waging war against India (121), waging war against any Asiatic power (123), sedition (124A), collecting arms (s.123), concealing design (s.124A).
  • Offences against Public Tranquillity: Unlawful assembly (141), rioting (146), affray (159), criminal conspiracy.
  • Offences against Human Body: Culpable homicide (s.299), murder (s.300), culpable homicide not amounting to murder (s.299 Exception 1-5), attempt to murder (s.307), causing death by negligence (s.304A), hurt (s.319), grievous hurt (s.322), wrongful restraint (s.339), wrongful confinement (s.340).
  • Offences against Property: Theft (s.378), extortion (s.383), robbery (s.390), dacoity (s.391), cheating (s.415), mischief (s.425), criminal trespass (s.441).
  • Offences relating to Marriage: Bigamy (s.494), adultery (s.497 - decriminalized by Joseph Shine v. Union of India 2018), cruelty by husband/relatives (s.498A).

  • Arrest (Chapter V): Arrest without warrant (s.41 - cognizable offences), arrest with warrant (s.42 - non-cognizable offences), arrest by private person (s.44), procedure on arrest (s.46-49), right to be informed of grounds of arrest (s.50), right to bail (s.50(2)), right to be produced before Magistrate within 24 hours (s.57).
  • Complaints to Magistrate (Chapter XV): Complaint to Magistrate (s.190), taking cognizance (s.190), inquiry by Magistrate (s.200), dismissal of complaint (s.203), issue of process (s.204).
  • Charge (Chapter XVII): Form of charge (s.211), particulars of charge (s.212), alteration of charge (s.216-217), joint trial (s.223).
  • Trial (Chapter XVII-XXIII): Trial before Sessions Court (s.225-237), trial of warrant cases by Magistrate (s.238-250), trial of summons cases (s.251-259), summary trials (s.260-265), attendance of persons (s.254-259), evidence in inquiries/trials (s.266-285), judgment (s.274-276).
  • Judgment (Chapter XXV): Language of judgment (s.275), contents of judgment (s.275), judgment on conviction for offences punishable with death/life imprisonment (s.276), confirmation of death sentence (s.366-368), confirmation of sentence (s.368).

  • Relevancy (Chapter II): Relevancy of facts (s.5-16), admissibility of evidence (s.165), facts forming part of same transaction (s.7), facts relating to occurrences (s.8), facts of body or mental state (s.11), motive (s.8), conduct (s.8).

Technique: IPC Sections answer
Question: 'What is the difference between culpable homicide and murder?'
Answer: Culpable homicide (Section 299): causing death by doing an act with intention of causing death, or with intention of causing bodily injury likely to cause death, or with knowledge that act is likely to cause death. Murder (Section 300): culpable homicide is murder if (a) intention to cause death, (b) intention to cause bodily injury likely to cause death, (c) intention to cause bodily injury sufficient to cause death, (d) act imminently dangerous and likely to cause death, with knowledge. All murders are culpable homicide but all culpable homicides are not murder. Exceptions to murder: grave and sudden provocation, private defence, public servant acting in good faith, sudden fight, consent (death of person above 18 years who consents).

Common Traps

  • Confusing culpable homicide with murder - all murders are culpable homicide but not vice versa.
  • Confusing theft with robbery - robbery requires violence or threat of violence.
  • Confusing robbery with dacoity - dacoity requires 5 or more persons.
  • Confusing wrongful restraint with wrongful confinement - wrongful restraint prevents movement in one direction, wrongful confinement prevents movement in all directions.
  • Missing that Section 300 contains exceptions (grave and sudden provocation, private defence, etc.) that reduce murder to culpable homicide not amounting to murder.

Memory Tricks

  • Stages of crime: Intention → Preparation → Attempt → Commission.
  • Degrees of homicide: Culpable homicide → Murder (with intent/intention to cause death) → Death by negligence (Section 304A).
  • Offences against property: Theft → Extortion (threat) → Robbery (violence/fear) → Dacoity (5+ persons).
  • Writs: Habeas Corpus (body), Mandamus (command), Prohibition (stop), Certiorari (transfer), Quo Warranto (authority).
Mains strategy
Criminal law answers: statutory provision (section number) → ingredients of offence → application to facts → case law. Quote landmark judgments: Kedar Nath Singh v. State of Bihar (1962 - Section 124A sedition), Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India (1978 - Article 21), DK Basu v. State of West Bengal (1997 - arrest guidelines), DK Basu guidelines (arrest procedure), DK Basu guidelines (arrest memo), DK Basu (right to lawyer), Hussainara Khatoon (right to speedy trial), Hussainara Khatoon (right to legal aid), Hussainara Khatoon (right to free legal aid).
