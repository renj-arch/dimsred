Key Concepts

Civil Engineering Optional — Syllabus Map & Strategy

Civil Engineering is a technical optional: two papers of 250 marks each, dominated by numericals, design problems and IS-code applications. It rewards engineers who can show working steps clearly; non-engineers should think twice because the formula and code load is heavy.

Paper I — Structural Engineering

  • Building Materials: stones, bricks, lime, cement, concrete, timber and steel — properties, standard tests (IS codes), defects and uses.
  • Solid Mechanics: stress–strain, elastic constants, shear force and bending moment diagrams, torsion, deflection of beams, columns, springs, strain energy.
  • Structural Analysis: determinate and indeterminate structures — slope-deflection, moment distribution, matrix methods, influence lines, arches and cables.
  • Design of Steel Structures: limit state design, bolted and welded connections, tension and compression members, beams, plate girders, columns, column bases (IS 800).
  • Design of Concrete and Masonry Structures: working stress and limit state methods, singly and doubly reinforced beams, T-beams, slabs, columns, footings, water tanks, prestressed concrete (IS 456, IS 1343).

Paper II — Infrastructure and Environment

  • Fluid Mechanics and Hydraulics: fluid statics, Bernoulli's theorem, pipe flow, open channel flow, hydraulic machines (pumps and turbines).
  • Hydrology: precipitation, infiltration, runoff, hydrographs, flood estimation, reservoir planning.
  • Irrigation Engineering: canals, duty and delta, waterlogging, dams, spillways, cross-drainage works.
  • Transportation Engineering: highway geometric design, pavement design, traffic engineering, railway, airport and harbour basics.
  • Environmental Engineering: water treatment, sewage treatment, BOD/COD, air pollution control, solid waste management.
  • Geotechnical and Foundation Engineering: soil properties, consolidation, shear strength, bearing capacity, shallow and deep foundations.
  • Surveying and Geodesy: chain, compass, levelling, theodolite, tacheometry, curves, remote sensing basics.
Solved Example: Degree of Static Indeterminacy
Question: A fixed beam is supported at both ends with all reactions fixed. Find the degree of static indeterminacy.
Answer: A fixed beam has 3 reactions at each end (vertical, horizontal, moment) = 6 reactions. Available equilibrium equations = 3. Degree of static indeterminacy = 6 − 3 = 3.

Common Traps

  • Writing the final answer without showing assumptions, units or IS-code clauses — examiners award step marks.
  • Confusing working stress method with limit state method; UPSC expects the current IS 456 limit state approach unless the question says otherwise.
  • Mixing sign conventions in shear force and bending moment diagrams.
  • Using the wrong formula for degree of indeterminacy (2D vs 3D frames).
  • Ignoring unit consistency (kN vs N, mm vs m) in numericals.
Mains strategy
Answer format: state the given data → assumptions → governing formula/IS clause → step-by-step calculation with units → sketch (SFD/BMD, reinforcement detail, flow diagram) → brief interpretation. Practise drawing neat, labelled sketches; a good diagram earns marks even when the final number slips. Revise IS 456, IS 800 and IS 1343 clause numbers for design questions.
