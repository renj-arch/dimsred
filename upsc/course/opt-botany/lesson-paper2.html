Key Concepts

Botany Paper II — Plant Physiology, Biochemistry, Genetics & Ecology

Plant Physiology

  • Water relations: Water potential (Ψw = Ψs + Ψp), osmosis, diffusion, imbibition, plasmolysis & deplasmolysis, aquaporins, water absorption (active & passive), transpiration (mechanism — cohesion-tension theory, stomatal opening/closing — K+ pump theory, anti-transpirants), guttation.
  • Photosynthesis: Light reaction (PSI & PSII, non-cyclic & cyclic photophosphorylation, Z-scheme), dark reaction (C3 cycle — Calvin cycle: carboxylation by RuBisCO, reduction, regeneration), photorespiration (C2 cycle — wasteful), C4 pathway (Kranz anatomy, PEP carboxylase, mesophyll & bundle sheath cells), CAM plants (stomata open at night, succulents), factors affecting photosynthesis (light, CO2, temperature, water, mineral nutrition), limiting factors (law of limiting factors — Blackman).
  • Respiration: Glycolysis (cytoplasm, glucose → 2 pyruvate + 2 ATP + 2 NADH), Krebs cycle (mitochondrial matrix, pyruvate → CO2 + NADH + FADH2 + ATP/GTP), oxidative phosphorylation (ETC — oxidative phosphorylation, chemiosmosis, ATP synthase), fermentation (alcoholic & lactic acid), RQ (respiratory quotient — carb 1.0, fat & protein < 1), pentose phosphate pathway.
  • Mineral nutrition: Essential elements (17 essential: C, H, O, N, P, K, Ca, Mg, S — macronutrients; Fe, Mn, Cu, Zn, Mo, B, Cl, Ni, Mo — micronutrients), functions & deficiency symptoms (chlorosis, necrosis), hydroponics, critical concentration, nitrogen cycle & biological nitrogen fixation (Rhizobium — symbiotic in legume root nodules, Azotobacter — free living, Azolla — cyanobacteria), nitrification (Nitrosomonas, Nitrobacter), denitrification.
  • Plant growth & development: Phytohormones — auxin (IAA — cell elongation, apical dominance, tropisms), gibberellin (stem elongation, seed germination, bolting), cytokinin (cell division, delay senescence), ethylene (fruit ripening, abscission, triple response), abscisic acid (ABA — stress hormone, stomatal closure, seed dormancy), florigen (flowering signal), photoperiodism (short-day, long-day, day-neutral plants, critical day length), vernalization (cold treatment induces flowering — wheat, vernalization genes), plant movements (tropic — phototropism, geotropism, hydrotropism, thigmotropism; nastic — nyctinasty, seismonasty), senescence & abscission (role of ABA & ethylene).

Biochemistry

  • Carbohydrates: Monosaccharides (glucose, fructose, galactose), disaccharides (sucrose, maltose, lactose), polysaccharides (starch — amylose & amylopectin, glycogen, cellulose, chitin), functions (energy, structure).
  • Proteins: Amino acids (20, essential & non-essential), peptide bond, levels of structure (primary, secondary — α-helix & β-pleated sheet, tertiary, quaternary), denaturation, functions (enzymes, transport, structural, contractile, storage, hormonal, protective).
  • Lipids: Fatty acids (saturated & unsaturated), triglycerides, phospholipids (lecithin, cephalin), glycolipids, sterols (cholesterol, phytosterols), functions (energy storage, membrane structure, insulation, hormones).
  • Nucleic acids: DNA (double helix, Watson-Crick model, base pairing A=T, G≡C, semiconservative replication — Meselson-Stahl experiment), RNA (mRNA, tRNA, rRNA), genetic code (triplet, degenerate, unambiguous, universal, non-overlapping), transcription, translation.
  • Enzymes: Classification (6 major classes — oxidoreductases, transferases, hydrolases, lyases, isomerases, ligases), mechanism (lock & key, induced fit), factors affecting enzyme activity (temperature, pH, substrate concentration, enzyme concentration, inhibitors — competitive & non-competitive, activators), cofactors & coenzymes, allosteric regulation, feedback inhibition.
  • Secondary metabolites: Alkaloids (morphine, quinine, nicotine, caffeine — defense & medicinal), terpenoids (rubber, camphor, carotenoids — vision, pigment), flavonoids & phenolics (anthocyanins — color, tannins — astringency, UV protection), functions (defense against herbivores & pathogens, UV protection, signaling).

Genetics

  • Mendelian genetics: Monohybrid cross (3:1 F2), dihybrid cross (9:3:3:1 F2), law of segregation, law of independent assortment (exceptions — linkage). Test cross & back cross.
  • Linkage & crossing over: Linked genes (same chromosome), coupling & repulsion, crossing over during prophase I (chiasma formation), recombination frequency & genetic mapping (map units / morgan), Sturtevant's mapping.
  • Gene interaction: Epistasis (dominant — 12:3:1, recessive — 9:3:4, duplicate — 15:1, complementary — 9:7, supplementary — 9:3:4), polygenic inheritance (quantitative traits — skin color, height, yield — normal distribution, multiple genes + environment).
  • Multiple alleles: ABO blood groups (IA, IB, i), Rh factor.
  • Cytoplasmic inheritance: Mitochondrial & chloroplast DNA (maternal inheritance), rho (ρ) in Chlamydomonas, poky in Neurospora.
  • Mutation: Spontaneous & induced (mutagens — physical: X-rays, UV, gamma rays; chemical: EMS, mustard gas; chromosomal aberrations — deletion, duplication, inversion, translocation; aneuploidy — monosomy (2n-1), trisomy (2n+1, Down syndrome in humans), euploidy — haploidy, diploidy, polyploidy (autopolyploidy — colchicine induced, allopolyploidy — Triticale, Raphanobrassica).
  • Molecular genetics: DNA structure (Watson-Crick double helix, antiparallel, semiconservative replication — Meselson-Stahl), transcription (RNA polymerase, promoter, terminator), translation (ribosome, mRNA, tRNA, anticodon, initiation/elongation/termination), genetic code (triplet code, 64 codons, 3 stop codons, 1 start codon AUG, degenerate, unambiguous, universal, non-overlapping, commaless), gene regulation — lac operon (Jacob & Monod — structural genes lacZ, lacY, lacA, operator, promoter, repressor, inducer lactose), trp operon (repressible).
Technique: Lac operon answer
Question: 'Explain lac operon regulation in E. coli.'
Answer: lac operon = structural genes lacZ (β-galactosidase), lacY (permease), lacA (transacetylase), controlled by promoter (RNA polymerase binding), operator (repressor binding), regulator gene lacI (repressor protein). Without lactose: repressor binds operator → blocks transcription. With lactose (allolactose): inducer binds repressor → repressor released → transcription occurs. CAP-cAMP complex enhances transcription when glucose low (positive regulation).

Plant Breeding

  • Objectives: Higher yield, disease/pest resistance, drought/frost tolerance, quality (protein, oil, vitamin content), shorter duration, synchronous maturity, non-shattering, responsive to fertilizers.
  • Germplasm: Collection, evaluation, conservation (in-situ — national parks, sanctuaries, biosphere reserves; ex-situ — seed banks, gene banks, field gene banks, cryopreservation, DNA banks), centers of origin (Vavilov's centers — 8 centers: Chinese, Indian, Central Asiatic, Near Eastern, Mediterranean, Abyssinian, South American Mexican & Chilean, South American Brazilian-Paraguayan). India is secondary center for rice, mango, citrus, banana, pigeonpea, black pepper, ginger, turmeric, cardamom.
  • Breeding methods: Self-pollinated crops — mass selection, pure line selection (Johannsen), pedigree method, bulk method, backcross method (transfer of single gene), pedigree vs bulk. Cross-pollinated crops — mass selection, recurrent selection, synthetic varieties, composite varieties, hybrid varieties (inbred lines, combining ability — GCA & SCA, heterosis, CMS — cytoplasmic male sterility for hybrid seed production), mutation breeding (mutagens — physical: X-rays, gamma rays, UV; chemical: EMS, mustard gas, colchicine for polyploidy), somaclonal variation, tissue culture (micropropagation, organogenesis, embryogenesis, protoplast culture & fusion — somatic hybridization), genetic engineering (Agrobacterium tumefaciens as vector, gene gun, Bt cotton, Bt brinjal, golden rice — vitamin A enriched), marker-assisted selection (MAS), DNA fingerprinting (RAPD, RFLP, AFLP, SSR/ISSR, DNA barcoding).
  • Seed technology: Seed quality (genetic, physical, physiological, health), seed certification (breeder, foundation, certified seed), seed treatment, storage.

Ecology

  • Ecosystem: Structure (abiotic — climate, soil, water, light, temperature; biotic — producers, consumers, decomposers), function (productivity — primary & secondary, decomposition, energy flow — food chain & food web, ecological pyramids — number, biomass, energy — upright in most, inverted pyramid of biomass in aquatic), nutrient cycling (gaseous — C, N, O; sedimentary — P, S), residence time.
  • Biogeochemical cycles: Carbon cycle (photosynthesis, respiration, decomposition, combustion, fossil fuels, oceanic exchange), Nitrogen cycle (nitrogen fixation — biological & industrial, nitrification — Nitrosomonas & Nitrobacter, denitrification — Pseudomonas, ammonification, assimilation), Phosphorus cycle (sedimentary, rock weathering), Sulphur cycle.
  • Population ecology: Population attributes (density, natality, mortality, age distribution, sex ratio, dispersal), population growth (J-shaped — exponential, S-shaped — logistic with carrying capacity K), r & K strategies (r — opportunistic, short-lived, high fecundity; K — competitive, long-lived, low fecundity, parental care), life tables & survivorship curves.
  • Community ecology: Community structure (stratification, dominance, keystone species, ecotone & edge effect), ecological succession (hydrosere — hydrarch, xerosere — xerarch, stages: pioneer → seral stages → climax, facilitation, tolerance & inhibition models), biomes (tundra, taiga/coniferous forest, temperate deciduous forest, tropical rainforest, savanna, desert, freshwater, marine).
  • Biodiversity: Levels — genetic, species, ecosystem; measurement (alpha, beta, gamma); hotspots (criteria — endemism & threat, 36 global, India has 4: Western Ghats, Eastern Himalayas, Indo-Burma, Sundaland), threats (habitat loss & fragmentation, overexploitation, alien invasive species — Lantana, Parthenium, Eichhornia, pollution, climate change), conservation — in-situ (national parks, wildlife sanctuaries, biosphere reserves, sacred groves) & ex-situ (botanical gardens, seed banks, gene banks, cryopreservation, tissue culture), IUCN Red List categories (Extinct, Extinct in Wild, Critically Endangered, Endangered, Vulnerable, Near Threatened, Least Concern), India's biodiversity hotspots, Project Tiger, Project Elephant, CITES, CBD (Convention on Biological Diversity), Nagoya Protocol (access & benefit sharing), Ramsar Convention (wetlands), UNESCO MAB (Man & Biosphere Programme — biosphere reserves), National Biodiversity Authority (NBA), Biological Diversity Act 2002.
  • Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA): Process — screening, scoping, impact identification, prediction, mitigation, monitoring, public hearing, decision making; Environmental Management Plan (EMP).

Plant Biotechnology

  • Tissue culture: Totipotency, dedifferentiation & redifferentiation, explant types, callus culture, organogenesis (direct & indirect), embryogenesis (somatic embryogenesis), micropropagation (stages: establishment, multiplication, rooting, hardening), somaclonal variation, haploid production (anther & pollen culture), protoplast isolation & fusion (somatic hybridization — protoplast fusion, cybrids), embryo rescue.
  • Transgenic plants: Methods — Agrobacterium tumefaciens (Ti plasmid — T-DNA transfer), gene gun (biolistics), electroporation, microinjection; transgenic crops — Bt cotton (cry1Ac/cry2Ab against bollworm), Bt brinjal (approved but moratorium), Golden Rice (vitamin A enriched — β-carotene), Flavr Savr tomato, Bt brinjal (Bangladesh approved), regulatory framework in India — GEAC (Genetic Engineering Approval Committee), RCGM (Review Committee on Genetic Manipulation), IBSC (Institutional Biosafety Committee).
  • Molecular markers: RAPD (Random Amplified Polymorphic DNA), RFLP (Restriction Fragment Length Polymorphism), AFLP (Amplified Fragment Length Polymorphism), SSR (Simple Sequence Repeats / microsatellites), ISSR, DNA barcoding (rbcL, matK, trnH-psbA for plants), applications — germplasm characterization, diversity analysis, fingerprinting, marker-assisted selection (MAS).
  • Bioinformatics: Databases (GenBank, EMBL, DDBJ, PDB), BLAST, sequence alignment, phylogenetics.
Technique: GM crop answer
Question: 'Discuss the status of Bt crops in India.'
Answer: Bt cotton (Bollgard I & II — cry1Ac & cry2Ab against bollworm) approved 2002 & widely grown (~95% of cotton area). Bt brinjal approved by GEAC 2009 but moratorium imposed (2010) — not commercially released. Golden Rice (vitamin A enriched) in pipeline but not approved. Regulatory: GEAC is apex body; IBSC at institution level; RCGM under DBT. Concerns: biosafety, biodiversity impact, farmer dependence on MNC seeds, farmer suicides debate, farmer suicides debate. Way forward: case-by-case evaluation, stringent biosafety, farmer education, public sector GM research (public good traits — nutrition, stress tolerance).

Memory Tricks

  • C3 vs C4: C3 — photorespiration losses (rice, wheat); C4 — Kranz anatomy, PEP carboxylase, no photorespiration (maize, sugarcane).
  • Double fertilization: Male gamete 1 + egg → zygote (2n); male gamete 2 + 2 polar nuclei → primary endosperm nucleus (3n) → endosperm.
  • Gymnosperm vs angiosperm: Gymnosperm — naked seeds (cones), no flowers, wind pollination mostly; Angiosperm — seeds enclosed in ovary (fruit), flowers, double fertilization.
  • Mitosis vs meiosis: Mitosis — equational (2n → 2n), somatic; Meiosis — reductional (2n → n), reproductive, crossing over in prophase I.
