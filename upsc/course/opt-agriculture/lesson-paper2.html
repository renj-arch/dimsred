Key Concepts

Agriculture Paper II — Crop Production, Horticulture & Allied Sectors

Field Crops & Package of Practices

  • Rice: Kharif crop; puddled transplanted rice vs direct-seeded rice (DSR); SRI (System of Rice Intensification) — wider spacing, younger seedlings, less water; varieties: IRRI, Samba Mahsuri; pest: stem borer, brown planthopper.
  • Wheat: Rabi crop; varieties: HD-2967, HD-3086; diseases: rust, smut; critical stage: crown root initiation.
  • Maize: Kharif/rabi/summer; hybrid vs composite varieties; pest: fall armyworm (Spodoptera frugiperda).
  • Pulses: Tur (arhar), moong, urad, gram — biological nitrogen fixation through Rhizobium; NFSM-pulses.
  • Oilseeds: Groundnut, mustard, soybean, sunflower, sesame — India imports edible oils; National Mission on Edible Oils (NMEO) — oil palm.
  • Commercial crops: Cotton (Bt cotton — only GM crop), sugarcane (ratooning), jute (retting).
  • Cropping intensity: Rice-wheat system (Indo-Gangetic plain) — issues: residue burning, water table decline, diversification needed.

Horticulture

  • Scope: Fruits (mango, banana, citrus, apple, grapes), vegetables (tomato, potato, onion), flowers (rose, marigold, gladiolus), spices (turmeric, pepper, cardamom), plantation crops (coconut, arecanut, cashew, tea, coffee, rubber).
  • Nursery management: Seed treatment, grafting, budding, layering, tissue culture (micro-propagation).
  • Canopy management: Training, pruning, high-density planting.
  • Post-harvest management: Maturity indices, harvesting, grading, packaging, cold chain, ripening (ethylene), value addition (juice, jam, pickles).
  • Post-harvest losses: 15-25% in fruits/vegetables — causes: poor storage, transport, handling; solutions: cold chain, processing, solar dryers.

Plant Protection

  • Pests: Sucking pests (aphid, jassid, whitefly), chewing (caterpillars), borers (stem borer, fruit borer).
  • Diseases: Fungal (blast, rust, blight), bacterial (blight), viral (mosaic, leaf curl — vector whitefly).
  • Weeds: Parasitic (Striga), noxious (Parthenium) — control: mechanical, chemical (herbicides: pre-emergence vs post-emergence), biological.
  • IPM (Integrated Pest Management): Ecological approach — cultural, mechanical, biological (biocontrol agents: Trichoderma, NPV, ladybird), chemical (last resort), action thresholds.
  • Pesticide residue: MRL (maximum residue limit), pre-harvest interval, safe use, PPE (personal protective equipment).

Animal Husbandry

  • Cattle & buffalo breeds: Cattle: Gir, Sahiwal, Red Sindhi (milch); Hallikar, Kangayam (draught); Buffalo: Murrah, Mehsana, Jaffarabadi (milch).
  • Poultry: Layers (eggs), broilers (meat); broiler management: brooding, litter, vaccination; layer management: lighting, cage/deep litter.
  • Feeding: Roughage (fodder, hay, silage), concentrates (grains, oil cakes), minerals, additives; green fodder: Napier, guinea, berseem, lucerne.
  • Breeding: Artificial insemination (AI), sex-sorted semen, embryo transfer (MOET), crossbreeding (Jersey/HF × indigenous for milk), indigenous conservation (Gir, Sahiwal).
  • Dairy economics: Milk production, procurement (dairy cooperatives — Amul model), value addition (cheese, ghee, powder), clean milk production.
  • Poultry diseases: Ranikhet (Newcastle), fowl pox, coccidiosis — vaccination schedule.

Agricultural Extension

  • Extension methods: Individual (farm visit, office call), group (method demonstration, result demonstration, farmer field school), mass (campaign, exhibition, kisan mela, radio, TV, ICT — Kisan Suvidha, mKisan).
  • Institutions: KVKs (Krishi Vigyan Kendras — technology assessment, refinement, demonstration), ATMA (Agricultural Technology Management Agency — district level), SAUs (State Agricultural Universities), ICAR (Indian Council of Agricultural Research — apex).
  • FPOs: Farmer Producer Organizations — collectivization, input procurement, output marketing, credit linkage.
  • e-NAM: Electronic National Agriculture Market — online trading platform connecting APMCs; quality assaying; transparent price discovery.
  • Adoption of innovations: Innovators, early adopters, early majority, late majority, laggards (Rogers).
Technique: IPM answer
Question: 'Discuss Integrated Pest Management (IPM) as a sustainable plant protection strategy.'
Answer: IPM = ecological approach combining cultural (crop rotation, sanitation), mechanical (handpicking, traps), biological (biocontrol agents: ladybird, Trichoderma, NPV), chemical (pesticides as last resort, pre-harvest interval), monitoring (action thresholds, pheromone traps). Benefits: reduced pesticide residues, resistance management, biodiversity conservation, cost-effectiveness. Constraints: lack of awareness, non-availability of biocontrol agents, market preference for cosmetic perfection.

Memory Tricks

  • Crop seasons: Kharif = monsoon (rice, cotton); Rabi = winter (wheat, mustard); Zaid = summer (watermelon, cucumber).
  • PMFSI/PMFBY: Premium 2% kharif/1.5% rabi/5% commercial crops.
  • Biocontrol agents: Trichoderma (fungal diseases), NPV (caterpillars), ladybird (aphids).
  • GM crop in India: Bt cotton — only approved GM crop.
Mains strategy
For Paper II, structure answers: crop → agro-climatic zone → package of practices → pests/diseases → value addition → government scheme → challenges → way forward. Use flow charts for crop production cycles. Quote NFHS/NFSA/NABARD data where relevant.
