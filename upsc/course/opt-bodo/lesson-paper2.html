Bodo Paper II — Prescribed Texts, Criticism, Explanation and Essays

Prescribed Texts and Close Reading

  • Bodo folk ballads — Oral tradition: Traditional narrative songs, including love and heroic ballads.
  • Bodo primers and early prose — Early Bodo writers: Primers and community publications that standardised the written language.
  • Modern Bodo poetry — Modern Bodo poets: Post-independence poetry collections in Devanagari.
  • Bodo drama and fiction — Modern Bodo writers: Twentieth-century drama and fiction on community and social themes.

Literary Criticism

oral-formulaic study of folk literature, socio-historical criticism and modern stylistic analysis

Explanation (Vyakhya) Strategy

  • Context: identify the text, author, period and speaker.
  • Literal meaning: paraphrase the passage in simple Bodo or English.
  • Theme: state the central idea and the emotional tone.
  • Form: comment on metre, imagery, diction and figures of speech.
  • Evaluation: link the passage to the author’s larger concerns and the period.

Essay Questions

  • Define the literary issue in the question, then take a clear position.
  • Support the argument with at least three texts or authors.
  • Include one counter-argument and respond to it.
  • Conclude with the significance of the issue for Bodo literature.

Solved Example: Explanation
Question: Explain a prescribed passage from Bodo folk ballads.
Answer: Identify the speaker and situation, paraphrase the lines, state the theme, comment on imagery and form, and connect the passage to the work’s central concern. A good explanation moves from literal meaning to literary evaluation.

Common Traps

  • Retelling the plot instead of analysing the passage.
  • Using a translation without naming the translator.
  • Ignoring the prescribed text and writing general remarks.
  • Mixing authors and works from different periods.
  • Making claims about influence without evidence.
Mains strategy
For explanation questions, use the five-step structure: context → literal meaning → theme → form → evaluation. For essay questions, write a thesis statement in the first paragraph, use three body paragraphs with textual evidence, and end with a balanced conclusion. Keep quotations short and accurate.
