Key Concepts

Governance — Meaning and Dimensions

Governance is the process of decision-making and the process by which decisions are implemented (or not implemented). Good governance, as defined by the UNDP and adopted in Indian policy discourse, rests on participation, rule of law, transparency, responsiveness, consensus orientation, equity, effectiveness, efficiency, accountability, and strategic vision.

In the Indian context, governance covers the interface between the state and citizens — service delivery, regulatory frameworks, corruption control, and citizen participation. It spans the Union, states, and local bodies.

Civil Services in India

Constitutional basis: Articles 308–323 (Part XIV) deal with services under the Union and states. Article 311 provides safeguards to civil servants — dismissal, removal, or reduction in rank only after an inquiry (with exceptions for criminal conviction or security reasons).

All India Services: Article 312 empowers Parliament to create All India Services by resolution in the Council of States. The Indian Administrative Service (IAS), Indian Police Service (IPS), and Indian Forest Service (IFoS) are AIS. Officers are recruited by the Centre but serve under states — ensuring national integration and uniform administration.

Central Services: Group A services like Indian Foreign Service (IFS), Indian Revenue Service (IRS), Indian Audit and Accounts Service (IAAS), recruited through UPSC Civil Services Examination.

State Services: Recruited by State Public Service Commissions; serve under state governments.

Role of civil servants: Policy implementation, advisory role to ministers, regulatory functions, developmental administration, and crisis management. Civil servants are expected to be anonymous (minister takes responsibility) and neutral (serve whichever party is in power).

Reforms: 2nd ARC recommendations — citizen-centric administration, performance appraisal, protection of honest officers, lateral entry at joint secretary level, Mission Karmayogi (capacity building), and the National Programme for Civil Services Capacity Building (NPCSCB).

E-Governance

E-governance is the application of ICT to government processes to achieve SMART governance — Simple, Moral, Accountable, Responsive, and Transparent.

  • National e-Governance Plan (NeGP): Approved in 2006; aimed at making all government services accessible to citizens in their locality through common service delivery outlets. Includes Mission Mode Projects (MMPs) such as e-District, e-Courts, MCA21 (corporate affairs), Passport Seva, and Income Tax e-filing.
  • Digital India (2015): Three vision areas — digital infrastructure as a utility to every citizen, governance and services on demand, and digital empowerment of citizens. Pillars include broadband highways, universal mobile connectivity, public internet access, e-governance, e-Kranti, information for all, electronics manufacturing, IT for jobs, and early harvest programmes.
  • Common Service Centres (CSCs): Rural delivery points for government, financial, social, and private sector services — operated by Village Level Entrepreneurs (VLEs).
  • MyGov: Citizen engagement platform for participatory governance.
  • DigiLocker: Cloud-based document storage linked to Aadhaar; reduces need for physical documents.
  • UMANG (Unified Mobile Application for New-age Governance): Single mobile app to access Centre and state government services.
  • Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT): Transfer of subsidies and benefits directly to bank accounts linked with Aadhaar, reducing leakages and middlemen.

Challenges: Digital divide, last-mile connectivity, data privacy, cybersecurity, capacity building of officials, and language barriers.

Right to Information Act, 2005

Constitutional basis: RTI flows from Article 19(1)(a) — freedom of speech and expression includes the right to know. The Supreme Court affirmed this in Raj Narain v. State of UP (1975) and S.P. Gupta v. President of India (1981).

  • Public Authority: Any body constituted under the Constitution or any law, or owned/controlled/substantially financed by the appropriate government. NGOs substantially funded by government are included.
  • Information: Any material in any form — records, documents, memos, emails, opinions, advice, press releases, circulars, orders, logbooks, contracts, reports, papers, samples, models, data material in electronic form.
  • Public Information Officer (PIO): Every public authority must designate a PIO to handle requests. First appeal goes to the First Appellate Authority; second appeal to the State Information Commission or Central Information Commission.
  • Timeline: Information normally within 30 days; 48 hours for life/liberty concerns; 5 days if request is transferred to another authority.
  • Exemptions (Section 8): Information affecting sovereignty/security, contempt of court, breach of legislative privilege, commercial confidence, fiduciary relationships, third-party information, endangering life/physical safety, impeding investigation, and cabinet papers. Partial disclosure allowed (Section 10 — severability).
  • Information Commissions: Central Information Commission (CIC) and State Information Commissions — headed by Chief Information Commissioner and Information Commissioners; status equivalent to Chief Election Commissioner / Election Commissioner.
  • Amendment Act 2019: Centre gained power to fix salaries, allowances, and tenure of Information Commissioners — critics say this impairs independence.

Citizen’s Charter

A Citizen’s Charter is a voluntary written declaration by a service provider about service standards, quality, timeframes, grievance redress, and transparency — enabling citizens to know what to expect and demand accountability.

  • Origin: UK (John Major government, 1991). In India, the Citizen’s Charter movement gained momentum after a 1997 conference in New Delhi; the Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances (DARPG) coordinates it.
  • Six principles: Quality, choice, standards, value, accountability, and transparency.
  • Components: Vision and mission statement, details of services, grievance redress mechanism, expectations from citizens, and additional commitments.
  • Sevottam Model: Developed by DARPG — a service delivery excellence model with three modules: citizen-centricity, information dissemination, and grievance redress. Adopted by many ministries and departments.
  • Challenges: Charters often remain on paper; lack of awareness; no legal enforceability (unlike RTI); poor grievance redress; and insufficient citizen participation in formulation.

Pressure Groups

Pressure groups are organised bodies seeking to influence public policy and decision-making without contesting elections. They are a link between citizens and government.

  • Types: Business groups (FICCI, CII, ASSOCHAM), trade unions (AITUC, INTUC, BMS), farmer organisations (BKU, Shetkari Sanghatana), professional bodies (Bar Council, IMA), student organisations (NSUI, ABVP), religious groups, tribal groups, women’s organisations, environmental groups (Narmada Bachao Andolan), and caste-based groups.
  • Methods: Lobbying, strikes and bandhs, demonstrations, public interest litigation (PIL), media campaigns, memoranda, and electioneering.
  • Role: Articulating interests, political socialisation, policy formulation input, watchdog on administration, and filling representation gaps in a large democracy.
  • Limitations: Uneven influence (business groups often dominate), regional imbalances, methods may disrupt public life, and lack of internal democracy in some groups.

Other Accountability Mechanisms

  • Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013: Statutory anti-corruption body at the Centre (Lokpal) and states (Lokayukta) to inquire into allegations of corruption against public functionaries, including the Prime Minister (with restrictions), Ministers, MPs, and Group A officers.
  • Central Vigilance Commission (CVC): Statutory body (2003) overseeing vigilance administration in Central government organisations; advises on vigilance cases.
  • Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG): Article 148 — audits all expenditure from Consolidated Fund, Contingency Fund, and Public Account; reports laid before Parliament/state legislatures.
  • Social Audit: Mandatory under MGNREGA — citizens verify implementation of works in gram sabhas; institutionalised by Meghalaya’s Social Audit Law (2017) as the first state to legislate it.
Technique: RTI exemptions
Question: A citizen seeks the file noting of the Cabinet on a policy decision under RTI. Is it disclosable?
Answer: No. Section 8(1)(i) exempts cabinet papers including records of deliberations of the Council of Ministers, Secretaries, and other officers. However, decisions of the Council of Ministers, reasons for decisions, and the material on which decisions are based must be made public after the decision is taken and the matter is complete (Section 8(1)(i) proviso).
Technique: Civil services neutrality
Question: A new government takes office. A civil servant who served the previous regime is transferred out. Is this a breach of neutrality?
Answer: Routine transfers are an executive prerogative. Neutrality means implementing the lawful policies of whichever party is in power without personal bias — it does not mean immunity from transfer. However, transfers motivated by vengeance or to punish honest officers violate civil service protection norms (Article 311 safeguards and 2nd ARC recommendations).

Common Traps

  • Confusing Central Services with All India Services — AIS officers serve under states but are recruited and controlled by the Centre.
  • Thinking RTI applies to private bodies — it applies only to public authorities (including substantially funded NGOs).
  • Believing Citizen’s Charter is legally enforceable — unlike RTI, it is not a statutory right.
  • Assuming pressure groups contest elections — they influence policy but do not field candidates (that is the role of political parties).
  • Missing that Article 311 protection applies to dismissal/removal/reduction in rank, not to ordinary transfer.

Memory Tricks

  • SMART: Simple, Moral, Accountable, Responsive, Transparent — e-governance goals.
  • RTI flow: PIO → First Appellate Authority → State/Central Information Commission.
  • Services pyramid: All India Services (Centre + State), Central Services (Centre), State Services (State).
  • Accountability trio: CAG (audit), CVC (vigilance), Lokpal (corruption).
Mains exam-day strategy
For governance questions, link constitutional provisions (Articles 308–323, 311, 312) with contemporary schemes (Digital India, DBT, Sevottam, Mission Karmayogi) and committees (2nd ARC). Always mention the accountability mechanism relevant to the theme — RTI for transparency, CAG for audit, Lokpal for corruption.
