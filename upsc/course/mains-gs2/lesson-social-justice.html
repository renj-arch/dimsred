Key Concepts

Poverty and Vulnerable Sections

Poverty in India is measured not only by income but by multidimensional deprivation — health, education, living standards, and social exclusion. The NITI Aayog’s National Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI) tracks these dimensions; the Tendulkar and Rangarajan committees set earlier income-based poverty lines.

  • Vulnerable groups: Scheduled Castes (SCs), Scheduled Tribes (STs), Other Backward Classes (OBCs), minorities, women, children, the elderly, persons with disabilities, migrant workers, and LGBTQ+ persons.
  • Constitutional safeguards: Article 15 (prohibition of discrimination), Article 16 (equality of opportunity in public employment), Article 17 (abolition of untouchability), Article 46 (promotion of educational and economic interests of SCs, STs, and weaker sections), Article 338 (National Commission for SCs), Article 338A (National Commission for STs), Article 338B (National Commission for Backward Classes).
  • SC/ST Sub-Plan (now Scheduled Caste Sub-Plan and Tribal Sub-Plan): Earmarking a proportion of state and Central plan funds for SC/ST areas and populations.
  • Atrocities prevention: Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989 — special courts, stringent punishment, and victim relief.
  • Women: Articles 15(3) and 39(a), (d); 73rd/74th Amendments (one-third reservation in Panchayats and Municipalities); Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace Act, 2013; Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005; Maternity Benefit (Amendment) Act, 2017 (26 weeks paid leave).
  • Children: Article 21A (right to education), Article 24 (prohibition of child labour below 14), Article 39(e),(f); Child Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Amendment Act, 2016; Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015; POSCO Act, 2012; National Commission for Protection of Child Rights (NCPCR).
  • Persons with disabilities: Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 — 21 recognised disabilities (expanded from 7), 4% reservation in government jobs and higher education, accessibility mandates.
  • Transgender: Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act, 2019, following NALSA v. Union of India (2014) which recognised the third gender.
  • Senior citizens: National Policy for Older Persons, 2011; Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act, 2007.
  • Migrant workers: Inter-State Migrant Workmen Act, 1979; Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020 subsumes it; e-Shram portal for unorganised workers.

Health

Constitutional basis: Article 47 (duty of the State to raise nutrition and living standards and improve public health); Article 21 interpreted by the Supreme Court to include the right to health (Paschim Banga Khet Mazdoor Samity v. State of West Bengal, 1996). Health is a state subject (List II, Entry 6), with concurrent public health and hospitals (List III, Entry 29).

  • National Health Mission (NHM): Umbrella programme including National Rural Health Mission (NRHM) and National Urban Health Mission (NUHM) — focuses on maternal and child health, immunisation, and communicable disease control.
  • Janani Suraksha Yojana (JSY): Cash assistance for institutional delivery to reduce maternal mortality.
  • Ayushman Bharat (Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana - PMJAY): Health insurance cover of ₹5 lakh per family per year for secondary and tertiary hospitalisation; targets ~10.74 crore poor and vulnerable families based on SECC 2011 data. Health and Wellness Centres (HWCs) deliver comprehensive primary care.
  • Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM): Digital health IDs, health facility registries, and unified health interface.
  • National Health Policy 2017: Targets raising government health spending to 2.5% of GDP by 2025; increasing life expectancy to 70 by 2025; reducing infant mortality, maternal mortality, and out-of-pocket expenditure.
  • COVID-19 response: Pradhan Mantri Garib Kalyan Package, PM CARES Fund, vaccination drive (CoWIN platform), and lessons for pandemic preparedness.
  • Issues: High out-of-pocket expenditure (~40–50% of health spending), shortage of doctors and specialists in rural areas, urban-rural divide, rising non-communicable diseases (diabetes, hypertension, cancer), and mental health neglect (Mental Healthcare Act, 2017 decriminalised suicide and guaranteed access to care).

Education

Constitutional basis: Article 21A (Right to Education — free and compulsory education for children aged 6–14, inserted by 86th Amendment, 2002); Article 45 (early childhood care and education for children below 6); Article 46 (weaker sections); Article 30 (right of minorities to establish educational institutions); Article 351 (development of Hindi). Education moved to the Concurrent List by the 42nd Amendment (1976).

  • Right to Education Act, 2009: Mandates free and compulsory education; 25% reservation for economically weaker sections in private unaided schools; prohibits detention and corporal punishment; sets norms for pupil-teacher ratio, infrastructure, and working days.
  • Samagra Shiksha Abhiyan: Integrated scheme for school education from pre-school to Class 12, subsuming Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan, RMSA, and Teacher Education.
  • Mid-Day Meal Scheme (PM POSHAN): Provides cooked meals to school children — addresses classroom hunger and improves enrolment and retention; governed by the National Food Security Act, 2013 (Schedule II).
  • National Education Policy 2020: Replaces NPE 1986; 5+3+3+4 curricular structure; early childhood care (age 3–6) under foundational stage; vocational education integration; multilingualism and mother-tongue instruction till at least Class 5; Higher Education Commission of India (HECI) proposed as single regulator; target 50% gross enrolment ratio in higher education by 2035; National Research Foundation for research funding.
  • Higher education: UGC (University Grants Commission), AICTE (technical education), NAAC (accreditation), NIRF (ranking), SWAYAM (online courses), and Institutions of Eminence (IoE) scheme.
  • Skill development: Pradhan Mantri Kaushal Vikas Yojana (PMKVY); National Skill Development Corporation (NSDC); National Skill Development Mission; Skill India Mission — aligning with demographic dividend.

Welfare Schemes — Major Programmes

  • Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (MGNREGA), 2005: Legal guarantee of 100 days of wage employment per rural household per year; unemployment allowance if work not provided within 15 days; social audit mandatory; wages linked to inflation. A rights-based approach rather than mere charity.
  • National Food Security Act (NFSA), 2013: Covers up to 75% of rural and 50% of urban population under Targeted Public Distribution System (TPDS); 5 kg foodgrains per person per month at subsidised rates (₹3/₹2/₹1 for rice/wheat/coarse grains); maternity benefits (₹6,000) and meals for children under Mid-Day Meal and ICDS.
  • Pradhan Mantri Ujjwala Yojana (PMUY): Free LPG connections to BPL women — reduces indoor air pollution and drudgery.
  • Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana (PMAY): Housing for all — urban (PMAY-U) and rural (PMAY-G); interest subsidy and direct house construction.
  • Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana (PMJDY): Financial inclusion — universal bank accounts, overdraft facility, RuPay debit card, and accident insurance.
  • Stand-Up India: Bank loans between ₹10 lakh and ₹1 crore for SC/ST and women entrepreneurs.
  • Pradhan Mantri Mudra Yojana (PMMY): Micro units development and refinance agency loans to small businesses (Shishu, Kishor, Tarun categories).
  • National Social Assistance Programme (NSAP): Indira Gandhi National Old Age Pension Scheme, Widow Pension Scheme, Disability Pension Scheme, and National Family Benefit Scheme — targeted social protection for the elderly, widows, disabled, and bereaved families.
  • Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS): Supplementary nutrition, pre-school education, immunisation, health check-ups, and referral services for children under 6 and pregnant/lactating mothers through Anganwadi centres. Saksham Anganwadi and Poshan 2.0 are recent extensions targeting malnutrition.
  • POSHAN Abhiyaan (National Nutrition Mission): Reducing stunting, undernutrition, anaemia, and low birth weight through convergence and technology.

Human Resources and Demographic Dividend

India has one of the youngest populations in the world — a potential demographic dividend. Realising it depends on education, skill, health, and employment generation. The Economic Survey and Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS) track unemployment and labour force participation.

  • Challenges: Jobless growth, informal sector (~90% of workforce), low female labour force participation, skill mismatch, and rural distress migration.
  • Opportunity: If skilled and employed, India’s youth can drive growth; if not, it becomes a demographic burden.
Technique: Welfare scheme vs right
Question: Is MGNREGA a welfare scheme or a legal entitlement?
Answer: MGNREGA is a legal entitlement — it creates a justiciable right to 100 days of wage employment under an Act of Parliament (not just an executive scheme). If employment is not provided within 15 days, the worker is entitled to an unemployment allowance. This distinguishes it from schemes like PMJAY or PMUY, which are executive welfare programmes without a statutory guarantee of the same kind.
Technique: Education policy analysis
Question: What is the significance of the 5+3+3+4 structure in NEP 2020?
Answer: It replaces the 10+2 structure. Foundational stage (age 3–8, 5 years: 3 years pre-school + Classes 1–2), preparatory (Classes 3–5, 3 years), middle (Classes 6–8, 3 years), and secondary (Classes 9–12, 4 years). It formally brings early childhood education (age 3–6) into the schooling system, aligning with Article 45’s directive on early childhood care.

Common Traps

  • Confusing NFSA coverage (75% rural / 50% urban) with universal coverage.
  • Thinking RTE covers children up to age 18 — it covers ages 6–14 only.
  • Assuming MGNREGA work is guaranteed in urban areas — it is rural only.
  • Missing that Ayushman Bharat targets SECC 2011-deprived families, not all citizens.
  • Overlooking that health is primarily a state subject — the Centre supplements through Centrally Sponsored Schemes.

Memory Tricks

  • Vulnerable groups mnemonic: SC-ST-OBC-Women-Children-Elderly-Disabled-Transgender-Migrants.
  • Major schemes: MGNREGA (employment), NFSA (food), PMJAY (health), PMUY (cooking gas), PMAY (housing), PMJDY (banking), ICDS (child nutrition).
  • Articles: 21A (education), 47 (health/nutrition), 46 (weaker sections), 17 (untouchability).
Mains exam-day strategy
For social justice questions, always connect the scheme to its constitutional or legal basis (e.g., MGNREGA = Act, RTE = Article 21A, PMJAY = executive scheme). Cite recent data sparingly and accurately (NFHS-5, PLFS, NITI Aayog MPI). Use Supreme Court judgments (NALSAS, Paschim Banga, Justice K.S. Puttaswamy for privacy in Aadhaar-linked welfare) where relevant. Conclude with gaps — exclusion errors, Aadhaar authentication failures, fund underutilisation — to show critical thinking.
