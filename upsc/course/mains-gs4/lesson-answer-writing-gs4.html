2. Directive Words in Ethics — Exact Meaning & Response Pattern

Misreading the directive is the single biggest mark-loser in GS IV. Each word demands a specific answer architecture:

DirectiveWhat Examiner WantsAnswer StructurePYQ Example
Define / What do you understand byPrecise definition + dimensions + 1-2 examplesDefinition → Dimensions → Thinker link → Example → Relevance to civil service2013 Q1: "What do you understand by 'values' and 'ethics'?"
DiscussMultiple perspectives, for and against, balanced viewThesis → Antithesis → Synthesis with your reasoned position2014 Q2: "Discuss the role of ethics in public administration"
AnalyseBreak into components, show interrelationshipsComponent 1 → Component 2 → Linkage → Systemic implication2015 Q3: "Analyse the ethical issues in corporate governance"
Critically analyse / Critically examineAnalyse + judge merits/demerits + reasoned conclusionAnalyse framework → Strengths → Weaknesses → Your verdict with justification2016 Q4: "Critically examine the role of civil society in promoting ethics"
Evaluate / AssessWeigh value/impact/ effectiveness + give verdictCriteria → Evidence for → Evidence against → Weighted judgment → Way forward2017 Q5: "Evaluate the effectiveness of whistleblower protection in India"
CommentInformed opinion on a statement/issue (shorter than discuss)Context → Your position with reasoning → Nuance/counter-view → Closing2018 Q6: "Comment on: 'Integrity without knowledge is weak'"
ExamineProbe deeply into causes, effects, implicationsSurface → Deeper causes → Implications → Systemic connect2019 Q4: "Examine the ethical dimensions of data privacy"
IllustrateUse concrete examples/case studies to clarifyConcept → Example 1 → Example 2 → What examples prove2020 Q3: "Illustrate with examples how conflict of interest arises"
JustifyProvide reasons supporting a position/decisionPosition → Reason 1 (ethical) → Reason 2 (legal) → Reason 3 (pragmatic)2021 Q5: "Justify civil disobedience in a democracy"

Directive Word Memory Trick
D-A-C-E-J-I-C: Discuss = debate; Analyse = break down; Critically examine = merits/demerits + judgment; Evaluate = weigh + verdict; Justify = reasons for; Illustrate = examples; Comment = informed opinion. Read the directive twice before writing.

3. Section A: 150-Word (10-Mark) & 250-Word (15-Mark) Answer Structures

3.1 Universal IBC Architecture

Every Section A answer follows Introduction → Body → Conclusion with strict word budgets:

Component10-Mark (150 words)15-Mark (250 words)Content
Introduction20-25 words (~15%)35-40 words (~15%)Define core term + thinker/constitutional anchor + thesis line
Body90-100 words (~65%)160-180 words (~70%)3-4 thematic points: dimension + thinker/theory + example + governance application
Conclusion20-25 words (~15%)35-40 words (~15%)Forward-looking synthesis + reform/way forward + one-line takeaway

3.2 10-Mark Answer Template (150 Words)

Template: "Define 'integrity' and explain its significance for civil servants." (2013 Q2 style)
Intro (22 words): Integrity is consistency between values, words, and actions — moral courage to uphold principles despite pressure [Nolan Committee 1995].

Body (98 words): (1) Public trust anchor — Citizens delegate authority expecting integrity; breach erodes legitimacy [2nd ARC 4th Report]. (2) Integrity enables other values — Impartiality, objectivity, compassion require integrity as foundation; without it, they become performative [Gandhi, Young India 1927]. (3) Challenges in practice — Political interference, corruption normalisation, transfer threats test integrity daily [CVC Annual Report 2022]. (4) Sustaining mechanisms — Conduct Rules 1968, asset declarations, whistleblower protection, peer culture [AIS Conduct Rules 1968].

Conclusion (25 words): Integrity is non-negotiable; it is the keystone holding all civil service values together — 2nd ARC's "integrity pacts" and e-governance must be fully implemented.
Word count: 145. Structure: Definition + 4 body points (trust, enabling, challenges, mechanisms) + reform conclusion. Each point: dimension + source + application.

3.3 15-Mark Answer Template (250 Words)

Template: "Critically examine the relationship between ethics and law in public administration." (2016 Q4 style)
Intro (38 words): Law sets minimum enforceable standards; ethics governs the "ought" beyond legality. Lon Fuller's "inner morality of law" argues law itself has ethical content [Fuller, Morality of Law 1964]; Kautilya's Arthashastra binds king to dharma above statute [Arthashastra 1.15].

Body (175 words): Convergence: (1) Constitutional morality (Preamble, Art 14, 19, 21) makes ethics legally justiciable [Navtej Johar 2018]. (2) RTI Act 2005, Lokpal Act 2013, Whistleblower Act 2014 encode ethics into law. (3) Conduct Rules 1968 translate integrity/impartiality into enforceable duties. Divergence: (1) Legal compliance ≠ ethical conduct — "technical legality" permits corruption within rules [2nd ARC 4th Report]. (2) Law lags ethics — AI bias, genetic editing, surveillance need ethical guardrails before legislation [NITI Aayog AI Ethics 2021]. (3) Discretionary power (Art 311, All India Services) requires ethical judgment where law is silent. Critique: Over-legalisation risks "compliance mindset" replacing moral reasoning; 2nd ARC recommends "ethics infrastructure" — ethics officers, training, dilemma resolution [2nd ARC 10th Report].

Conclusion (37 words): Law is the floor; ethics is the ceiling. Effective governance needs both — legal framework for accountability, ethical culture for excellence. Implement 2nd ARC's ethics infrastructure and mandatory mid-career ethics training.
Word count: 250. Structure: Intro with dual anchor (Western + Indian thinker). Body: Convergence (3 pts), Divergence (3 pts), Critique (1 pt). Conclusion: Synthesis + specific reform.

4. Section B: Case Study Frameworks — The Complete 8-Step Method

4.1 The F-D-S-O-E-D-I-W Framework

For 20-mark cases (~300-350 words, 20-25 minutes), follow this exact sequence:

StepLabelContentWordsTime
1FactsList key facts neutrally — who, what, where, when, role30-402 min
2DilemmaState the core ethical conflict in one line (Value A vs Value B)15-201 min
3StakeholdersMap 5-7 stakeholders with their interests/rights at stake40-503 min
4OptionsGenerate 3-4 distinct, feasible courses of action50-604 min
5EvaluationTest each option against ethical tests + legal/constitutional validity60-805 min
6DecisionState ONE clear decision in bold — never fence-sit20-302 min
7ImplementationConcrete steps: authority, timeline, safeguards, communication40-503 min
8Way ForwardSystemic reform to prevent recurrence20-302 min

4.3 Option Generation — The 3-Criteria Filter

Every option must pass three filters before inclusion:

FilterQuestionReject If
LegalityIs it permitted under Constitution, Acts, Conduct Rules, service law?Violates any law — even for "good cause" [PCA 1988]
Ethical ValidityDoes it pass Publicity, Reversibility, Universalisation tests?Fails any ethical test [Nolan 1995]
FeasibilityCan it be implemented within hierarchy, resources, time constraints?Requires superhuman authority or ignores real constraints
Option Generation Example: "Senior takes bribes for postings — whistleblow or stay silent?" (2018 Q12 style)
Option A — Silent Complicity: Ignore to protect career. Fails Legality (abetment under PCA 1988 S.7-12), Ethics (violates integrity, public trust). Reject.

Option B — Anonymous Leak: Leak to media/vigilance anonymously. Fails Feasibility (unverifiable, risks defamation), Ethics (cowardice, bypasses due process). Reject.

Option C — Formal Complaint with Evidence: Document specifics, file with CVC/Lokpal under Whistleblower Protection Act 2014, seek legal counsel, maintain confidentiality. Passes all three filters. Select.

Option D — Confront Senior Directly: Demand cessation. Fails Feasibility (risk of retaliation, evidence destruction), Ethics (may compromise investigation). Reject.
Always show rejected options with reasons — it demonstrates ethical reasoning depth. The chosen option must be specific: "file written complaint with CVC under Section 11 of Whistleblower Protection Act 2014" not "report it."

4.4 Ethical Tests for Option Evaluation

TestQuestionApplication
Publicity TestWould I be comfortable if this decision appeared on front page tomorrow?Exposes actions driven by fear/hidden motives
Reversibility TestWould I accept this decision if I were the affected party?Checks fairness, empathy, golden rule
Universalisation TestWould I want everyone in my position to act this way?Kant's categorical imperative — consistency
Constitutional Values TestDoes it uphold Preamble values — Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity?GS IV specific — links to Ambedkar's constitutional morality

4.5 Course of Action — Decision Statement Formula

Your decision paragraph must follow this exact structure:

Decision Statement Template
"I will [specific action] under [specific legal provision] because [ethical reasoning: thinker + constitutional value + stakeholder impact], while ensuring [safeguard 1], [safeguard 2], and [safeguard 3]."
Example: "I will file a written complaint with the CVC under Section 11 of the Whistleblower Protection Act 2014 because Kant's categorical imperative demands universalisable duty-reporting, constitutional morality (Ambedkar) requires upholding public trust, and the honest officers (stakeholders) deserve protection — while ensuring my family's safety through police protection, maintaining confidentiality of the investigation, and requesting transfer if retaliation occurs."

4.6 Implementation Steps — The 5-Point Checklist

  1. Authority & Channel: Name the exact body (CVC, Lokpal, Departmental Vigilance, NIA, CERT-IN) and legal section.
  2. Evidence & Documentation: What proof, how collected, chain of custody.
  3. Safeguards: Whistleblower protection, witness protection, transfer request, legal counsel.
  4. Communication: Who needs to know, when, how much (need-to-know basis).
  5. Follow-up: Timeline for review, escalation if stalled, systemic fix proposal.

5.3 High-Yield PYQ Deconstructions (Top 5)

PYQ 1: 2013 Q12 — Disaster Relief Favouritism (20 marks)

Question
You are DM of a flood-hit district. Relief materials are limited. Local MLA demands priority for his constituency. Your junior officer suggests diverting some relief to MLA's area to "maintain political goodwill." What do you do?
Model Answer Structure: Facts — DM, limited relief, MLA pressure, junior's suggestion. Dilemma — Impartiality (constitutional duty) vs Political expediency (career survival). Stakeholders — Flood victims (all constituencies), MLA, junior officer, administration credibility, state government. Options — (A) Divert (fails legality/ethics), (B) Refuse & report (passes all), (C) Escalate to Chief Secretary (passes), (D) Media leak (fails feasibility). Decision — Option B + C combined: Distribute per needs-assessment protocol, document MLA's demand, report to Chief Secretary/Election Commission. Implementation — Activate needs-based criteria, involve civil society in verification, daily public dashboard. Way forward — Disaster management SOPs with political interference clauses; 2nd ARC crisis management reforms.

PYQ 2: 2017 Q13 — Communal Tension & Police Inaction (20 marks)

Question
As SP, you receive intelligence of planned communal violence. Local politicians pressure you to "go slow" on one community. Your officers are demoralised. What steps will you take?
Model Answer Structure: Facts — SP, intelligence input, political pressure, demoralised force. Dilemma — Rule of law (Art 14, 21) vs Political survival. Stakeholders — Potential victims (both communities), police force, politicians, judiciary, NHRC, public trust. Options — (A) Comply (illegal, unethical), (B) Resist & act per law (passes), (C) Resign (abandons duty), (D) Leak to media (bypasses chain). Decision — Option B: Deploy per intelligence, invoke Section 144 CrPC, arrest instigators regardless of affiliation, document political interference, inform DGP/Home Secretary in writing. Implementation — Daily situation reports, inter-faith peace committees, officer counselling, legal cell for officer protection. Way forward — Police Act reform (2nd ARC, Prakash Singh), fixed tenure, insulation from political control.

PYQ 3: 2020 Q14 — COVID Ventilator Triage (20 marks)

Question
As Health Secretary, you have 50 ventilators for 200 critical patients. A minister's relative needs one. Disability rights groups demand non-discriminatory criteria. Design allocation protocol.
Model Answer Structure: Facts — 50 ventilators, 200 patients, VIP pressure, disability rights concern. Dilemma — Utilitarian maximisation (save most lives) vs Equity (non-discrimination) vs VIP pressure. Stakeholders — Patients, families, disability groups, minister, medical staff, judiciary, public trust. Options — (A) First-come-first-served (ignores medical utility), (B) Pure utilitarian SOFA score (risks disability discrimination), (C) Multi-criteria protocol (medical benefit + life-stage + lottery for ties) — passes all tests. Decision — Option C: Publish transparent protocol (SOFA score + age tie-breaker + random allocation for equal scores), exclude VIP status, constitute ethics committee with disability representation, audit daily. Implementation — Real-time dashboard, grievance redressal, legal vetting. Way forward — National triage guidelines (ICMR), disability-inclusive disaster framework, ventilator stockpiling.

PYQ 4: 2022 Q14 — AI Recruitment Bias (20 marks)

Question
An AI tool for civil service recruitment shows bias against women and rural candidates. The vendor claims "data-driven objectivity." You head the recruitment board.
Model Answer Structure: Facts — AI bias, vendor claim, constitutional equality mandate (Art 14, 16). Dilemma — Efficiency/tech adoption vs Constitutional equality/non-discrimination. Stakeholders — Aspirants (women/rural), vendor, govt, judiciary, public trust in meritocracy. Options — (A) Deploy anyway (violates Art 14/16, Indra Sawhney), (B) Reject AI entirely (luddite, ignores potential), (C) Audit → Mitigate → Conditional deploy — passes. Decision — Option C: Suspend deployment; mandate third-party algorithmic audit (NITI AI Ethics 2021); require bias mitigation (re-weighting, adversarial debiasing); re-test on diverse dataset; deploy only if parity metrics met; human-in-loop for final decisions. Implementation — Audit timeline (60 days), transparency portal, applicant appeal mechanism. Way forward — Algorithmic Accountability Act (proposed), mandatory bias audits for all govt AI, diverse training data mandate.

PYQ 5: 2024 Q13 — Deepfake Election Misinformation (20 marks)

Question
As CEO of a social media platform, you detect deepfake videos targeting a candidate 48 hours before polling. Removal takes 6 hours via current process. Election Commission demands immediate action. Your legal team warns of intermediary liability.
Model Answer Structure: Facts — Deepfake detected, 48h to poll, 6h removal SLA, EC demand, IT Rules 2021 Sec 3(1)(b), intermediary safe harbour Sec 79 IT Act. Dilemma — Electoral integrity (Art 324, free/fair election) vs Due process/safe harbour vs Free speech (Art 19(1)(a)). Stakeholders — Voters, candidates, EC, platform users, judiciary, democracy. Options — (A) Wait 6h (risk viral spread), (B) Emergency takedown without review (violates due process, Shreya Singhal), (C) Expedited 1h review + interim label — passes. Decision — Option C: Activate "election integrity fast-track" — 1h human review by trained team, apply "unverified" label immediately, notify EC/uploader, preserve evidence for appeal, document decision log. Implementation — Pre-election SOP with EC, dedicated election team, transparency report post-poll. Way forward — IT Rules amendment for election-specific timelines, deepfake detection AI mandate, Election Commission tech capacity building.

7. Exam-Day Strategy Checklist

Mains GS IV Exam-Day Protocol
Before writing: Read all 14 questions in 5 min. Mark Section B cases by difficulty. Order: Attempt highest-confidence case first → remaining cases → Section A 15-markers → Section A 10-markers. Per question: Underline directive word. Jot 3-word skeleton (F-D-S-O-E-D-I-W or I-D-T-A-C-C). Write. Formatting: Black ink main text. Blue ink for subheadings/thinker names. Leave 2 lines between paragraphs. Bold final decision in cases. Draw stakeholder matrix if space permits. Last 15 min: Complete incomplete answers in bullet points. Verify every case has bold decision. Check all 14 attempted.

Long Answer Practice — Model Structure

Practice Question (20 marks): "As District Collector, you discover a widespread fake caste certificate racket involving revenue officials, politicians, and coaching centres. Beneficiaries include serving government employees. The racket has operated for a decade. What will you do?"
Model Answer Skeleton (use F-D-S-O-E-D-I-W):

Facts: DC, decade-long fake caste certificate racket, revenue officials + politicians + coaching centres, serving govt employees as beneficiaries.

Dilemma: Rule of law & social justice (Art 14, 16, 341/342) vs Political-administrative nexus & systemic corruption.

Stakeholders (7): (1) Genuine SC/ST/OBC candidates (rights violated), (2) Fraudulent beneficiaries (serving employees), (3) Revenue officials (complicit), (4) Politicians (patrons), (5) Coaching centres (facilitators), (6) Administration credibility, (7) Judiciary/NCSC/NCST (oversight).

Options: (A) Quiet cleanup (fails publicity/legality), (B) Full criminal investigation + departmental action (passes), (C) Transfer complicit officials only (incomplete), (D) Public exposé without due process (fails reversibility).

Evaluation: Option B passes all tests — Legality (PCA 1988, IPC 420/468/471, SC/ST Act 1989), Ethics (constitutional morality, Ambedkar), Feasibility (DC has magisterial powers, can request SIT).

Decision: I will initiate a three-track response: (1) Criminal — FIRs under IPC/PCA/SC-ST Act, request SIT from state govt; (2) Departmental — Suspend complicit revenue officials, initiate major penalty proceedings; (3) Verification — Audit all caste certificates issued in 10 years, cancel fraudulent ones, recover salaries from serving employees.

Implementation: (1) Written order to SP for FIR + SIT request to Home Dept. (2) Show-cause notices to officials under CCA Rules. (3) Special verification camps with NCSC/NCST observers. (4) Digital certificate blockchain pilot to prevent recurrence. (5) Weekly progress report to Chief Secretary.

Way Forward: Amend caste certificate issuance to require biometric + blockchain verification; fixed tenure for revenue officers; social audit of certificates by Gram Sabhas; 2nd ARC recommendation for "certificate authorities" independent of revenue dept.
Word target: 320-350. Time: 22 min. Every stakeholder named. Every option tested. Decision bold and specific. Implementation has 5 concrete steps. Way forward cites 2nd ARC.
