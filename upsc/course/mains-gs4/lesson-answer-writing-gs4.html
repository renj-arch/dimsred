Key Concepts

GS IV Answer Writing Framework

GS IV (Ethics, Integrity, Aptitude) has two sections: Section A (theoretical questions, ~125 marks, 10/15 marks each) and Section B (case studies, ~125 marks, 20 marks each). Total 250 marks, 3 hours.

  • Section A approach: Define the concept, explain dimensions, cite thinkers/examples, apply to civil service/administration, discuss challenges, conclude with a way forward. Use ethical theories where relevant (Aristotle, Kant, Mill, Gandhi, Ambedkar, Rawls).
  • Section B (case study) approach: Facts → dilemma → stakeholders → options → evaluation → decision → implementation → way forward. State a clear decision; never fence-sit; never suggest illegality.

Structure for Section A Questions

  • Introduction (15–20%): Define the term, cite a thinker or constitutional value, state the relevance to public service.
  • Body (60–70%): Dimensions/components, examples, thinkers, application to governance, challenges, issues. Use bullet points or short paragraphs with subheadings.
  • Conclusion (10–15%): Balanced, forward-looking, no new facts. One-line takeaway.

Common Section A Themes

  • Ethics and human interface (essence, determinants, dimensions).
  • Human values (lessons from leaders, role of family/society/education).
  • Attitude (content, structure, function, formation, change, influence, persuasion).
  • Aptitude and foundational values (integrity, impartiality, non-partisanship, objectivity, dedication, empathy, tolerance, compassion — Nolan principles).
  • Emotional intelligence (concepts, dimensions, utilities in administration).
  • Contributions of moral thinkers and philosophers from India and world.
  • Public/civil service values and ethics in governance (probity, transparency, accountability, citizen's charter, work culture, service quality, utilisation of public funds, corruption challenges).

Dimension Enrichment for Ethics Answers

  • Thinkers: Gandhi (truth, non-violence, trusteeship, ends-means), Aristotle (virtue ethics, golden mean), Kant (deontology, categorical imperative), Mill/Bentham (utilitarianism, greatest good), Rawls (justice as fairness, veil of ignorance), Ambedkar (constitutional morality), Buddha (compassion, middle path), Vivekananda (service, fearlessness), Socrates (virtue is knowledge).
  • Constitutional values: Preamble — justice, liberty, equality, fraternity; dignity of individual; unity and integrity.
  • Civil service values (Nolan + 2nd ARC): Selflessness, integrity, objectivity, accountability, openness, honesty, leadership; plus impartiality, non-partisanship, empathy, compassion, tolerance.
  • Institutions: CVC, CAG, Lokpal, CIC, NHRC, NCSC, NCST, NGT, ECI, UPSC, state PSC, civil services conduct rules.
  • Laws: RTI Act 2005, Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act 2013, Whistleblower Protection Act 2014, Prevention of Corruption Act, IPC sections on misconduct, Conduct Rules (All India Services Conduct Rules 1968).

Writing Ethics Case Studies

  • Time budget: Section A 10-mark ~7 min, 15-mark ~10 min; Section B 20-mark case ~20–25 min each. Total 3 hours for ~14 questions.
  • Language: Simple, precise, ethical vocabulary (integrity, probity, impartiality, empathy, accountability, transparency, dignity, trust, legitimacy). Avoid jargon without explanation.
  • Formatting: Use bullet points, number options in cases, bold the final decision, leave space between paragraphs. Diagrams rarely needed but stakeholder maps can help.
Technique: 15-mark theory answer
Question: “Integrity is the most important value for a civil servant.” Discuss.
Answer: Intro — define integrity (consistency between values, words, actions; honesty + moral courage). Body — why integrity matters (public trust, legitimacy, impartial decision-making, resistance to corruption); relationship with other values (impartiality flows from integrity; compassion without integrity is sentimentality); challenges (political pressure, corruption tolerance, personal cost — transfer, ostracism); how to sustain (code of conduct, vigilance, peer support, inner conviction, 2nd ARC recommendations). Examples — Armstrong Pame (Manipur road, "miracle man"), Ashok Khemka, Sanjiv Chaturvedi. Conclusion — integrity is non-negotiable; it is the anchor that holds all other civil service values together.
Technique: 20-mark case study
Question: You discover your senior is taking bribes for postings. Whistleblow or stay silent?
Answer: Stakeholders — you, senior, honest officers, department integrity, public exchequer, your career/family. Options — (a) silent complicity (unethical, illegal under PCA if you benefit), (b) anonymous leak (risky, unverified), (c) documented complaint to CVC/Lokpal with evidence, invoking Whistleblower Protection Act. Decision — Option 3 with due process: gather evidence, file complaint through proper channel, seek legal advice, protect family, maintain confidentiality. Way forward — strengthen whistleblower protection, periodic integrity audits, e-posting to reduce discretion, culture of reporting.

Common Traps

  • Preaching without application — ethics answers must connect to civil service reality.
  • No thinkers/theories — generic opinions score low; cite Aristotle, Kant, Gandhi, Ambedkar.
  • Fence-sitting in cases — state a clear decision and justify it.
  • Suggesting illegality — never recommend violating law, even for a good cause.
  • Ignoring constraints — resources, time, law, hierarchy are real constraints.
  • No way forward — every answer needs a systemic solution.

Memory Tricks

  • Section structure: Intro → Dimensions → Thinkers → Application → Challenges → Conclusion.
  • Case skeleton: Facts → Dilemma → Stakeholders → Options → Decision → Steps → Way forward.
  • Nolan 7: Selflessness, Integrity, Objectivity, Accountability, Openness, Honesty, Leadership.
  • Ethical tests: Publicity, Reversibility, Universalisation.
Mains exam-day strategy
Read each case fully before writing. Allocate time strictly — Section B cases carry more marks per question. Define terms precisely, cite thinkers and laws, use stakeholder analysis, state a clear defensible decision, and give concrete steps. Never be vague — "I will act ethically" scores zero; "I will file a complaint with CVC under the Whistleblower Protection Act" scores marks. Attempt all questions; partial answers score more than blanks.
