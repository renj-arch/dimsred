Key Concepts

What are Environment & Ecology?

Environment and Ecology is a significant topic in UPSC Civil Services Examination prelims and mains. It covers the study of ecosystems, biodiversity, conservation, environmental pollution, climate change, and related international conventions. Questions range from basic ecological concepts to current environmental issues and policies.

Key Concepts in Ecology

1. Ecosystem Structure and Function: An ecosystem includes all living organisms (biotic) and non-living components (abiotic) interacting in a defined area. Key functions: energy flow (10% law), nutrient cycling (carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus cycles), food chains (producer → primary consumer → secondary consumer → decomposer) and food webs.

2. Biomes of the World: Tropical rainforests (Amazon, Congo), temperate forests, taiga/tundra, grasslands (savanna, steppe, prairie), deserts (hot/cold), aquatic biomes (marine, freshwater, estuarine). Each biome has characteristic climate, vegetation, and animal life.

3. Biodiversity and its Levels: Genetic diversity (variation within species), species diversity (variety of species in an area), ecosystem diversity (variety of habitats). India is one of 17 megadiverse countries with 4 biodiversity hotspots: Western Ghats, Eastern Himalayas, Indo-Burma, Sundaland.

4. Conservation Approaches: In-situ conservation (protected areas — national parks, wildlife sanctuaries, biosphere reserves, UNESCO World Heritage sites) and ex-situ conservation (zoos, botanical gardens, gene banks, seed banks). Important: Project Tiger (1973), Project Elephant (1992), Project Rhino, Gir Lion Project.

5. Environmental Pollution: Air pollution (PM2.5, PM10, SOx, NOx, ozone), water pollution (BOD, COD, eutrophication), soil pollution, noise pollution, and plastic pollution. National Air Quality Index (AQI), National Clean Air Programme (NCAP).

6. Climate Change: Greenhouse gases (CO2, CH4, N2O, CFCs), global warming, IPCC reports, UNFCCC, Kyoto Protocol, Paris Agreement (2015, goal to limit warming to 1.5°C), carbon footprint, carbon offsetting, INDCs (Nationally Determined Contributions).

7. Indian Environmental Laws: Wildlife Protection Act (1972), Forest Conservation Act (1980), Environment Protection Act (1986), Air Act (1981), Water Act (1974), National Green Tribunal Act (2010), Coastal Regulation Zone (CRZ) notification.

Solved Examples

Example 1
Which of the following is the correct sequence in a food chain?

a) Grass → Frog → Grasshopper → Snake → Eagle b) Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Eagle c) Grass → Snake → Frog → Grasshopper → Eagle d) Grass → Eagle → Snake → Frog → Grasshopper
Solution: b) The correct food chain is Grass (producer) → Grasshopper (primary consumer) → Frog (secondary consumer) → Snake (tertiary consumer) → Eagle (apex predator).
Example 2
Which Indian state has the largest forest cover by area?

a) Arunachal Pradesh b) Madhya Pradesh c) Chhattisgarh d) Odisha
Solution: b) Madhya Pradesh has the largest forest cover in India by area, followed by Arunachal Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Odisha, and Maharashtra (according to ISFR reports).
Example 3
The term '10% law' in ecology refers to:

a) Only 10% of solar energy is captured by plants b) Only 10% of energy is transferred from one trophic level to the next c) Only 10% of species survive extinction events d) Only 10% of forests remain undisturbed
Solution: b) The 10% law, proposed by Lindeman, states that only about 10% of energy from one trophic level is transferred to the next level. The remaining 90% is lost as heat, respiration, and other metabolic processes.

Shortcut Techniques

  • Create mind maps linking related topics — e.g., link national parks with their states, important species found there, and conservation projects.
  • For environmental conventions, create a timeline: Stockholm (1972) → Montreal Protocol (1987) → Rio Earth Summit (1992) → Kyoto (1997) → Paris (2015) → Glasgow COP26 (2021).
  • Learn Indian biodiversity hotspots by region: Western Ghats (Maharashtra to Kerala), Eastern Himalayas (Darjeeling, Sikkim, Arunachal), Indo-Burma (Northeast hill states).
  • Use ISFR (India State of Forest Report) data for UPSC-specific facts — forest cover trends, carbon stock, mangrove cover.
Pro Tip
In UPSC prelims, environment questions are increasingly application-based rather than rote memorization. Focus on understanding ecological processes (succession, nutrient cycling, energy flow) and current environmental issues (climate change, biodiversity loss, pollution) rather than just static facts.
