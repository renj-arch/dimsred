Key Concepts

Indian Geography for UPSC

Indian Geography is a core subject for UPSC Prelims (GS Paper I) and Mains (GS Paper I). It covers physical geography (landforms, climate, rivers, soils, vegetation), human geography (population, settlements, migration, agriculture, industries, transport), and economic geography (resources, infrastructure, trade). UPSC questions increasingly integrate geography with environment, disaster management, and current affairs.

Key Concepts

Physical Geography — Structure & Relief: India sits on the Indian Plate (formerly Gondwanaland), colliding with Eurasian Plate ~50 mya, forming the Himalayas (convergent boundary). Physiographic divisions: (1) Northern Mountains — Himalayas (three parallel ranges: Great Himalayas/Himadri — avg 6000m, Lesser Himalayas/Himachal — 3500-4500m, Shiwaliks/Outer Himalayas — 900-1200m). Trans-Himalayas (Karakoram, Ladakh, Zanskar), Purvanchal (extensions in northeast — Patkai, Barail, Mizo hills). Important peaks: K2 (8611m, Karakoram), Kanchenjunga (8598m), Nanga Parbat (8126m). Glacial regions — Siachen, Gangotri. Passes — Zoji La, Nathu La, Shipki La, Rohtang. (2) Northern Plains — formed by Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra basins; three zones: Bhabar (porous, river disappearance), Tarai (marshy, rich soil), Bhangar (older alluvium, kankar nodules), Khadar (newer alluvium, fertile, floodplains). (3) Peninsular Plateau — Oldest landmass (Precambrian shield). Central Highlands (Aravallis — oldest fold mt, Guru Shikhar 1722m; Vindhyas, Satpuras, Maikal range, Chotanagpur plateau). Deccan Plateau (Western Ghats/Sahyadris — biodiversity hotspot, Eastern Ghats — discontinuous). (4) Coastal Plains — Western (Gujarat to Kerala, narrow, indented) and Eastern (West Bengal to Tamil Nadu, wide, deltas of Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri). (5) Islands — Andaman & Nicobar (volcanic, tectonic, 572 islands), Lakshadweep (coral atolls, 36 islands).

River Systems: Himalayan Rivers (perennial, glacier-fed): Indus (3180 km, originates near Mansarovar, 5 tributaries — Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Sutlej — flow through Pakistan), Ganga (2525 km, Bhagirathi from Gangotri, Alaknanda from Badrinath, Devprayag confluence; major tributaries: Yamuna, Ghaghara, Gandak, Kosi, Son; Ganga-Brahmaputra delta — Sunderbans, largest delta). Brahmaputra (2900 km, originates in Tibet as Tsangpo, enters India at Arunachal, river of floods, Majuli island). Peninsular Rivers (seasonal, rain-fed): East-flowing — Mahanadi (Hirakud dam), Godavari (1465 km, 'Dakshin Ganga', largest peninsular, Krishna, Kaveri (origin Brahmagiri, disputes among states). West-flowing — Narmada (1312 km, rift valley, Marble Gorge, Dhuandhar falls), Tapi (rift valley). Disputed rivers: Sutlej-Yamuna Link canal, Mullaperiyar (Kerala-TN), Cauvery water dispute.

Climate & Monsoon: Tropical monsoon climate. Factors controlling: latitude, altitude, distance from sea, pressure systems (ITCZ shifting), jet streams (subtropical westerly, tropical easterly), El Nino Southern Oscillation (ENSO — El Nino = warming of Pacific, weak monsoon; La Nina = cooling, strong monsoon), Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD), Tibetan plateau heating. Seasons: Winter (Jan-Feb), Summer/Pre-monsoon (Mar-May, 'Mango Showers' in Kerala, 'Norwesters'/Kal Baisakhi in Bengal), Southwest Monsoon (Jun-Sep — onset over Kerala 1 June, branches: Arabian Sea (stronger) and Bay of Bengal; orographic rainfall on Western Ghats, decreasing eastwards; rainfall variability: Mawsynram (NE, highest in India), Jaisalmer (lowest). Retreating/Post-monsoon (Oct-Dec — cyclones in Bay of Bengal). Climate types: Humid subtropical (N India), Tropical wet (W Ghats, NE), Tropical dry (Rajasthan).

Soils: Alluvial (43% — northern plains, most fertile, wheat-rice-sugarcane). Black/Regur (15% — Deccan trap, cotton, rich in Ca/Mg, self-ploughing). Red (18% — peninsular, poor in N/P/humus). Laterite (8% — high rainfall areas, rich in Fe/Al, poor in organic matter, used for bricks). Forest/Mountain (8% — Himalayas). Arid/Desert (4% — Rajasthan, low organic, high salts). Saline/Alkaline (2% — waterlogged areas, poor drainage). Soil degradation — erosion (water, wind), salinisation, desertification, nutrient depletion. Conservation — contour ploughing, terrace farming, afforestation, watershed management.

Agriculture: Land use pattern — net sown area ~46%, forest ~24%, fallow ~8%. Cropping seasons: Kharif (Jun-Oct — rice, maize, millets, cotton, jute, sugarcane), Rabi (Oct-Mar — wheat, barley, mustard, gram, peas), Zaid (Mar-Jun — watermelon, cucumber, vegetables). Green Revolution (1960s) — HYV seeds, fertilisers, irrigation; focused on wheat-rice in Punjab-Haryana-UP; uneven regional benefits, environmental costs (groundwater depletion, soil degradation). White Revolution (Operation Flood) — dairy, Amul, India top milk producer. Blue Revolution — fisheries. Rainbow Revolution — integrated agriculture. Organic farming — Sikkim fully organic, Zero Budget Natural Farming (Andhra Pradesh). Agricultural marketing — APMCs, e-NAM, contract farming, farmer producer organisations (FPOs). MSP (minimum support price) — 23 crops, CACP recommends, government announces at sowing time. Agricultural subsidies — fertiliser (urea), food (PDS), electricity, credit (Kisan Credit Card). Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (crop insurance). PM-KISAN (₹6000/year).

Mineral & Energy Resources: Iron ore — Haematite/Magnetite (Odisha-Jharkhand belt, Durg-Bastar belt, Bellary-Chitradurga, Goa). Coal — Gondwana (Damodar valley — Jharia, Raniganj, Bokaro, Talcher) and Tertiary (NE — Makum, Namchik-Namphuk). Petroleum — Mumbai High, Gujarat (Ankleshwar), Assam (Digboi, Naharkatiya). Natural gas — KG basin, Mumbai High, CBM. Atomic minerals — Uranium (Jaduguda, Jharkhand; Tummalapalle, AP), Thorium (monazite sands, Kerala-TN-Odisha). Renewable energy — Solar (Rajasthan, Gujarat, Ladakh — JNNSM target 500 GW by 2030), Wind (Tamil Nadu, Gujarat, Rajasthan — 7th largest globally), Hydropower (Bhakra, Tehri, Sardar Sarovar), Biomass, Geothermal, Tidal. Critical issues — energy security, energy transition, strategic petroleum reserves.

Population & Human Geography: Census 2011 — 1.21 billion, decadal growth 17.7%, density 382/sqkm. States with highest population: UP, Maharashtra, Bihar. Highest density: Delhi (11,320), lowest: Arunachal (17). Sex ratio 943 (2011), child sex ratio 919 — declining concern. Literacy 74% (male 82%, female 65%). Urbanisation — 31% (2011), growing to ~34% (2023 est). Million-plus cities: 53. Migration — rural-urban, seasonal; remittances important for states like Kerala, Bihar. NITI Aayog's SDG India Index — performance across health, education, water, sanitation.

Solved Examples

Example 1 (Prelims 2022)
With reference to Indian soils, consider the following statements:
1. Alluvial soils are generally rich in potash and lime.
2. Black soils are rich in phosphorous and nitrogen.
3. Laterite soils are rich in iron and aluminium.

Which of the above statements are correct?

a) 1 and 2 only b) 2 and 3 only c) 1 and 3 only d) 1, 2 and 3
Solution: c) 1 and 3 only. Alluvial soils are rich in potash (K) and lime (CaO), and poor in nitrogen and phosphorous — correct. Black soils (Regur) are rich in calcium, magnesium, iron, and alumina but POOR in phosphorous and nitrogen — so statement 2 is incorrect. Laterite soils are rich in iron and aluminium oxides, with high Fe₂O₃ and Al₂O₃ content, but poor in organic matter, nitrogen, and lime — statement 3 is correct. Thus, only 1 and 3 are correct.
Example 2 (Mains 2023)
Explain the mechanism of the Indian Monsoon. How do El Nino and La Nina affect the monsoon pattern in India?
Solution: The Indian Monsoon is a seasonal reversal of wind systems driven by differential heating of land and sea. Mechanism: (1) Summer — intense heating of Tibetan Plateau creates low pressure over Central Asia; high pressure over Indian Ocean. Air flows from high to low — Southwest monsoon. (2) Inter-Tropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ) shifts northwards over Gangetic plains during summer, bringing rainfall. (3) Upper air circulation — subtropical westerly jet stream (north of Himalayas in summer) and tropical easterly jet stream (over Peninsula) interact. (4) Cross-equatorial flow — Somali Jet (low-level jet) brings moisture from Southern Hemisphere across the equator. El Nino (ENSO warm phase) — warming of central/eastern Pacific, weakens monsoon winds, causes below-normal rainfall and drought in India (e.g., 2002, 2009, 2015). La Nina (ENSO cold phase) — cooling of Pacific, strengthens monsoon, associated with above-normal rainfall (e.g., 2010, 2020, 2022 — severe floods). However, the relationship is not deterministic — 1997 El Nino had normal monsoon (Indian Ocean Dipole positive offset the effect). IOD — positive IOD (warmer Indian Ocean) enhances monsoon, counteracts El Nino. Thus, monsoon prediction requires multi-factor analysis of ENSO, IOD, and other regional factors.
Example 3 (Prelims 2021)
Consider the following rivers:
1. Brahmani
2. Nagavali
3. Subarnarekha
4. Vamsadhara

Which of the above rivers flow through Odisha?

a) 1, 2, 3 and 4 b) 1, 2 and 3 only c) 1, 3 and 4 only d) 2, 3 and 4 only
Solution: c) 1, 3 and 4 only. Brahmani (formed by confluence of Koel and Sankh, flows through Odisha). Subarnarekha (originates in Jharkhand, flows through West Bengal and Odisha). Vamsadhara (originates in Odisha, flows through Odisha and Andhra). Nagavali flows through Odisha and Andhra Pradesh as well, but wait — Nagavali actually originates in Odisha (Kalahandi district) and flows through Rayagada and Koraput districts of Odisha before entering Andhra. So actually all four flow through Odisha. But the correct answer as per UPSC key is: The question had yes/no. Let me re-verify: Brahmani (Odisha), Nagavali (Odisha + AP), Subarnarekha (Jharkhand+WB+Odisha), Vamsadhara (Odisha+AP). So all 4 flow through Odisha. However, UPSC 2021 official answer key marks option (a) 1,2,3,4 as correct. So all four rivers pass through Odisha. Nagavali does flow through Odisha's Nabarangpur and Koraput districts.
