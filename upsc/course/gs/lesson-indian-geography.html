Indian Geography

Physical, social, economic geography of India and the world.
Physical Geography · Economic Geography · World Geography

Learning Objectives

  • Understand the fundamental concepts of Indian Geography
  • Apply key formulas and techniques to solve problems
  • Practice with exam-level questions to build speed and accuracy

Key Concepts

What is Indian Geography?

Indian Geography for SSC CGL covers the physical features (rivers, mountains, plains, coasts), climate patterns (monsoons, seasons), natural resources (soils, minerals, forests), and economic geography (agriculture, industries, transportation).

Key Concepts

1. Physical Divisions: The Himalayas (Great Himalayas, Lesser Himalayas, Shiwaliks — transverse divisions: Kashmir, Himachal, Garhwal, Kumaon, Nepal, Assam Himalayas), Northern Plains (Indus-Ganga-Brahmaputra basins), Peninsular Plateau (Central Highlands, Deccan Plateau), Indian Desert (Thar), Coastal Plains (Western Ghats, Eastern Ghats), Islands (Andaman & Nicobar, Lakshadweep).

2. River Systems: Himalayan Rivers (Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra — perennial, glacier-fed) and Peninsular Rivers (Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri, Mahanadi, Narmada, Tapi — seasonal, rain-fed).

3. Climate & Monsoon: India has a tropical monsoon climate. Southwest Monsoon (June-September) brings most rainfall. Retreating Monsoon (October-December). Factors: El Niño, La Niña, ITCZ, Jet Streams.

4. Agriculture: Major crops — Rice (Kharif), Wheat (Rabi), Sugarcane, Cotton, Jute, Tea, Coffee. Green Revolution (1960s) — high-yield varieties, fertilizers, Punjab & Haryana benefited most.

Solved Examples

Example 1
Which river is known as the 'Dakshin Ganga'?

a) Krishna b) Godavari c) Kaveri d) Narmada
Solution: b) Godavari. It is the largest peninsular river (1465 km). It originates from Trimbakeshwar in Maharashtra and flows into the Bay of Bengal. It is called 'Dakshin Ganga' (Ganga of the South).
Example 2
The 'Western Ghats' are also known by which name?

a) Sahyadri b) Purvanchal c) Vindhyas d) Aravalli
Solution: a) Sahyadri. The Western Ghats run parallel to the west coast from Gujarat to Kanyakumari. They are a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the world's eight 'hottest hotspots' of biological diversity.

Shortcut Techniques

  • Remember the Seven Sister States: 'AMNMT' — Arunachal Pradesh, Meghalaya, Nagaland, Mizoram, Tripura + Manipur, Assam (not a true sister but included). Mnemonic: 'All Mizo Nagas Meet Tripura' — add Arunachal, Manipur, Assam.
  • For soil types and states: Alluvial (UP, Bihar, Punjab, WB), Black/Regur (Maharashtra, MP, Gujarat — cotton), Red (TN, Karnataka, Andhra), Laterite (Kerala, Karnataka, Goa).
  • Biosphere reserves: 'NMSK' — Nilgiri, Nanda Devi, Sundarbans, Gulf of Mannar are the first four declared.
  • For national parks: 'Rajasthan has Desert (Ranthambore, Sariska) and MP has Forests (Kanha, Bandhavgarh, Pench)'.
Pro Tip: Draw a rough outline map of India and mark rivers, mountains, and neighbouring countries. Visual memory works best for geography. For climate, focus on monsoon mechanisms — this fetches repeated questions.
