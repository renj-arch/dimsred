Direction
The rule. Paper A is one Indian language from the Eighth Schedule. It is 300 marks and three hours; you need 25% (75/300) to qualify, and the marks are not counted in the merit list. A candidate who fails Paper A is not ranked, however well the scored papers went.

The Gurmukhi script

U+0A00–U+0A7F

The UPSC Punjabi paper is written in Gurmukhi. Shahmukhi is used by Punjabi speakers in Pakistan, but it is not the script of this paper. Write Gurmukhi and use the script's own digits when the passage does.

Direction and characters
Gurmukhi runs left to right. The page uses the Unicode range U+0A00–U+0A7F. Write the language in its own script, not in a Latin transliteration.
Traps in Punjabi
  • Use Gurmukhi, not Shahmukhi or a Latin transliteration.
  • Keep the vowel signs attached to the consonants.
  • Use Gurmukhi digits ੦–੯ when the passage uses Punjabi numerals.

Your Punjabi course path

5 modules · do them in order

Paper A is five skills, not one. Work through these modules, then use the workbook and script checker below. Each module links to the section of this page that carries the material.

1. Script and decoding
Do not read Punjabi through a transliteration. Work directly in Gurmukhi (U+0A00–U+0A7F, left-to-right). Spend the first sessions learning the conjuncts and half-forms you meet most: a slow first read costs minutes in the exam, a fast second read is the skill that scores.
2. Comprehension method
Read the passage once for the argument, once for the detail. Before answering, write the claim in one line in Punjabi. Answer in the language of the question paper, cite the passage rather than your opinion, and keep each answer to two or three sentences. Inference questions reward "the passage suggests", not outside knowledge.
3. Precis method
Compress to roughly one-third of the original. Keep the claim, the reasons and the tone; drop examples and repetition. Do not add a conclusion the author did not write. After drafting, check every sentence still belongs to the Punjabi register of the original — a precis that drifts into essay style loses marks.
4. Translation drills
Translate the same English paragraph both ways. Meaning and register beat word order. Watch the traps listed under the script section — for Punjabi they are not generic: Use Gurmukhi, not Shahmukhi or a Latin transliteration. Check your Punjabi output with the script checker before comparing meaning.
5. Essay structure
250 words in Punjabi: one opening that states the position, two developed points with an example each, one closing. Plan in Punjabi for five minutes before writing. The examiner reads organisation and language control first; a correct but shapeless answer scores less than a structured one.

Suggested pace for a December attempt: one module per week, workbook every other day, script check on every written answer.

Practice workbook

Original study prompts · not PYQs

These are original tasks, not previous-year questions. Write your answers in Punjabi; use the script checker below before you ask for feedback.

1. Comprehension
Read a 300–400 word Punjabi passage. Answer in Punjabi:
  1. What is the main claim?
  2. What are the two strongest supporting reasons?
  3. What does the writer want the reader to do?
Then close the passage and write a three-sentence summary.
2. Precis
Write a precis of the same passage in about one-third of its length. Keep the claim, the reasons and the tone. Do not add your opinion. Check the script before you compare with the original.
3. Usage and vocabulary
Choose ten words from the passage that you would not use in everyday speech. Write one Punjabi sentence for each and note the English meaning. Then write one sentence using a common idiom from the passage.
4. Translate English → Punjabi
Translate the paragraph below into Punjabi. Then translate your version back into English without looking at the original. Compare meaning, not word order.
Reading a newspaper carefully is a skill that improves with practice. A reader who notices the main claim, the evidence and the tone can summarize an article in a few sentences. This habit helps in the examination because it saves time and prevents answers from becoming a list of disconnected points. It also builds vocabulary, which is useful in the translation section. Ten minutes a day is enough if the reading is active.
5. Translate Punjabi → English
Take the first paragraph of your passage. Translate it into English. Underline the phrases that had no direct equivalent. Rewrite those phrases in plain English.
6. Short essay
Write 250 words in Punjabi on one of these topics: the value of public libraries; why local languages matter in a democracy; how a city can protect its trees. Use a beginning, two developed points and a close.

Advanced Grammar Patterns

Mastering complex verb forms is essential for the descriptive paper. The causative construction, formed by adding ਆਉਣਾ to the root, shifts agency from the subject to an implied cause. For example, ਉਹ ਪੁਸਤਕ ਲਿਖਦਾ ਹੈ becomes ਉਹ ਪੁਸਤਕ ਲਿਖਵਾਉਂਦਾ ਹੈ when the subject makes someone else write. The passive voice uses the auxiliary ਜਾਣਾ with the past participle, as in ਕੰਮ ਕੀਤਾ ਗਿਆ. Conditional sentences often employ the ਜੇ … ਤੇ pattern, requiring the subjunctive in the protasis and the future in the apodosis. Practise converting active sentences into causative and passive forms to increase syntactic flexibility.

Causative Transformation
ਉਹ ਬੱਚੇ ਨੂੰ ਕਹਾਣੀ ਸੁਣਾਉਂਦਾ ਹੈ।
ਉਹ ਬੱਚੇ ਨੂੰ ਕਹਾਣੀ ਸੁਣਵਾਉਂਦਾ ਹੈ।

Essay Structuring Strategies

A well‑organised essay follows a clear introduction–body–conclusion framework. Begin with a concise thesis statement that directly addresses the prompt. Each body paragraph should open with a topic sentence, provide evidence or examples, and close with a linking sentence that anticipates the next point. Use cohesive devices such as ਪਹਿਲਾਂ, ਫਿਰ, ਅਖੀਰਕਾਰ to guide the examiner. Allocate roughly 150 words to the introduction, 300–350 words to the body, and 100 words to the conclusion. Time management is critical; practise writing a full essay within the 45‑minute limit.

Paragraph Cohesion
Insert a transitional phrase at the start of every paragraph to signal logical progression.

Idiomatic Usage in Formal Writing

Formal Punjabi prose relies on a stable set of idioms that convey precision without colloquial flavour. Phrases such as “ਸਮਝੌਤੇ ਦੀ ਪਾਲਣਾ” (adherence to agreement) and “ਕਾਨੂੰਨੀ ਢਾਂਚੇ ਦੇ ਅਨੁਸਾਰ” (in accordance with the legal framework) replace everyday expressions like “ਮੰਨ ਲਿਆ” or “ਕਾਨੂੰਨ ਅਨੁਸਾਰ”. Mastery of these idioms enables candidates to produce answers that meet the evaluator’s expectation of administrative tone. Practice by rewriting newspaper editorials, replacing each colloquial clause with its formal counterpart, and then comparing the register shift.

Syntactic Variation for Emphasis

Punjabi permits flexible word order; the default Subject‑Object‑Verb can be altered to Object‑Subject‑Verb or Verb‑Subject‑Object to highlight the element the writer wishes to stress. For instance, “ਰਿਪੋਰਟ ਸਰਕਾਰ ਨੇ ਪੇਸ਼ ਕੀਤੀ” (The report was submitted by the government) foregrounds the report, while “ਪੇਸ਼ ਕੀਤੀ ਸਰਕਾਰ ਨੇ ਰਿਪੋਰਟ” foregrounds the act of submission. In answer writing, place the most critical noun phrase at the clause‑initial position to guide the examiner’s attention.

Example
ਅਧਿਕਾਰੀ ਨੇ ਫੈਸਲਾ ਲਿਆ।
ਫੈਸਲਾ ਅਧਿਕਾਰੀ ਨੇ ਲਿਆ। (Emphasis on the decision)
Tip
When converting a sentence for emphasis, keep the verb form unchanged; only reorder the noun phrases and adjust case markers accordingly.

Precision Vocabulary for Administrative Contexts

Administrative Punjabi demands lexical items that map directly to English bureaucratic terminology. Use ਨਿਰਧਾਰਤ (designated) instead of ਚੁਣਿਆ ਗਿਆ, ਅਮਲ ਵਿੱਚ ਲਿਆਉਣਾ (implement) rather than ਲਾਗੂ ਕਰਨਾ, and ਪ੍ਰਾਪਤ ਕਰਨਾ (acquire) in place of ਲੈਣਾ. Building a personal glossary of such pairs and revising it weekly reduces the risk of vague language that can lower marks for clarity.

Paragraph Cohesion and Discourse Markers

Effective Punjabi prose for the mains examination relies on clear logical flow between sentences. Discourse markers such as ਅਤੇ, ਪਰ, ਜਿਸ ਕਾਰਨ, and ਇਸ ਲਈ signal addition, contrast, cause, and consequence respectively. Mastery of these connectors allows candidates to build arguments that examiners can follow without re‑reading.

When a paragraph shifts from a factual statement to an analytical comment, a marker like ਇਸ ਨਾਲ or ਜਿਸ ਨਾਲ binds the two ideas. Overuse of simple coordinators (ਅਤੇ) can make writing feel repetitive; varying markers demonstrates syntactic maturity.

  • ਅਤੇ – addition
  • ਪਰ – contrast
  • ਜਿਸ ਕਾਰਨ – reason
  • ਇਸ ਲਈ – purpose
  • ਜਿਸ ਨਾਲ – result
Example: Discourse Marker Usage
Original: ਪੰਜਾਬ ਸਰਕਾਰ ਨੇ ਨਵੀਂ ਨੀਤੀ ਜਾਰੀ ਕੀਤੀ। ਇਸ ਨਾਲ ਕਿਸਾਨਾਂ ਨੂੰ ਲਾਭ ਹੋਵੇਗਾ। Revised: ਪੰਜਾਬ ਸਰਕਾਰ ਨੇ ਨਵੀਂ ਨੀਤੀ ਜਾਰੀ ਕੀਤੀ, ਜਿਸ ਨਾਲ ਕਿਸਾਨਾਂ ਨੂੰ ਲਾਭ ਹੋਵੇਗਾ।
The conjunction “ਜਿਸ ਨਾਲ” links cause and effect, improving cohesion.

Register Shifts in Official Correspondence

Official Punjabi communication demands a formal register that differs markedly from everyday speech. Honorifics such as ਸ਼੍ਰੀਮਾਨ, ਮਹੋਦਯ, and the use of passive constructions (ਕिया ਗਿਆ ਹੈ) convey respect and objectivity. When addressing a senior officer, the writer should avoid direct imperatives; instead, employ polite requests like ਕਿਰਪਾ ਕਰਕੇ ਵੇਰਵਾ ਦਿਓ.

Register also shifts with the document type. A notification (ਸੂਚਨਾ) uses concise declarative sentences, while a memorandum (ज्ञਾਪਨ) may include explanatory clauses. Recognising these patterns helps candidates produce texts that meet the expectations of the evaluation rubric.

Tip
When drafting official letters, always match the register to the recipient’s rank; use honorifics and passive constructions for senior officers.

Advanced Morphological Derivation in Official Punjabi

Official Punjabi texts frequently create new lexical items by attaching derivational suffixes to verbal roots. The most productive suffixes in administrative writing are -ਤਾ, -ਪਣਾ, and -ਦਾਰ. When a root such as ਲਿਖ (write) receives -ਤਾ it yields ਲਿਖਤਾ (writer), while -ਪਣਾ produces ਲਿਖਪਣਾ (the act of writing). Understanding these patterns enables candidates to decode unfamiliar terms without a dictionary.

  • Identify the root verb in a given noun.
  • Recognise the semantic shift caused by each suffix.
  • Apply the rule to generate the appropriate form for a formal report.
Example: Deriving Noun Forms
Root: ਸੰਚਾਲ (manage). Adding -ਦਾਰ gives ਸੰਚਾਲਦਾਰ (manager). Adding -ਤਾ gives ਸੰਚਾਲਤਾ (management).
Both forms appear in official circulars; the former denotes a person, the latter an abstract process.

Strategic Use of Passive Constructions in Administrative Reports

Passive voice is preferred when the agent is unknown, irrelevant, or deliberately omitted. In Punjabi, the passive is formed by adding the auxiliary ਜਾਣਾ to the past participle of the main verb, e.g., ਕਾਰਵਾਈ ਕੀਤੀ ਗਈ (action was taken). This construction shifts focus to the action and its outcome, which aligns with the impersonal tone required in government communications.

  1. Convert active sentences to passive without altering meaning.
  2. Maintain agreement of the participle with the subject in gender and number.
  3. Avoid overuse; retain active voice for directives and responsibilities.
Tip
When drafting a report, first write in active voice, then systematically transform sentences that describe completed procedures into passive forms to achieve the formal register.

Complex Sentence Construction in Official Punjabi

Official Punjabi frequently employs subordinate clauses to convey cause, condition, or time without breaking the formal flow. The most common subordinators are ਕਿ (that), ਜੇ (if), ਜਦੋਂ (when), and ਜਿਵੇਂ (as). These particles attach to the verb of the dependent clause, which usually appears in the subjunctive or future form, while the main clause retains the indicative mood.

Relative clauses are introduced by the pronoun ਜੋ (who/which) followed by the appropriate case marker. The antecedent precedes the relative clause, and the verb agrees with the antecedent in gender and number. Mastery of this pattern allows writers to embed detailed specifications within a single sentence, a hallmark of administrative reports.

Example: Relative Clause
ਅਧਿਕਾਰੀ ਜੋ ਰਿਪੋਰਟ ਤਿਆਰ ਕਰਦਾ ਹੈ, ਉਸ ਨੂੰ ਸਬਮਿਟ ਕਰਨੀ ਚਾਹੀਦੀ ਹੈ।
The clause “ਜੋ ਰਿਪੋਰਟ ਤਿਆਰ ਕਰਦਾ ਹੈ” modifies “ਅਧਿਕਾਰੀ”. The verb “ਕਰਦਾ” agrees with the masculine singular antecedent.
Tip
When chaining multiple subordinate clauses, keep the main verb at the end of the sentence to preserve the expected SOV order of formal Punjabi.

Honorifics and Politeness Strategies in Administrative Writing

Respectful address is encoded through lexical honorifics and pronoun choice. The honorific particle ਜੀ follows names or titles (e.g., ਸਰਦਾਰ ਜੀ). The plural pronoun ਤੁਸੀਂ replaces the singular ਤੂੰ when addressing superiors or the public. Verbs correspondingly take the plural agreement suffix ‑ਦੇ ਹੋ or ‑ਦੇ ਨੇ.

Politeness can also be expressed by the modal auxiliary ਸਕਦਾ (can) combined with ਕਿਰਪਾ ਕਰਕੇ (please). This softens directives without diminishing authority. In written orders, the passive construction ਕਰਵਾਇਆ ਜਾਵੇ (should be got done) further distances the issuer from the action, enhancing formality.

Example: Honorific Usage
ਕਿਰਪਾ ਕਰਕੇ ਤੁਸੀਂ ਰਿਪੋਰਟ ਜੀ ਸਬਮਿਟ ਕਰੋ।
“ਤੁਸੀਂ” and the plural verb “ਕਰੋ” show respect; “ਜੀ” after “ਰਿਪੋਰਟ” honors the document as an official entity.
Tip
Avoid mixing singular and plural honorifics in the same clause; consistency signals professional competence.
