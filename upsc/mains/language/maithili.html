Direction
The rule. Paper A is one Indian language from the Eighth Schedule. It is 300 marks and three hours; you need 25% (75/300) to qualify, and the marks are not counted in the merit list. A candidate who fails Paper A is not ranked, however well the scored papers went.

The Devanagari script

U+0900–U+097F

Maithili is written in Devanagari for the UPSC paper. Tirhuta is the historical script of the language, but the examination paper uses Devanagari. Maithili has its own grammar and vocabulary; do not treat it as a Hindi variant.

Direction and characters
Devanagari runs left to right. The page uses the Unicode range U+0900–U+097F. Write the language in its own script, not in a Latin transliteration.
Traps in Maithili
  • Write Maithili, not Hindi. The shared script does not make the languages interchangeable.
  • Use the Maithili verb forms and honorifics that the passage uses.
  • Do not switch to Tirhuta unless the question paper itself is printed in it.

Your Maithili course path

5 modules · do them in order

Paper A is five skills, not one. Work through these modules, then use the workbook and script checker below. Each module links to the section of this page that carries the material.

1. Script and decoding
Do not read Maithili through a transliteration. Work directly in Devanagari (U+0900–U+097F, left-to-right). Spend the first sessions learning the conjuncts and half-forms you meet most: a slow first read costs minutes in the exam, a fast second read is the skill that scores.
2. Comprehension method
Read the passage once for the argument, once for the detail. Before answering, write the claim in one line in Maithili. Answer in the language of the question paper, cite the passage rather than your opinion, and keep each answer to two or three sentences. Inference questions reward "the passage suggests", not outside knowledge.
3. Precis method
Compress to roughly one-third of the original. Keep the claim, the reasons and the tone; drop examples and repetition. Do not add a conclusion the author did not write. After drafting, check every sentence still belongs to the Maithili register of the original — a precis that drifts into essay style loses marks.
4. Translation drills
Translate the same English paragraph both ways. Meaning and register beat word order. Watch the traps listed under the script section — for Maithili they are not generic: Write Maithili, not Hindi. The shared script does not make the languages interchangeable. Check your Maithili output with the script checker before comparing meaning.
5. Essay structure
250 words in Maithili: one opening that states the position, two developed points with an example each, one closing. Plan in Maithili for five minutes before writing. The examiner reads organisation and language control first; a correct but shapeless answer scores less than a structured one.

Suggested pace for a December attempt: one module per week, workbook every other day, script check on every written answer.

Practice workbook

Original study prompts · not PYQs

These are original tasks, not previous-year questions. Write your answers in Maithili; use the script checker below before you ask for feedback.

1. Comprehension
Read a 300–400 word Maithili passage. Answer in Maithili:
  1. What is the main claim?
  2. What are the two strongest supporting reasons?
  3. What does the writer want the reader to do?
Then close the passage and write a three-sentence summary.
2. Precis
Write a precis of the same passage in about one-third of its length. Keep the claim, the reasons and the tone. Do not add your opinion. Check the script before you compare with the original.
3. Usage and vocabulary
Choose ten words from the passage that you would not use in everyday speech. Write one Maithili sentence for each and note the English meaning. Then write one sentence using a common idiom from the passage.
4. Translate English → Maithili
Translate the paragraph below into Maithili. Then translate your version back into English without looking at the original. Compare meaning, not word order.
Reading a newspaper carefully is a skill that improves with practice. A reader who notices the main claim, the evidence and the tone can summarize an article in a few sentences. This habit helps in the examination because it saves time and prevents answers from becoming a list of disconnected points. It also builds vocabulary, which is useful in the translation section. Ten minutes a day is enough if the reading is active.
5. Translate Maithili → English
Take the first paragraph of your passage. Translate it into English. Underline the phrases that had no direct equivalent. Rewrite those phrases in plain English.
6. Short essay
Write 250 words in Maithili on one of these topics: the value of public libraries; why local languages matter in a democracy; how a city can protect its trees. Use a beginning, two developed points and a close.

Advanced Grammar Nuances

Maithili exhibits a rich system of verb morphology that distinguishes tense, aspect, mood, and honorific levels. The language uses three primary past forms — simple past, habitual past, and perfective past — each marked by distinct suffixes attached to the verb root. For instance, the root कर (to do) becomes करल (simple past), करैत (habitual past), and करलेल (perfective past). Honorific agreement is signaled by the auxiliary अछि for respectful subjects and अछ for neutral ones. Noun‑case endings follow the Devanagari pattern but retain Maithili‑specific vowel lengthening in the oblique case. Mastery of these nuances is essential for accurate translation and precis writing.

Writing Effective Precis for Maithili

A precis must condense the source while preserving logical flow and key terminology. Begin by identifying the central thesis, then segment the passage into thematic units. Replace verbose clauses with concise nominal phrases; for example, जे कारणवश कार्य पूरा नहि भेल can become कार्य अपूर्ण रहल. Preserve proper nouns, technical terms, and any quoted material verbatim. Maintain the original tense sequence; if the source mixes past and present, reflect that mixture. Avoid inserting personal interpretation. Practice by summarising editorials from Maithili newspapers, then compare your version with the original word count to gauge compression ratio.

Advanced Vocabulary Building for Maithili

Expanding a precise lexical repertoire is essential for scoring well in Paper A. Focus on three layers: core administrative terminology, literary idioms, and regional dialectal variants. Create thematic flashcards grouped by subjects such as governance, agriculture, and cultural heritage. Review each card daily, pronouncing the Devanagari form aloud to reinforce script‑sound mapping.

  • Collect words from official notifications, Maithili newspapers, and classic texts.
  • Tag each entry with part‑of‑speech, gender, and case‑ending patterns.
  • Practice constructing sentences that mirror the formal register used in UPSC answers.

Effective Translation Strategies for Paper A

Translation tasks demand fidelity to meaning while preserving the target language’s syntactic norms. Adopt a two‑stage workflow: first, produce a literal gloss; second, restructure into natural Maithili prose. Pay special attention to compound verbs, honorific markers, and the use of postpositions versus prepositions.

  1. Identify the source clause boundaries.
  2. Map each lexical item to its Maithili equivalent, noting gender agreement.
  3. Reorder to follow SOV order, inserting appropriate case markers.
  4. Proofread for idiomatic flow and official tone.
Quick Tip
Keep a bilingual glossary of recurring administrative phrases; it reduces lookup time during the exam.

Complex Sentence Structures in Maithili

Maithili allows multiple clause types to be combined without altering the basic SOV order. Coordinating conjunctions such as अँ (and), किंतु (but) and वा (or) link independent clauses, while subordinating particles like कि, जब and यदि introduce dependent clauses. Mastery of these connectors enables candidates to produce the long, nuanced sentences required in the essay and precis papers.

Coordination

  • राम गेल अछि अँ श्याम आइत अछि।
  • सीता पढल अछि किंतु राम खेलल अछि।

Subordination

  • हम जइत अछि कि तोहर काम पूरा भ गेल।
  • यदि वर्षा भैत तौ हम नहि जइत।
Example: Compound Sentence
राम गेल अछि अँ श्याम आइत अछि।
Two independent clauses joined by 'अँ' (and).

Idiomatic Expressions and Proverbs

Idioms enrich answers and demonstrate cultural fluency. Frequently used proverbs include “जे करै सो भरै” (as you sow, so shall you reap) and “आम के आम, गुठली के दाम” (double benefit). When translating such phrases, retain the metaphorical sense rather than a literal rendering; this preserves the rhetorical force expected in the language paper.

Common Idioms

  • नाक काटल – to suffer humiliation.
  • घर के भेदी लंका ढाए – insider causes ruin.
Tip
Use 'कि' to embed subordinate clauses for clarity in formal writing.

Precision in Official Correspondence

Official letters demand concise, respectful language. Begin with the proper salutation श्रीमान् or श्रीमती, followed by the subject line introduced by विषय:. Employ the honorific plural आप and avoid colloquial contractions. Close with भवदीय and the sender’s designation. Practising this format improves scores in the translation and precis sections.

Standard Layout

  1. प्रेषक का नाम और पद
  2. प्राप्तकर्ता का नाम और पद
  3. विषय: संक्षिप्त विवरण
  4. संदर्भ संख्या (यदि लागू)
  5. मुख्य विषयवस्तु
  6. समापन: भवदीय, हस्ताक्षर

Advanced Syntax Patterns

Maithili allows layered verb constructions where a main verb combines with one or more auxiliaries to express aspect, mood, and evidentiality. The most frequent pattern is the perfective auxiliary अछि attached to the past participle, yielding forms such as कइल अछि (has done). A second layer can add the modal सकैत for ability, producing कइल सकैत अछि (has been able to do). Understanding the fixed order — participle → perfective → modal → tense — prevents agreement errors in formal writing.

Example: Complex Verb Construction
उँहाँ पुस्तक पढ़ल सकैत अछि।
Solution: The sentence follows participle (पढ़ल) → modal (सकैत) → perfective (अछि). It means “He/She has been able to read the book.”

When a negative particle नै precedes the auxiliary chain, it scopes over the entire construction: नै कइल सकैत अछि (has not been able to do). Practise rewriting simple sentences into this layered form to internalise the hierarchy.

Stylistic Variation in Formal Writing

Official correspondence in Maithili distinguishes three registers: सामान्य (plain), आदरपूर्ण (honorific), and विधिक (legal). The honorific register replaces second‑person pronouns with अपना and adds the respectful suffix ‑जी to titles. The legal register prefers passive constructions using कइल गेल and avoids colloquial particles such as हो. Selecting the appropriate register signals professionalism and ensures compliance with UPSC language standards.

Tip
Maintain a register checklist: pronoun choice, verb voice, particle usage, and honorific suffixes. Review each draft against the list before finalising.

Consistent use of the legal register also requires fixed formulaic openings (e.g., श्रीमान्,) and closings (e.g., भवदीय,). Memorise these templates to reduce composition time during the exam.

Error Analysis and Self‑Correction

Common errors in Maithili Paper A include mismatched gender agreement in adjectives, incorrect case endings after postpositions, and overuse of the simple present where the habitual aspect is required. A systematic error‑log — recording the sentence, the error type, and the corrected form — accelerates learning. After each practice session, classify errors into morphological, syntactic, or lexical categories and target the weakest group in the next drill.

Advanced Morphological Derivation

Maithili builds a large portion of its formal vocabulary through systematic derivational morphology. Understanding the productive suffixes and prefixes enables candidates to expand lexical range without rote memorisation. The most frequent derivational processes involve noun‑forming suffixes, adjective‑forming suffixes, and verbal nominalisers that appear repeatedly in official documents.

Key Noun‑forming Suffixes

  • -आन (e.g., करान from कर ‘to do’ → ‘action’)
  • -ई (e.g., लिखई from लिख ‘to write’ → ‘writing’)
  • -पन (e.g., सुन्दरपन from सुन्दर ‘beautiful’ → ‘beauty’)

Adjective‑forming Suffixes

  • -मय (e.g., ज्ञानमय ‘knowledge‑filled’)
  • -वान (e.g., बलवान ‘strong’)

When a root ends in a vowel, the suffix often triggers sandhi adjustments; the vowel may be lengthened or a semivowel inserted. Mastery of these phonological rules prevents spelling errors in the Devanagari script.

Example: Deriving a Formal Noun
Root: निर्णय (decision) → add -पन → निर्णयपन (decisiveness)
The suffix -पन attaches directly because the root ends in a consonant; no sandhi change occurs.
Tip
Create a personal suffix chart grouped by part‑of‑speech; review it weekly while reading official gazettes to internalise the patterns.

Discourse Cohesion in Formal Maithili

Coherent official writing relies on explicit cohesive devices that guide the reader through logical progression. Beyond simple coordination, Maithili employs a set of discourse markers that signal contrast, cause‑effect, enumeration, and concession. Recognising and producing these markers improves precis writing and translation accuracy.

Major Cohesive Markers

  1. अतः – ‘therefore’, introduces a conclusion.
  2. तथापि – ‘nevertheless’, marks concession.
  3. यथार्थतः – ‘in fact’, reinforces a statement.
  4. प्रथमतः, द्वितीयतः – ‘firstly, secondly’, structures enumeration.
  5. कारण कि – ‘because’, signals causality.

These markers typically occupy the initial position of a clause and are followed by a comma in Devanagari punctuation. Their consistent use creates a predictable macro‑structure that examiners reward in Paper A answers.

Advanced Pragmatic Usage in Official Maithili

Official correspondence in Maithili demands a balance between respectful honorifics and concise information delivery. The choice of pronoun forms such as आप versus तू signals hierarchy, while the particle कृपया softens directives. Mastery of these pragmatic cues prevents misinterpretation in administrative letters.

Honorific Selection

  • Use आप for superiors and external agencies.
  • Reserve तू for internal memos among peers of equal rank.
  • Apply श्रीमान्/श्रीमती before names in formal address lines.
Example: Request for Leave
श्रीमान् प्रधानाचार्य महोदय, कृपया मोर अवकाश स्वीकृत करब।
The sentence employs honorific address, polite particle, and direct request suitable for a formal leave application.
Pragmatic Tip
Always match the level of formality in the closing formula (e.g., भवदीय) to the opening honorific.

Morphological Reduplication Patterns

Reduplication in Maithili creates expressive intensity, distributive meaning, or iterative aspect. Two productive patterns are full reduplication of adjectives and partial reduplication of verbs. Recognising these forms aids both comprehension and precise writing.

Adjective Reduplication

Full reduplication of qualitative adjectives yields a plural‑like emphasis: सुन्दर सुन्दर (very beautiful), बड़ा बड़ा (very large). The duplicated form functions as an intensifier without altering grammatical number.

Verb Partial Reduplication

Partial reduplication of the verb root followed by the suffix -अ marks repetitive action: चल‑चलअ (keep walking), पढ़‑पढ़अ (keep reading). This construction appears frequently in procedural instructions.

  • Identify the root, duplicate the first syllable, add -अ.
  • Use only with dynamic verbs; stative verbs rarely reduplicate.

Discourse Markers for Cohesive Argumentation

Effective essays and reports rely on discourse markers that signal logical relations. Maithili provides a set of connective particles and adverbials that guide the reader through argument structure.

Major Markers

  1. तब – temporal sequence (then).
  2. कारण – causal link (because).
  3. अतः – inferential conclusion (therefore).
  4. तथापि – concessive contrast (nevertheless).

Place markers at clause boundaries; avoid stacking more than two in a single sentence to maintain clarity.
