The Kannada script

U+0C80–U+0CFF

Kannada is written left to right in the Kannada script. The script has a large set of consonant letters and vowel signs, and its own digits. Kannada and Telugu look similar to a new reader, so keep the two scripts separate in your notes.

Direction and characters
Kannada runs left to right. The page uses the Unicode range U+0C80–U+0CFF. Write the language in its own script, not in a Latin transliteration.
Traps in Kannada
  • Do not mix Kannada and Telugu letters; they are different scripts even where the shapes are close.
  • Preserve vowel length and the correct vowel sign.
  • Use Kannada digits ೦–೯ when the passage uses Kannada numerals.

Your Kannada course path

5 modules · do them in order

Paper A is five skills, not one. Work through these modules, then use the workbook and script checker below. Each module links to the section of this page that carries the material.

1. Script and decoding
Do not read Kannada through a transliteration. Work directly in Kannada (U+0C80–U+0CFF, left-to-right). Spend the first sessions learning the conjuncts and half-forms you meet most: a slow first read costs minutes in the exam, a fast second read is the skill that scores.
2. Comprehension method
Read the passage once for the argument, once for the detail. Before answering, write the claim in one line in Kannada. Answer in the language of the question paper, cite the passage rather than your opinion, and keep each answer to two or three sentences. Inference questions reward "the passage suggests", not outside knowledge.
3. Precis method
Compress to roughly one-third of the original. Keep the claim, the reasons and the tone; drop examples and repetition. Do not add a conclusion the author did not write. After drafting, check every sentence still belongs to the Kannada register of the original — a precis that drifts into essay style loses marks.
4. Translation drills
Translate the same English paragraph both ways. Meaning and register beat word order. Watch the traps listed under the script section — for Kannada they are not generic: Do not mix Kannada and Telugu letters; they are different scripts even where the shapes are close. Check your Kannada output with the script checker before comparing meaning.
5. Essay structure
250 words in Kannada: one opening that states the position, two developed points with an example each, one closing. Plan in Kannada for five minutes before writing. The examiner reads organisation and language control first; a correct but shapeless answer scores less than a structured one.

Suggested pace for a December attempt: one module per week, workbook every other day, script check on every written answer.

Practice workbook

Original study prompts · not PYQs

These are original tasks, not previous-year questions. Write your answers in Kannada; use the script checker below before you ask for feedback.

1. Comprehension
Read a 300–400 word Kannada passage. Answer in Kannada:
  1. What is the main claim?
  2. What are the two strongest supporting reasons?
  3. What does the writer want the reader to do?
Then close the passage and write a three-sentence summary.
2. Precis
Write a precis of the same passage in about one-third of its length. Keep the claim, the reasons and the tone. Do not add your opinion. Check the script before you compare with the original.
3. Usage and vocabulary
Choose ten words from the passage that you would not use in everyday speech. Write one Kannada sentence for each and note the English meaning. Then write one sentence using a common idiom from the passage.
4. Translate English → Kannada
Translate the paragraph below into Kannada. Then translate your version back into English without looking at the original. Compare meaning, not word order.
Reading a newspaper carefully is a skill that improves with practice. A reader who notices the main claim, the evidence and the tone can summarize an article in a few sentences. This habit helps in the examination because it saves time and prevents answers from becoming a list of disconnected points. It also builds vocabulary, which is useful in the translation section. Ten minutes a day is enough if the reading is active.
5. Translate Kannada → English
Take the first paragraph of your passage. Translate it into English. Underline the phrases that had no direct equivalent. Rewrite those phrases in plain English.
6. Short essay
Write 250 words in Kannada on one of these topics: the value of public libraries; why local languages matter in a democracy; how a city can protect its trees. Use a beginning, two developed points and a close.

Advanced Grammar: Compound Verbs and Aspect

Kannada forms compound verbs by combining a main verb with an auxiliary that conveys aspect, modality or direction. The most frequent auxiliaries are ಇರು (to be), ಹೋಗು (to go), ಬರು (to come) and ಕೊಡು (to give). When the auxiliary follows the past participle of the main verb, the construction expresses completed action with a nuance of result (ಮಾಡಿ ಇರು – “have done”). When the auxiliary follows the infinitive, it indicates intention or future movement (ಮಾಡಲು ಹೋಗು – “go to do”). Mastery of these patterns is essential for precise answer‑writing in Paper A.

Example: Compound Verb Formation
ಅವನು ಪಠ್ಯವನ್ನು ಓದಿ ಇರು.
He has read the lesson (completed action with present relevance).

Essay Structuring for Paper A

A high‑scoring essay follows a clear three‑part architecture: introduction, body, and conclusion. The introduction must state the thesis in one concise sentence and outline the main arguments. Each body paragraph should begin with a topic sentence, provide evidence from Kannada literature or contemporary sources, and end with a linking sentence that anticipates the next point. The conclusion restates the thesis in fresh language, synthesises the arguments, and offers a forward‑looking remark. Practising this scaffold under timed conditions improves coherence and lexical range.

Tip
Write the outline in Kannada first; it forces vocabulary retrieval and prevents translation‑lag during the actual exam.

Translation Strategies and Model‑Generated Practice

Effective translation from English to Kannada relies on three strategies: (1) identify the core predicate and map it to the appropriate verb class; (2) preserve idiomatic collocations by using established Kannada equivalents rather than literal word‑for‑word rendering; (3) adjust word order to the SOV pattern while keeping thematic emphasis. Regular drills with short passages sharpen these skills.

Advanced Syntax: Non‑finite Clauses

Non‑finite clauses allow a writer to compress two actions into a single predicate, a skill essential for the concise style demanded in Paper A. In Kannada the most common non‑finite forms are the infinitive (‑ಉ), the gerund (‑ಉತ್ತ), and the past participle (‑ಿದ). Each attaches directly to the verb stem and inherits the subject of the main clause.

The table below summarises the three forms with their typical functions.

FormSuffixFunction
Infinitive‑ಉPurpose or intention
Gerund‑ಉತ್ತSimultaneous or preceding action
Past participle‑ಿದCompleted action before the main verb

When converting a finite clause to a non‑finite one, drop the subject pronoun and adjust the verb to the appropriate suffix. The resulting construction often removes the need for a conjunction such as ಮತ್ತು (and) or ಹಾಗಾಗಿ (therefore).

Example: Non‑finite clause transformation
Original: ಅವರು ಓದಿ ಬಂದರು। (They came after reading.)
Non‑finite: ಓದಿ ಬಂದರು। (Reading, they came.)
Tip
Use the gerund form (‑ಉತ್ತ) to express simultaneous actions concisely.

Precision Vocabulary for Administrative Writing

Administrative Kannada favours a restricted set of formal lexical items that convey authority, neutrality, and procedural clarity. Replacing colloquial equivalents with their official counterparts improves both readability and scoring.

  • ಸ್ವೀಕರಿಸು (accept) → ಅಂಗೀಕರಿಸು (officially acknowledge)
  • ಹೇಳು (say) → ವಿವರಿಸು (explain in detail)
  • ಮಾಡು (do) → ಕ್ರಮಗೊಳಿಸು (implement)
  • ಬೇರು (different) → ವಿಭಿನ್ನ (distinct)

Practice rewriting sentences by substituting each colloquial verb with its formal counterpart. This habit also trains the mind to recognise the register expected in official correspondence.

Pragmatic Discourse Markers in Administrative Kannada

Administrative writing relies on discourse markers to signal logical relations between sentences. Mastery of these markers improves cohesion and ensures that directives, reports, and orders are read without ambiguity.

  • ಆದರೆ (however) – introduces a contrast to the preceding statement.
  • ಹಾಗಾಗಿ (therefore) – marks a consequence or inference.
  • ಅದರಿಂದ (hence) – links cause to effect in formal orders.
  • ಇದರೊಂದಿಗೆ (along with this) – adds supplementary information.

When drafting a circular, place the marker at the beginning of the clause it governs, and follow it with a comma in the romanised transcription. This mirrors the syntactic position in Kannada and preserves the formal tone.

Example
ಸರ್ಕಾರವು ಹೊಸ ನಿಯಮವನ್ನು ಪ್ರಕಟಿಸಿದೆ, ಆದರೆ ಅನ್ನು ಅನುಸರಿಸುವಂತೆ ಎಲ್ಲಾ ಕಚೇರಿಗಳಿಗೆ ಸೂಚನೆ ನೀಡಲಾಗಿದೆ।
The marker ಆದರೆ signals a contrast between the announcement and the directive that follows.

Error Analysis: Common Morphological Pitfalls

Even advanced learners frequently misapply case endings and verb‑form agreement in official prose. The most recurrent errors involve the genitive ‑ನ, the dative ‑ಗೆ, and the finite verb agreement with honorific subjects.

  • Using ‑ನ where the instrumental ‑ದ್ವಾರ is required.
  • Omitting the dative marker on the beneficiary of an order.
  • Mismatching plural verb endings with singular honorific nouns (e.g., ಅಧಿಕಾರಿ treated as plural).

Systematic proofreading against a checklist of these patterns reduces the error rate dramatically.

Proofreading Tip
Read the draft aloud; the rhythmic cadence of Kannada often reveals missing case markers that silent reading misses.

Advanced Morphology: Derivational Suffixes

Kannada builds a large part of its administrative vocabulary by attaching derivational suffixes to verbal roots or nominal bases. The most productive suffixes for forming abstract nouns are -ತೆ, -ಿಕೆ, and -ಣ. For example, the root ನಿರ್ವಹಿಸು (to administer) yields ನಿರ್ವಹಣೆ (administration) with -ಣ, while ಸಮರ್ಪಿಸು (to dedicate) gives ಸಮರ್ಪಣೆ (dedication). The suffix -ಕಾರ creates agent nouns such as ನಿರ್ವಾಹಕ (administrator). Recognising these patterns lets a candidate decode unfamiliar terms in official orders without a dictionary.

  • -ತೆ forms quality/state nouns: ಸ್ವಚ್ಛತೆ (cleanliness).
  • -ಿಕೆ forms action/result nouns: ನಿರೀಕ್ಷಿಕೆ (inspection).
  • -ಕಾರ forms doer nouns: ಸಂಯೋಜಕ (coordinator).
Derivation Drill
Convert the verb ಪರಿಶೀಲಿಸು (to examine) into three nouns using the suffixes above.
ಪರಿಶೀಲನೆ (examination), ಪರಿಶೀಲಿಕೆ (inspection), ಪರಿಶೀಲಕ (examiner).

Discourse Cohesion in Official Correspondence

Administrative Kannada relies on a small set of discourse markers that signal logical relations between clauses. The most frequent are ಆದರೆ (however), ಹಾಗಾಗಿ (therefore), ಒಂದಿಗೂ (furthermore), and ಅथವಾ (or). Their placement at the beginning of a sentence or after a comma guides the reader through cause‑effect, contrast, or enumeration. Overuse of ಮತ್ತು (and) without stronger connectors produces a flat style that examiners mark as lacking cohesion.

  1. Identify the marker that introduces a concession.
  2. Replace a chain of ಮತ್ತು with a single ಹಾಗಾಗಿ where appropriate.
  3. Check that each paragraph opens with a marker that reflects its rhetorical function.
Cohesion Checklist
Before finalising a draft, underline every discourse marker. Verify that each serves a distinct logical purpose and that no two consecutive sentences start with the same marker.

Stylistic Variation for Administrative Reports

Reports submitted to the Karnataka Public Service Commission should alternate between concise bullet‑style statements and full‑sentence narratives. Bullet points are ideal for listing data points, while narrative paragraphs explain policy rationale. Use the passive voice sparingly; prefer active constructions such as ವಿಭಾಗವು ನಿರ್ಧರಿಸಿದೆ (the department decided) over ನಿರ್ಧರಿಸಲ್ಪಟ್ಟಿದೆ (it was decided). Maintain a formal register by avoiding colloquial contractions like ಇಲ್ಲ for ಇಲ್ಲವೆ.

Advanced Syntax: Conditional Constructions

Conditional sentences in Kannada rely on a combination of verbal suffixes and post‑positional particles. The most common pattern uses the past‑tense verb followed by ಆದರೆ (if) or the conditional suffix ‑ದೆ attached to the verb stem. Both convey “if … then” but differ in register: ಆದರೆ is preferred in formal administrative writing, while ‑ದೆ appears in narrative style.

  • Real condition: ಅವನು ಬಂದಾದರೆ, ನಾವು ಆರಂಭಿಸುತ್ತೇವೆ (If he comes, we will start.)
  • Hypothetical condition: ಅವನು ಬಂದಿದೆಯೇ, ನಾವು ಆರಂಭಿಸುತ್ತಿದ್ದೇವೆ (Had he come, we would have started.)

When embedding a conditional clause inside a larger sentence, retain the same verb‑final order and place the conditional marker immediately after the verb. Avoid inserting extra pronouns; the subject is understood from the verb morphology.

Example: Conditional in an Official Circular

Advanced Pragmatics: Honorifics and Politeness Strategies

Official Kannada correspondence relies on a layered system of honorifics that signal respect, hierarchy, and social distance. The choice between ನೀವು (formal ‘you’) and ನೀನು (informal) is only the surface; verbs, pronouns, and even case markers shift to reflect the addressee’s rank. Mastery of these forms prevents unintended bluntness in reports, circulars, and public notices.

  • Pronominal honorifics – use ಅವರು for third‑person respect, ನೀವು for second‑person formal.
  • Verb morphology – add the suffix ‑ಇರಿ (e.g., ಮಾಡಿರಿ) to convey deference.
  • Lexical politeness markers – particles such as ದಯವಿಟ್ಟು, ಕ್ಷಮಿಸಿ soften directives.
Example: Transforming a Direct Order
Direct: ಅವನು ಪತ್ರವನ್ನು ಬರೆದನು।
Polite: ಅವರು ಪತ್ರವನ್ನು ಬರೆದಿರಿ।
Tip
When drafting a circular for senior officers, default to the ‑ಇರಿ verb form and the pronoun ಅವರು; reserve ನೀನು only for internal peer communication.

Syntactic Ambiguity Resolution in Official Texts

Administrative Kannada often packs multiple clauses into a single sentence, creating scope ambiguities for modifiers and quantifiers. A systematic approach—identify the head noun, trace the nearest verb, and test each reading against the regulatory context—helps the writer choose the unambiguous structure.

  1. Mark the main verb and its arguments.
  2. Locate every subordinate clause introducer (ಯಾವುದೇ, ಎಂದು, ಆದರೆ).
  3. Apply the “nearest‑attachment” principle; if two readings survive, rewrite using a relative clause or a separate sentence.

Applying this checklist reduces misinterpretation in orders such as “ವಿಭಾಗದ ಅಧಿಕಾರಿಗಳು ಎಲ್ಲರೂ ವೇಳೆಗೂ ಹಾಜರಾಗಬೇಕು” where “ಎಲ್ಲರೂ” could modify “ಅಧಿಕಾರಿಗಳು” or “ವಿಭಾಗದ”. The clarified version: “ವಿಭಾಗದ ಎಲ್ಲಾ ಅಧಿಕಾರಿಗಳು ವೇಳೆಗೂ ಹಾಜರಾಗಬೇಕು”.

Advanced Morphology: Reduplication and Echo Words

Reduplication in Kannada creates expressive or distributive meanings by repeating a root or a syllable. Full reduplication (e.g., ಹೆಚ್ಚು‑ಹೆಚ್ಚು) intensifies the adjective, while partial reduplication (e.g., ಕುಡು‑ಕುಡು) often conveys a repetitive action. Echo words pair a base with a phonologically altered counterpart (e.g., ಬೆಳ್ಳಿ‑ಬೆಳ್ಳಿ → ಬೆಳ್ಳಿ‑ಬೆಳ್ಳಿ) to indicate a vague or approximate quality. In administrative writing, controlled use of these forms adds nuance without sacrificing clarity.

Example
ಅಧಿಕಾರಿ ವೃಂದವು ಸಮಯ‑ಸಮಯದಲ್ಲಿ ವರದಿಗಳನ್ನು ಸಲ್ಲಿಸುತ್ತದೆ।
The reduplicated ಸಮಯ‑ಸಮಯದಲ್ಲಿ means “from time to time” and distributes the action across intervals.
Tip
Prefer full reduplication for emphasis in formal reports; avoid echo pairs that may sound colloquial.

Pragmatic Evidentiality in Administrative Kannada

Evidential markers signal the source of information—direct observation, hearsay, inference, or documented proof. Kannada uses clitics such as ‑ಆಗಿದು (reported), ‑ಕಾಣುವಂತೆ (as seen), and ‑ಅಂದಾಜು (estimated). In official correspondence, selecting the appropriate marker clarifies responsibility and legal standing. For instance, a circular stating ಅಧಿಕೃತವಾಗಿ ದಾಖಲಿಸಲಾಗಿದೆ (officially recorded) carries higher authority than one prefixed with ಸೂಚನೆಗідно (as per indication). Mastery of these markers improves precision in policy drafts and audit replies.

Example
ಹೆಚ್ಚುವರಿ ಹಣಕಾಸು ವಿನಿಯೋಗವನು ಅಂದಾಜು‑ಅಂದಾಜು ಆಗಿದೆ।
ಅಂದಾಜು‑ಅಂದಾಜು marks the figure as an estimate, not a verified amount.
Tip
When drafting replies to RTI queries, attach the evidential clitic that matches the document’s provenance.

Advanced Morphology: Nominal Compounds in Official Kannada

Official Kannada frequently builds precise terminology by compounding two or more nouns. The resulting nominal compounds (ಸಮಾಸಗಳು) convey a single administrative concept, such as ಬಜೆಟ್‌ಅಂದಾಜು (budget estimate) or ಕರ್ತವ್ಯನಿರ್ವಹಣೆ (duty discharge). Understanding the internal structure — whether the compound is a tatpuruṣa (determinative), karmadhāraya (appositional), or dvandva (copulative) — helps the examinee decode unfamiliar terms without a dictionary.

Key points for analysis:

  • Identify the head noun (usually the final element).
  • Determine the semantic relation between the components (purpose, location, agency, etc.).
  • Check for sandhi changes at the junction (e.g., ಗ್ರಾಮ+ಪಂಚಾಯತ್ = ಗ್ರಾಮಪಂಚಾಯತ್).
Example: Decoding a Compound
ಪದ: ನಿರ್ವಾಹಣಾ‑ಅಧಿಕಾರಿ (administrative officer)
Head = ಅಧಿಕಾರಿ (officer). Modifier = ನಿರ್ವಾಹಣಾ (administration). Relation = tatpuruṣa (officer of administration). Sandhi: ನಿಱ್ವಾಹಣಾ + ಅಧಿಕಾರಿ → ನಿರ್ವಾಹಣಾಧಿಕಾರಿ.

Discourse Strategies for Policy Briefs

Policy briefs demand a tight logical flow: context → problem → evidence → recommendation → implementation note. Kannada official writing uses discourse markers such as ಆದರೆ (however), ಹಾಗಾಗಿ (therefore), and ಈ ಪ್ರಕಾರ (in this manner) to signal transitions. Mastery of these markers lets a candidate produce coherent paragraphs that satisfy the examiner’s expectation of clarity, brevity, and authority.

Typical structure:

  1. ಪೂರ್ವಭೂಮಿ (background) – one or two sentences.
  2. ಸಮಸ್ಯೆ (problem statement) – quantified if possible.
  3. ಪ್ರಮಾಣಗಳು (evidence) – data, reports, precedents.
  4. ಶಿಫಾರಸು (recommendation) – imperative mood.
  5. ಕ್ರಮವಿಧಿ (implementation steps) – numbered list.
Tip: Marker Placement
Place contrastive markers (ಆದರೆ, ಕिंतु) at the start of the sentence that introduces a limitation. Place consequential markers (ಹಾಗಾಗಿ, ಆದರಿಂದ) at the start of the recommendation sentence. This mirrors the logical hierarchy examiners reward.

Error Analysis: Syntactic Misalignment in Translations

When translating English administrative texts into Kannada, candidates often preserve English word order, producing syntactic misalignment. Common patterns include:

  • Retaining pre‑positional phrases before the verb (in the meeting discussed → ಸಭೆಯಲ್ಲಿ ಚರ್ಚಿಸಲಾಯಿತು instead of ಚರ್ಚಿಸಲಾದ ಸಭೆಯಲ್ಲಿ).
  • Using passive voice where Kannada prefers active with an explicit agent (the order was issued → ಆದೇಶವನ್ನು ಜಾರಿ ಮಾಡಲಾಯಿತು vs. ಅಧಿಕಾರಿ ಆದೇಶವನ್ನು ಜಾರಿ ಮಾಡಿದನು).
  • Misplacing adverbial clauses, leading to scope ambiguity.

Systematic revision — first restructure to Kannada SOV order, then insert appropriate case markers (‑ಗೆ, ‑ದ್ವಾರ, ‑ ಮೂಲಕ) — eliminates most errors.
