The rule. Paper A is one Indian language from the Eighth Schedule. It is 300 marks and three hours; you need 25% (75/300) to qualify, and the marks are not counted in the merit list. A candidate who fails Paper A is not ranked, however well the scored papers went.

Your Tamil course path

5 modules · do them in order

Paper A is five skills, not one. Work through these modules, then use the workbook and script checker below. Each module links to the section of this page that carries the material.

1. Script and decoding
Do not read Tamil through a transliteration. Work directly in Tamil (U+0B80–U+0BFF, left-to-right). Spend the first sessions learning the conjuncts and half-forms you meet most: a slow first read costs minutes in the exam, a fast second read is the skill that scores.
2. Comprehension method
Read the passage once for the argument, once for the detail. Before answering, write the claim in one line in Tamil. Answer in the language of the question paper, cite the passage rather than your opinion, and keep each answer to two or three sentences. Inference questions reward "the passage suggests", not outside knowledge.
3. Precis method
Compress to roughly one-third of the original. Keep the claim, the reasons and the tone; drop examples and repetition. Do not add a conclusion the author did not write. After drafting, check every sentence still belongs to the Tamil register of the original — a precis that drifts into essay style loses marks.
4. Translation drills
Translate the same English paragraph both ways. Meaning and register beat word order. Watch the traps listed under the script section — for Tamil they are not generic: Do not mix Malayalam letters into a Tamil answer. Check your Tamil output with the script checker before comparing meaning.
5. Essay structure
250 words in Tamil: one opening that states the position, two developed points with an example each, one closing. Plan in Tamil for five minutes before writing. The examiner reads organisation and language control first; a correct but shapeless answer scores less than a structured one.

Suggested pace for a December attempt: one module per week, workbook every other day, script check on every written answer.

Practice workbook

Original study prompts · not PYQs

These are original tasks, not previous-year questions. Write your answers in Tamil; use the script checker below before you ask for feedback.

1. Comprehension
Read a 300–400 word Tamil passage. Answer in Tamil:
  1. What is the main claim?
  2. What are the two strongest supporting reasons?
  3. What does the writer want the reader to do?
Then close the passage and write a three-sentence summary.
2. Precis
Write a precis of the same passage in about one-third of its length. Keep the claim, the reasons and the tone. Do not add your opinion. Check the script before you compare with the original.
3. Usage and vocabulary
Choose ten words from the passage that you would not use in everyday speech. Write one Tamil sentence for each and note the English meaning. Then write one sentence using a common idiom from the passage.
4. Translate English → Tamil
Translate the paragraph below into Tamil. Then translate your version back into English without looking at the original. Compare meaning, not word order.
Reading a newspaper carefully is a skill that improves with practice. A reader who notices the main claim, the evidence and the tone can summarize an article in a few sentences. This habit helps in the examination because it saves time and prevents answers from becoming a list of disconnected points. It also builds vocabulary, which is useful in the translation section. Ten minutes a day is enough if the reading is active.
5. Translate Tamil → English
Take the first paragraph of your passage. Translate it into English. Underline the phrases that had no direct equivalent. Rewrite those phrases in plain English.
6. Short essay
Write 250 words in Tamil on one of these topics: the value of public libraries; why local languages matter in a democracy; how a city can protect its trees. Use a beginning, two developed points and a close.

Advanced Sandhi and Morphology

Paper A expects candidates to recognise and apply the full range of Tamil sandhi (இணை) rules when reading, transcribing, or composing text. These rules govern vowel coalescence, consonant assimilation, and the insertion or deletion of euphonic increments (உருப்பு) at word boundaries. Mastery of the five primary sandhi types — குற்றியலுகரம், விலங்கியலுகரம், உருப்பு, இடைசந்தி, and இறைசந்தி — allows a candidate to reconstruct the underlying lexical forms from a printed passage and to produce grammatically flawless prose in the essay component.

In addition to sandhi, the paper tests derivational morphology: the formation of nouns, verbs, and adjectives through primary (தொடர்பு) and secondary (விகிதி) suffixes. Understanding how tense, aspect, mood, and voice markers attach to verb roots, and how case endings interact with the final phoneme of a stem, is essential for accurate translation and for spotting morphological errors in the comprehension passages.

Illustrative Sandhi Transformation
Original: புத்தகம் + உடன் → புத்தகமுடன்
Explanation: The final ம் of புத்தகம் assimilates to the initial உ of உடன், producing the euphonic increment உ (உருப்பு) and yielding புத்தகமுடன்.

Essay Structuring for Paper A

A high‑scoring essay follows a clear three‑part architecture: an introductory paragraph that states the thesis and outlines the main arguments, a body of three to four paragraphs each developing a single point with evidence drawn from Tamil literary, historical, or contemporary sources, and a concise conclusion that restates the thesis in light of the discussion. Each body paragraph should begin with a topic sentence, provide at least two concrete examples, and end with a linking sentence that anticipates the next argument.

Language precision is rewarded: use varied sentence structures, employ appropriate register (formal literary Tamil), and avoid colloquialisms. Pay attention to the correct use of honorifics (நீங்கள், அவர்கள்) when referencing persons, and ensure that all quotations are reproduced exactly, including sandhi‑adjusted forms.

Time‑Management Tip
Allocate 10 minutes for planning, 30 minutes for writing, and 5 minutes for revision. During revision, check sandhi consistency, case‑ending agreement, and the logical flow between paragraphs.

Advanced Syntax and Register Variation

Tamil exhibits a clear distinction between the literary register (செந்தமிழ்) and the spoken register (கொடுந்தமிழ்). In formal writing, especially for Paper A, candidates must control verb morphology, case endings, and honorific particles that differ across registers. For instance, the literary past tense uses the suffix -ந்த while the colloquial form often reduces it to -ன். Mastery of these variations enables precise translation of administrative orders, legal texts, and literary excerpts.

Another critical area is the use of sandhi rules at word boundaries. The literary register applies full vowel‑consonant assimilation (e.g., அவன் + உடன் = அவனுடன்), whereas spoken Tamil may retain a glottal stop or drop the final consonant. Recognising when to apply full sandhi versus a relaxed form is essential for both comprehension and composition tasks.

Example: Register Shift
Literary: அவർ வீட்டிற்கு சென்றார்கள்.
Colloquial: அவンガ வீட்டுக்குப் போயிருக்காங்க.
The literary sentence uses the honorific plural அவர் and the full past tense சென்றார்கள். The colloquial version replaces the pronoun with அவンガ, shortens the verb to போயிருக்காங்க, and applies a reduced case marker குப்.

Translation Strategies for Administrative Texts

Administrative Tamil relies heavily on fixed formulae such as அறிவிப்பு (notification), நிர்ணయం (decision), and அமலாக்கம் (implementation). When translating these into English, preserve the hierarchical tone by using passive constructions (“It is hereby notified that…”) and retaining the original clause order where possible. Avoid over‑interpretation; the goal is functional equivalence, not literary flair.

Compound nouns formed with the suffix -ம் (e.g., நிர்வாகம் – administration) often function as single lexical units. Treat them as terminology rather than decomposing them, unless the target language lacks a direct counterpart. Consistent glossaries across practice sessions reduce lexical drift and improve speed.

Tip
Create a personal bilingual glossary of the 50 most frequent administrative terms. Review it weekly and test yourself by translating short gazette extracts without a dictionary.

Lexical Semantics and Polysemy in Tamil

Tamil vocabulary exhibits extensive polysemy, where a single lexical item can convey multiple related meanings depending on syntactic context and register. Understanding these shifts is essential for precise translation of administrative texts, where a term like அधிகாரம் may denote “authority”, “department”, or “jurisdiction”. The semantic range is often constrained by collocational patterns: அதிகாரி collocates with நியமனம் (appointment) in service rules, while அதிகாரம் collocates with சட்டம் (act) in legislative drafting.

  • Identify the core sense using dictionary entries.
  • Trace sense extensions through corpus examples.
  • Apply the appropriate sense in target register.
Example: Polysemy of “நிர்வாகம்”
நிர்வாகம் என்பது நிர்வாக துறை, நிர்வாக செயல்பாடு, அல்லது நிர்வாக அமைப்பு என பல அர்த்தங்களை ஏற்றுக்கொள்கிறது.
In a Government Order, “நிர்வாகம்” refers to the administrative department; in a procedural manual, it denotes the administrative process.

Discourse Cohesion Devices in Official Tamil

Official Tamil prose relies on a limited set of cohesive devices to maintain clarity across long sentences. The most frequent are conjunctive particles (ஆனால், எனவே, அதனால்), referential pronouns (அது, இவை), and lexical repetition of key nouns. Unlike literary Tamil, which exploits poetic ellipsis, administrative writing prefers explicit anaphora to avoid ambiguity.

  1. Use ஆனால் to signal contrast between clauses.
  2. Employ எனவே for causal linkage.
  3. Repeat the head noun instead of substituting with a pronoun when the referent is distant.
Cohesion Tip
When drafting a circular, place the main directive in the first clause and follow with எனவே + consequence; this mirrors the logical flow expected by reviewers.

Pragmatic Use of Honorifics in Official Tamil

Official Tamil documents employ a layered honorific system that signals respect, hierarchy, and familiarity. The choice between உங்கள் (your‑polite), தங்கள் (your‑respectful), and உங்கள் (your‑intimate) depends on the rank of the addressee and the genre of the text.

In administrative orders, the second‑person plural உங்கள் is standard for addressing a department, while தங்கள் appears in circulars directed at senior officers. Mis‑selection can change the perceived authority of the order and may be flagged in the language paper.

Candidates should practice rewriting a single sentence in three honorific registers, noting the morphological changes in verb endings and pronoun forms. This drill builds sensitivity to register variation required for translation and essay tasks.

  • Identify the target addressee and document type.
  • Rewrite the sentence using each honorific pronoun.
  • Compare verb agreement and case marking across versions.
Example: Honorific Shift
அதிகாரி உங்கள் அறிக்கையை சமர்ப்பிக்க வேண்டும்.
அதிகாரி தங்கள் அறிக்கையை சமர்ப்பிக்க வேண்டும். / அதிகாரி உங்கள் அறிக்கையை சமர்ப்பிக்க வேண்டும். (intimate)

Corpus-Based Error Analysis for Mains

A corpus of past mains answer scripts reveals recurring error patterns such as incorrect sandhi application, misuse of case markers, and over‑use of Sanskritised vocabulary. By tagging each error type, learners can prioritize the most frequent problems.

The analysis proceeds in three steps: (1) extract all sentences containing a target construction, (2) annotate deviations from the normative grammar, (3) compute frequency ratios. Tools like a simple spreadsheet or a Python script can automate the counting.

Focus on the top five error categories; allocate revision time proportionally. This data‑driven approach reduces guesswork and aligns preparation with the examiner’s expectations.

  1. Collect a representative sample of answered scripts.
  2. Tag each sentence for sandhi, case, and lexical errors.
  3. Rank error types by frequency and plan targeted drills.
Tip
Maintain a personal error log; review it weekly to track reduction in high‑frequency mistakes.

Stylistic Variation in Official Tamil

Official Tamil documents employ a range of registers that differ from everyday spoken Tamil. The formal register uses honorific verb forms, passive constructions, and a higher proportion of Sanskrit‑derived vocabulary. Legal texts favour precise terminology, while public notices adopt a more directive tone with imperative mood. Understanding these shifts helps candidates recognise the intended authority and audience of a passage.

Key Registers

  • Administrative – impersonal, passive, extensive use of சட்டத்துறை vocabulary.
  • Public‑service – direct address, polite imperatives, frequent honorifics.
  • Judicial – highly technical, dense nominalisation, minimal colloquial forms.
Example: Register Shift
அறிவிப்பு: “அனைத்து விதிமுறைகளும் பூர்த்தியாக பின்பற்றப்பட வேண்டும்.”
The sentence uses the passive verb “பின்பற்றப்பட வேண்டும்” and the formal noun “விதிமுறைகள்”, marking an administrative register.

Syntactic Complexity in Administrative Drafts

Administrative Tamil often stacks multiple subordinate clauses before the main verb, creating long periodic sentences. Relative clauses introduced by “என்ற” or “எனப்படும்” embed definitions, while conditional clauses with “என்றால்” set out procedural steps. Candidates should practice breaking such sentences into constituent clauses to improve comprehension speed and translation accuracy.

Common Patterns

  1. Embedded definition: “நிர்வாகம், எனப்படும் அரசு துறை, …”
  2. Conditional procedure: “ஆவணம் சமர்ப்பிக்கப்பட்டால், செயல்பாடு தொடங்கப்படும்.”
  3. Concessive clause: “தவறுகள் இருந்தாலும், நடவடிக்கை எடுக்கப்படும்.”
Tip
When translating, first isolate the main verb; then render each subordinate clause in the order it appears, preserving logical flow.

முதன்மை தேர்வில் தமிழ் பேப்பர் ஏயில் மாணவர்கள் அடிக்கடி செய்யும் பிழைகள் நான்கு முக்கிய வகைகளில் வகைப்படுத்தப்படலாம்: வார்த்தை தேர்வு, வாக்கிய அமைப்பு, விரிவாக்கம் மற்றும் வினைமுறை sựரூபம். வார்த்தை தேர்வு பிழைகள் பொதுவாக பொருத்தமற்ற சொற்கள் அல்லது அர்த்தம் மாறும் சொற்கள் பயன்படுத்துவதால் ஏற்படுகின்றன. வாக்கிய அமைப்பு பிழைகள் துணை வாக்கியங்கள், இணை வாக்கியங்கள் மற்றும் தொடர்ச்சி வாக்கியங்கள் தவறாக இணைக்கப்படும் போது ஏற்படுகின்றன. விரிவாக்கம் பிழைகள் தேவையற்ற விவரங்கள் அல்லது முக்கிய தகவல்கள் தவிர்க்கப்படும் போது காணப்படுகின்றன. வினைமுறை sựரூபம் பிழைகள் காலம், வினைமுறை மற்றும் வச்சனம் தவறாக பயன்படுத்தப்படும்போது ஏற்படுகின்றன.

Strategic Revision Techniques for Tamil Paper A

தேர்வு முன்பு திறமையான திருத்தம் செய்ய மாணவர்கள் பின்வரும் நடைமுறைகளை अपनाலாம். முதலில், முந்தைய ஆண்டு வினாத்தாள்களை நேரடி நேரத்தில் தீர்க்க முயற்சி செய்யவும்; இது நேர மேலாண்மை மற்றும் வினாத்தாள் வடிவத்தை அறிய உதவும். இரண்டாவது, முக்கிய இலக்கண விதிகள் மற்றும் பொதுவான பிழைகள் பட்டியலாக்கி ஒரு குறிப்பு தயாரிக்கவும்; இது வேகமாக திருத்தம் செய்ய உதவும். மூன்றாவது, ஒவ்வொரு வார்த்தையும் அதன் அர்த்தம், பயன்பாடு மற்றும் சரியான வினைமுறை sựரூபத்துடன் இணைத்து ஒரு சொல் புத்தகம் உருவாக்கவும். நான்காவது, மாதிரி உத்தரங்களை வாசித்து அவற்றின் அமைப்பு, மொழி திறன் மற்றும் விவரங்களை பகுப்பாய்வு செய்யவும். இறுதியாக, நாள் ஒரே நேரத்தில் குறைந்தهなくとも ஒரு மணி நேரம் தமிழ் எழுத்து பயிற்சிக்கு ஒதுக்கவும்.
