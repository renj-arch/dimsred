Direction
The rule. Paper A is one Indian language from the Eighth Schedule. It is 300 marks and three hours; you need 25% (75/300) to qualify, and the marks are not counted in the merit list. A candidate who fails Paper A is not ranked, however well the scored papers went.

The Malayalam script

U+0D00–U+0D7F

Malayalam is written left to right in the Malayalam script. The script uses chillu letters and a large set of conjunct forms. Malayalam and Tamil are separate scripts; do not mix them in the same answer.

Direction and characters
Malayalam runs left to right. The page uses the Unicode range U+0D00–U+0D7F. Write the language in its own script, not in a Latin transliteration.
Traps in Malayalam
  • Use chillu letters where standard Malayalam requires them.
  • Do not mix Tamil letters into a Malayalam answer.
  • Preserve vowel signs and the correct conjunct form.

Your Malayalam course path

5 modules · do them in order

Paper A is five skills, not one. Work through these modules, then use the workbook and script checker below. Each module links to the section of this page that carries the material.

1. Script and decoding
Do not read Malayalam through a transliteration. Work directly in Malayalam (U+0D00–U+0D7F, left-to-right). Spend the first sessions learning the conjuncts and half-forms you meet most: a slow first read costs minutes in the exam, a fast second read is the skill that scores.
2. Comprehension method
Read the passage once for the argument, once for the detail. Before answering, write the claim in one line in Malayalam. Answer in the language of the question paper, cite the passage rather than your opinion, and keep each answer to two or three sentences. Inference questions reward "the passage suggests", not outside knowledge.
3. Precis method
Compress to roughly one-third of the original. Keep the claim, the reasons and the tone; drop examples and repetition. Do not add a conclusion the author did not write. After drafting, check every sentence still belongs to the Malayalam register of the original — a precis that drifts into essay style loses marks.
4. Translation drills
Translate the same English paragraph both ways. Meaning and register beat word order. Watch the traps listed under the script section — for Malayalam they are not generic: Use chillu letters where standard Malayalam requires them. Check your Malayalam output with the script checker before comparing meaning.
5. Essay structure
250 words in Malayalam: one opening that states the position, two developed points with an example each, one closing. Plan in Malayalam for five minutes before writing. The examiner reads organisation and language control first; a correct but shapeless answer scores less than a structured one.

Suggested pace for a December attempt: one module per week, workbook every other day, script check on every written answer.

Practice workbook

Original study prompts · not PYQs

These are original tasks, not previous-year questions. Write your answers in Malayalam; use the script checker below before you ask for feedback.

1. Comprehension
Read a 300–400 word Malayalam passage. Answer in Malayalam:
  1. What is the main claim?
  2. What are the two strongest supporting reasons?
  3. What does the writer want the reader to do?
Then close the passage and write a three-sentence summary.
2. Precis
Write a precis of the same passage in about one-third of its length. Keep the claim, the reasons and the tone. Do not add your opinion. Check the script before you compare with the original.
3. Usage and vocabulary
Choose ten words from the passage that you would not use in everyday speech. Write one Malayalam sentence for each and note the English meaning. Then write one sentence using a common idiom from the passage.
4. Translate English → Malayalam
Translate the paragraph below into Malayalam. Then translate your version back into English without looking at the original. Compare meaning, not word order.
Reading a newspaper carefully is a skill that improves with practice. A reader who notices the main claim, the evidence and the tone can summarize an article in a few sentences. This habit helps in the examination because it saves time and prevents answers from becoming a list of disconnected points. It also builds vocabulary, which is useful in the translation section. Ten minutes a day is enough if the reading is active.
5. Translate Malayalam → English
Take the first paragraph of your passage. Translate it into English. Underline the phrases that had no direct equivalent. Rewrite those phrases in plain English.
6. Short essay
Write 250 words in Malayalam on one of these topics: the value of public libraries; why local languages matter in a democracy; how a city can protect its trees. Use a beginning, two developed points and a close.

Advanced Grammar: Sandhi, Samasa, and Compound Formation

Malayalam morphology relies heavily on sandhi (euphonic combination) and samasa (compound formation). Mastery of these processes is essential for precise writing in Paper A.

Major Sandhi Types

  • Lopa sandhi – elision of a vowel when a word ending in a vowel meets a word beginning with a vowel. Example: പഴ + അം = പഴം.
  • Agama sandhi – insertion of a semivowel (യ, വ, ര, ല) to avoid hiatus. Example: മഹാ + ആര്യ = മഹാര്യ.
  • Adesa sandhi – substitution of one sound by another, often seen in Sanskrit loanwords. Example: സത് + കർമ്മ = സക്കർമ്മ.

Samasa Classification

  1. Dvandva – copulative compounds (e.g., പിതാ + മാതാ = പിതാമാതാക്കൾ).
  2. Tatpurusha – determinative compounds where the first member qualifies the second (e.g., രാജ + പുരുഷൻ = രാജപുരുഷൻ).
  3. Karmadharaya – appositional compounds (e.g., മഹാ + രാഷ്ട്രം = മഹാരാഷ്ട്രം).
  4. Bahuvrihi – exocentric compounds denoting a possessor (e.g., ചിറക് + ഉള്ള = ചിറകുള്ള).

Regular practice of sandhi tables and samasa identification sharpens both reading speed and writing accuracy.

Essay and Precis Writing Strategies for Paper A

Paper A rewards clear structure, logical flow, and precise vocabulary. An effective essay follows a three‑part framework: introduction, body, conclusion.

Introduction

State the thesis in one sentence. Use a hook – a relevant proverb, statistic, or brief anecdote – to engage the examiner.

Body Paragraphs

  • Each paragraph addresses a single supporting point.
  • Begin with a topic sentence, follow with evidence (data, quotation, example), and end with a linking sentence.
  • Maintain cohesion through transitional devices (അതേസമയം, കൂടാതെ, പരിണാമമായി).

Conclusion

Restate the thesis in fresh words, summarise key arguments, and close with a forward‑looking statement or call to action.

Precis Techniques

  1. Read the passage twice – once for gist, once for detail.
  2. Identify the central idea and supporting facts.
  3. Rewrite in one‑third the original length, preserving tone and order.
  4. Avoid personal opinion; use indirect speech where needed.
Quick Revision Tip
Create a personal “sandhi‑samasa cheat sheet” on a single A4 page. Review it daily for two weeks before the exam; the patterns will become automatic during writing.

Translation Techniques for Paper A

Effective translation in the Malayalam paper requires a balance between literal fidelity and natural expression. Begin by segmenting the source sentence into logical units — subject, verb, object, and modifiers — then map each unit to its Malayalam counterpart while respecting the target language’s SOV order. Pay special attention to case endings (വിഭക്തി) and postpositions, because a direct word‑for‑word rendering often produces awkward constructions. Practice back‑translation: render a Malayalam paragraph into English, then translate it back, comparing the result with the original to spot systematic shifts.

When handling idiomatic phrases, identify the underlying metaphor and search for an equivalent Malayalam idiom rather than translating component words. Keep a personal glossary of recurring official terminology (e.g., administrative, legal, scientific terms) and review it before each writing session. Consistent use of approved terminology improves coherence and scores higher on the language‑accuracy rubric.

Vocabulary Enrichment Strategies

Building a robust lexical reservoir is essential for both essay and precis tasks. Allocate daily time to read high‑quality Malayalam prose — editorials, literary criticism, and government reports — and underline unfamiliar words. Group new entries by semantic field (e.g., governance, environment, technology) and create flashcards that include the word, its morphological breakdown, and a contextual sentence. Regular spaced‑repetition review consolidates retention.

Supplement reading with active production: write a short paragraph each week using at least ten newly learned items. This forces retrieval and reveals collocational constraints. Over time, the active vocabulary will surface spontaneously during timed writing, reducing hesitation and lexical errors.
