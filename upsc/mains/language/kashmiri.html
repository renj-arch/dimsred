Direction
The rule. Paper A is one Indian language from the Eighth Schedule. It is 300 marks and three hours; you need 25% (75/300) to qualify, and the marks are not counted in the merit list. A candidate who fails Paper A is not ranked, however well the scored papers went.

What Paper A tests

300 marks · 3 hours
Comprehension
Read a passage and answer questions on its meaning, inference, tone and vocabulary.
Precis
Compress a passage to about one-third without losing its argument or tone.
Usage and vocabulary
Grammar, sentence construction, idioms and word choice in Kashmiri.
Short essays
Organised writing on a general topic, usually in a few hundred words.
Translation
English to Kashmiri and Kashmiri to English. Meaning and register matter more than word order.
No fixed section split
The notification does not publish a fixed mark split for each section. Prepare all five areas rather than optimising for a guessed weighting.

The Perso-Arabic script

U+0600–U+06FF

Kashmiri is written right to left in the Perso-Arabic script. It uses additional letters and diacritics that are not the same as Urdu or Arabic. The UPSC paper is printed in this script, so write Kashmiri forms rather than an Urdu transliteration.

Direction and characters
Perso-Arabic runs right to left. The page uses the Unicode range U+0600–U+06FF. Write the language in its own script, not in a Latin transliteration.
Traps in Kashmiri
  • Kashmiri is not Urdu. A Perso-Arabic passage can still be the wrong language.
  • Keep the text right-to-left; do not paste it into a left-to-right block without setting dir="rtl".
  • Use the Kashmiri vowel signs and letters, not the Arabic or Urdu defaults.

Practice workbook

Original study prompts · not PYQs

These are original tasks, not previous-year questions. Write your answers in Kashmiri; use the script checker below before you ask for feedback.

1. Comprehension
Read a 300–400 word Kashmiri passage. Answer in Kashmiri:
  1. What is the main claim?
  2. What are the two strongest supporting reasons?
  3. What does the writer want the reader to do?
Then close the passage and write a three-sentence summary.
2. Precis
Write a precis of the same passage in about one-third of its length. Keep the claim, the reasons and the tone. Do not add your opinion. Check the script before you compare with the original.
3. Usage and vocabulary
Choose ten words from the passage that you would not use in everyday speech. Write one Kashmiri sentence for each and note the English meaning. Then write one sentence using a common idiom from the passage.
4. Translate English → Kashmiri
Translate the paragraph below into Kashmiri. Then translate your version back into English without looking at the original. Compare meaning, not word order.
Reading a newspaper carefully is a skill that improves with practice. A reader who notices the main claim, the evidence and the tone can summarize an article in a few sentences. This habit helps in the examination because it saves time and prevents answers from becoming a list of disconnected points. It also builds vocabulary, which is useful in the translation section. Ten minutes a day is enough if the reading is active.
5. Translate Kashmiri → English
Take the first paragraph of your passage. Translate it into English. Underline the phrases that had no direct equivalent. Rewrite those phrases in plain English.
6. Short essay
Write 250 words in Kashmiri on one of these topics: the value of public libraries; why local languages matter in a democracy; how a city can protect its trees. Use a beginning, two developed points and a close.
