Direction
The rule. Paper A is one Indian language from the Eighth Schedule. It is 300 marks and three hours; you need 25% (75/300) to qualify, and the marks are not counted in the merit list. A candidate who fails Paper A is not ranked, however well the scored papers went.

The Bengali script

U+0980–U+09FF

Bengali is written left to right in the Bengali script. The script has independent vowels, consonant letters, vowel signs and conjunct forms. The paper tests standard written Bengali, not transliterated Bengali in Latin letters.

Direction and characters
Bengali runs left to right. The page uses the Unicode range U+0980–U+09FF. Write the language in its own script, not in a Latin transliteration.
Traps in Bengali
  • The vowel sign ে is written before the consonant but typed after it; do not reverse the order.
  • Use conjunct forms where standard Bengali requires them instead of writing each consonant separately.
  • Distinguish শ, ষ and স in spelling; they are not interchangeable in formal writing.

Your Bengali course path

5 modules · do them in order

Paper A is five skills, not one. Work through these modules, then use the workbook and script checker below. Each module links to the section of this page that carries the material.

1. Script and decoding
Do not read Bengali through a transliteration. Work directly in Bengali (U+0980–U+09FF, left-to-right). Spend the first sessions learning the conjuncts and half-forms you meet most: a slow first read costs minutes in the exam, a fast second read is the skill that scores.
2. Comprehension method
Read the passage once for the argument, once for the detail. Before answering, write the claim in one line in Bengali. Answer in the language of the question paper, cite the passage rather than your opinion, and keep each answer to two or three sentences. Inference questions reward "the passage suggests", not outside knowledge.
3. Precis method
Compress to roughly one-third of the original. Keep the claim, the reasons and the tone; drop examples and repetition. Do not add a conclusion the author did not write. After drafting, check every sentence still belongs to the Bengali register of the original — a precis that drifts into essay style loses marks.
4. Translation drills
Translate the same English paragraph both ways. Meaning and register beat word order. Watch the traps listed under the script section — for Bengali they are not generic: The vowel sign ে is written before the consonant but typed after it; do not reverse the order. Check your Bengali output with the script checker before comparing meaning.
5. Essay structure
250 words in Bengali: one opening that states the position, two developed points with an example each, one closing. Plan in Bengali for five minutes before writing. The examiner reads organisation and language control first; a correct but shapeless answer scores less than a structured one.

Suggested pace for a December attempt: one module per week, workbook every other day, script check on every written answer.

Practice workbook

Original study prompts · not PYQs

These are original tasks, not previous-year questions. Write your answers in Bengali; use the script checker below before you ask for feedback.

1. Comprehension
Read a 300–400 word Bengali passage. Answer in Bengali:
  1. What is the main claim?
  2. What are the two strongest supporting reasons?
  3. What does the writer want the reader to do?
Then close the passage and write a three-sentence summary.
2. Precis
Write a precis of the same passage in about one-third of its length. Keep the claim, the reasons and the tone. Do not add your opinion. Check the script before you compare with the original.
3. Usage and vocabulary
Choose ten words from the passage that you would not use in everyday speech. Write one Bengali sentence for each and note the English meaning. Then write one sentence using a common idiom from the passage.
4. Translate English → Bengali
Translate the paragraph below into Bengali. Then translate your version back into English without looking at the original. Compare meaning, not word order.
Reading a newspaper carefully is a skill that improves with practice. A reader who notices the main claim, the evidence and the tone can summarize an article in a few sentences. This habit helps in the examination because it saves time and prevents answers from becoming a list of disconnected points. It also builds vocabulary, which is useful in the translation section. Ten minutes a day is enough if the reading is active.
5. Translate Bengali → English
Take the first paragraph of your passage. Translate it into English. Underline the phrases that had no direct equivalent. Rewrite those phrases in plain English.
6. Short essay
Write 250 words in Bengali on one of these topics: the value of public libraries; why local languages matter in a democracy; how a city can protect its trees. Use a beginning, two developed points and a close.

Advanced Grammar Patterns

Mastering complex verb formations is essential for Paper A. Bengali frequently combines a primary verb with a secondary verb to express aspect, mood, or completion. The pattern verb‑stem + আ/ই + auxiliary creates compound verbs such as খেয়ে ফেলা (to eat up) or লিখে রাখা (to write down for later). Recognising these structures helps you parse long sentences in the comprehension passage and produce precise translations.

  • Identify the primary verb and its semantic contribution.
  • Note the auxiliary’s aspectual nuance (completion, continuity, benefactive).
  • Practice rewriting simple sentences into compound forms.
Example: Complex Compound Verbs
বইটি পড়ে ফেলা হয়েছে।
The verb “পড়ে ফেলা” combines “পড়া” (to read) and “ফেলা” (to throw away) to convey completion.

Essay Structuring Strategies

A well‑organised essay scores higher on coherence and argumentation. Begin with a clear thesis statement in the opening paragraph. Follow with three to four body paragraphs, each developing a distinct point supported by textual evidence or linguistic analysis. Conclude by restating the thesis in light of the discussed arguments and suggesting a broader implication.

  1. Draft a one‑sentence thesis before writing.
  2. Allocate one paragraph per supporting idea.
  3. Use linking devices (অতএব, তবে, অপরদিকে) to guide the reader.
Tip
Outline your essay in three parts: introduction, argument development, and conclusion. Keep each paragraph focused on a single idea.

Translation Techniques for Mains

Effective translation in the Bengali paper requires a balance between literal fidelity and idiomatic fluency. Begin by segmenting the source sentence into logical units — subject, verb, object, and modifiers — then map each unit to its Bengali counterpart while preserving the original tone. Pay special attention to honorifics, case endings, and verb aspects that have no direct English equivalent. Practice with parallel texts from official reports and literary excerpts to internalise common collocations.

  • Identify the grammatical mood before choosing the Bengali verb form.
  • Retain cultural references; translate them with explanatory glosses when necessary.
  • Use back‑translation as a self‑check: render your Bengali version back into English and compare.

Vocabulary Enrichment Strategies

Building a robust lexical reservoir is essential for both comprehension and essay writing. Focus on high‑frequency administrative terminology, synonyms for abstract concepts, and register‑appropriate phrasing. Create thematic word‑banks — governance, economics, environment — and review them through spaced repetition. Incorporate new words into daily writing drills to move them from passive recognition to active use.

Sample Vocabulary Drill

Answer Writing Practice Framework

A structured answer‑writing routine sharpens clarity, coherence, and time management. Start with a quick outline: thesis statement, three supporting points, and a concluding remark. Allocate fixed minutes for each section during mock tests. After writing, evaluate against the marking rubric — content relevance, language accuracy, and presentation. Iterate the cycle weekly to track progress.

Time‑Boxing Tip
Divide the 3‑hour paper into 30‑minute blocks per question. Reserve the final 15 minutes for review and minor corrections.

Advanced Syntax Nuances

Bengali sentences often embed multiple clauses without explicit conjunctions. Understanding the role of post‑positions, verb‑final order, and the subtle shift between simple and compound verbs is essential for precise answer writing. The language relies heavily on case endings (কারক) to indicate grammatical relations, and the correct use of the ergative marker ‑এ in perfective transitive constructions distinguishes formal prose from colloquial speech.

  • Identify the subject‑object‑verb (SOV) backbone before adding modifiers.
  • Watch for the optional এবং versus the more literary অথচ when linking clauses.
  • Use the causative suffix ‑আন only when the agent directly causes the action.
Example: Conditional Construction
যদি তুমি পড়ো, তবে তুমি उत्तীর্ণ হবে।
The clause যদি তুমি পড়ো sets a condition; তবে marks the consequence. Note the verb পড়ো (second‑person familiar) matches the subject তুমি.

Effective Summarisation Techniques

Condensing a 300‑word passage into a 75‑word précis demands three disciplined steps: (1) isolate the central thesis, (2) map supporting arguments in logical order, and (3) rewrite using nominalised verbs and compound nouns to preserve density. Avoid copying phrasing; instead, transform active clauses into participial phrases (e.g., লেখা হয়েছে → লিখিত). Practice with timed drills to internalise the rhythm of concise Bengali prose.

  1. Read the passage twice — first for gist, second for structure.
  2. Highlight keywords that carry the argumentative weight.
  3. Draft a skeleton outline in bullet form before writing the final summary.
Tip
When the original uses a series of coordinate clauses, replace them with a single compound noun phrase (e.g., শিক্ষা, স্বাস্থ্য ও পরিবেশ → শিক্ষা‑স্বাস্থ্য‑পরিবেশ) to save words without losing meaning.

Idiomatic Expressions in Formal Writing

Formal Bengali prose demands the replacement of everyday idioms with precise lexical equivalents. Colloquial constructions such as “কাজটা খুব সহজ” convey ease but lack the register required for Mains answers. Writers should identify the semantic core of each idiom and select a standard term that preserves meaning while elevating tone.

Key Principles

  • Map each idiom to a single formal counterpart.
  • Avoid literal translation; retain nuance.
  • Consult authoritative dictionaries for sanctioned usage.

Precision in Technical Terminology

Technical subjects — law, economics, science — require terminology that is unambiguous and recognized by examiners. Borrowing English terms without Bengali equivalents can obscure meaning. The preferred approach is to use the accepted Bengali term, and where none exists, provide a concise parenthetical gloss.

Guidelines

  1. Verify the term in the latest Bangla Academy publications.
  2. Maintain consistency throughout the answer.
  3. Include a brief definition on first occurrence.

Time‑Management Strategies for Language Paper

Effective allocation of the three‑hour window determines completeness. A practical split reserves 45 minutes for reading and planning, 90 minutes for drafting, and 45 minutes for revision and polishing. Practising timed mock papers trains the mind to respect these limits.

Practical Tips

  • Use a visible timer during practice.
  • Prioritise high‑mark sections first.
  • Leave a buffer for unexpected difficulty.
Example: Rewriting a Colloquial Sentence
Original: "সে কাজটা খুব সহজে করল।"
Formal: "তিনি উক্ত কাজটি অত্যন্ত সহজে সম্পন্ন করেছেন।"
Tip
Maintain a personal glossary of formal equivalents for common colloquial phrases; review it weekly.
