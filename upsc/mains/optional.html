Mains · Optional subject

Two papers. 500 marks. One choice.

The optional is the largest block you control. It is two papers of 250 marks each, taken on the same subject, and it can decide the rank. The subject you pick matters less than whether you can write two full papers on it from memory, month after month.

The rule. You choose one optional subject, and both papers are on it. Paper I and Paper II are 250 marks each; the syllabus for each is published by UPSC and is not the same as a university degree syllabus. The optional is not a qualifying paper: every mark counts for the merit list. Choose before you start writing, and do not switch subjects in the last three months.

How to choose

1 · Overlap with GS
A subject that shares ground with GS II or GS III reduces total load: Political Science, Public Administration, Sociology, Geography and Economics all overlap. But overlap is a dividend, not the reason to choose; you still have to master the optional's own syllabus.
2 · Material you can actually finish
Count the pages, not the prestige. Two papers need a syllabus you can revise three times. A subject with a short, stable syllabus and good past papers beats a fashionable one you cannot close.
3 · Writing, not reading
Take the subject you can write a 250-mark paper on without the book open. If you have never written a full optional paper under time, you do not yet know whether the choice works.
4 · Do not chase scoring folklore
Every subject has candidates who score 300+ and candidates who score 120. The distribution matters less than where you will land. Past-paper familiarity and honest self-assessment are better guides than a rumour about which subject "scales".
