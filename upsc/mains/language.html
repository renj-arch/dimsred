Mains · Qualifying papers

Two papers that must not end your attempt

Paper A and Paper B are 300 marks each, but they are qualifying: you need 25% in each, and the marks are not counted in the merit list. That makes them easy to ignore and expensive to fail. This page covers what they test and gives you a precis tool to keep the skill alive.

Papers
The rule. Paper A is one Indian language chosen from the Eighth Schedule. Paper B is English. Each is 300 marks and three hours; each must be passed at 25%. The marks do not add to your 1,750 written total or to the 2,025 merit total. A candidate who fails either paper is not ranked, however well the scored papers went.

What each paper tests

Paper A · Indian language
Comprehension of a passage; precis writing; usage and vocabulary; short essays; translation from English to the chosen language and from the language to English. The paper tests control of the language, not literary criticism.
300 marks3 hours
Paper B · English
Comprehension; precis; usage and vocabulary; short essays. The standard is a careful, formal English — not a literature paper and not a grammar quiz.
300 marks3 hours

Where marks are lost

Precis that is a summary
A precis keeps the argument and the tone in about one-third of the words. Cutting sentences is not compression. Practise finding the passage's main claim, then rebuild it in your own words.
Translation that is word-for-word
Translation is judged on meaning and register. A literal sentence that preserves word order but breaks the target language loses more than a freer sentence that reads naturally.
Essay with no structure
A short language essay still needs a beginning, two or three developed points and a close. The examiner is checking whether you can organise a page, not whether you have a novel thesis.
Leaving the paper early
Qualifying papers reward completion. Attempt every section, even briefly. An unanswered section is a guaranteed loss; a rough answer can still earn marks.

A four-week routine

Week 1 · Comprehension and precis
One passage a day. Read once for the claim, once for the structure. Write the precis without looking back at the passage, then compare.
Week 2 · Usage and vocabulary
Twenty minutes a day on sentence correction, idioms and word choice in the target language. Keep a single notebook of errors; do not collect lists you never revisit.
Week 3 · Translation
Translate one short editorial both ways, English to your language and back. Compare meaning, not word order. Note the phrases that had no direct equivalent.
Week 4 · Full paper
Sit one full Paper A or Paper B under three hours. The point is stamina and section management, not a score. Then fix the section that ran over time.
