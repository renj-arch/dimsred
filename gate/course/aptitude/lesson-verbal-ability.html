Verbal Ability

Grammar, vocabulary, sentence correction, comprehension, para jumbles.
Grammar · Vocabulary · Sentence Correction · Comprehension

Learning Objectives

  • Understand the fundamental concepts of Verbal Ability
  • Apply key formulas and techniques to solve problems
  • Practice with exam-level questions to build speed and accuracy

Key Concepts

What is Verbal Ability?

Verbal Ability is a key component of the GATE General Aptitude section (15% of total marks — approximately 5 questions). It tests your command of the English language including grammar, vocabulary, sentence correction, reading comprehension, and para jumbles. Strong verbal ability is essential for all engineering branches.

Key Concepts

1. Grammar Basics: Parts of speech (noun, verb, adjective, adverb, pronoun, preposition, conjunction, interjection). Tenses — present, past, future (simple, continuous, perfect, perfect continuous). Subject-verb agreement — singular subject takes singular verb, plural subject takes plural verb. Articles (a, an, the) — 'a' before consonant sound, 'an' before vowel sound, 'the' for specific references.

2. Vocabulary: Synonyms (words with similar meanings) and Antonyms (opposite meanings). One-word substitutions (e.g., philatelist = stamp collector, ambidextrous = able to use both hands). Idioms and phrases (e.g., 'bite the bullet' = face a difficult situation, 'piece of cake' = very easy). Commonly confused words (affect/effect, complement/compliment, principal/principle).

3. Sentence Correction: Common errors include subject-verb disagreement, incorrect tense usage, wrong prepositions, misplaced modifiers, parallelism errors, and incorrect word choice. Read the full sentence, identify the error type, and check which option fixes it without changing the intended meaning.

4. Reading Comprehension: For GATE, RC passages are typically shorter (200-300 words) and focus on science, technology, or general topics. Questions test main idea, inference, vocabulary in context, and author's purpose.

Solved Examples

Example 1 (Sentence Correction)
Identify the error: 'The committee have decided to postpone the meeting.'

a) No error b) Replace 'have' with 'has' c) Replace 'decided' with 'decides' d) Replace 'the meeting' with 'meeting'
Solution: b) Replace 'have' with 'has'. 'Committee' is a collective noun acting as a single unit, so it takes a singular verb 'has'. The correct sentence: 'The committee has decided to postpone the meeting.'
Example 2 (Vocabulary — One Word Substitution)
A person who looks at the bright side of things is called:

a) Optimist b) Pessimist c) Realist d) Idealist
Solution: a) Optimist. An optimist always expects positive outcomes. A pessimist expects the worst. A realist sees things as they are. An idealist pursues perfect standards.
Example 3 (Reading Comprehension)
Passage: 'The concept of sustainable development emerged from growing awareness of the environmental costs of unchecked economic growth. It emphasises meeting present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs. This requires balancing economic, social, and environmental considerations in policy-making.'

What is the central idea of sustainable development according to the passage?

a) Economic growth must stop to protect the environment b) Meeting present needs while protecting future generations' ability to meet theirs c) Focusing only on environmental protection d) Maximising economic growth at all costs
Solution: b) Meeting present needs while protecting future generations' ability to meet theirs. The passage directly defines sustainable development as balancing present and future needs. Option a is extreme. Option c is too narrow. Option d contradicts the passage.

Shortcut Techniques

  • For sentence correction, read the sentence and first try to 'hear' what sounds wrong — your ear for grammar is often correct.
  • In GATE, vocabulary questions are usually direct — eliminate obviously wrong options first (words with opposite or unrelated meanings).
  • For para jumbles, look for logical connectors (however, therefore, firstly, in conclusion) to identify the first and last sentences.
  • Read RC passages actively — underline the main idea in each paragraph. GATE RC questions are typically literal, not inferential.
  • For idioms, memorise the 20-30 most common ones — GATE tends to repeat commonly tested idioms.
Pro Tip
GATE verbal ability is about accuracy, not speed — you have approximately 3 minutes per question in the aptitude section. Read each question carefully. For sentence correction, ensure your chosen option maintains the original meaning while fixing only the grammatical error.
