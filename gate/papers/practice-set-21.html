The natural frequency of a spring-mass system is ω_n. If the mass is increased to 4 times its original value and the spring stiffness is doubled, the ratio of the new natural frequency to the original natural frequency is:
A. 0.5
B. 1.0
C. 0.707
D. 1.414
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut: ω_n ∝ √(k/m). New ratio = √((2k)/(4m) ÷ (k/m)) = √(2/4) = 1/√2 ≈ 0.707. Full solution: Original ω_n = √(k/m). New ω'_n = √(2k/(4m)) = √(k/(2m)) = (1/√2)√(k/m) = ω_n/√2. Hence ω'_n/ω_n = 1/√2 ≈ 0.707.

Q14. Core Subjectmodel-generated · 2026-11-25
An ideal Brayton cycle operates with a pressure ratio of 8 and specific heat ratio γ = 1.4. If the pressure ratio is increased to 16 while γ remains unchanged, the percentage point increase in thermal efficiency is approximately:
A. 5%
B. 10%
C. 15%
D. 20%
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: For Brayton cycle, η = 1 - (1/r_p)^((γ-1)/γ). With γ=1.4, exponent = 0.2857. η₁ = 1 - 8^{-0.2857} ≈ 0.448; η₂ = 1 - 16^{-0.2857} ≈ 0.547. Increase ≈ 0.099 ≈ 10%. Full steps: Compute exponent (γ-1)/γ = 0.4/1.4 = 2/7. η₁ = 1 - (1/8)^{2/7} = 1 - 0.552 = 0.448. η₂ = 1 - (1/16)^{2/7} = 1 - 0.453 = 0.547. Difference = 0.099 → 9.9% ≈ 10%.
Q15. Core Subjectmodel-generated · 2026-11-23
Steam enters an adiabatic turbine at 10 MPa and 500°C with an isentropic enthalpy drop of 1200 kJ/kg. If the isentropic efficiency of the turbine is 85%, the actual work output per kg of steam (in kJ/kg) is approximately:
A. 900
B. 1020
C. 1100
D. 1200
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: For a turbine, Isentropic Efficiency (η) = Actual Work / Isentropic Work. Actual Work = η × Isentropic Work = 0.85 × 1200 = 1020 kJ/kg. Full Solution: Isentropic efficiency is defined as η_t = (h₁ - h₂_actual) / (h₁ - h₂s). Given (h₁ - h₂s) = 1200 kJ/kg and η_t = 0.85. Rearranging, Actual Work = h₁ - h₂_actual = η_t × (h₁ - h₂s) = 0.85 × 1200 = 1020 kJ/kg.
