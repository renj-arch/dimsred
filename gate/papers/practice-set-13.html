Q2. Core Subject
A 3×3 real matrix has eigenvalues 2, 3, and 5. What is the determinant of the inverse of this matrix?
A. 1/30
B. 30
C. 1/10
D. 10
Correct Answer: A
Shortcut: Determinant of a matrix equals the product of its eigenvalues (2×3×5=30). Determinant of the inverse is the reciprocal of the determinant (1/30). Full Solution: Let λ₁, λ₂, λ₃ be the eigenvalues. det(A) = λ₁λ₂λ₃ = 30. Eigenvalues of A⁻¹ are 1/λ₁, 1/λ₂, 1/λ₃. det(A⁻¹) = (1/λ₁)(1/λ₂)(1/λ₃) = 1/(λ₁λ₂λ₃) = 1/30.
Q3. Core Subject
A 5‑digit number is formed using the digits 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 without repetition. What is the probability that the number is divisible by 5?
A. 1/5
B. 1/4
C. 1/2
D. 2/5
Correct Answer: A
Shortcut: For divisibility by 5, the last digit must be 0 or 5. Since only 5 is available, the last place is fixed as 5. The remaining 4 positions can be filled in 4! = 24 ways. Total possible numbers = 5! = 120. Probability = 24/120 = 1/5. Full solution: Count favourable outcomes (24) and total outcomes (120), simplify to 1/5.
Q4. Core Subject
In a turning operation using a carbide tool, the tool life is 60 minutes at a cutting speed of 60 m/min. If the Taylor's tool life exponent (n) is 0.5, what is the cutting speed (in m/min) required to achieve a tool life of 15 minutes?
A. 120
B. 90
C. 100
D. 80
Correct Answer: A
Shortcut: Taylor's equation VT^n = C. Tool life decreases from 60 to 15 min (factor of 1/4). Since n=0.5, speed must increase by factor (4)^0.5 = 2. New speed = 60 × 2 = 120 m/min. Step-by-step: V1×T1^n = V2×T2^n → 60 × (60)^0.5 = V2 × (15)^0.5 → V2 = 60 × √(60/15) = 60 × √4 = 60 × 2 = 120 m/min.
Q5. Core Subject
In an epicyclic gear train, the sun gear has 20 teeth, the planet gear has 30 teeth, and the ring gear has 80 teeth. The sun gear is the input rotating at 1000 rpm clockwise. The ring gear is fixed. What is the speed of the carrier (arm) in rpm?
A. 250
B. 300
C. 350
D. 200
Correct Answer: D
Shortcut: For Sun input, Ring fixed, Arm output: N_arm/N_sun = T_sun/(T_sun + T_ring) = 20/(20+80) = 1/5. N_arm = 1000/5 = 200 rpm. Step-by-step (Tabular Method): Arm fixed, Sun +1 rev → Ring = -T_sun/T_ring = -20/80 = -1/4 rev. Add 'x' to all. Ring fixed: x - 1/4 = 0 → x = 1/4. Sun speed = x + 1 = 5/4 units = 1000 rpm. Arm speed = x = 1/4 units = (1/4)/(5/4) × 1000 = 200 rpm.
Q6. General Aptitude
Two pipes A and B can fill a tank in 12 hours and 15 hours respectively. A third pipe C can empty the full tank in 20 hours. If all three pipes are opened simultaneously when the tank is empty, in how many hours will the tank be filled?
A. 8 hours
B. 10 hours
C. 12 hours
D. 15 hours
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut (LCM Method): Take LCM of 12, 15, 20 = 60 units (tank capacity). Efficiency of A = 60/12 = 5 units/hr; B = 60/15 = 4 units/hr; C = -60/20 = -3 units/hr. Net efficiency = 5 + 4 - 3 = 6 units/hr. Time = 60/6 = 10 hours. Full Solution: Part filled by A in 1 hr = 1/12. Part filled by B in 1 hr = 1/15. Part emptied by C in 1 hr = 1/20. Net part filled in 1 hr = 1/12 + 1/15 - 1/20 = (5+4-3)/60 = 6/60 = 1/10. Time required = 1 / (1/10) = 10 hours.
Q7. General Aptitude
What is the unit digit of (13^44 + 15^27)?
A. 0
B. 2
C. 4
D. 6
Correct Answer: D
Shortcut: The unit digit of any power of 15 is always 5. For 13^44, the unit digit follows the cycle of 3: 3, 9, 7, 1. Since 44 mod 4 = 0, the unit digit is 1. Adding the unit digits: 1 + 5 = 6. Full solution: 15^27 ends with 5. 13^44 = (13^4)^11, and 13^4 ends with 1, so 13^44 ends with 1. Sum of unit digits = 1 + 5 = 6.
Q8. Core Subject
A simply supported beam of span 6 m carries a uniformly distributed load of 10 kN/m. The maximum bending moment (in kNm) in the beam is:
A. 45
B. 60
C. 75
D. 90
Correct Answer: A
Shortcut: For a simply supported beam with UDL, maximum bending moment = wL²/8 = 10×6²/8 = 45 kNm. Full solution: The reactions at each support are wL/2 = 30 kN. The bending moment at distance x from left support is M = 30x - 10x²/2. Setting dM/dx = 0 gives x = 3 m (midspan). Substituting x = 3 gives Mmax = 30×3 - 5×9 = 90 - 45 = 45 kNm.

Q10. Core Subject
The characteristic equation of a closed-loop control system is s³ + 4s² + 5s + K = 0. The range of K for the system to be stable is:
A. 0 < K < 20
B. 0 < K < 10
C. K > 20
D. K < 0
Correct Answer: A
Shortcut: For a 3rd-order characteristic equation s³ + a₁s² + a₂s + a₃ = 0, Routh-Hurwitz stability requires all coefficients > 0 and a₁a₂ > a₃. Here a₁=4, a₂=5, a₃=K → 4×5 > K ⇒ K < 20, and K > 0. Thus 0 < K < 20. Full solution: Construct Routh array: s³: 1 5 s²: 4 K s¹: (20−K)/4 0 s⁰: K For stability, all first-column elements must be positive: 1 > 0, 4 > 0, (20−K)/4 > 0 ⇒ K < 20, and K > 0. Hence 0 < K < 20.
Q11. Core Subject
One kg of air (ideal gas, c_p = 1.005 kJ/kg·K, R = 0.287 kJ/kg·K) undergoes a reversible process from State 1 (300 K, 100 kPa) to State 2 (600 K, 200 kPa). The change in specific entropy (in kJ/kg·K) is approximately:
A. 0.35
B. 0.50
C. 0.69
D. 0.72
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: For an ideal gas, Δs = c_p ln(T_2/T_1) - R ln(P_2/P_1). Here T_2/T_1 = 2 and P_2/P_1 = 2. So Δs = (c_p - R) ln 2 = c_v ln 2. c_v = 1.005 - 0.287 = 0.718 kJ/kg·K. Δs = 0.718 × 0.693 ≈ 0.50 kJ/kg·K. Full Solution: The entropy change for an ideal gas is given by s_2 - s_1 = c_p ln(T_2/T_1) - R ln(P_2/P_1). Substituting the given values: T_2/T_1 = 600/300 = 2, P_2/P_1 = 200/100 = 2. Thus, Δs = 1.005 ln(2) - 0.287 ln(2) = (1.005 - 0.287) ln(2) = 0.718 × 0.6931 = 0.4976 ≈ 0.50 kJ/kg·K.
Q12. Core Subject
A thin cylindrical pressure vessel with closed ends is subjected to internal pressure p. The ratio of the circumferential (hoop) stress to the longitudinal (axial) stress developed in the vessel wall is:
A. 0.5
B. 1.0
C. 4.0
D. 2.0
Correct Answer: D
Shortcut: For a thin cylinder, Hoop Stress σ_h = pd/2t and Longitudinal Stress σ_l = pd/4t. The ratio σ_h / σ_l = (pd/2t) / (pd/4t) = 2. This is a standard result: Hoop stress is always twice the longitudinal stress. Full Solution: Consider a thin cylinder of internal diameter d and wall thickness t under internal pressure p. Circumferential stress is found by balancing force on a longitudinal section: σ_h × 2t × L = p × d × L ⇒ σ_h = pd/2t. Longitudinal stress is found by balancing force on a transverse section: σ_l × πdt = p × πd^2/4 ⇒ σ_l = pd/4t. Ratio = σ_h / σ_l = (pd/2t) / (pd/4t) = 2.
Q13. Core Subject
A company has an annual demand of 10,000 units, ordering cost of Rs. 100/order, and holding cost of Rs. 2/unit/year. If the annual demand doubles and the ordering cost reduces to half, what is the ratio of the new Economic Order Quantity (EOQ) to the original EOQ?
A. 0.707
B. 1.0
C. 1.414
D. 2.0
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: EOQ ∝ √(D×K). New Demand D' = 2D, New Ordering Cost K' = 0.5K. Product D'K' = (2D)×(0.5K) = DK. Since product unchanged, EOQ remains same. Ratio = 1. Step-by-step: EOQ = √(2DK/h). New EOQ = √(2 × 2D × 0.5K / h) = √(2DK/h) = Original EOQ. Ratio = 1.

Q15. Core Subject
A ball bearing operates at a radial load of 5 kN and has a rated life (L10) of 10,000 hours. If the load is increased to 10 kN, what is the new rated life (in hours)? Assume load-life exponent p = 3 for ball bearings.
A. 5000
B. 2500
C. 1250
D. 625
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut: Bearing life L ∝ (1/P)^p. Load doubles (×2), so life reduces by 2^3 = 8 times. New life = 10,000 / 8 = 1,250 hours. Step-by-step: L10 = (C/P)^p. L2/L1 = (P1/P2)^p = (5/10)^3 = (1/2)^3 = 1/8. L2 = 10,000 × (1/8) = 1,250 hours.
