Q1. Core Subject
A cantilever beam of length L, flexural rigidity EI, carries a point load P at its free end. The maximum deflection at the free end is:
A. PL³/(2EI)
B. PL³/(4EI)
C. PL³/(3EI)
D. PL³/(6EI)
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut: The tip deflection of a cantilever with end load P is a standard formula δ = PL³/(3EI). Full solution: Integrating the moment M(x)=−P(L−x) twice and applying boundary conditions y(0)=0, y'(0)=0 yields y(L)=PL³/(3EI).

Q3. Core Subject
A fair die is rolled twice. What is the probability that the sum of the outcomes is 7?
A. 1/6
B. 1/12
C. 1/9
D. 1/8
Correct Answer: A
Shortcut: There are 6 favorable pairs (1,6),(2,5),(3,4),(4,3),(5,2),(6,1) out of 36 equally likely outcomes, giving 1/6. Full solution: Total outcomes = 6×6 = 36. Favorable outcomes for sum 7 = 6. Probability = 6/36 = 1/6.
Q4. Core Subject
Water flows through a horizontal pipe of varying cross-section. At point 1, pressure is 200 kPa and velocity is 2 m/s. At point 2, velocity is 4 m/s. Assuming incompressible, inviscid flow, the pressure at point 2 is:
A. 206 kPa
B. 194 kPa
C. 200 kPa
D. 188 kPa
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: The dynamic pressure drop = ½ρ(v2²−v1²) = 0.5×1000×(16−4) = 6000 Pa = 6 kPa, so p2 = 200−6 = 194 kPa. Full solution: By Bernoulli, p1+½ρv1² = p2+½ρv2² → p2 = 200 kPa + 0.5×1000×(2²−4²) = 200 kPa − 6 kPa = 194 kPa.
Q5. Core Subject
The rank of the matrix [[1, 2, 3], [2, 4, 6], [3, 6, 9]] is:
A. 0
B. 1
C. 2
D. 3
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: Observe that Row 2 = 2 × Row 1 and Row 3 = 3 × Row 1, so only one linearly independent row exists; hence Rank = 1 immediately. Full: Perform row operations R2→R2−2R1 and R3→R3−3R1 to obtain two zero rows; the number of non-zero rows in the row echelon form is 1, confirming rank = 1.
Q6. Core Subject
A steam pipe with an outer radius of 2 mm is to be insulated with a material of thermal conductivity 0.05 W/m·K. The convective heat transfer coefficient at the outer surface is 10 W/m²·K. The minimum thickness of insulation required to reduce heat loss compared to the bare pipe is:
A. 3 mm
B. 5 mm
C. 8 mm
D. 10 mm
Correct Answer: A
Shortcut: Critical radius rc = k/h = 0.05/10 = 5 mm. Since pipe radius (2 mm) < rc, insulation must reach rc. Minimum thickness = 5 - 2 = 3 mm. Full Solution: For a cylinder, critical radius of insulation is rc = k/h = 0.05/10 = 0.005 m = 5 mm. The bare pipe radius is 2 mm. As r_pipe < rc, adding insulation initially increases heat loss until the outer radius equals rc. To ensure heat loss is less than the bare pipe, the outer radius of insulation must be at least rc. Thus, minimum thickness = rc - r_pipe = 5 - 2 = 3 mm.

Q9. Core Subject
The resonant frequency of a series RLC circuit with L = 1 mH and C = 1 μF is approximately:
A. 1.59 kHz
B. 5.03 kHz
C. 15.9 kHz
D. 50.3 kHz
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: f = 1/(2π√(LC)). √(LC) = √(10⁻³ × 10⁻⁶) = √10⁻⁹ = 3.16×10⁻⁵. 2π ≈ 6.28. Denominator ≈ 6.28 × 3.16×10⁻⁵ ≈ 2×10⁻⁴. f ≈ 1/(2×10⁻⁴) = 5000 Hz = 5 kHz. Option B (5.03 kHz) is closest. Full solution: Resonant frequency f₀ = 1/(2π√(LC)). Given L = 1 mH = 10⁻³ H, C = 1 μF = 10⁻⁶ F. LC = 10⁻⁹. √(LC) = 10⁻⁴.⁵ = 3.162×10⁻⁵ s. 2π√(LC) = 6.283 × 3.162×10⁻⁵ = 1.987×10⁻⁴ s. f₀ = 1/(1.987×10⁻⁴) = 5033 Hz ≈ 5.03 kHz.
Q10. Core Subject
An ideal air-standard Diesel cycle has a compression ratio of 16 and a cut-off ratio of 2. If the ratio of specific heats γ = 1.4, the thermal efficiency (in %) is approximately:
A. 55
B. 58
C. 61
D. 65
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut: Use η = 1 − (1/γ) × (r_c^γ − 1) / [r^(γ−1)(r_c − 1)]. Approximate 16^0.4 ≈ 3.03 and 2^1.4 ≈ 2.64; η ≈ 1 − (1/1.4) × (1.64)/(3.03) ≈ 61.4%, closest to 61%. Full: Substitute r=16, r_c=2, γ=1.4 into formula: η = 1 − (1/1.4) × (2^1.4 − 1) / [16^0.4 × (2−1)] = 1 − (1/1.4) × 1.639 / 3.031 = 0.614 ≈ 61%.
Q11. Core Subject
A solid circular shaft of diameter 50 mm transmits 50 kW power at 600 rpm. The maximum shear stress induced in the shaft (in MPa) is approximately:
A. 68.8
B. 17.2
C. 34.4
D. 137.6
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut: Torque T = P/ω = 50000/(2π×10) ≈ 795.8 N·m. τ_max = 16T/(πd³) = 16×795.8/(π×0.05³) ≈ 34.4 MPa. Full Solution: Angular velocity ω = 2πN/60 = 20π rad/s. Torque T = P/ω = 50000/(20π) = 2500/π N·m. Polar section modulus Z_p = πd³/16 = π(0.05)³/16. τ_max = T/Z_p = (2500/π) / (π×1.25×10⁻⁴/16) = 40000/(π²×1.25×10⁻⁴) ≈ 34.4×10⁶ Pa = 34.4 MPa.
Q12. Core Subject
A column of length L and flexural rigidity EI is fixed at one end and free at the other. The critical buckling load (Euler's crippling load) is:
A. π²EI/4L²
B. π²EI/L²
C. 4π²EI/L²
D. 2π²EI/L²
Correct Answer: A
Shortcut: Memorize effective length L_e for end conditions: Fixed-Free → L_e = 2L. Substitute into P_cr = π²EI/L_e² = π²EI/(2L)² = π²EI/4L². Option elimination: π²EI/L² is for Hinged-Hinged; 4π²EI/L² is for Fixed-Fixed. Full: The boundary conditions for a fixed-free column yield an effective length factor K=2. Using Euler's formula P_cr = π²EI/(KL)² gives π²EI/4L².
Q13. General Aptitude
A shopkeeper marks up the price of an article by 40% and then offers a discount of 20% on the marked price. What is his profit percentage?
A. 8%
B. 12%
C. 15%
D. 20%
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: For successive percentage changes of a% and b%, net change = a + b + ab/100. Here a = +40% (markup), b = -20% (discount). Net = 40 - 20 + (40 × -20)/100 = 20 - 8 = 12% profit. Step-by-step: Assume CP = ₹100. Marked Price = 100 + 40% of 100 = ₹140. Selling Price after 20% discount = 140 - 20% of 140 = 140 - 28 = ₹112. Profit = 112 - 100 = ₹12. Profit% = (12/100) × 100 = 12%.
Q14. General Aptitude
A shopkeeper sells an article at a loss of 10%. If he had sold it for ₹50 more, he would have gained 10%. What is the cost price of the article?
A. ₹200
B. ₹250
C. ₹300
D. ₹350
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: The difference between a 10% loss and a 10% gain is 20% of the cost price. This 20% equals ₹50, so cost price = 50 / 0.20 = ₹250. Full solution: Let CP = x. SP at 10% loss = 0.9x. SP at 10% gain = 1.1x. The difference is 1.1x - 0.9x = 0.2x = 50, giving x = 250.
Q15. Core Subject
The number of edges in a complete graph with 10 vertices is:
A. 45
B. 55
C. 90
D. 100
Correct Answer: A
Shortcut: Use formula n(n-1)/2. For n=10, 10×9/2=45. Unit-digit trick: 10×9=90 (ends in 0), half of number ending in 0 ends in 0 or 5. Only option A (45) ends in 5. Full solution: A complete graph K_n has exactly n(n-1)/2 edges because each of the n vertices connects to (n-1) others, and each edge is counted twice. Substituting n=10 gives 10×9/2 = 45 edges.
