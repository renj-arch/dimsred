Donaldson-Thomas Invariants — Sheaf Counting, the MNOP Conjecture & Wall-Crossing

Donaldson-Thomas (DT) theory is the virtual counting of coherent sheaves on Calabi-Yau threefolds. For a smooth projective Calabi-Yau threefold X (K_X = 0), the DT invariants count ideal sheaves of 1-dimensional subschemes (curves with possible embedded points). The moduli space I_n(X, β) of ideal sheaves I_Z with χ(O_Z) = n and [Z] = β carries a virtual fundamental class via the Behrend's obstruction theory, and the DT invariant is the integral of 1 over this virtual class — equivalently, the weighted Euler characteristic with the Behrend function ν. The MNOP conjecture (Maulik-Nekrasov-Okounkov-Pandharipande, 2003) states that the DT partition function equals the Gromov-Witten partition function after the change of variable q = −e^{iu}, giving the GW/DT correspondence. Pandharipande-Thomas (PT) stable pairs provide an alternative compactification via pairs (F, s) with F a pure 1-dimensional sheaf. The wall-crossing formulas of Kontsevich-Soibelman and Joyce-Song describe how DT invariants transform under variations of stability conditions, using the quantum dilogarithm and the Hall algebra. The Cohomological Hall algebra (CoHA) categorifies DT invariants by giving the cohomology of moduli spaces an algebra structure.

Part 1

📏 Moduli of Sheaves and Ideal Sheaves

Sheaf Counting
Stable Sheaves, Ideal Sheaves, and the DT Invariant
Let X be a smooth projective Calabi-Yau threefold (K_X = 0). For a fixed Chern character (r, c₁, c₂, c₃), consider the moduli space M of sheaves that are Gieseker stable (or µ-stable) with respect to a fixed polarization. Of central interest is the Hilbert scheme of curves: I_n(X, β) = {Z ⊂ X : dim Z ≤ 1, [Z] = β, χ(O_Z) = n}, parameterizing 1-dimensional subschemes Z (possibly with embedded points). The ideal sheaf I_Z fits into the short exact sequence 0 → I_Z → O_X → O_Z → 0. The Donaldson-Thomas invariant is defined as DT_n,β = ∫_{[I_n(X,β)]^{vir}} 1, where the virtual fundamental class [I_n(X,β)]^{vir} is constructed via the Behrend's obstruction theory — a perfect obstruction theory E^• → L_{I_n(X,β)} with E^• = RHom_{π}(𝔼, 𝔼)_0[−1] using the universal sheaf 𝔼. Behrend's key insight: the DT invariant equals the weighted Euler characteristic e(I_n(X,β), ν) where ν is the Behrend function, a constructible function that locally expresses the multiplicity of the virtual fundamental class. The generating function: Z_DT(q, v) = Σ_{n ∈ ℤ, β ∈ H₂(X, ℤ)} DT_n,β qⁿ v^β.
DT invariants virtually count ideal sheaves of curves in Calabi-Yau threefolds; the Behrend obstruction theory provides the virtual fundamental class, and the Behrend function gives an alternative weighted Euler characteristic formulation.
Part 2

🌍 The MNOP Conjecture and the GW/DT Correspondence

Correspondence
Gromov-Witten Invariants, the MNOP Conjecture, and Gopakumar-Vafa Integrality
Gromov-Witten invariants count stable maps f: C → X from a nodal curve C of genus g to X with f_*[C] = β. The GW invariant GW_{g,β} is defined by integration over the virtual fundamental class of the moduli stack of stable maps. The GW generating function is Z_GW(u, v) = exp(Σ_{g ≥ 0, β ≠ 0} GW_{g,β} u^{2g−2} v^β). The MNOP conjecture (Maulik-Nekrasov-Okounkov-Pandharipande, 2003) states: Z_DT(q, v) = Z_GW(u, v) under the change of variable q = −e^{iu}. This provides a deep link between enumerative geometry of curves (GW) and sheaf counting (DT). The conjecture was proved by Maulik-Oblomkov-Okounkov-Pandharipande in the toric case using localization to fixed points of a torus action. A corollary: the Gopakumar-Vafa invariants n_{g,β} (BPS state counts from M-theory) are integers, and the GW invariants decompose as GW_{g,β} = Σ_{g' ≤ g} n_{g',β} · (something genus-dependent) — the integrality follows from the DT side via the MNOP conjecture. The degree 0 MNOP gives DT_0(q) = Σ_{n ≥ 0} χ(X^{[n]}) qⁿ = M(q)^{χ(X)} where M(q) = ∏_{k ≥ 1} (1 − qᵏ)^{-k} is the MacMahon function (the generating function of 3D partitions).
The MNOP conjecture equates the DT and GW partition functions via q = −e^{iu}, linking curve counting to sheaf counting and proving integrality of Gopakumar-Vafa BPS invariants.
Part 3

🧮 Pandharipande-Thomas Stable Pairs

Stable Pairs
PT Invariants, the DT/PT Correspondence, and the Topological Vertex
A Pandharipande-Thomas stable pair (F, s) consists of a pure 1-dimensional coherent sheaf F on X and a section s: O_X → F whose cokernel is 0-dimensional. The moduli space P_n(X, β) of stable pairs with χ(F) = n and [F] = β (where [F] is the support class) is a proper projective scheme. The PT invariant is PT_n,β = ∫_{[P_n(X,β)]^{vir}} 1, with generating function Z_PT(q, v) = Σ_{n,β} PT_n,β qⁿ v^β. The DT/PT correspondence: the DT generating function decomposes as Z_DT(q, v) = Z_PT(q, v) · Z_{DT}^{degree 0}(q), where Z_{DT}^{degree 0}(q) = Σ_{n ≥ 0} χ(X^{[n]}) qⁿ counts ideal sheaves of points (Hilbert scheme of points). This reflects the fact that ideal sheaves of curves can have extra 0-dimensional "embedded points." The PT theory has a simpler virtual cycle due to the "stability" condition eliminating the degree 0 factor from the DT side. The topological vertex of Iqbal-Kozçaz-Vafa, reinterpreted by Li-Liu-Liu-Zhou, computes PT invariants for toric Calabi-Yau threefolds via a combinatorial algorithm using sums over 3D partitions (plane partitions) weighted by Macdonald functions. The PT invariants are more directly related to the BPS state counts of M-theory via the Gopakumar-Vafa formula.
PT stable pairs provide an alternative compactification for curve counting with a cleaner virtual cycle; the DT/PT correspondence splits DT invariants into a PT part and a degree-0 point-counting part.
Part 4

🔥 Wall-Crossing and BPS Invariants

Wall-Crossing
Bridgeland Stability, the KS Formula, Joyce-Song, and the CoHA
DT invariants depend on stability conditions. As one varies the Bridgeland stability condition on the derived category D^b(Coh(X)), the moduli of stable sheaves changes only when crossing walls — codimension-1 loci in the stability manifold where a strictly semistable object appears. The Kontsevich-Soibelman wall-crossing formula (KS formula): when crossing a wall with central charge ratio α, the generating function transforms by multiplying by ∏_{γ with Ω(γ)} E(q^{⟨γ,·⟩}; q)^{Ω(γ)}, where E is the quantum dilogarithm E(x; q) = ∏_{k ≥ 0} (1 + q^{k+½} x)^{-1} and Ω(γ) are the BPS invariants (generalized DT invariants). The formula takes place in the quantum torus ℂ⟨e_γ⟩ / (e_γ e_δ = q^{⟨γ,δ⟩} e_{γ+δ}). The Joyce-Song formula gives an alternative wall-crossing framework using the Hall algebra of the abelian category of coherent sheaves, defining generalized DT invariants via the Joyce's "logarithm" of the stack function. The Cohomological Hall algebra (CoHA) of Kontsevich-Soibelman gives the cohomology of moduli spaces of sheaves an associative algebra structure via the "wall-crossing" of the moduli stack. The categorification of DT invariants means lifting numerical invariants to objects in the derived category of the moduli space — the CoHA provides a rich algebraic structure whose decategorification recovers the DT invariants. The quiver DT invariants (for a quiver Q with relations) arise as the moduli of representations and provide the toy model for the full threefold DT theory — the Kac's conjecture (proved by Hausel-Letellier-Rodriguez-Villegas using DT theory) relates the number of absolutely indecomposable representations to the roots of the Kac-Moody algebra associated to Q.
Wall-crossing formulas (KS and Joyce-Song) govern how DT invariants change with stability; the CoHA categorifies DT theory, and quiver DT invariants provide the prototype for the full theory.
