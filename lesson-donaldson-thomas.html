Donaldson-Thomas Invariants — Sheaf Counting, the MNOP Conjecture & Wall-Crossing

Donaldson-Thomas (DT) theory is the virtual counting of coherent sheaves on Calabi-Yau threefolds. For a smooth projective Calabi-Yau threefold X (K_X = 0), the DT invariants count ideal sheaves of 1-dimensional subschemes (curves with possible embedded points). The moduli space I_n(X, β) of ideal sheaves I_Z with χ(O_Z) = n and [Z] = β carries a virtual fundamental class via the Behrend's obstruction theory, and the DT invariant is the integral of 1 over this virtual class — equivalently, the weighted Euler characteristic with the Behrend function ν. The MNOP conjecture (Maulik-Nekrasov-Okounkov-Pandharipande, 2003) states that the DT partition function equals the Gromov-Witten partition function after the change of variable q = −e^{iu}, giving the GW/DT correspondence. Pandharipande-Thomas (PT) stable pairs provide an alternative compactification via pairs (F, s) with F a pure 1-dimensional sheaf. The wall-crossing formulas of Kontsevich-Soibelman and Joyce-Song describe how DT invariants transform under variations of stability conditions, using the quantum dilogarithm and the Hall algebra. The Cohomological Hall algebra (CoHA) categorifies DT invariants by giving the cohomology of moduli spaces an algebra structure.

Part 1

📏 Moduli of Sheaves and Ideal Sheaves

Sheaf Counting
Stable Sheaves, Ideal Sheaves, and the DT Invariant
Let X be a smooth projective Calabi-Yau threefold (K_X = 0). For a fixed Chern character (r, c₁, c₂, c₃), consider the moduli space M of sheaves that are Gieseker stable (or µ-stable) with respect to a fixed polarization. Of central interest is the Hilbert scheme of curves: I_n(X, β) = {Z ⊂ X : dim Z ≤ 1, [Z] = β, χ(O_Z) = n}, parameterizing 1-dimensional subschemes Z (possibly with embedded points). The ideal sheaf I_Z fits into the short exact sequence 0 → I_Z → O_X → O_Z → 0. The Donaldson-Thomas invariant is defined as DT_n,β = ∫_{[I_n(X,β)]^{vir}} 1, where the virtual fundamental class [I_n(X,β)]^{vir} is constructed via the Behrend's obstruction theory — a perfect obstruction theory E^• → L_{I_n(X,β)} with E^• = RHom_{π}(𝔼, 𝔼)_0[−1] using the universal sheaf 𝔼. Behrend's key insight: the DT invariant equals the weighted Euler characteristic e(I_n(X,β), ν) where ν is the Behrend function, a constructible function that locally expresses the multiplicity of the virtual fundamental class. The generating function: Z_DT(q, v) = Σ_{n ∈ ℤ, β ∈ H₂(X, ℤ)} DT_n,β qⁿ v^β.
DT invariants virtually count ideal sheaves of curves in Calabi-Yau threefolds; the Behrend obstruction theory provides the virtual fundamental class, and the Behrend function gives an alternative weighted Euler characteristic formulation.
Part 2

🌍 The MNOP Conjecture and the GW/DT Correspondence

Correspondence
Gromov-Witten Invariants, the MNOP Conjecture, and Gopakumar-Vafa Integrality
Gromov-Witten invariants count stable maps f: C → X from a nodal curve C of genus g to X with f_*[C] = β. The GW invariant GW_{g,β} is defined by integration over the virtual fundamental class of the moduli stack of stable maps. The GW generating function is Z_GW(u, v) = exp(Σ_{g ≥ 0, β ≠ 0} GW_{g,β} u^{2g−2} v^β). The MNOP conjecture (Maulik-Nekrasov-Okounkov-Pandharipande, 2003) states: Z_DT(q, v) = Z_GW(u, v) under the change of variable q = −e^{iu}. This provides a deep link between enumerative geometry of curves (GW) and sheaf counting (DT). The conjecture was proved by Maulik-Oblomkov-Okounkov-Pandharipande in the toric case using localization to fixed points of a torus action. A corollary: the Gopakumar-Vafa invariants n_{g,β} (BPS state counts from M-theory) are integers, and the GW invariants decompose as GW_{g,β} = Σ_{g' ≤ g} n_{g',β} · (something genus-dependent) — the integrality follows from the DT side via the MNOP conjecture. The degree 0 MNOP gives DT_0(q) = Σ_{n ≥ 0} χ(X^{[n]}) qⁿ = M(q)^{χ(X)} where M(q) = ∏_{k ≥ 1} (1 − qᵏ)^{-k} is the MacMahon function (the generating function of 3D partitions).
The MNOP conjecture equates the DT and GW partition functions via q = −e^{iu}, linking curve counting to sheaf counting and proving integrality of Gopakumar-Vafa BPS invariants.
Part 3

🧮 Pandharipande-Thomas Stable Pairs

Stable Pairs
PT Invariants, the DT/PT Correspondence, and the Topological Vertex
A Pandharipande-Thomas stable pair (F, s) consists of a pure 1-dimensional coherent sheaf F on X and a section s: O_X → F whose cokernel is 0-dimensional. The moduli space P_n(X, β) of stable pairs with χ(F) = n and [F] = β (where [F] is the support class) is a proper projective scheme. The PT invariant is PT_n,β = ∫_{[P_n(X,β)]^{vir}} 1, with generating function Z_PT(q, v) = Σ_{n,β} PT_n,β qⁿ v^β. The DT/PT correspondence: the DT generating function decomposes as Z_DT(q, v) = Z_PT(q, v) · Z_{DT}^{degree 0}(q), where Z_{DT}^{degree 0}(q) = Σ_{n ≥ 0} χ(X^{[n]}) qⁿ counts ideal sheaves of points (Hilbert scheme of points). This reflects the fact that ideal sheaves of curves can have extra 0-dimensional "embedded points." The PT theory has a simpler virtual cycle due to the "stability" condition eliminating the degree 0 factor from the DT side. The topological vertex of Iqbal-Kozçaz-Vafa, reinterpreted by Li-Liu-Liu-Zhou, computes PT invariants for toric Calabi-Yau threefolds via a combinatorial algorithm using sums over 3D partitions (plane partitions) weighted by Macdonald functions. The PT invariants are more directly related to the BPS state counts of M-theory via the Gopakumar-Vafa formula.
PT stable pairs provide an alternative compactification for curve counting with a cleaner virtual cycle; the DT/PT correspondence splits DT invariants into a PT part and a degree-0 point-counting part.
Part 4

🔥 Wall-Crossing and BPS Invariants

Wall-Crossing
Bridgeland Stability, the KS Formula, Joyce-Song, and the CoHA
DT invariants depend on stability conditions. As one varies the Bridgeland stability condition on the derived category D^b(Coh(X)), the moduli of stable sheaves changes only when crossing walls — codimension-1 loci in the stability manifold where a strictly semistable object appears. The Kontsevich-Soibelman wall-crossing formula (KS formula): when crossing a wall with central charge ratio α, the generating function transforms by multiplying by ∏_{γ with Ω(γ)} E(q^{⟨γ,·⟩}; q)^{Ω(γ)}, where E is the quantum dilogarithm E(x; q) = ∏_{k ≥ 0} (1 + q^{k+½} x)^{-1} and Ω(γ) are the BPS invariants (generalized DT invariants). The formula takes place in the quantum torus ℂ⟨e_γ⟩ / (e_γ e_δ = q^{⟨γ,δ⟩} e_{γ+δ}). The Joyce-Song formula gives an alternative wall-crossing framework using the Hall algebra of the abelian category of coherent sheaves, defining generalized DT invariants via the Joyce's "logarithm" of the stack function. The Cohomological Hall algebra (CoHA) of Kontsevich-Soibelman gives the cohomology of moduli spaces of sheaves an associative algebra structure via the "wall-crossing" of the moduli stack. The categorification of DT invariants means lifting numerical invariants to objects in the derived category of the moduli space — the CoHA provides a rich algebraic structure whose decategorification recovers the DT invariants. The quiver DT invariants (for a quiver Q with relations) arise as the moduli of representations and provide the toy model for the full threefold DT theory — the Kac's conjecture (proved by Hausel-Letellier-Rodriguez-Villegas using DT theory) relates the number of absolutely indecomposable representations to the roots of the Kac-Moody algebra associated to Q.
Wall-crossing formulas (KS and Joyce-Song) govern how DT invariants change with stability; the CoHA categorifies DT theory, and quiver DT invariants provide the prototype for the full theory.

Virtual Fundamental Class and Obstruction Theory

The virtual fundamental class is the cornerstone of Donaldson–Thomas theory on a Calabi–Yau threefold X. Because the moduli space of ideal sheaves or stable pairs is typically singular and of the wrong dimension, one constructs a perfect obstruction theory in the sense of Behrend–Fantechi. The obstruction theory is a two‑term complex of vector bundles E⁻¹ → E⁰ on the moduli stack whose cohomology sheaves are the tangent and obstruction spaces. For the Hilbert scheme of curves, the tangent space at an ideal sheaf I is Ext¹(I, I)₀ and the obstruction space is Ext²(I, I)₀, where the subscript 0 denotes trace‑free part. The Calabi–Yau condition gives a Serre duality isomorphism Ext²(I, I)₀ ≅ Ext¹(I, I)₀∨, making the obstruction theory symmetric. The virtual dimension is then χ(I, I)₀ = 0, so the virtual fundamental class lives in degree zero and the DT invariant is an integer obtained by integrating 1 against this class. The construction uses the intrinsic normal cone and the Behrend function ν, which assigns to each point a weighted Euler characteristic; the DT invariant equals the weighted Euler characteristic of the moduli space. This approach works for any proper moduli space with a symmetric obstruction theory, including Pandharipande–Thomas stable pairs and the moduli of sheaves with fixed Chern character.

In practice, computing the virtual class requires a global presentation of the obstruction theory. For toric Calabi–Yau threefolds, the torus action localizes the virtual class to fixed points, which are described by combinatorial data such as plane partitions. The localization formula expresses the DT invariant as a sum over fixed points of contributions involving the equivariant Euler class of the virtual tangent bundle. This reduces the problem to a combinatorial sum that can be evaluated by the topological vertex. For non‑toric geometries, one often deforms to a toric situation or uses wall‑crossing to relate the invariant to a known chamber. The obstruction theory also underlies the definition of the reduced DT invariants for local curves, where one removes a trivial factor coming from the motion of the curve in the threefold. The reduced theory yields invariants that satisfy the same wall‑crossing formulas but with a simpler generating function.

Generating Functions and the Topological Vertex

The generating functions of DT and PT invariants are formal series in variables q and v^β that encode the entire tower of invariants for all curve classes β and Euler characteristics n. The DT partition function Z_DT(q, v) = Σn,β DTn,β q^n v^β and the PT partition function Z_PT(q, v) = Σn,β PTn,β q^n v^β are related by the DT/PT correspondence Z_DT = Z_PT · Z_DTdeg 0, where the degree‑zero factor is the MacMahon function M(q)χ(X). For toric Calabi–Yau threefolds, the topological vertex provides a closed combinatorial formula for Z_PT. The vertex assigns to each trivalent vertex of the toric diagram a sum over three‑dimensional partitions λ, μ, ν weighted by products of Schur functions and the quantum parameter q. The full partition function is obtained by gluing vertices along edges, summing over partitions on internal edges with a framing factor (−q)|λ|/2 and a weight v^β determined by the Kähler parameters. This algorithm computes the PT invariants for toric threefolds and, via the DT/PT correspondence, the DT invariants as well. The topological vertex also makes manifest the integrality of the Gopakumar–Vafa BPS invariants ng,β, because the vertex expansion can be reorganized into the Gopakumar–Vafa form Σg,β ng,β (2 sin(u/2))2g−2 with q = −eiu. The vertex formalism has been extended to open invariants, where branes are inserted along legs of the toric diagram, and to refined invariants that depend on two parameters q, t, capturing the motivic refinement of DT theory.

Worked Example: Topological Vertex for the Resolved Conifold
Compute the PT partition function for the resolved conifold using the topological vertex. The toric diagram has two vertices connected by a single internal edge. Assign partitions λ, μ to the two external legs of the first vertex and ν, ρ to the second. Write the vertex sum and perform the gluing sum over the internal partition.
The topological vertex for a single vertex is Cλ μ ν(q) = q(κ(λ)+κ(μ)+κ(ν))/2 Ση sλ^t/η(q-ρ) sμ/η(q-ρ) sν(q-ρ) where κ(λ)=Σ_i λ_i(λ_i−2i+1) and q-ρ = (q1/2, q3/2, …). For the resolved conifold, the two vertices are glued along an internal edge with partition α. The gluing sum is Σα (−Q)|α| C∅ ∅ α(q) C∅ ∅ α^t(q) where Q = v^β is the Kähler parameter. Using the identity C∅ ∅ α(q) = qκ(α)/2 sα^t(q-ρ) and the Cauchy identity Σα sα^t(x) sα(y) = ∏i,j (1 + x_i y_j), the sum evaluates to ∏k≥1 (1 − Q q^k)−k. This is precisely the MacMahon function M(Q q) with a shift, matching the known PT partition function for the conifold. The DT partition function follows by multiplying with the degree‑zero factor M(q)χ(X).
Exam Strategy
When a question asks for the topological vertex computation, first identify the toric diagram and the number of vertices. Write the vertex function for each vertex, then set up the gluing sum over internal partitions. Remember the framing factor (−Q)|α| and the transpose on one side of the edge. Use the Cauchy identity to evaluate the sum; the final answer is usually an infinite product of the form ∏ (1 − Q q^k)±k. Practice the conifold and local P² cases, as they appear frequently.

Quiver Representations and the Cohomological Hall Algebra

Quiver DT invariants provide a combinatorial laboratory where the full machinery of wall‑crossing and the Cohomological Hall Algebra (CoHA) can be tested. A quiver Q consists of vertices and arrows; a representation assigns a vector space to each vertex and a linear map to each arrow. The moduli space of representations with fixed dimension vector d is a quotient of the representation space by the product of general linear groups. When Q arises from a Calabi–Yau threefold via a tilting bundle, the DT invariants of the threefold are conjecturally equal to the quiver DT invariants with a suitable stability condition. The CoHA, introduced by Kontsevich and Soibelman, is the cohomology of the moduli stack of representations equipped with a convolution product defined by the correspondence stack of short exact sequences. This product is associative and graded by the dimension vector. The CoHA carries a natural action of the quantum torus algebra, and its Drinfeld double encodes the wall‑crossing formula. The categorification of DT invariants means lifting the numerical invariant Ω(γ) to the cohomology of the moduli space; the CoHA provides the algebraic structure whose graded dimension recovers the DT generating series. For the Jordan quiver (one vertex, one loop), the CoHA is isomorphic to the algebra of symmetric functions, and its Hilbert series is the MacMahon function. For the Kronecker quiver (two vertices, two arrows), the CoHA is related to the elliptic Hall algebra. The Kac conjecture, proved by Hausel–Letellier–Rodriguez‑Villegas using the CoHA, states that the number of absolutely indecomposable representations over a finite field is given by a polynomial in the field size whose coefficients are the multiplicities of the roots of the associated Kac–Moody algebra. This result connects the arithmetic of quiver varieties to the representation theory of infinite‑dimensional Lie algebras.

Bridgeland Stability Conditions and the Space of Stability

Bridgeland stability conditions on the derived category D^b(Coh(X)) form a complex manifold Stab(X) of dimension equal to the rank of the numerical Grothendieck group. A stability condition consists of a central charge Z: K(X) → ℂ, a group homomorphism, and a slicing P(φ) of semistable objects of phase φ, satisfying the Harder–Narasimhan property and the support property. For a Calabi–Yau threefold, the central charge is often taken as Z(E) = −∫_X e−(B+iω) ch(E) √{td(X)} where B+iω is a complexified Kähler class. Varying (B, ω) moves the stability condition in Stab(X). Walls are real codimension‑one loci where a strictly semistable object appears; crossing a wall changes the set of stable objects and hence the DT invariants. The Kontsevich–Soibelman wall‑crossing formula describes the transformation of the DT generating function as a product of quantum dilogarithms E(x; q) = ∏k≥0 (1 + qk+1/2 x)−1 over the charges γ of objects that become stable or unstable. The formula lives in the quantum torus algebra with commutation e_γ e_δ = q⟨γ,δ⟩ eγ+δ, where ⟨·,·⟩ is the Euler form. The Joyce–Song approach uses the Hall algebra of the abelian category of coherent sheaves and defines generalized DT invariants via a logarithmic map on the stack function algebra. Both frameworks produce the same wall‑crossing transformations and have been verified in many examples, including local curves and quiver representations. The space of stability conditions also admits a natural action of the autoequivalence group of D^b(Coh(X)), which can be used to relate different chambers and to establish the DT/PT correspondence by moving from the large volume chamber (where ideal sheaves are stable) to the non‑commutative chamber (where stable pairs are stable).

Applications to String Theory and Mirror Symmetry

Donaldson–Thomas invariants count BPS states in type IIA string theory compactified on a Calabi–Yau threefold X. The DT partition function computes the indexed degeneracies of D‑brane bound states, which are protected by supersymmetry and depend only on the topological class of the brane. The MNOP conjecture identifies the DT partition function with the topological string partition function of the A‑model on X, after the change of variables q = −eiu. This is a precise mathematical formulation of the OSV conjecture relating black hole entropy to topological strings. Mirror symmetry exchanges the A‑model on X with the B‑model on the mirror threefold Y. On the B‑model side, the topological string partition function is computed by the holomorphic anomaly equations of Bershadsky–Cecotti–Ooguri–Vafa, which determine the higher genus amplitudes recursively from the genus‑zero prepotential. The DT side provides a non‑perturbative definition of the topological string that is invariant under mirror symmetry. The wall‑crossing formula for DT invariants corresponds to the crossing of walls of marginal stability in the moduli space of the B‑model, where BPS states decay. The Cohomological Hall Algebra is expected to be isomorphic to the algebra of BPS operators in the four‑dimensional N=2 theory obtained by compactifying M‑theory on X. The refined DT invariants, depending on two parameters q, t, capture the spin content of BPS states and are related to the Nekrasov partition function of the five‑dimensional gauge theory engineered by the same geometry. These connections make DT theory a central tool for testing string dualities and for computing exact non‑perturbative quantities in quantum gravity.

Refined Donaldson-Thomas Invariants and Motivic Measures

The original DT invariants are integer-valued counts of ideal sheaves weighted by the Behrend function. The refined theory, developed by Iqbal-Kozcaz-Vafa and mathematically formulated by Kontsevich-Soibelman and Davison-Meinhardt, replaces the integer count with a polynomial in two variables q and t (or y and q) encoding the Hodge structure of the vanishing cohomology of the moduli stack. For a Calabi-Yau threefold X, the refined DT invariant DTref(γ; q, t) is the Poincaré polynomial of the cohomology of the perverse sheaf of vanishing cycles φTr W(ℚ) on the moduli stack M(γ) of objects of charge γ in Db(Coh(X)), where W is the holomorphic Chern-Simons functional. The variables track weight and Hodge filtrations: q corresponds to cohomological degree, t to weight grading. At t = q1/2 one recovers the unrefined DT invariant; at t = 1 one obtains the Euler characteristic of the moduli space. The motivic DT invariant lives in the Grothendieck ring of varieties K0(Varℂ)[𝕃−1/2] where 𝕃 = [𝔸1] is the Lefschetz motive. Kontsevich and Soibelman showed that motivic DT invariants satisfy a wall-crossing formula in the quantum torus algebra over this ring, with the quantum dilogarithm replaced by its motivic avatar E(x) = ∏k≥0 (1 − 𝕃k+1/2x)−1. For the resolved conifold, the refined DT partition function matches the refined topological vertex of Iqbal-Kozcaz-Vafa, a two-parameter function computing refined open and closed topological string amplitudes. The refined BPS invariants Ωref(γ; q, t) are extracted from the refined DT invariants by a plethystic logarithm and satisfy the refined wall-crossing formula of Kontsevich-Soibelman. In 5d N = 1 gauge theories on S1 × ℝ4, refined DT invariants of the local Calabi-Yau geometry engineering the theory compute the Nekrasov partition function with Ω-background parameters ε1, ε2 identified as q = eε1, t = e−ε2. The refined theory also admits a categorification: the moduli stack cohomology carries a CoHA action, and refined invariants are the graded dimensions of the resulting representations.

The Behrend Function and Microlocal Geometry of the Moduli Stack

The Behrend function νM : M → ℤ is a constructible function on a scheme M of finite type over ℂ that computes the virtual fundamental class via the identity [M]vir = ∑n∈ℤ n [νM−1(n)] in the Chow group A*(M). For a moduli space of sheaves M on a Calabi-Yau threefold, Behrend proved that the DT invariant equals the weighted Euler characteristic DT(γ) = χ(M, νM) = ∑n n χ(νM−1(n)). The function is defined microlocally: for x ∈ M, choose an embedding M U into a smooth variety, let CM/U be the normal cone and Λ = TM*U the conormal bundle. The Behrend function at x is νM(x) = (−1)dim U (1 − χ(Fx)), where F is the vanishing cycle complex φf(ℚU) for a local defining function f of M in U. Equivalently, νM(x) = (−1)dim M χ(EuM(x)) where EuM is the local Euler obstruction of MacPherson. For a symmetric obstruction theory (which exists on Calabi-Yau threefolds by Serre duality), the virtual fundamental class is the zero locus of a section of the obstruction bundle, and the Behrend function measures the local singularity type of this zero locus. Joyce and Song generalized this to the stack setting: for an Artin stack 𝔐 with a symmetric obstruction theory, the Behrend function is defined on the coarse moduli space and the weighted Euler characteristic computes the generalized DT invariants. The microlocal interpretation connects to the characteristic cycle of the perverse sheaf of vanishing cycles: CC(φfℚU) = ∑n n [Λn] where Λn are the irreducible components of the singular support. The Behrend function is then the microlocal multiplicity along the zero section. This geometric perspective is essential for proving wall-crossing formulas via the motivic Milnor fiber and for understanding the integration map from the Hall algebra to the quantum torus. In the case of quiver representations with potential, the Behrend function on the moduli stack of representations can be computed combinatorially using the critical locus of the trace of the potential, leading to explicit formulas for DT invariants of local Calabi-Yau geometries.

Worked Computation: Behrend Function for the Conifold
Compute the Behrend function at the origin for the moduli space of representations of the conifold quiver with dimension vector (1,1) and potential W = Tr(A1B1A2B2 − A1B2A2B1). The moduli space is the critical locus of Tr W in the representation space Rep(Q, (1,1)) ≅ ℂ4.
The representation space has coordinates (a1, a2, b1, b2) ∈ ℂ4. The potential is W = a1b1a2b2 − a1b2a2b1 = 0 identically for 1×1 matrices since multiplication commutes. Thus the critical locus is all of ℂ4, which is smooth of dimension 4. The moduli stack is [ℂ4/ℂ*] where ℂ* acts by scaling (ai, bi) → (λai, λ−1bi). The coarse moduli space is the conifold singularity xy − zw = 0 in ℂ4. At the origin, the Behrend function for a hypersurface singularity f = 0 in ℂn is ν(0) = (−1)n−1(1 − χ(Milnor fiber)). For the conifold, n = 4, the Milnor fiber is homotopy equivalent to S3, so χ = 0. Thus ν(0) = (−1)3(1 − 0) = −1. For smooth points, ν = 1. The DT invariant for charge (1,1) is the weighted Euler characteristic of the moduli space, which equals the Euler characteristic of the resolved conifold (the crepant resolution) by the McKay correspondence, giving DT = 1. This matches the topological vertex computation.
Exam Strategy: Behrend Function Sign Conventions
The Behrend function sign depends on the dimension of the ambient smooth variety used in the embedding. For a Calabi-Yau threefold, the moduli space of sheaves has virtual dimension 0, but the actual dimension varies. Remember: νM(x) = (−1)dim U(1 − χ(Fx)) where U is the smooth ambient space. For a symmetric obstruction theory, the virtual dimension is 0, so the DT invariant is an integer. In wall-crossing formulas, the quantum dilogarithm E(x; q) = ∏(1 + qk+1/2x)−1 uses the convention that the Euler form ⟨γ, δ⟩ is antisymmetric. Always check whether the problem uses the physicist's convention (q = −eiu) or the mathematician's convention (q = eiu) for the MNOP correspondence — this flips signs in the partition function.

Crystal Melting Models and the Topological Vertex for Non-Compact CY3

The topological vertex, introduced by Aganagic-Klemm-Marino-Vafa, computes the all-genus open and closed topological string amplitudes on toric Calabi-Yau threefolds. For a non-compact CY3 given by a toric diagram, the partition function is a sum over 3d plane partitions (crystal melting configurations) with weights determined by the geometry. The DT partition function for ideal sheaves on a toric CY3 X equals the generating function of 3d partitions asymptotic to a fixed boundary condition determined by the toric diagram. Each vertex of the toric diagram contributes a factor Cλμν(q) — the topological vertex — a symmetric function of three partitions λ, μ, ν. The vertex is given by Cλμν(q) = qκ(λ)/2 + κ(μ)/2 + κ(ν)/2 ∑η sλt/η(q−ρ) sμ/η(q−ρ) sν(q−ρ) where sλ/μ are skew Schur functions, q−ρ = (q1/2, q3/2, ...), and κ(λ) = ∑i λi(λi − 2i + 1). Gluing vertices along edges introduces framing factors (−q)f|λ|/2 where f is the framing integer determined by the toric geometry. The crystal melting interpretation: a 3d partition π is a set of boxes in ℤ≥03 such that if (i,j,k) ∈ π then all (i',j',k') with i' ≤ i, j' ≤ j, k' ≤ k are also in π. The generating function ∑π q|π| = ∏n≥1 (1 − qn)−n is the MacMahon function M(q). For the resolved conifold, the partition function is ZDT = M(q)2 ∑λ (−Q)|λ| qκ(λ)/2 sλ(q−ρ)2 where Q = e−t is the Kähler parameter. This matches the closed topological string amplitude on the resolved conifold. For open strings, adding a D-brane on a toric divisor introduces a boundary partition λ and the amplitude is the open topological string amplitude computed by the vertex with one leg fixed to λ. The crystal melting model also describes the BPS states in the 5d gauge theory engineered by the geometry: the 3d partitions correspond to fixed points of the torus action on the moduli space of instantons, and the weight q|π| is the equivariant weight. The refined topological vertex generalizes this to two parameters q, t using Macdonald polynomials instead of Schur functions, and computes the refined DT invariants. The melting crystal model has been rigorously proven to compute DT invariants for toric CY3 by Okounkov-Reshetikhin-Vafa and by Maulik-Oblomkov-Okounkov-Pandharipande using the theory of stable pairs and the topological vertex.

Categorification: Cohomological DT Invariants and BPS Algebras

The categorification program seeks to replace numerical DT invariants with graded vector spaces (or chain complexes) whose Euler characteristics recover the invariants. For a Calabi-Yau threefold X, the cohomological DT invariants are the cohomology groups H*(M, φTr Wℚ) of the perverse sheaf of vanishing cycles on the moduli stack M of objects in Db(Coh(X)). These carry a mixed Hodge structure, and the refined DT invariants are the Hodge polynomials. Kontsevich and Soibelman conjectured that the CoHA of the category of coherent sheaves on X acts on these cohomology groups, making them modules over the CoHA. For quivers with potential, the CoHA is the cohomology of the moduli stack of representations with convolution product, and the DT invariants are the graded dimensions of the cohomology of the critical locus of the potential. The BPS algebra is the Lie algebra of primitive elements in the CoHA, and its universal enveloping algebra is isomorphic to the CoHA (by the Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt theorem for the CoHA). The wall-crossing formula is then the statement that the BPS algebra is independent of the stability condition, while the DT invariants (which are the dimensions of the weight spaces of the BPS algebra representation) change according to the KS formula. In the case of the resolved conifold, the CoHA is isomorphic to the positive half of the quantum affine algebra Uq(ŝl2), and the DT invariants are the dimensions of the weight spaces of the Fock representation. For local curves, the CoHA is related to the spherical Hall algebra of the curve. The categorification also connects to the work of Khovanov-Rozansky on link homology: the cohomology of the moduli space of sheaves on the resolved conifold with a D-brane on a Lagrangian cycle is isomorphic to the triply-graded Khovanov-Rozansky homology of the corresponding link. The refined DT invariants then give the Poincaré polynomial of this homology. The BPS algebra in this context is the algebra of line operators in the 4d N = 2 theory, and the wall-crossing formula is the statement that the spectrum of line operators changes by the KS transformation when crossing walls of marginal stability. The mathematical formulation by Davison-Meinhardt uses the critical CoHA of a quiver with potential, a cohomological Hall algebra defined using the vanishing cycle functor, and proves the integrality conjecture for the BPS invariants.

Higher Rank DT Theory: ADHM Sheaves and Non-Abelian Localization

The classical DT theory counts ideal sheaves, corresponding to rank 1 torsion-free sheaves with trivial determinant. Higher rank DT theory counts Gieseker-stable sheaves of rank r > 1 with fixed Chern classes. The moduli space MX(r, c1, c2, c3) of stable sheaves on a Calabi-Yau threefold X carries a perfect obstruction theory, and the DT invariants are defined by integrating against the virtual class. For X = ℂ3 or a local CY3, the moduli space of framed sheaves (trivialized at infinity) is the ADHM moduli space of instantons on ℝ4 × ℂ, described as the moduli space of representations of the ADHM quiver with relations. The DT invariants for higher rank on ℂ3 are computed by the Nekrasov partition function of 5d N = 1 U(r) gauge theory, a sum over r-tuples of partitions. The non-abelian localization formula of Nekrasov expresses the partition function as a sum over fixed points of the torus action T = (ℂ*)2 × (ℂ*)r on the ADHM moduli space. Each fixed point corresponds to an r-tuple of Young diagrams, and the contribution is a product of weights of the tangent-obstruction complex. The DT partition function for rank r on ℂ3 is ZDT(r)(q, Q1, ..., Qr) = ∑λ(1),...,λ(r) q∑|λ(a)| ∏a,b Nλ(a)λ(b)(q, Qa/Qb) where Nλμ are the Nekrasov factors. For compact CY3, higher rank DT invariants are much harder to compute. The MNOP conjecture has been generalized to higher rank by Toda and others: the generating function of higher rank DT invariants is related to the Gromov-Witten invariants of the symmetric product stack Symr(X) or the moduli space of stable maps to the Grassmannian. The wall-crossing formula for higher rank DT invariants involves the CoHA of the category of coherent sheaves of arbitrary rank, which is much larger than the rank 1 CoHA. The Joyce-Song generalized DT invariants are defined for all stability conditions and all ranks, and satisfy the wall-crossing formula. In the case of K3 surfaces (CY2), higher rank DT invariants relate to Vafa-Witten invariants and S-duality of N = 4 SYM. For CY3, the higher rank theory connects to 5d gauge theory on S1 × ℝ4 with gauge group U(r) and matter content determined by the geometry. Refined higher rank DT invariants compute the Nekrasov partition function with Ω-background, and the categorification gives the cohomology of the ADHM moduli space, which carries an action of the affine Yangian of gl(r). Non-abelian localization has been extended to the topological vertex for higher rank by Awata-Kanno and Iqbal-Kozcaz-Vafa, giving a combinatorial formula for the refined partition function in terms of Macdonald polynomials and r-tuples of partitions.

DT/PT Correspondence and the Wall-Crossing Between Ideal Sheaves and Stable Pairs

The Donaldson-Thomas invariants counting ideal sheaves IZ of subschemes Z ⊂ X and the Pandharipande-Thomas invariants counting stable pairs (F, s) with F a pure dimension-1 sheaf and s : OX → F a section with 0-dimensional cokernel are related by a wall-crossing formula in the space of stability conditions on Db(Coh(X)). The DT invariants correspond to the large-volume limit where the central charge is Z(E) = −∫X e−iω ch(E) with ω = B + iJ, J → ∞. The PT invariants correspond to the "PT chamber" where the stability condition is defined by the tilted heart APT = ⟨OX[1], Coh≤1(X)⟩. The wall-crossing between these chambers is governed by the Kontsevich-Soibelman formula applied to the category of objects with Chern character (1, 0, −β, −n). The generating functions are related by ZDT(q, Q) = ZPT(q, Q) · M(−q)χ(X)/2 where M(q) = ∏k≥1 (1 − qk)−k is the MacMahon function. The degree-zero DT invariants contribute the MacMahon factor, while the PT invariants have no degree-zero contribution by definition. The wall-crossing formula expresses the DT invariants as a sum over PT invariants with coefficients given by the Euler characteristics of certain Quot schemes. For a fixed curve class β, the DT partition function is ZDT,β(q) = ∑n DTβ,n qn and the PT partition function is ZPT,β(q) = ∑n PTβ,n qn. The wall-crossing identity is ZDT,β(q) = ZPT,β(q) · ∏k≥1 (1 − (−q)k)−k·β·c2(X) for the local contribution. The correspondence was established by Bridgeland and Toda using Hall algebra techniques for the category of perverse coherent sheaves. The DT/PT correspondence is a special case of the more general wall-crossing between different stability conditions on the derived category, and it provides a powerful computational tool: PT invariants are often easier to compute via localization on the moduli space of stable pairs, which has a simpler fixed-point structure than the Hilbert scheme of points and curves.

Motivic Donaldson-Thomas Invariants and the Motivic Hall Algebra

The motivic Donaldson-Thomas invariants refine the numerical DT invariants by taking values in the Grothendieck ring of varieties K0(Varℂ)[L−1/2] where L = [𝔸1] is the Lefschetz motive. For a Calabi-Yau threefold X and a stability condition σ, the motivic DT invariant DTmot(γ, σ) ∈ K0(Varℂ)[L−1/2] is defined using the motivic Hall algebra H(A) of the heart A of a bounded t-structure on Db(Coh(X)). The motivic Hall algebra has a product defined by [𝔛] · [𝔜] = [𝔛 ×𝔐 𝔜] where the fiber product is over the moduli stack 𝔐 of objects in A, and the integration map ∫ : H(A) → K0(Varℂ)[L−1/2] sends a stack to its motivic class. The motivic DT invariants are defined by the factorization of the motivic generating series Amot(σ) = ∑γ DTmot(γ, σ) xγ in the quantum torus algebra. The wall-crossing formula for motivic DT invariants states that Amot(σ+) = Amot(σ−) · ∏γ: arg Z+(γ) > arg Z−(γ) 𝔼mot(xγ)Ωmot(γ) where 𝔼mot(x) = ∏k≥0 (1 − Lk+1/2 x)−1 is the motivic quantum dilogarithm and Ωmot(γ) are the motivic BPS invariants. The numerical DT invariants are recovered by applying the Euler characteristic specialization χ : K0(Varℂ) → ℤ. The refined DT invariants are recovered by applying the Hodge-Deligne polynomial specialization E : K0(Varℂ) → ℤ[y, y−1]. The motivic Hall algebra approach, developed by Joyce and by Kontsevich-Soibelman, provides a rigorous mathematical framework for the wall-crossing formula and establishes the integrality of the BPS invariants in the cases where it applies. For quivers with potential, the motivic DT invariants are computed by the motivic Donaldson-Thomas series of the Jacobian algebra, and the wall-crossing formula is equivalent to the mutation formula for the motivic quantum dilogarithm. The motivic DT invariants for the resolved conifold and for local ℙ2 have been computed explicitly and match the refined topological vertex predictions.

DT Invariants for Toric Calabi-Yau Threefolds: The Topological Vertex and Gluing Rules

The topological vertex provides a combinatorial algorithm for computing the DT partition function of toric Calabi-Yau threefolds X. The toric diagram of X is a planar trivalent graph with edges labeled by integers (framing). Each vertex of the diagram corresponds to a copy of ℂ3, and the topological vertex Cλμν(q) is the DT partition function for ℂ3 with three stacks of D-branes on the coordinate planes, labeled by partitions λ, μ, ν. The vertex is given by Cλμν(q) = q(κ(λ)+κ(μ)+κ(ν))/2 sλt(q−ρ) ∑η sμt/η(q−ρ xλ) sν/η(q−ρ xλ) where sλ are Schur functions, xλ are the power-sum variables specialized to the partition λ, q−ρ = (q1/2, q3/2, …), and κ(λ) = ∑i λi(λi − 2i + 1). The gluing rule for an internal edge with framing f connecting two vertices with partitions λ and μ is a sum over partitions ν with weight (−q)f|ν|/2 Cλνt∅ Cμν∅. The framing factor (−q)f|ν|/2 is crucial and determined by the toric diagram: for an edge with normal vectors v1, v2 in the toric fan, the framing is f = det(v1, v2). The full DT partition function is ZDT(X) = ∑partitions on internal edges ∏vertices Cλμν ∏internal edges (−q)f|ν|/2. For the resolved conifold, the toric diagram has two vertices connected by one internal edge with framing 0, giving ZDT = ∑ν Cν∅∅ Cνt∅∅ = ∏k≥1 (1 − qk)−k · ∏k≥1 (1 − Qk)−k where Q is the Kähler parameter. For local ℙ2, the toric diagram has three vertices and three internal edges with framings (−3, 0, 0) up to cyclic permutation, and the partition function reproduces the Nekrasov partition function of 5d U(1) gauge theory with Chern-Simons term. The refined topological vertex generalizes this to two parameters (q, t) using Macdonald polynomials Pλ(x; q, t) instead of Schur functions, and computes the refined DT invariants. The crystal melting interpretation identifies the topological vertex sum with the statistical mechanics of a 3d crystal whose ground state is the plane partition corresponding to the empty partition, and excitations are atoms added at the corners.

The Gopakumar-Vafa Invariants and the DT/GW/GV Correspondence

The Gopakumar-Vafa invariants ng,β ∈ ℤ are integer invariants of a Calabi-Yau threefold X that count BPS states of M2-branes wrapping a holomorphic curve of class β with genus g. They are defined by the expansion of the Gromov-Witten potential FGW(gs, t) = ∑g≥0 gs2g−2 Fg(t) = ∑β>0 ∑g≥0 ng,β ∑k≥1 (1/k) (2 sin(k gs/2))2g−2 e−k t·β. The DT/GW correspondence (MNOP conjecture, proved for toric CY3 by Maulik, Oblomkov, Okounkov, and Pandharipande) states that the DT partition function ZDT(q, Q) = ∑β,n DTβ,n qn Qβ is related to the GW partition function ZGW(gs, Q) = exp(FGW) by the change of variables q = −e−i gs. The GV invariants are then extracted from the DT invariants via the formula ZDT(q, Q) = ∏β>0 ∏g≥0 ∏k≥1 (1 − (−q)k Qβ)−k ng,β · M(−q)χ(X)/2. The integrality of the GV invariants is a prediction of M-theory and is established for toric CY3 using the topological vertex. The DT invariants DTβ,n are related to the GV invariants by DTβ,n = ∑g≥0 ng,β · Ng,n(β) where Ng,n(β) are universal coefficients given by the expansion of the quantum dilogarithm. For the resolved conifold, the only non-zero GV invariant is n0,β = 1, and all higher genus invariants vanish. For local ℙ2, the GV invariants ng,d for degree d and genus g are known explicitly and satisfy the integrality and symmetry properties predicted by string theory. The DT/GW/GV correspondence extends to the refined theory: the refined DT invariants DTβ,n(q, t) are related to the refined GV invariants ng,β(q, t) by a similar product formula with the refined quantum dilogarithm. The refined GV invariants are polynomials in q, t with integer coefficients, and their specialization at t = q gives the unrefined GV invariants. The categorification of GV invariants is conjectured to be given by the cohomology of the moduli space of stable sheaves, which is expected to carry an action of the BPS algebra.

The Joyce-Song Generalized DT Invariants and the Wall-Crossing Formula

The Joyce-Song theory constructs generalized Donaldson-Thomas invariants DTγ(τ) ∈ ℚ for stability data τ = (Z, P) on a triangulated category D satisfying the Calabi-Yau 3 property, without assuming the existence of a fine moduli space. The invariants are defined inductively on the rank of the charge lattice using the Hall algebra of the heart A = P((0,1]) of the t-structure associated to τ. For a class γ with Z(γ) ≠ 0, the generalized DT invariant is defined by DTγ(τ) = ∑n≥1 (−1)n−1/n ∑γ1+…+γn=γ g(γ1,…,γn) ∏i=1n DTγi(τ) where g are the stack functions counting extensions. The wall-crossing formula states that for a path τ(t) crossing a wall of marginal stability where Z(γ1) and Z(γ2) align, the invariants transform by DTγ(τ+) = ∑n≥1 (−1)n−1/n ∑γ1+…+γn=γ U(γ1,…,γn; τ−, τ+) ∏i=1n DTγi(τ−) where U are universal coefficients given by the Kontsevich-Soibelman product of quantum dilogarithms. The Joyce-Song framework includes a support property ensuring that the wall-crossing formula has only finitely many terms. The generalized DT invariants specialize to the numerical DT invariants when a fine moduli space exists and the Behrend function identity holds. For quivers with potential, the Joyce-Song invariants coincide with the DT invariants defined via the critical CoHA. The theory has been extended to the motivic setting by Joyce and to the refined setting by Kontsevich-Soibelman. The wall-crossing formula for the generalized DT invariants is equivalent to the associativity of the Hall algebra product and the pentagon identity for the quantum dilogarithm. In the context of Bridgeland stability conditions on Db(Coh(X)), the Joyce-Song invariants provide a mathematical definition of the BPS invariants Ω(γ, τ) that appear in the physical wall-crossing formula.

The virtual fundamental class is constructed via the perfect obstruction theory on the relative moduli stack, and the open DT invariant is defined by integrating the Behrend function against this virtual class. For toric CY3, the open topological vertex of Aganagic and Vafa computes the generating function of open DT invariants. The wall-crossing formula for open DT invariants involves the open BPS invariants Ω(γ, y) which depend on the Kähler parameters and the open string modulus y. The open DT/PT correspondence extends the Pandharipande-Thomas stable pairs to the relative setting, replacing ideal sheaves of curves with relative stable pairs (F, s) where s: O_X(−D) → F is a section with 1-dimensional cokernel supported on curves meeting the divisor D. The Crepant Resolution Conjecture for DT Invariants

The crepant resolution conjecture (CRC) for Donaldson-Thomas invariants asserts that the DT partition function of a Calabi-Yau threefold orbifold X and its crepant resolution Y are equal after a suitable change of variables. For a toric orbifold C3/G with G ⊂ SL(3, C) finite, the DT invariants are defined on the moduli stack of G-equivariant sheaves on C3, and the virtual class is constructed via the Behrend function on the stack. The generating function Z_DT^X(q, Q) is computed by the orbifold topological vertex, which inserts G-invariant projectors into the standard vertex. For the crepant resolution Y, the DT partition function Z_DT^Y(q, Q) is given by the standard topological vertex with gluing rules determined by the toric diagram of Y. The CRC states that Z_DT^X(q, Q) = Z_DT^Y(q, Q') where Q' = Q · qρ and ρ is a shift vector determined by the age grading of the orbifold sectors. This has been proven for all cyclic orbifolds C3/Z_n by Bryan and Gholampour using the wall-crossing formula for the generalized DT invariants of Joyce-Song. The DT/PT correspondence for orbifolds is also expected to satisfy a crepant resolution conjecture: the PT partition function of X should equal the PT partition function of Y after the same change of variables. The generalized DT invariants DT_γ(τ) for the orbifold category D^b_G(Coh(C3)) and the resolved category D^b(Coh(Y)) are conjecturally related by the Kontsevich-Soibelman wall-crossing formula across the wall where the central charges of the exceptional collections align. The motivic DT invariants of the orbifold, defined via the motivic Hall algebra of the heart of the t-structure, are expected to specialize to the numerical DT invariants and obey the same crepant transformation rule.

The Remodeling Conjecture and DT Invariants for Local Calabi-Yau Threefolds

The remodeling conjecture, formulated by Bouchard, Klemm, Mariño, and Pasquetti, provides a recursive algorithm to compute all-genus topological string amplitudes on a local Calabi-Yau threefold — the total space of the canonical bundle over a toric surface — from the spectral curve of the mirror geometry. For a toric surface S, the local Calabi-Yau threefold is X = Tot(K_S). The DT partition function Z_DT(X) is conjectured to equal the topological string partition function Z_top(X) computed by the remodeling conjecture. The spectral curve is given by the mirror equation H(x, y) = 0 where H(x, y) = ∑(i,j)∈Δ ci,j x^i y^j and Δ is the Newton polygon of the toric surface. The remodeling conjecture uses the Eynard-Orantin topological recursion on this spectral curve to produce the genus g free energies F_g. The DT invariants DTβ,n are then extracted from the expansion of Z_DT = exp(∑g≥0 g_s2g-2 F_g) in the large volume frame. For local P2, the spectral curve is x + y + 1/xy = 0 (after appropriate scaling) and the recursion reproduces the DT invariants computed by the topological vertex. For local F0 = P1 × P1, the spectral curve is x + 1/x + y + 1/y = 0. The remodeling conjecture has been proven for local P2 and local F0 by Liu, Liu, and Zhou using the Givental formalism and the mirror theorem for toric stacks. The conjecture also extends to open DT invariants: the open topological string amplitudes computed by the recursion with boundary conditions on the spectral curve correspond to open DT invariants counting sheaves with Lagrangian boundary conditions. The framing ambiguity in the topological vertex corresponds to the choice of polarization in the topological recursion. The refined remodeling conjecture replaces the Eynard-Orantin recursion with the refined topological recursion of Krefl and Walcher, which computes refined DT invariants DTβ,n(q, t) depending on two equivariant parameters. The refined spectral curve is a quantum curve [ŷ, x̂] = ℏ where ℏ = log q, and the refined recursion produces the Nekrasov partition function of the corresponding 5d gauge theory. The remodeling conjecture provides a powerful computational tool that bypasses the gluing rules of the topological vertex and directly accesses the BPS invariants Ω(γ) through the product expansion of the partition function.

The Kontsevich-Soibelman Wall-Crossing Formula and the Spectrum Generator

The Kontsevich-Soibelman wall-crossing formula is the mathematical formulation of the physical statement that the BPS spectrum of a supersymmetric theory changes discontinuously across walls of marginal stability in the space of stability conditions, but the product of quantum dilogarithms ordered by the phase of the central charge remains invariant. Let Γ be a lattice of charges equipped with a skew-symmetric intersection form ⟨·, ·⟩. For each charge γ ∈ Γ, let Ω(γ) ∈ ℤ be the BPS index (the generalized DT invariant). The quantum dilogarithm is Ψ_q(x) = ∏k≥0 (1 − qk+1/2 x)-1 where q = eiℏ. The KS formula states that for any path in the space of stability conditions that does not cross a wall, the ordered product ∏γ: arg Z(γ) increasing Ψ_q((−1)⟨γ,·⟩ X_γ)Ω(γ) is constant, where X_γ are generators of the quantum torus algebra satisfying X_γ X_γ' = q⟨γ,γ'⟩ Xγ+γ'. When a wall of marginal stability is crossed, the BPS indices jump according to the KS formula: the product on one side of the wall equals the product on the other side with the factors reordered. The spectrum generator is the operator S = ∏γ: arg Z(γ) increasing Ψ_q((−1)⟨γ,·⟩ X_γ)Ω(γ). The wall-crossing formula is equivalent to the statement that S is independent of the stability condition. For the conifold, the charge lattice is Γ = ℤγ0 ⊕ ℤγ1 with ⟨γ0, γ1⟩ = 1. The BPS indices are Ω(γ0) = Ω(γ1) = 1 and Ω(nγ0 + mγ1) = 0 for n,m > 0 in one chamber. In the other chamber, Ω(γ0) = Ω(γ1) = 1 and Ω(γ0 + γ1) = −1. The KS formula gives Ψ_q(X0) Ψ_q(X1) = Ψ_q(X1) Ψ_q(−X0 X1) Ψ_q(X0), which is the pentagon identity for the quantum dilogarithm. For local P2, the charge lattice has rank 3 and the wall-crossing formula reproduces the DT invariants computed by the topological vertex. The KS formula is deeply connected to cluster algebras: the mutations of the cluster algebra correspond to wall-crossing, and the quantum dilogarithm identities are the quantum cluster mutations. The space of stability conditions Stab(D^b(Coh(X))) for a Calabi-Yau threefold X has a wall-and-chamber structure where the walls are real codimension one loci where the phase of the central charge aligns for two charges. The KS formula provides a consistency condition for the BPS invariants across all chambers. The motivic KS formula replaces the quantum dilogarithm with the motivic quantum dilogarithm Ψ_ℒ(x) = ∏k≥0 (1 − ℒk+1/2 x)-1 in the Grothendieck ring of varieties, and the cohomological KS formula operates in the derived category of constructible sheaves on the space of stability conditions.

Consider the resolved conifold X = Tot(O(−1) ⊕ O(−1) → P1). The charge lattice is Γ = ℤ[D0] ⊕ ℤ[D2] where [D0] is the class of a point (D0-brane) and [D2] is the class of the P1 (D2-brane wrapping the zero section). Compute the BPS indices in the new chamber using the KS formula.

Cohomological Donaldson-Thomas Invariants and the Perverse Filtration

The cohomological Donaldson-Thomas invariants, introduced by Davison and Meinhardt, categorify the numerical DT invariants by constructing a graded vector space whose Euler characteristic recovers the DT invariant. For a Calabi-Yau threefold X and a stability condition τ, let M_τ(v) be the moduli stack of τ-semistable sheaves with Chern character v. The cohomological DT invariant is the compactly supported cohomology H_c^*(M_τ(v), φ_ℒ) where φ_ℒ is the vanishing cycle functor applied to the natural potential function ℒ on the moduli stack (the Chern-Simons functional). This cohomology carries a natural perverse filtration P_• induced by the perverse t-structure on the category of constructible sheaves on the moduli stack. The associated graded pieces gr_k^P H_c^*(M_τ(v), φ_ℒ) are finite-dimensional vector spaces whose dimensions are the refined BPS invariants. The Poincaré polynomial of the cohomological DT invariant in the two gradings (cohomological degree and perverse degree) gives the refined DT invariant DTβ,n(q, t). The perverse filtration is expected to satisfy the P = W conjecture: the perverse filtration on the cohomology of the moduli space of Higgs bundles (which is mirror to the DT moduli space) coincides with the weight filtration on the cohomology of the corresponding character variety. For the resolved conifold, the cohomological DT invariants have been computed explicitly by Davison: the cohomology is concentrated in perverse degree 0 and the Poincaré polynomial is the quantum dilogarithm. For local P2, the cohomological DT invariants are related to the cohomology of the moduli space of representations of the quiver with potential (the McKay quiver of P2). The cohomological Hall algebra (CoHA) of Kontsevich and Soibelman acts on the cohomological DT invariants, providing a representation-theoretic structure. The CoHA is the direct sum over dimension vectors of the cohomology of the moduli stack of representations of a quiver with potential, equipped with a multiplication given by the Hall algebra product. The cohomological DT invariants form a module over the CoHA. The wall-crossing formula for cohomological DT invariants is an isomorphism of graded vector spaces between the cohomology in adjacent chambers, induced by the action of the quantum dilogarithm in the CoHA. The motivic DT invariants of Kontsevich and Soibelman take values in the Grothendieck ring of varieties K0(Var_ℂ) and specialize to the cohomological DT invariants via the Hodge-Deligne polynomial. The categorification program aims to construct a triangulated category of BPS states whose Grothendieck group recovers the numerical DT invariants and whose Hochschild cohomology recovers the cohomological DT invariants. This category is expected to be the Fukaya category of the mirror Calabi-Yau threefold, realizing the homological mirror symmetry conjecture for DT theory.

Relative Donaldson-Thomas Theory and the Degeneration Formula

Relative Donaldson-Thomas theory, developed by Li, Wu, and others, counts stable sheaves on a Calabi-Yau threefold X relative to a smooth divisor D ⊂ X. The moduli space MX/D(v, μ) parameterizes stable sheaves F on X with Chern character v together with a framing at D, i.e., an isomorphism F|_D ≅ ⊕_i O_D(−μ_i) for a partition μ. The relative DT invariants DTX/D(v, μ) are defined by integrating the virtual fundamental class against the obstruction theory relative to D. The degeneration formula expresses the absolute DT invariants of X in terms of relative DT invariants of a degeneration X → Δ where the central fiber X0 = X1 ∪_D X2 is a normal crossing union of two threefolds glued along a common divisor D. The formula states that the absolute DT invariant of the general fiber equals a sum over splittings of the Chern character v = v1 + v2 and matchings of the relative framings μ on D of the product of relative DT invariants of X1/D and X2/D, weighted by the gluing rules for the virtual classes. The gluing involves the relative virtual classes [MX1/D(v1, μ)]^vir and [MX2/D(v2, μ^t)]^vir where μ^t is the transpose partition, and the evaluation maps to the moduli space of sheaves on D. The degeneration formula is the DT analogue of the Gromov-Witten degeneration formula of Li and Ruan. It is a crucial tool for computing DT invariants of complicated threefolds by degenerating them to simpler pieces. For example, the DT invariants of a quintic threefold can be approached by degenerating to a union of a degree 4 hypersurface and a hyperplane in P4, glued along a K3 surface. The relative DT invariants of the hyperplane (which is P3) relative to a K3 surface are related to the DT invariants of the K3 surface, which are known from the Yau-Zaslow formula. The relative DT/PT correspondence relates relative DT invariants to relative Pandharipande-Thomas invariants, which count stable pairs relative to a divisor. The relative theory also has a wall-crossing formula as the stability condition varies, which is compatible with the absolute wall-crossing formula under the degeneration formula. The logarithmic DT theory of Gross, Siebert, and others extends the relative theory to normal crossing divisors and provides a tropical interpretation of the degeneration formula in terms of scattering diagrams on the tropicalization of the degeneration. The relative DT invariants for toric Calabi-Yau threefolds relative to a toric divisor are computed by the topological vertex with a preferred direction, and the degeneration formula corresponds to the gluing of topological vertices along edges. The refined relative DT invariants depend on two equivariant parameters and satisfy a refined degeneration formula involving the refined topological vertex.

The bounded derived category of coherent sheaves on a Calabi-Yau threefold X, denoted D^b(Coh X), is the prototypical example. More generally, the category of finite-dimensional representations of a quiver with superpotential (Q, W) that is 3-Calabi-Yau (in the sense of Ginzburg) provides a non-commutative analogue. The DT invariants are then defined as the Euler characteristics of the moduli stacks of objects in C weighted by the Behrend function, or equivalently as the dimensions of the cohomology of the associated cohomological Hall algebra (CoHA). The categorical perspective allows one to transport DT invariants across derived equivalences: if C and C′ are derived equivalent 3-Calabi-Yau categories, their DT invariants coincide after an appropriate identification of charge lattices. This invariance under derived equivalence is a categorical incarnation of the physical statement that DT invariants count BPS states in a topological string theory that depends only on the underlying 3-Calabi-Yau category. The construction of the CoHA, due to Kontsevich and Soibelman, uses the Hall algebra of the abelian category of representations of the Jacobian algebra J(Q, W) and a certain perverse filtration to produce an associative algebra whose graded pieces carry the DT invariants. The integration map from the CoHA to the quantum torus algebra encodes the wall-crossing formula: the product of quantum dilogarithms associated to stable objects remains invariant as the stability condition varies. This categorical approach also clarifies the relation between DT invariants and the motivic Milnor fiber of the superpotential W, as the CoHA can be identified with the vanishing cohomology of W on the representation variety. In practice, computing DT invariants for a given 3-Calabi-Yau category often reduces to studying the representation theory of the associated quiver with superpotential, applying the integration map, and using the wall-crossing formula to relate different chambers of stability.

DT Invariants and the Wall-Crossing for Quivers with Superpotentials

Wall-crossing in the context of quivers with superpotentials is governed by the Kontsevich-Soibelman (KS) formula, which expresses the invariance of the product of quantum dilogarithms attached to stable representations as the stability condition crosses a wall. Let (Q, W) be a quiver with superpotential whose Jacobian algebra J(Q, W) is 3-Calabi-Yau. Fix a stability condition Z on the category of finite-dimensional J(Q, W)-modules, given by a central charge Z: K0 → ℂ. For each dimension vector γ, the moduli stack of Z-semistable representations of dimension γ carries a Behrend function ν_γ, and the DT invariant Ω(γ; Z) is the weighted Euler characteristic ∫ ν_γ dχ. When Z varies continuously, the set of stable objects changes only when Z crosses a wall where the phases of two charge vectors align. The KS wall-crossing formula states that the ordered product ∏γ: arg Z(γ)=θ Φq1/2(x_γ)Ω(γ; Z) is independent of θ, where Φ_q(x) = ∏k≥0 (1 - q2k+1 x)-1 is the quantum dilogarithm and x_γ are generators of the quantum torus algebra satisfying x_γ x_δ = q⟨γ, δ⟩ x_δ x_γ with the Euler form ⟨·,·⟩. This identity encodes all wall-crossing phenomena for the quiver. In the case of the conifold quiver, the formula reproduces the known DT/PT correspondence and the topological vertex. For more general quivers, such as those arising from dimer models on toric Calabi-Yau threefolds, the wall-crossing formula can be solved recursively using the scattering diagram technique of Gross-Hacking-Keel, yielding explicit generating functions for DT invariants in all chambers. The wall-crossing formula also implies the existence of a universal DT invariant, the BPS index, which is independent of the stability condition and coincides with the protected spin character of the corresponding 4d N=2 theory. The KS formula has been proved in the motivic setting by Efimov and in the cohomological setting by Davison-Meinhardt, using the integration map on the CoHA and the purity of the vanishing cohomology of the superpotential.

Worked Example: Wall-Crossing for the Kronecker Quiver
Consider the Kronecker quiver with two vertices 1, 2 and two arrows a, b: 1 → 2. Take the trivial superpotential W = 0. The Jacobian algebra is the path algebra of the quiver. Fix a stability condition with central charge Z(1) = -1, Z(2) = τ where τ ∈ ℂ with Im τ > 0. Compute the DT invariants Ω(γ) for dimension vectors γ = (d1, d2) in the chamber where τ is near the positive imaginary axis, and then apply the KS wall-crossing formula to find the invariants in the adjacent chamber after crossing the wall where arg Z(1) = arg Z(2).
The dimension vectors are γ = (d1, d2). The Euler form is ⟨(d1,d2), (d1',d2')⟩ = d1 d1' + d2 d2' - 2 d1 d2'. For the initial chamber, τ = iε with ε → 0⁺, so Z(1) = -1 (phase π) and Z(2) = iε (phase π/2). The stable objects are the simple representations S1 = (1,0) and S2 = (0,1) with DT invariants Ω(S1) = Ω(S2) = 1. All other representations are unstable because their phases lie between π/2 and π. The ordered product of quantum dilogarithms is Φ(x(1,0)) Φ(x(0,1)). Crossing the wall where arg Z(1) = arg Z(2) corresponds to rotating τ through the positive real axis. In the new chamber, the stable objects include the simple S1, S2, and the indecomposable representations of dimension (1,1) (the two arrows give a 2-dimensional family). The KS identity Φ(x(1,0)) Φ(x(0,1)) = Φ(x(0,1)) Φ(x(1,1)) Φ(x(1,0)) holds in the quantum torus with q = -1 (since the Euler form is skew-symmetric). Using the pentagon identity for the quantum dilogarithm, one finds Ω(1,1) = 2. Thus the DT invariants in the new chamber are Ω(1,0)=1, Ω(0,1)=1, Ω(1,1)=2, and all others zero. This matches the known BPS spectrum of the Argyres-Douglas theory of type (A1, A2).
Exam Strategy: Recognising the KS Product Ordering
When a question asks for the wall-crossing formula for a given quiver, first identify the central charge and the ordering of phases. The KS product is always taken in increasing order of the phase arg Z(γ). Write the product explicitly for the initial chamber, then apply the pentagon identity (or the general quantum dilogarithm identity) to reorder the product for the target chamber. The exponents of the quantum dilogarithms give the DT invariants. Remember that the quantum parameter q is q1/2 = -1 for symmetric Euler forms, and q⟨γ,δ⟩ for the commutation relation. Practice with the Kronecker quiver and the conifold quiver until the reordering becomes automatic.

DT Invariants and the Integration Map in the Cohomological Hall Algebra

The cohomological Hall algebra (CoHA) of a 3-Calabi-Yau category provides a categorification of the quantum torus algebra that underlies the KS wall-crossing formula. For a quiver with superpotential (Q, W), the CoHA is defined as the direct sum over dimension vectors γ of the equivariant Borel-Moore homology (or cohomology) of the representation variety Rep_γ(J(Q,W)) with coefficients in the vanishing cycle complex of the superpotential W. The multiplication is given by a convolution diagram that corresponds to extensions of representations. The resulting algebra H is graded by the charge lattice and carries a natural action of the quantum torus algebra via the integration map. The integration map Int: H → 𝕋_q sends a class in the CoHA to an element of the quantum torus by taking the trace of the action on the cohomology of the moduli stack. Concretely, for each dimension vector γ, the component Int_γ maps the fundamental class of the stack of semistable objects to the quantum dilogarithm Φq1/2(x_γ)Ω(γ). The key theorem of Kontsevich-Soibelman and its cohomological refinement by Davison-Meinhardt states that the integration map is an algebra homomorphism from the CoHA (with the Hall product) to the quantum torus (with the twisted product). This homomorphism property is equivalent to the KS wall-crossing formula. Moreover, the CoHA carries a perverse filtration whose associated graded pieces are the cohomology of the moduli spaces of stable objects, and the integration map respects this filtration. The integration map also provides a bridge to the motivic Hall algebra: after applying the motivic weight, the integration map becomes the motivic DT invariant. In practice, computing the integration map for a given quiver reduces to evaluating the cohomology of the vanishing cycles of W on the representation variety, which can often be done using the dimensional reduction technique of Efimov or the critical cohomology of the superpotential. The integration map is also central to the proof of the integrality of DT invariants and to the construction of the BPS Lie algebra as the primitive part of the CoHA.

DT Invariants and the Motivic Milnor Fiber of the Superpotential

The motivic Milnor fiber of the superpotential W on the representation variety provides a geometric incarnation of the DT invariants that is independent of the choice of stability condition. For a quiver with superpotential (Q, W), the representation variety Rep_γ is an affine space, and W is a regular function on it. The motivic Milnor fiber ψ_W is an element of the Grothendieck ring of varieties (or its completion) that encodes the vanishing cycles of W. The motivic DT invariant DT_mot(γ) is defined as the class of the motivic Milnor fiber of W on the stack of representations of dimension γ, weighted by the inverse of the motivic class of the gauge group. The fundamental result of Kontsevich-Soibelman and its motivic refinement by Efimov states that the generating series of motivic DT invariants satisfies the motivic KS wall-crossing formula, where the quantum dilogarithm is replaced by the motivic quantum dilogarithm 𝔼_q(x) = ∏k≥0 (1 - 𝕃k+1/2 x)-1 with 𝕃 the Lefschetz motive. The motivic Milnor fiber also carries a mixed Hodge structure, and its Hodge-Deligne polynomial specializes to the refined DT invariants with parameters q, t. The connection to the CoHA is that the cohomology of the motivic Milnor fiber (with its monodromy action) is isomorphic to the CoHA as a graded vector space. This isomorphism is constructed via the dimensional reduction of the vanishing cycles to the critical locus of W, which is the moduli stack of representations of the Jacobian algebra. The motivic Milnor fiber approach has the advantage that it does not require a choice of stability condition; the wall-crossing formula emerges from the factorization property of the motivic Milnor fiber under the decomposition of the representation variety into strata indexed by Harder-Narasimhan types. In the case of the conifold, the motivic Milnor fiber reproduces the refined topological vertex. For general toric Calabi-Yau threefolds, the motivic Milnor fiber can be computed using the dimer model and the associated brane tiling, leading to explicit formulas for the refined DT invariants in all chambers.

DT Invariants and the Non-Commutative Crepant Resolutions of Singularities

Non-commutative crepant resolutions (NCCRs) provide a powerful algebraic framework for defining DT invariants on singular Calabi-Yau threefolds where the usual moduli spaces of sheaves are not well-behaved. Let R be a Gorenstein normal domain of dimension three with trivial canonical module, for example the coordinate ring of a Calabi-Yau threefold with isolated singularities. An NCCR of R is an R-algebra A = End_R(M) where M is a reflexive R-module that is a generator and such that A has finite global dimension and is a maximal Cohen-Macaulay R-module. The derived category D^b(mod A) is then a 3-Calabi-Yau category that serves as a non-commutative resolution of Spec R. The DT invariants of the singular threefold are defined as the DT invariants of this 3-Calabi-Yau category, i.e., the Euler characteristics of the moduli stacks of finite-dimensional A-modules weighted by the Behrend function. Van den Bergh proved that NCCRs exist for all three-dimensional Gorenstein terminal singularities and that any two NCCRs are derived equivalent, so the DT invariants are independent of the choice of NCCR. In the case of the conifold singularity xy - zw = 0, the NCCR is given by the endomorphism algebra of the direct sum of the two maximal Cohen-Macaulay modules, which is the path algebra of the conifold quiver with the standard superpotential. The DT invariants computed via this NCCR coincide with the usual DT invariants of the resolved conifold after the appropriate wall-crossing. For more complicated singularities, such as the quotient singularities ℂ3/G with G ⊂ SL(3,ℂ) finite, the NCCR is given by the skew group algebra ℂ[x,y,z] ⋊ G, and the DT invariants can be computed using the McKay correspondence and the representation theory of G. The NCCR approach also clarifies the relation between DT invariants and the Gopakumar-Vafa invariants: the GV invariants count BPS states in the non-commutative resolution, and the DT/GV correspondence becomes a statement about the integration map on the CoHA of the NCCR. This perspective has been used to define DT invariants for Calabi-Yau threefolds with non-isolated singularities and for non-commutative Calabi-Yau threefolds that do not admit a commutative crepant resolution.

  • Confusing the CoHA product with the quantum torus product; the CoHA product is a convolution on the representation variety, while the quantum torus product is twisted by the Euler form.
  • Assuming the KS wall-crossing formula holds for any stability condition without checking the support property; the formula requires a stability condition that satisfies the support property (Bridgeland).
  • Neglecting the shift in the quantum parameter when passing from the motivic quantum dilogarithm to the ordinary one; the motivic parameter is 𝕃1/2 while the ordinary parameter is q1/2 = -1 for symmetric forms.
  • Treating the integration map as a map of vector spaces only; it is an algebra homomorphism, and its compatibility with the product is exactly the wall-crossing identity.
  • Overlooking the role of the perverse filtration on the CoHA; the associated graded pieces give the cohomology of the moduli spaces of stable objects, and the integration map respects this filtration.
  • Confusing NCCRs with commutative crepant resolutions; NCCRs exist for singularities that admit no commutative crepant resolution, and the DT invariants are defined via the non-commutative category.
  • Forgetting that the motivic Milnor fiber carries a monodromy action; the refined DT invariants are obtained by taking the equivariant Hodge-Deligne polynomial with respect to this monodromy.

DT Invariants and the Cohomological Gopakumar-Vafa Invariants: The Categorification of BPS State Counting

The Gopakumar-Vafa (GV) invariants ng,β ∈ ℤ count BPS states in M-theory on a Calabi-Yau threefold X, or equivalently the integer BPS degeneracies in type IIA string theory. The original definition by Gopakumar and Vafa (1998) used the topological string partition function and the Schwinger loop computation to extract integer invariants from the Gromov-Witten (GW) invariants. The DT/GV correspondence, proven for toric CY3 by Maulik, Oblomkov, Okounkov, and Pandharipande (2011) using the topological vertex, states that the DT partition function equals the GV partition function after a change of variables. The categorification of GV invariants constructs a bigraded vector space HGV(X) = ⊕g,β,i,j Hg,βi,j whose graded Euler characteristic recovers the GV invariants. This was achieved by Maulik and Toda (2018) using the perverse filtration on the cohomology of the moduli stack of one-dimensional sheaves, and independently by Bousseau, Brini, and van Garrel (2020) using the logarithmic GW theory and the tropical vertex. The cohomological GV invariants carry an action of the BPS Lie algebra, which is the Lie algebra of the CoHA integration map. The perverse filtration on the CoHA induces a filtration on the cohomological GV invariants, and the associated graded pieces are the cohomology of the moduli spaces of stable sheaves. The categorification also clarifies the integrality of the GV invariants: the GV invariants are the Euler characteristics of the cohomological GV invariants, which are finite-dimensional vector spaces. For the resolved conifold, the cohomological GV invariants are computed by the refined topological vertex and match the HOMFLY-PT homology of the unknot. For local ℙ2, the cohomological GV invariants are related to the cohomology of the Hilbert scheme of points on ℂ2 and the Maulik-Okounkov quantum group. The categorification program extends to open GV invariants, where the cohomological open GV invariants are the HOMFLY-PT homology of knots and links, and to refined GV invariants, where the cohomological refined GV invariants carry an action of the quantum toroidal algebra of 𝔤𝔩1.

DT Invariants and the Categorical Topological Vertex: The Categorification of the Topological Vertex

The topological vertex, introduced by Aganagic, Klemm, Marino, and Vafa (2003), computes the open and closed topological string amplitudes on toric Calabi-Yau threefolds using a gluing rule for trivalent vertices. The vertex Cλμν(q) is a symmetric function of three partitions λ, μ, ν, and the partition function of a toric CY3 is obtained by summing over partitions on internal edges with appropriate framing factors. The categorification of the topological vertex constructs a triply-graded vector space Vλμν whose graded Euler characteristic is the topological vertex. This was achieved by Gorsky, Oblomkov, Rasmussen, and Shende (2016) using the representation theory of the quantum toroidal algebra of 𝔤𝔩1 (also known as the Ding-Iohara-Miki algebra). The categorical vertex Vλμν carries an action of the quantum toroidal algebra Uq,t(𝔤𝔩1), and the gluing of vertices corresponds to the tensor product of representations. The categorification explains the symmetry of the vertex under the exchange of partitions and the framing factor as the action of the R-matrix of the quantum toroidal algebra. The categorical vertex also provides a categorification of the refined topological vertex, where the two parameters q and t correspond to the two gradings on the vector space. For the resolved conifold, the categorical vertex recovers the HOMFLY-PT homology of the unknot and the Hopf link. For local ℙ1×ℙ1, the categorical vertex is related to the categorification of the quantum dilogarithm and the wall-crossing formula. The categorical topological vertex has been extended to the open topological vertex by Aganagic and Shakirov (2012) using the refined Chern-Simons theory, and to the K-theoretic topological vertex by Awata, Feigin, and Shiraishi (2012) using the quantum toroidal algebra. The categorical vertex also connects to the Maulik-Okounkov quantum group: the integration map on the CoHA of the resolved conifold factors through the categorical vertex, and the wall-crossing formula for the categorical vertex is the R-matrix relation in the quantum toroidal algebra.

Worked Example: Categorical Vertex for the Resolved Conifold
Compute the categorical topological vertex for the resolved conifold and show how it recovers the HOMFLY-PT homology of the unknot.
The resolved conifold is the total space of 𝒪(−1)⊕𝒪(−1) → ℙ1. Its toric diagram has two vertices connected by an internal edge. The categorical vertex Vλμν is the space of intertwiners between three Fock representations of the quantum toroidal algebra Uq,t(𝔤𝔩1). For the conifold, we glue two vertices with partitions λ, μ on the external legs and sum over the internal partition ν with framing factor (−1)|ν|q−κ(ν)/2t−κ(ν)/2, where κ(ν) = ∑i νi(νi−2i+1). The glued space is ⊕ν V∅∅ν ⊗ Vνt∅∅ with the framing twist. The Fock representation Fu has basis |ν⟩ indexed by partitions, and the intertwiner space Vλμν = HomU(Fu, Fv ⊗ Fw) is one-dimensional for generic parameters. The graded dimension of the glued space is ∑ν (−1)|ν| q−κ(ν)/2 t−κ(ν)/2 dimq,t V∅∅ν dimq,t Vνt∅∅. Exam Strategy: Categorification and Graded Dimensions
When a question asks for the categorification of a partition function, identify the underlying algebra (quantum toroidal, CoHA, Yangian) and the representation category (Fock, Verma, finite-dimensional). The graded Euler characteristic is the alternating sum of dimensions across the homological grading. For the topological vertex, the key is the intertwiner space of the quantum toroidal algebra. Remember the framing factor (−1)|ν|q−κ(ν)/2t−κ(ν)/2 and the arm/leg formula for the refined vertex. The HOMFLY-PT homology of the unknot is the universal building block; all other knot homologies are obtained by cabling and the R-matrix action.

DT Invariants and the BPS Spectrum of 5d N=2 Gauge Theories: The Nekrasov Partition Function and the Refined Topological Vertex

Five-dimensional N=2 supersymmetric gauge theories on ℝ4×S1 have a BPS spectrum counted by the Nekrasov partition function ZNek(q, t, a, Λ), where q = e−ϵ1, t = eϵ2 are the Omega-background parameters, a are the Coulomb branch parameters, and Λ is the dynamical scale. The Nekrasov partition function computes the equivariant integral over the instanton moduli space, which is the moduli space of framed torsion-free sheaves on ℙ2 (for pure gauge theory) or on a local surface (for quiver gauge theories). The refined topological vertex computes the same partition function for gauge theories engineered by M-theory on toric CY3. The 5d N=2 gauge theory with gauge group SU(N) and Nf fundamental hypermultiplets is engineered by M-theory on the local CY3 given by the total space of 𝒪(−1)⊕𝒪(−1) → ℙ1 with N D4-branes and Nf D6-branes. The Nekrasov partition function equals the refined topological string partition function on this CY3, which is computed by the refined topological vertex with appropriate boundary conditions. The BPS spectrum of the 5d theory is captured by the refined BPS invariants NjL,jRβ ∈ ℤ, which are the coefficients of the refined topological string free energy. These refined BPS invariants are the refined DT invariants of the CY3, and the Nekrasov partition function is the refined DT partition function. The wall-crossing formula for the 5d BPS spectrum is the Kontsevich-Soibelman wall-crossing formula for the refined DT invariants, which is the quantum dilogarithm identity in the quantum torus algebra. The 5d N=2 theory on a circle of radius R reduces to a 4d N=2 theory in the limit R → 0, and the Nekrasov partition function reduces to the Seiberg-Witten prepotential. The refined BPS invariants reduce to the ordinary BPS invariants (GV invariants) in the unrefined limit t → q. The categorification of the 5d BPS spectrum is given by the cohomology of the moduli space of instantons, which carries an action of the quantum toroidal algebra Uq,t(𝔤𝔩1) for the pure gauge theory, and of the Yangian of 𝔤𝔩N for quiver gauge theories. The Maulik-Okounkov quantum group acts on the cohomology of the Hilbert scheme of points on ℂ2, which is the instanton moduli space for U(N) gauge theory on ℝ4.

DT Invariants and the Stable Pair Invariants for Local Calabi-Yau Four-Folds: The DT/PT Correspondence for CY4

Donaldson-Thomas theory for Calabi-Yau four-folds (CY4) was developed by Cao, Maulik, and Toda (2019) and by Borisov and Joyce (2017). A CY4 is a smooth projective four-fold X with trivial canonical bundle. The DT invariants count ideal sheaves of curves and surfaces in X, or more generally, sheaves with one-dimensional or two-dimensional support. The moduli space of stable sheaves on a CY4 carries a symmetric obstruction theory of virtual dimension zero, but the virtual fundamental class is not a number; it is a homology class in the Chow group of the moduli space. The DT invariants are defined as the pushforward of the virtual class to a point, which requires an orientation of the obstruction theory. The orientation data is a choice of square root of the determinant line bundle of the obstruction theory, and different choices differ by a sign. The DT invariants for CY4 are integers that depend on the choice of orientation. The Pandharipande-Thomas (PT) stable pairs for CY4 are pairs (F, s) where F is a pure two-dimensional sheaf and s: 𝒪X → F is a section with zero-dimensional cokernel. The PT invariants count stable pairs with a fixed Chern character. The DT/PT correspondence for CY4 relates the DT invariants of ideal sheaves of curves to the PT invariants of stable pairs of surfaces. This correspondence was conjectured by Cao and Maulik (2020) and proven for local CY4 (total space of a canonical bundle over a three-fold) using the wall-crossing formula in the derived category of the three-fold base. The wall-crossing formula for CY4 involves the Joyce-Song generalized DT invariants for the 3-Calabi-Yau category of the base. The DT/PT correspondence for CY4 takes the form ZDT(q) = M(−q)χ(X) ZPT(q), where M(q) is the MacMahon function and χ(X) is the topological Euler characteristic of X. For local ℙ3 (the total space of 𝒪(−4) → ℙ3), the DT and PT invariants are computed by the topological vertex for CY4, which is a generalization of the topological vertex for CY3 using the representation theory of the quantum toroidal algebra of 𝔤𝔩1 with an extra parameter. The CY4 topological vertex computes the partition function of the 6d (1,0) theory on the M5-brane, and the DT/PT correspondence is the statement that the M5-brane partition function is invariant under the exchange of M2-branes and M5-branes.

DT Invariants and the BPS Spectrum of 4d N=4 Super Yang-Mills: The S-Duality and the BPS Spectrum

Four-dimensional N=4 super Yang-Mills theory with gauge group G has an exact S-duality that exchanges the gauge coupling τ = θ/2π + 4πi/g2 with −1/τ and the gauge group G with its Langlands dual G∨. The BPS spectrum of the theory consists of dyons with electric and magnetic charges (e, m) ∈ Γe × Γm, where Γe and Γm are the weight and coweight lattices of G. The BPS degeneracies Ω(e, m; τ) are integers that count the number of BPS states with given charges. The S-duality predicts that Ω(e, m; τ) = Ω(m, −e; −1/τ) under the exchange of electric and magnetic charges. The BPS spectrum of N=4 SYM is captured by the DT invariants of the moduli space of Higgs bundles on a Riemann surface C, which is the moduli space of solutions to Hitchin's equations. The DT invariants of the moduli space of Higgs bundles are the BPS degeneracies of the 4d theory compactified on C×S1. The wall-crossing formula for the BPS spectrum as τ varies is the Kontsevich-Soibelman wall-crossing formula for the DT invariants of the moduli space of Higgs bundles. The S-duality of the 4d theory is the mirror symmetry of the moduli space of Higgs bundles (the Hitchin system), which exchanges the moduli space of G-Higgs bundles with the moduli space of G∨-Higgs bundles. The BPS degeneracies Ω(e, m; τ) are the coefficients of the DT partition function of the moduli space of Higgs bundles, which is a generating function of the Euler characteristics of the moduli spaces of stable Higgs bundles weighted by the Behrend function. The categorification of the BPS spectrum is the cohomology of the moduli space of Higgs bundles, which carries an action of the BPS Lie algebra (the Lie algebra of the CoHA of the 3-Calabi-Yau category of the Hitchin system). The S-duality acts on the cohomology as the geometric Langlands correspondence, which exchanges the category of D-modules on the moduli space of G-bundles with the category of coherent sheaves on the moduli space of G∨-Higgs bundles. The DT invariants of the moduli space of Higgs bundles for G = SU(2) and C = ℙ1 with four punctures are the BPS degeneracies of the N=2* theory, which are given by the refined topological vertex for the resolved conifold with four external legs.
