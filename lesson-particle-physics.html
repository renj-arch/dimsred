Particle Zoo
Quarks, Leptons, and the Three Generations of Matter
The Standard Model is a quantum field theory with gauge group SU(3)_C × SU(2)_L × U(1)_Y. Matter particles are fermions (spin-1/2) organized into three generations. Quarks (6 flavors): up (u), down (d) — first generation; charm (c), strange (s) — second generation; top (t), bottom (b) — third generation. Each quark carries color charge (red, green, blue) under SU(3)_C and fractional electric charge (+2/3 for u, c, t; -1/3 for d, s, b). Leptons (6 particles): electron e?, electron neutrino ?_e; muon µ?, muon neutrino ?_µ; tau t?, tau neutrino ?_t. Leptons do not carry color charge; charged leptons have electric charge -1, neutrinos are neutral. Antiparticles exist for each fermion with opposite quantum numbers. Hadrons are composite particles made of quarks: baryons (three quarks, e.g., proton = uud, neutron = udd) and mesons (quark-antiquark pairs, e.g., pion p? = u d¯). The confinement phenomenon: quarks are never observed in isolation; they are always confined within hadrons through the strong force mediated by gluons. The running coupling of QCD: a_s(Q²) decreases with energy (asymptotic freedom), allowing perturbative calculations at high energies. Deep inelastic scattering experiments at SLAC in 1969 provided the first evidence for quarks as point-like constituents of nucleons.
Matter consists of three generations of quarks and leptons; the Standard Model organizes them into a gauge theory with SU(3)_C × SU(2)_L × U(1)_Y symmetry.

?? CP Violation, Neutrino Oscillations, and Beyond the Standard Model

Frontiers
CP Violation, Neutrino Mass, Dark Matter, Supersymmetry, and Grand Unification
CP violation (combined charge conjugation and parity) was discovered in K°-K¯° mixing by Cronin and Fitch (1964). In the Standard Model, CP violation arises from the complex phase in the CKM matrix (Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa, 3×3 unitary matrix describing quark mixing: V_{CKM} = [[V_{ud}, V_{us}, V_{ub}], [V_{cd}, V_{cs}, V_{cb}], [V_{td}, V_{ts}, V_{tb}]]). The single irreducible phase d causes CP violation in weak interactions. The unitarity triangle tests the consistency of the CKM description. CP violation is crucial for explaining the baryon asymmetry of the universe (why matter dominates antimatter) via Sakharov's three conditions. Neutrino oscillations: neutrinos produced in one flavor (?_e, ?_µ, ?_t) can be detected in a different flavor, requiring non-zero neutrino masses. The PMNS matrix parametrizes the mixing of neutrino flavor eigenstates and mass eigenstates. The solar neutrino problem (deficit of ?_e from the Sun) was resolved by the discovery of oscillations at SNO and Super-Kamiokande. Beyond the Standard Model directions: supersymmetry (SUSY) — each fermion has a boson superpartner (and vice versa), stabilizes the Higgs mass, and provides a dark matter candidate (the lightest neutralino ?~1°). Grand Unified Theories (GUTs) — unification of SU(3)_C × SU(2)_L × U(1)_Y into a larger gauge group like SU(5) or SO(10) at ~10¹6 GeV. Dark matter: the 26% of the universe's energy density not accounted for by Standard Model particles. String theory: fundamental objects are 1-dimensional strings rather than point particles, incorporating gravity and quantum mechanics.
CP violation explains matter-antimatter asymmetry; neutrino oscillations prove physics beyond the Standard Model; SUSY, GUTs, and string theory are proposed extensions with testable predictions.
