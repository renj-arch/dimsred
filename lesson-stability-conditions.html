Stability Conditions — Bridgeland Stability, the Hall Algebra & Donaldson-Thomas Invariants

Stability conditions on triangulated categories, introduced by Tom Bridgeland in 2002, provide a powerful geometric framework for understanding the structure of derived categories. A stability condition σ = (Z, P) consists of a central charge Z: K(D) → ℂ — a group homomorphism from the Grothendieck group — and a slicing P(φ) ⊂ D, a full additive subcategory of semistable objects of phase φ ∈ ℝ, satisfying axioms that generalize the Harder-Narasimhan property of vector bundles on curves. The set Stab(D) of all stability conditions on D forms a complex manifold (Bridgeland's theorem, 2007), and its connected components encode rich information about the derived category. As the stability parameter varies, the set of semistable objects changes only when crossing real codimension-one walls — the wall-crossing phenomenon that governs the behavior of Donaldson-Thomas (DT) invariants, integer counts of sheaves on Calabi-Yau threefolds. The motivic Hall algebra (Joyce) categorifies the cluster algebra and provides a unified setting for DT invariants of quivers via the Kontsevich-Soibelman wall-crossing formula. Stability conditions on derived categories of coherent sheaves (on K3 surfaces, on ℙ²) and on Fukaya categories connect to mirror symmetry, where the space of stability conditions of a K3 surface is identified with the Teichmüller space of its mirror (Bridgeland's K3 identification). The Cecotti-Vafa approach to wall-crossing in N=2 supersymmetric field theories and the tt* equations provide a physical realization of the same structure.

"Stability conditions give a geometric structure to derived categories."
— Tom Bridgeland
Part 1

📏 Triangulated Categories and the Definition of Stability

Triangulated
Triangulated Categories, Slicings, and the Bridgeland Axioms
Let D be a triangulated category with Grothendieck group K(D). A stability condition σ = (Z, P) on D consists of:

• A group homomorphism Z: K(D) → ℂ (the central charge) such that for any nonzero E ∈ P(φ), we have Z(E) = m(E)·e^{iπφ} with m(E) > 0 (the mass).

• For each φ ∈ ℝ, a full additive subcategory P(φ) ⊂ D whose objects are called semistable of phase φ. The objects of P(φ) are also called σ-semistable, and the simple objects of P(φ) are called σ-stable.

Axioms:
(i) If 0 ≠ E ∈ P(φ), then Z(E) = m(E)·e^{iπφ} for some m(E) > 0.
(ii) P(φ+1) = P(φ)[1] (the shift functor rotates phases).
(iii) Hom-vanishing: Hom_D(A, B) = 0 whenever A ∈ P(φ₁), B ∈ P(φ₂) with φ₁ > φ₂.
(iv) Harder-Narasimhan (HN) property: every nonzero object E ∈ D admits a finite filtration 0 = E₀ ⊂ E₁ ⊂ ... ⊂ E_n = E such that each HN factor F_i = E_i/E_{i-1} is semistable and the phases satisfy φ₁ > φ₂ > ... > φ_n. This filtration is unique and functorial.

The heart of the stability condition is the full subcategory A(σ) = P((0,1]) (the extension-closed subcategory generated by semistable objects with phase in (0,1]) — it is an abelian category (the heart of the t-structure). The central charge Z defines a stability function on A(σ) in the sense of King's stability for abelian categories.
A stability condition packages a t-structure (the heart) and a central charge into a single geometric object that controls the homological algebra of the triangulated category.
Part 2

🌍 The Space of Stability Conditions

Moduli
Stab(D) as a Complex Manifold, Wall-Crossing, and Chamber Structure
Let Stab(D) denote the set of all stability conditions on D. The topology on Stab(D) is the coarsest such that for every object E ∈ D, the map σ ↦ Z(E) ∈ ℂ is continuous, and the phases of the HN factors vary continuously. Equivalently, Stab(D) is a metric space via the Bridgeland distance d(σ₁, σ₂) = sup_{0≠E∈D} { |φ⁻_σ₂(E) − φ⁻_σ₁(E)|, |φ⁺_σ₂(E) − φ⁺_σ₁(E)|, |log m_σ₂(E)/m_σ₁(E)| } where φ⁻ and φ⁺ are the minimal and maximal phases in the HN filtration.

Bridgeland's theorem (2007): Stab(D) is a complex manifold of dimension equal to the rank of K(D). The forgetful map π: Stab(D) → Hom_ℤ(K(D), ℂ), sending (Z, P) ↦ Z, is a local homeomorphism — it gives the complex structure on Stab(D).

Group actions:
• GL⁺(2, ℝ)-action: for g ∈ GL⁺(2, ℝ), define (Z, P) ↦ (g∘Z, P_g) where P_g(φ) = P(φ') for appropriately rotated phases. This rotates the central charge.
• Aut(D)-action: any autoequivalence Φ ∈ Aut(D) acts by (Z, P) ↦ (Z∘Φ^{-1}_*, Φ(P)).

Wall-crossing: As σ varies continuously, the set of σ-semistable objects is locally constant except at points where some object E satisfies Z(E)/Z(E') ∈ ℝ_{>0} for another object E' (i.e., their phases align). These loci are real codimension-one submanifolds called walls. The complement of the walls is a union of open chambers where the set of semistable objects is constant. When crossing a wall, a semistable object may become unstable and decompose into objects with aligned phases — this is the wall-crossing phenomenon central to the theory of DT invariants.
The space of stability conditions Stab(D) is a finite-dimensional complex manifold whose chamber-decomposition encodes the birational geometry of the moduli spaces of objects in D.
Part 3

🧮 Donaldson-Thomas Invariants

DT
DT Invariants, Wall-Crossing Formulas, and the KS Transformation
Donaldson-Thomas (DT) invariants are integer-valued counts of sheaves on a Calabi-Yau threefold X, defined originally via the moduli space of ideal sheaves of curves (Donaldson-Thomas, 1998). In the derived category perspective, DT invariants are the Euler characteristics of the moduli spaces of stable objects in D^b(Coh(X)). Using a Bridgeland stability condition whose central charge involves the Chern character and the Mukai vector v = ch(E)·√Td(X), the DT invariants are defined for each numerical class v as:

DT_v(σ) = Σ_{[E] ∈ M_σ(v)} (−1)^{dim Ext¹(E,E)}

where M_σ(v) is the moduli space of σ-semistable objects with Mukai vector v.

Joyce-Song theory (2012): DT invariants are defined for any stability condition on a Calabi-Yau 3-fold and satisfy a universal wall-crossing formula: when crossing a wall, the generating function of DT invariants transforms by a Poisson automorphism of the quantum torus (the algebra of formal variables indexed by K(D) with commutation relations X_v X_w = (−1)^{χ(v,w)} X_{v+w}).

Kontsevich-Soibelman wall-crossing formula (2008): The crossing of a wall with a primitive charge v produces a transformation of the form:

U_v = exp( Σ_{n≥1} DT_{nv} · X_{nv} / (n²) )

and the product over all walls of these operators (ordered by phase) gives a representation of the fundamental group of the complement of the discriminant in the space of stability parameters. This is the KS transformation — a factorization identity in the quantum torus that encodes all DT invariants simultaneously.

For quivers with a potential, the DT invariants are computed by the motivic generating function (the motivic DT invariant of Kontsevich-Soibelman) which takes values in the Hall algebra of the category of representations of the quiver.
DT invariants are the enumerative invariants of Calabi-Yau threefolds that count stable sheaves; their wall-crossing is governed by a universal factorization in the quantum torus (the KS formula).
Part 4

🔥 Hall Algebras and Cluster Integrals

Hall
Hall Algebras, Motivic Hall Algebra, Cluster Algebras, and Mirror Symmetry
Let A be an abelian category satisfying the finiteness condition (every object has finite length and Ext groups are finite). The Hall algebra H(A) is the vector space of functions on isomorphism classes of objects with convolution product:

(f ∗ g)(E) = Σ_{X ⊂ E} f(E/X) g(X)

where the sum runs over all subobjects X ⊂ E. This product is associative. For the category of representations of a quiver Q over a finite field 𝔽_q, the Hall algebra H(Rep_𝔽_q(Q)) recovers the quantum group U_q(𝔫) (Ringel's theorem, 1990) — the positive part of the quantum enveloping algebra associated to the quiver.

The motivic Hall algebra (Joyce, 2006) replaces functions on isomorphism classes with the Grothendieck ring of stacks, giving a refined categorification. For a Calabi-Yau 3-fold, the motivic Hall algebra of coherent sheaves encodes the DT invariants as certain elements (the Hall algebra DT invariants).

Cluster algebra connection: The cluster variables in a cluster algebra (Fomin-Zelevinsky) correspond to certain rigid objects in the Hall algebra: for a quiver Q, the cluster monomials are represented by the indecomposable objects in the cluster category (the categorification of cluster algebras by Buan-Marsh-Reineke-Reiten-Todorov, 2006). The Feigin-Frenkel duality relates the Hall algebra of a quiver to the affine Lie algebra via the geometric Langlands correspondence.

Mirror symmetry: On a K3 surface, Bridgeland (2008) identified the space of stability conditions Stab(D^b(Coh(K3))) with the Teichmüller space of the mirror K3 surface — a concrete realization of homological mirror symmetry. The Fukaya category of a Calabi-Yau manifold carries a stability condition whose central charge is given by the periods of the holomorphic volume form — connecting stability to the complex structure moduli space (the mirror map).

Cecotti-Vafa approach: In N=2 supersymmetric field theories, the BPS spectrum is governed by wall-crossing formulas identical to those of DT invariants. The tt* equations (topological-antitopological fusion) give a differential geometric realization of the same structure, linking stability conditions to the special Kähler geometry of the moduli space of vacua and to the Frobenius manifolds of Dubrovin.
Hall algebras categorify quantum groups and cluster algebras; combined with stability conditions they provide the algebraic foundation for DT invariants and mirror symmetry.
