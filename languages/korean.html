  • Family: Koreanic — a language isolate (the one living member).
  • Speakers: ~81M (South Korea, North Korea, Yanbian, diaspora).
  • Script: Hangul (한글) — a featural alphabet, voted by linguists as the most scientific writing system.

📖 Lesson 1 — Hangul: the consonants (자음)

Building syllable blocks

Consonants and vowels stack into blocks: consonant top-left, vowel right or under. ㅎ + ㅏ + ㄴ → 한 (han). Blocks, then words: 한 + 국 + 어 → 한국어 (Korean language, hangugeo).

BlockLettersBlockLetters
한ㅎ+ㅏ+ㄴ국ㄱ+ㅜ+ㄱ
어ㅇ+ㅓ사ㅅ+ㅏ
람ㄹ+ㅏ+ㅁ음ㅇ+ㅡ+ㅁ
💡 Pronunciation quirk: when ㄹ alone starts a syllable block it's an "r"-flap; when it ends a block it's an "l". 런던 (London) ends with ㄴ not ㄹ — Romanized "Leondeon".

🔉 Lesson 3 — Sound rules that change words

  • Batchim blockers: a syllable ending in a consonant blocks the next syllable's lenition; ㅅ after a final ㅎ etc. Pronounce ㄱㄷㅂㅅㅈ at the end of a block as "k t p t t".
  • Liaison (연음): a final consonant borrows the next syllable's vowel: 있어요 (isseoyo, "there is") is heard as 이써요 i-sseo-yo.
  • Aspiration after ㅎ: ㄱ+ㅎ → ㅋ, ㅂ+ㅎ → ㅍ: 좋아요 → 조아요 joayo (good, the ㅎ vanishes).
  • No Korean word starts with ㄹ (r) in native words, and ㅇ is silent at block start — the two letters are invisible partners.
  • Tensing: after a final ㅂ, ㄱ, ㄷ, the next plain consonant turns tense (ㄱ→ㄲ).
  • Pitch is fixed: Korean has no lexical tones, but ㅏㅗ (bright) vs ㅓㅜㅡ (dark) vowels shape native "sound symbolism" — 밝다 (bright) pairs with 어둡다 (dark) by vowel switch.

🧩 Grammar Reference — the SOV skeleton

저는 한국어를 공부해요 — I (topic) Korean (object) study. Subject first, verb last, everything else tagged with a particle.

The core particles

Politeness — the verb ending ladder

LevelEndingWho uses itExample
formal polite-습니다/-니까news, announcements, strangers갑니다 (I go)
polite casual-아요/-어요most everyday conversation가요 (I go)
blunt-아/-어close friends, lyrics가 (go)
formal plain-다books, diaries, news headlines간다 (goes)

Verbs: present → past → future

  • Present: 가다 → 가요 (I go / am going — one form covers both).
  • Past: 가요 → 갔어요 (went). Rule: add 았/었, then 요.
  • Future: 갈 거예요 (will go) — "go-ing thing-is".
  • Negative: 안 가요 (don't go) / 못 가요 (can't go); polite formal: 가지 않아요.
  • Honorific: speakers add -시- to verbs about someone respected: 드세요 (please eat, for a senior).

There is / isn't — a different verb

Native "exist" is 있다 (isseuda): 사과가 있어요 (there are apples). To have something: 았/있 again as the possessive: 시간이 없어요 (I don't have time).

Counters and the two number systems

Native Korean numbers (1–99) count things: 한 명 (one person), 두 개 (two items), 세 병 (three bottles). Sino-Korean numbers count money, dates, phone numbers: 일 (1), 이 (2), 삼 (3), 사 (4), 오 (5), 육 (6), 칠 (7), 팔 (8), 구 (9), 십 (10). 10 items = 열 개, 20 items = 스무 개.

Joining clauses

And: -고 (가고 = "go and"); but: -지만 (가지만); because: -서 (가서 = "go, so"); if: -면; while: -(으)면서. Korean chains verbs like LEGOs — a sentence may have five connected verbs before one final ending.

💡 Verb endings are the whole grammar. Memorize the polite -아요/-어요 set as your default and you can survive any conversation.

🧠 Cultural Notes & Next Steps

  • Wait a second — 나/너 vs 저/당신: "you" (너/당신) is rare; Koreans call people by name + title, or just drop the subject (저, "I") entirely.
  • Speak politely first, relax later: strangers start at -요. A friend asking you to switch to 반말 (blunt form) is a mark of closeness.
  • Age is not a secret: politeness level depends on relative age; the second question after "nice to meet you" is usually age/grade.
  • Konglish is everywhere: 컴퓨터 (keompyuteo, computer), 피자 (pija), 셀카 (selfie, "selka"). Every loanword you already know is free vocabulary.
  • Two measures of gratitude: 감사합니다 is the safe one; 고맙습니다 is warmer. Never say "no" with just 아니요 to elders — soften with 아니에요/괜찮아요.
