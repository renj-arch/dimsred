Schramm-Loewner Evolution — Conformal Maps, Random Curves & Critical Phenomena

Deep dive into Schramm-Loewner Evolution (SLE) — the Loewner differential equation (encoding a simple curve γ: [0,∞) → ℍ in the upper half-plane via conformal maps g_t: ℍ \ γ[0,t] → ℍ satisfying ∂_t g_t(z) = 2/(g_t(z) − W_t) with g_0(z) = z and the driving function W_t = √κB_t — a Brownian motion with speed κ), the chordal SLE_κ (the random curve whose driving process is Brownian motion of variance κ), the radial SLE (defined on the unit disk with the driving process on the boundary), the phase transitions in SLE (for 0 ≤ κ ≤ 4, SLE_κ is a simple curve; for 4 < κ < 8, it is a self-touching curve; for κ ≥ 8, it is space-filling), the dimension of the SLE trace d = min(2, 1 + κ/8) (proved by Beffara), the restriction property (SLE_κ for κ ≤ 8/3 is the unique random curve satisfying conformal restriction — the distribution is invariant under conformal maps that preserve the domain), the relationship to the Gaussian free field (the SLE_κ curve can be coupled with the Gaussian free field as level lines — the Sheffield–Dubédat coupling), the scaling limits of critical statistical mechanics models: SLE_2 = loop-erased random walk, SLE_3 = critical Ising model interfaces (proved by Smirnov, 2010), SLE_4 = harmonic explorer (GFF level lines), SLE_6 = critical percolation (proved by Smirnov, 2001), SLE_8 = uniform spanning tree, and the connection to the Brownian map (the scaling limit of random planar maps as the SLE_8-decorated Liouville quantum gravity).

"SLE is the canonical process of random conformally invariant curves in the plane, describing the scaling limits of many critical lattice models."
— Oded Schramm
Part 1

📐 The Loewner Equation and Chordal SLE

Loewner
The Loewner Differential Equation, Driving Functions, and the Definition of SLE
The Loewner equation, introduced by Charles Loewner in 1923 as a tool to study the Bieberbach conjecture, describes the evolution of conformal maps from a slit domain to the upper half-plane. Let γ: [0, T) → ℍ be a simple curve starting at the origin (γ(0) = 0) with γ(0, ∞) ⊂ ℍ. The conformal map g_t: H_t → ℍ where H_t = ℍ \ γ[0, t] is the unique conformal bijection satisfying the hydrodynamic normalization: g_t(z) = z + 2t/z + O(1/z²) as z → ∞. The Loewner equation: ∂_t g_t(z) = 2/(g_t(z) − W_t), where W_t = g_t(γ(t)) ∈ ℝ is the driving function. Conversely, given any continuous driving function W_t: [0, ∞) → ℝ, the Loewner equation defines a family of conformal maps g_t, and the tip γ(t) = lim_{y→0⁺} g_t^{-1}(W_t + iy). Schramm–Loewner Evolution (SLE_κ) (Schramm, 2000) is the random curve obtained by taking the driving function to be W_t = √κ B_t, where B_t is a standard Brownian motion and κ ≥ 0 is a parameter. The Schramm principle: if a random family of curves in simply connected domains is conformally invariant in law and satisfies the domain Markov property (the conditional distribution of the curve in the remaining domain is the same as the original law), then it must be SLE_κ for some κ. The locality property: for κ = 6, SLE_6 is local — it does not feel the boundary of the domain before hitting it (critical percolation). The restriction property: for κ ≤ 8/3, the law of SLE_κ in a domain D restricted to a subdomain D' is absolutely continuous with respect to SLE_κ in D'.
SLE is defined by the Loewner equation driven by Brownian motion; conformal invariance and the domain Markov property uniquely characterize it among random curves.
Part 2

🧮 Phase Transitions and the Geometry of SLE

Phases
The Three Phases of SLE: Simple Curves, Self-Touching Curves, and Space-Filling Curves
The parameter κ controls the fractal geometry of the SLE curve γ. There are three distinct phases: (1) 0 ≤ κ ≤ 4: SLE_κ is a simple curve (it does not intersect itself). The curve is a continuous, non-self-intersecting path from 0 to ∞ in ℍ. Examples: SLE_0 is deterministic (a vertical line), SLE_2 corresponds to the scaling limit of the loop-erased random walk (LERW), SLE_4 corresponds to the harmonic explorer (the level lines of the Gaussian free field). (2) 4 < κ < 8: SLE_κ is self-touching but not space-filling. The curve intersects itself but does not fill the domain. The boundary of the curve has Hausdorff dimension d = 1 + κ/8, while the trace has dimension d = 1 + κ/8 as well (which equals 2 only at κ = 8). Example: SLE_6 corresponds to critical percolation (the boundary of the percolation cluster). (3) κ ≥ 8: SLE_κ is space-filling — the curve fills the entire domain (its image has Hausdorff dimension 2). Example: SLE_8 corresponds to the uniform spanning tree (the Peano curve that traces the boundary of a spanning tree). The Hausdorff dimension of the SLE_κ trace (Beffara, 2008) for κ ≤ 8 is d = min(2, 1 + κ/8). The intersection exponents for SLE (Lawler–Schramm–Werner, 2001) describe the probability that two SLE curves avoid each other, and these exponents are used to compute the multifractal spectrum of the curve. The duality for SLE: SLE_κ and SLE_{16/κ} are related by the κ → 16/κ duality (for κ < 4, SLE_κ is the outer boundary of SLE_{16/κ}).
The SLE parameter κ determines the phase of the curve: simple for κ ≤ 4, self-touching for 4 < κ < 8, and space-filling for κ ≥ 8; the fractal dimension is d = min(2, 1 + κ/8).
Part 3

🔬 SLE as the Scaling Limit of Discrete Models

Scaling Limits
Critical Percolation, Loop-Erased Random Walk, the Ising Model, and the Harmonic Explorer
SLE is believed to describe the scaling limit of interfaces in critical statistical mechanics models — as the lattice spacing a → 0, the random interfaces converge in law to SLE_κ. Key results: Critical percolation → SLE_6 (Smirnov, 2001): the exploration path of critical site percolation on a triangular lattice converges to SLE_6. This was the first rigorous proof of convergence of a discrete model to SLE, using the Cardy–Carleson formula (the probability that a percolation cluster connects two boundary arcs is given by the hypergeometric function satisfy the Cardy equation). Smirnov showed that crossing probabilities converge to the conformally invariant Cardy formula, and the interface converges to SLE_6. Loop-erased random walk → SLE_2 (Lawler–Schramm–Werner, 2004): the outer boundary of the LERW (a simple curve obtained by erasing loops from a simple random walk) converges to SLE_2. Uniform spanning tree → SLE_8 (Lawler–Schramm–Werner, 2004): the UST Peano curve (the space-filling curve tracing the boundary of the spanning tree) converges to SLE_8. Critical Ising model → SLE_3 (Chelkak–Smirnov, 2012): the spin interface of the critical Ising model converges to SLE_3, and the fermionic observable satisfies a discrete Cauchy–Riemann equation. Harmonic explorer → SLE_4 (Schramm–Sheffield, 2005): the harmonic explorer (a random curve defined by evolving the harmonic measure) converges to SLE_4; the level lines of the Gaussian free field (GFF) are SLE_4 curves (the Dubédat–Schramm–Sheffield coupling). The Schramm–Loewner evolution also appears as the scaling limit of the XXZ spin chain, the ABP model, and the random cluster model (via the Fortuin–Kasteleyn representation).
SLE is the universal scaling limit of interfaces in critical statistical mechanics: percolation (SLE_6), Ising (SLE_3), LERW (SLE_2), and UST (SLE_8) are all described by SLE with specific κ values.
Part 4

🌌 The Restriction Property, the Gaussian Free Field, and Liouville Quantum Gravity

GFF
The Gaussian Free Field Coupling, the Restriction Property, and the Brownian Map
The Gaussian free field (GFF) is the natural Gaussian random distribution on functions φ: D → ℝ with Dirichlet energy ∫_D |∇φ|² dx dy. Its law is the Gaussian measure with covariance G(x,y) = (2π)^{-1} log(1/|x−y|) + harmonic correction. The Sheffield–Dubédat coupling (Schramm–Sheffield, 2009): the level lines of the GFF are SLE_4 curves. For κ ≠ 4, the contour lines of the GFF with a certain height gap are SLE_κ curves coupled with the GFF. The restriction property characterizes SLE_κ for κ ≤ 8/3: a random curve Γ in a simply connected domain D satisfies the restriction property if for any D' ⊂ D, the law of Γ conditioned to stay in D' equals the law of SLE_κ in D' (up to a weight given by the Brownian loop measure). The imaginary geometry (Miller–Sheffield, 2012) constructs SLE_κ as flow lines of the complex Gaussian free field h + iχ where χ = (2/√κ) − (√κ)/2. The Liouville quantum gravity (LQG) (David, Distler–Kawai, 1992) is a random Riemannian metric on a domain D defined by e^{γ φ} times the Euclidean metric, where φ is the GFF and γ ∈ [0,2). The Brownian map (Le Gall, 2007; Miermont, 2013) is the scaling limit of random planar maps (uniform quadrangulations of the sphere) — it is a random metric space homeomorphic to the 2-sphere with Hausdorff dimension 4. The conformal welding of LQG with SLE produces a canonical coupling between random planar maps and SLE curves (the quantum Loewner evolution of Miller–Sheffield). The Sheffield bijection (peanosphere): the mating of trees (the SLE-decorated LQG sphere) corresponds to the Brownian map decorated by the SLE_8 curve.
The GFF is the canonical random distribution on surfaces; its coupling with SLE curves (imaginary geometry) and Liouville quantum gravity provides a deep connection between random conformal geometry and random planar maps.
