Homological Mirror Symmetry — Fukaya Categories, Derived Categories & the HMS Conjecture

Deep dive into Homological Mirror Symmetry — the Fukaya category Fuk(X) of a symplectic manifold (X, ω) whose objects are Lagrangian submanifolds with flat U(1) connections and whose morphisms are Floer cohomology groups, the A∞-category structure (Stasheff's associahedra governing homotopy associativity of composition), the derived category D^b Coh(Y) of coherent sheaves on a complex algebraic variety Y, the HMS conjecture (Kontsevich 1994) stating that for a mirror pair (X, Y), the derived Fukaya category of X is equivalent to the derived category of coherent sheaves on Y, the SYZ fibration (Strominger–Yau–Zaslow) interpreting mirror symmetry as T-duality along special Lagrangian tori, the Hodge theoretic aspects (variation of Hodge structures, the Griffiths period map), and applications to enumerative geometry (Gromov–Witten invariants, Donaldson–Thomas invariants via the DT/GW correspondence), and the categorical crepant resolution conjecture.

"Mirror symmetry is a phenomenon at the interface of geometry and physics in which two seemingly distinct geometries are secretly the same."
— Maxim Kontsevich
Part 1

🌀 A∞-Categories and the Fukaya Category

A∞ Structures
Stasheff's Associahedra, A∞-Algebras, and the Fukaya Category Construction
An A∞-category (Stasheff, 1963) is a category enriched over chain complexes with composition maps m_k: Hom(A_{k−1}, A_k) ⊗ … ⊗ Hom(A_0, A_1) → Hom(A_0, A_k) of degree 2−k satisfying the A∞-relations: Σ_{i+j+k = n} (−1)^{ε} m_{i+1+k} (id^{⊗i} ⊗ m_j ⊗ id^{⊗k}) = 0. For k=1, m_1² = 0 (the differential). For k=2, m_1 m_2 = m_2 (m_1 ⊗ id + id ⊗ m_1) (Leibniz rule). For k=3, the associator m_2 (m_2 ⊗ id − id ⊗ m_2) = m_1 m_3 + m_3 (m_1 ⊗ id⊗ id + id ⊗ m_1 ⊗ id + id ⊗ id ⊗ m_1) — the failure of associativity is controlled by m_3, which is itself chain homotopic to zero via m_4, and so on. The coherence is encoded by the Stasheff associahedra K_n, whose vertices correspond to the ways of parenthesizing n arguments. The Fukaya category Fuk(X) of a symplectic manifold (X, ω) has objects: Lagrangian submanifolds L ⊂ X (dim L = ½ dim X, ω|_L = 0) equipped with a spin structure and a flat U(1) connection (a local system). Morphisms: Hom(L_0, L_1) = CF^*(L_0, L_1), the Lagrangian Floer cohomology chain complex generated by intersection points p ∈ L_0 ∩ L_1. The composition m_2: CF^*(L_0, L_1) ⊗ CF^*(L_1, L_2) → CF^*(L_0, L_2) counts pseudoholomorphic triangles with boundary on the three Lagrangians. Higher compositions m_k count pseudoholomorphic (k+1)-gons. The weak bounding cochains and obstruction theory (Fukaya–Oh–Ohta–Ono) handle the obstructions to defining A∞-structures when b_1(L) ≠ 0, using the Maurer–Cartan equation m(1) + m_1(b) + m_2(b, b) + … = 0 for bounding cochain b ∈ H^1(L; ℂ). The Donaldson–Fukaya category includes objects with unitary local systems and bounding cochains.
The Fukaya category encodes the symplectic geometry of Lagrangian submanifolds through an A∞-structure whose higher products count pseudoholomorphic polygons.
Part 2

🌿 Derived Categories of Coherent Sheaves

Derived Categories
The Bounded Derived Category, Triangulated Structure, and the Reconstruction Theorem
For a smooth projective variety Y over ℂ, let Coh(Y) be the abelian category of coherent sheaves. The bounded derived category D^b Coh(Y) has objects bounded complexes of coherent sheaves, with quasi-isomorphisms inverted. It is a triangulated category with the shift functor [1] and distinguished triangles X → Y → Z → X[1] coming from short exact sequences in Coh(Y). The Ext groups: Hom_{D^b}(A, B[k]) = Ext^k(A, B). The Serre functor S: D^b Coh(Y) → D^b Coh(Y) given by S(ℱ) = ℱ ⊗ ω_Y [dim Y] satisfies Hom(A, B) ≅ Hom(B, S(A))^* (Serre duality). The Orlov spectrum and Bondal–Kapranov enhanced triangulated categories. Semiorthogonal decompositions D^b Coh(Y) = ⟨𝒜_1, …, 𝒜_n⟩ where each 𝒜_i is a full triangulated subcategory and Hom(𝒜_j, 𝒜_i) = 0 for j > i. For projective space ℙ^n, D^b Coh(ℙ^n) = ⟨𝒪, 𝒪(1), …, 𝒪(n)⟩ (Beilinson's exceptional collection). The Reconstruction theorem (Bondal–Orlov): a smooth projective variety Y with ample canonical or anticanonical bundle can be reconstructed from its derived category D^b Coh(Y) up to isomorphism — the Néron–Severi lattice, the Chow motive and the Mukai vector v(ℱ) = ch(ℱ) · √td(Y) play key roles. The Fourier–Mukai transform: for two varieties Y₁, Y₂, a Fourier–Mukai kernel 𝒫 ∈ D^b Coh(Y₁ × Y₂) defines an equivalence Φ_𝒫: D^b Coh(Y₁) → D^b Coh(Y₂) by Φ_𝒫(−) = Rπ_{2*}(π₁^*(−) ⊗^L 𝒫). Orlov's theorem: any fully faithful exact functor D^b Coh(Y₁) → D^b Coh(Y₂) with left and right adjoints is a Fourier–Mukai transform.
The derived category of coherent sheaves is a fundamental invariant of an algebraic variety, encoding its geometry through triangulated and Fourier–Mukai structures.
Part 3

🔮 The Homological Mirror Symmetry Conjecture

HMS
Kontsevich's Conjecture, Calabi–Yau Varieties, and Mirror Pairs
The Homological Mirror Symmetry conjecture (Kontsevich, 1994, ICM Zürich): For a mirror pair of Calabi–Yau varieties (X, Y) of dimension n, there is a quasi-equivalence of A∞-categories D^π Fuk(X) ≅ D^b Coh(Y), where D^π Fuk(X) is the split-closed derived Fukaya category (the idempotent completion of the A∞-category of twisted complexes). The mirror map identifies the complex moduli of Y (the space of complex structures) with the Kähler moduli of X (the space of symplectic forms and B-fields). The SYZ conjecture (Strominger–Yau–Zaslow, 1996): Mirror symmetry arises from a special Lagrangian torus fibration f: X → B and its dual fibration f^∨: Y → B (T-duality). Each smooth fiber is a special Lagrangian torus T^n ⊂ X, and the mirror Y is obtained by dualizing the torus fibers. The Fukaya category of X is built from Lagrangian tori (fibers of f) and their intersections; the derived category of Y is built from skyscraper sheaves of points (dual tori). The Gross–Siebert program builds mirror pairs via tropical geometry and log structures — extending SYZ to the degenerate case (walls, scattering diagrams, and slab functions). The Kontsevich–Soibelman approach: the mirror is the Maurer–Cartan space of the Fukaya category — the set of solutions to the A∞-Maurer–Cartan equation modulo gauge equivalence. The Seidel representation: the Dehn twist around a Lagrangian sphere L ≅ S^n in a symplectic Calabi–Yau of dimension n gives an autoequivalence of Fuk(X), corresponding under mirror symmetry to the spherical twist around the corresponding sheaf on Y.
The HMS conjecture provides a categorical framework for mirror symmetry, equating the symplectic geometry of one manifold with the complex geometry of its mirror.
Part 4

⚡ Evidence and Applications

Proofs
HMS for Tori, Quartic Surfaces, and the Categorical Crepant Resolution Conjecture
The first proved case: HMS for elliptic curves (Polishchuk–Zaslow, 1997). For an elliptic curve E_τ = ℂ/(ℤ + τℤ), its mirror is the same elliptic curve E_{τ^∨} with the dual complex parameter. The Fukaya category of E_τ is generated by Lagrangian branes: straight lines of rational slope with flat connections. The derived category D^b Coh(E_{τ^∨}) has an exceptional collection generating it. The equivalence sends a Lagrangian brane of slope r/s to a vector bundle of rank r and degree s on the mirror. HMS for the quartic K3 surface (Seidel, 2004): the Fano variety of lines on a quartic surface in ℙ³ is a symplectic manifold; HMS relates its Fukaya category to the derived category of the mirror quartic. The HMS for the quintic threefold (the original Calabi–Yau example from Candelas–delaOssa–Green–Parkes) is still open but deep progress exists via orbi-curves (Sheridan, 2015) proving HMS for many Calabi–Yau hypersurfaces in toric varieties using wrapped Fukaya categories. The categorical crepant resolution conjecture (Bondal–Orlov, Kawamata): for a variety with Gorenstein singularities, different crepant resolutions have equivalent derived categories — the McKay correspondence for G-Hilbert schemes. The Gromov–Witten/Donaldson–Thomas correspondence (Maulik–Nekrasov–Okounkov–Pandharipande) relates GW invariants of X to DT invariants of its mirror Y via the topological vertex. The Kontsevich–Soibelman wall-crossing formula for DT invariants. The Virasoro conjecture and Crepant resolution conjecture in Gromov–Witten theory.
HMS has been proved for many important examples (elliptic curves, abelian varieties, toric Calabi–Yaus) and continues to drive progress in enumerative geometry and categorical algebra.
