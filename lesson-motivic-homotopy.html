Motivic Homotopy — the A¹-Homotopy Theory, the Morel-Voevodsky Category & Motives

Motivic homotopy theory is a synthesis of algebraic geometry and algebraic topology. It begins with the category Smk of smooth schemes of finite type over a field k and equips it with the Nisnevich topology (a Grothendieck topology finer than Zariski, coarser than étale) to define a model structure on simplicial presheaves. A¹-localization formally inverts the projections X × 𝔸¹ → X, yielding the motivic homotopy category H(k). Stabilizing with respect to the motivic spheres S^{1,0} (the simplicial circle) and S^{1,1} (𝔸¹\{0} with the multiplicative action) gives the stable motivic homotopy category SH(k) — the algebraic analogue of the classical stable homotopy category. Motivic cohomology H^{p,q}(X, ℤ) is the bigraded cohomology theory represented by the motivic Eilenberg-MacLane spectrum Hℤ in SH(k). Voevodsky used motivic Steenrod operations to prove the Milnor conjecture (relating Milnor K-theory to étale cohomology mod 2) and, with Rost, the full Bloch-Kato conjecture (the norm residue isomorphism theorem) — a 2010 Fields Medal. Morel's A¹-connectedness theorem and the Levine-Morel algebraic cobordism further cement the subject as a cornerstone of modern arithmetic geometry.

"Motivic homotopy theory brings algebraic topology to algebraic geometry."
— Fabien Morel
Part 1

📏 The A¹-Homotopy Category

Foundations
The Category Smk, Nisnevich Topology, A¹-Localization, and H(k)
Let Smk denote the category of smooth schemes of finite type over a field k. A Nisnevich cover is an étale cover {Ui → X} such that for every field point Spec(k(x)) → X, there exists a Ui → X and a lifting Spec(k(x)) → Ui. The Nisnevich topology sits strictly between the Zariski and étale topologies. The category of simplicial presheaves sPre(Smk) carries the injective model structure with weak equivalences the Nisnevich local weak equivalences (those inducing isomorphisms on all Nisnevich sheaves of homotopy groups). A¹-localization is the left Bousfield localization that forces the projections X × 𝔸¹ → X to be weak equivalences. The resulting homotopy category is the motivic homotopy category H(k). The A¹-connected components sheaf π0A¹(X) is the Nisnevich sheaf associated to the presheaf U ↦ [U, X]A¹ (maps in H(k)). Morel's theorem: for any field k, π0A¹(X)(Spec(K)) = X(K)/R where R is the equivalence relation generated by elementary A¹-connections — i.e., two K-points are A¹-homotopic iff they can be linked by a chain of maps 𝔸¹K → X. Classifying spaces BA¹G for an algebraic group G are defined as the A¹-homotopy type of the simplicial bar construction; they classify A¹-homotopy classes of Nisnevich-locally trivial G-torsors.
The motivic homotopy category H(k) is built by declaring the affine line 𝔸¹ to be contractible — the algebraic analogue of making the unit interval contractible in classical homotopy theory.
Part 2

🌍 Motivic Cohomology and the Bloch-Kato Conjecture

Cohomology
Motivic Cohomology H^{p,q}, the Motivic Steenrod Algebra, and the Norm Residue Isomorphism
Motivic cohomology H^{p,q}(X, ℤ) is the bigraded cohomology theory defined as HomSH(k)(Σp,qX+, Hℤ), where Hℤ is the motivic Eilenberg-MacLane spectrum (the spectrum representing motivic cohomology). The Bloch-Quillen formula: H^{p,q}(X, ℤ) ≅ CHq(X, 2q−p) where CHq(X, n) are Bloch's higher Chow groups — an exact complex computing algebraic cycles modulo rational equivalence. The motivic Steenrod algebra A** is the bigraded algebra of cohomology operations on motivic cohomology with ℤ/ℓ coefficients; Voevodsky constructed Steenrod squares Sqi in the motivic setting, acting on Hp,q(X, ℤ/2). The Milnor conjecture (proved by Voevodsky, 1996): the norm residue homomorphism KMn(k)/2 → Hnét(k, ℤ/2) is an isomorphism — equivalently, the étale cohomology ring of a field mod 2 is the Milnor K-theory mod 2. The Bloch-Kato conjecture (now the Norm Residue Isomorphism Theorem, proved by Voevodsky with contributions from Rost, Weibel, and others, 2010): for any field k, any prime ℓ invertible in k, and any n ≥ 0, the norm residue map KMn(k)/ℓ → Hnét(k, μℓ⊗n) is an isomorphism. The proof uses motivic cohomology, the motivic Steenrod algebra, and the Beilinson-Lichtenbaum conjecture.
Motivic cohomology is the universal bigraded cohomology theory for smooth schemes; the Bloch-Kato conjecture shows it computes étale cohomology after inverting ℓ.
Part 3

🧮 The Stable Motivic Homotopy Category

Stability
SH(k), the Bigraded Spheres S^{p,q}, MGL, the Slice Spectral Sequence, and η-Periodicity
The stable motivic homotopy category SH(k) is obtained from H(k) by stabilizing with respect to the motivic sphere spectrum S0,0 and its suspensions. The bigraded spheres are Sp,q = Sp−q,0 ∧ S0,q where S1,0 is the simplicial circle Δ¹/∂Δ¹ and S1,1 = 𝔸¹\{0} pointed by 1 (the Tate circle). The Chow-Witt ring CH̲n(X) is the (2n,n)-cohomology of X with coefficients in the unramified sheaf given by Milnor-Witt K-theory. The algebraic cobordism spectrum MGL is the motivic analogue of MU: it is the Thom spectrum of the universal rank-n tautological bundles over BA¹GLn. The Levine-Morel theorem: the algebraic cobordism ring Ω*(k) = MGL2*,*(Spec k) ≅ L ⊗ ℤ (the Lazard ring) — algebraic cobordism is the universal oriented cohomology theory for schemes. The slice spectral sequence (Voevodsky) is a spectral sequence converging to πp,q(E) whose E₁-page consists of the cohomology of the slices sqE; for MGL, the slices are given by suspensions of Hℤ. The theory of η-periodicity studies the algebraic Hopf map η: 𝔸²\{0} → ℙ¹ (the motivic analogue of the Hopf fibration). The η-localized motivic sphere S0,0[η⁻¹] is the localization inverting η; its homotopy groups are ℤ[η, η⁻¹]-modules and exhibit strong periodicity phenomena connecting classical stable homotopy with arithmetic.
SH(k) is a bigraded, highly structured symmetric monoidal category that simultaneously generalizes the classical stable homotopy category and encodes deep arithmetic invariants of the base field.
Part 4

🔥 Applications to Number Theory and Algebraic Geometry

Arithmetic
Milnor Conjecture Proof, Bloch-Kato, A¹-Classifying Spaces, and Arithmetic Geometry
Voevodsky's proof of the Milnor conjecture (1996): construct the motivic Steenrod operations Sqi on motivic cohomology mod 2, then produce the Milnor operations Qi as certain compositions of Sqi; the vanishing of these operations on the motivic cohomology of fields gives the Milnor conjecture. The full Bloch-Kato conjecture (the Norm Residue Isomorphism Theorem, 2010): Voevodsky (with Rost handling ℓ = 2 and the general case using symmetric power constructions) reduced the problem to the existence of certain norm varieties — varieties whose existence is proved via the Merkurjev-Suslin and Rost theorems on the degree of a field extension. The Beilinson-Soulé vanishing conjecture predicts H^{p,q}(Spec k, ℚ) = 0 for q > 0 and p ≤ 0; it implies the motivic Tate conjecture relating rational cycles and étale cohomology. Morel's A¹-homotopy classification of vector bundles: for a smooth affine scheme X over a field of characteristic ≠ 2, rank n vector bundles on X (up to isomorphism) are in bijection with A¹-homotopy classes [X, BA¹GLn]A¹ — vastly generalizing the classification of algebraic vector bundles. In arithmetic geometry, the A¹-homotopy theory of torsors shows that every Nisnevich-locally trivial G-torsor over the affine line 𝔸¹k is trivial when G is a special algebraic group — a result with applications to the study of rational points.
Motivic homotopy theory provides the most powerful known toolkit for solving long-standing problems in arithmetic, from the Milnor and Bloch-Kato conjectures to the classification of vector bundles.
