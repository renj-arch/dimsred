Mock Modular Forms — Ramanujan's Theta Functions, Harmonic Maass Forms & Quantum Modularity

Deep dive into Mock Modular Forms — classical modular forms for SL₂(ℤ) (the Eisenstein series E_k, the discriminant Δ = η(τ)²⁴, the j-invariant), Ramanujan's mock theta functions (the 17 original functions from his last letter to Hardy, 1920, with asymptotic expansions that mock modular behavior), harmonic Maass forms (Bruinier–Funke, 2004: non-holomorphic modular forms of weight k with eigenvalue (1−k/2) under the hyperbolic Laplacian Δ_k, whose holomorphic part is a mock modular form), Zwegers's completion (the breakthrough 2002 thesis showing that mock theta functions become harmonic Maass forms when corrected by a non-holomorphic Eichler integral), indefinite theta series, quantum modular forms (Zagier, 2010: functions defined on ℚ that transform modularly with a periodic error), Ramanujan's lost notebook identities, and connections to partitions (the Hardy–Ramanujan asymptotic formula p(n) ∼ e^{π√(2n/3)}/(4√3 n)), moonshine (the Mathieu and Umbral moonshine conjectures relating mock modular forms to finite groups), and knot invariants (the colored Jones polynomial and the volume conjecture).

"I have discovered a new class of functions which I call mock theta functions. They are very interesting and have many properties analogous to those of ordinary theta functions."
— Srinivasa Ramanujan, in his last letter to G. H. Hardy, 1920
Part 1

🌙 Classical Modular Forms and Theta Series

Modular Forms
The Modular Group SL₂(ℤ), Eisenstein Series, and Classical Theta Functions
The modular group Γ = SL₂(ℤ) acts on the upper half-plane ℍ = {τ ∈ ℂ | Im(τ) > 0} by τ ↦ (aτ + b)/(cτ + d). A modular form of weight k ∈ ℤ for Γ is a holomorphic function f: ℍ → ℂ satisfying the transformation f((aτ + b)/(cτ + d)) = (cτ + d)^k f(τ) and holomorphicity at the cusp (the Fourier expansion f(τ) = Σ_{n≥0} a(n) q^n, q = e^{2πiτ}). The simplest examples: the Eisenstein series E_k(τ) = ½ Σ_{(m,n)≠(0,0)} (mτ + n)^{-k} for even k ≥ 4. Their Fourier expansions: E_k(τ) = 1 − (2k/B_k) Σ_{n≥1} σ_{k−1}(n) q^n where σ_r(n) = Σ_{d|n} d^r and B_k are Bernoulli numbers. The discriminant Δ(τ) = (E_4(τ)³ − E_6(τ)²)/1728 = q ∏_{n≥1} (1 − q^n)²⁴ = η(τ)²⁴ (Dedekind eta function). The j-invariant j(τ) = E_4(τ)³/Δ(τ) is a modular function (weight 0) giving a bijection between ℍ/Γ and ℂ. Classical theta functions: the Jacobi theta function θ(τ, z) = Σ_{n∈ℤ} (−1)^n q^{n²/2} e^{2πinz} transforms as a modular form in τ (weight ½) and a quasi-periodic function in z. The Hecke operators T_n act on the space M_k(Γ) of modular forms, commuting and being self-adjoint with respect to the Petersson inner product. The Eichler–Shimura theory relates modular forms to elliptic curves via the L-function L(f, s) = Σ a(n) n^{-s} — the modularity theorem (Taniyama–Shimura–Weil, proved by Wiles et al.: every elliptic curve over ℚ is modular).
Classical modular forms are automorphic functions on the upper half-plane whose Fourier coefficients encode deep arithmetic information, via Hecke operators and L-functions.
Part 2

📝 Ramanujan's Mock Theta Functions

Mock Theta
Ramanujan's Last Letter, Asymptotic Expansions, and the Failure of Modularity
In his last letter to Hardy (1920), Ramanujan introduced 17 mock theta functions: series like f(q) = 1 + Σ_{n≥1} q^{n²}/((1+q)²(1+q²)²…(1+q^n)²), φ(q) = 1 + Σ_{n≥1} q^{n²}/((1+q²)(1+q⁴)…(1+q^{2n})), and ψ(q) = Σ_{n≥1} q^{n²}/((1−q)(1−q³)…(1−q^{2n−1})). These series have exponential singularities at roots of unity: as q → e^{2πir/s} radially, f(q) has asymptotics of the form (−1)^{s−1} e^{πi(1−2r)/24s} (4s/3)^{1/2} (πi/s)^{1/2} e^{π²/s(1−2r)} + O(1), involving a modular transformation of the asymptotic series — but the sum differs from any modular form. For decades they defied understanding: they are q-series with q-expansions like modular forms, but their theta-like behavior near roots of unity suggested a hidden modular completion. The Watson–Whipple transformation formulas gave identities relating mock theta functions to basic hypergeometric series ₂φ₁ and ₈φ₇. Andrews's combinatorial interpretations of mock theta functions in terms of partitions (e.g., the rank of a partition and the Andrews–Garvan crank). The mock theta conjectures (Andrews–Dyson–Hickerson, 1988) expressed certain mock theta functions as indefinite theta series, providing the first structural understanding.
Ramanujan's mock theta functions exhibit modular asymptotic behavior at roots of unity despite not being modular forms — they mock modularity.
Part 3

✨ Harmonic Maass Forms and the Zwegers Completion

Completion
Zwegers's Thesis, Harmonic Maass Forms, and the Bruinier–Funke Theory
The key breakthrough came from Sander Zwegers's PhD thesis (Utrecht, 2002). A harmonic Maass form (Bruinier–Funke, 2004) of weight k ∈ ½ℤ for Γ is a smooth function F: ℍ → ℂ that transforms like a modular form of weight k, is annihilated by the weight-k hyperbolic Laplacian Δ_k = −y²(∂_x² + ∂_y²) + iky(∂_x + i∂_y), and has at most linear exponential growth at the cusps. A harmonic Maass form F decomposes as F = F⁺ + F⁻ where F⁺ is the holomorphic part (a mock modular form) and F⁻ is the non-holomorphic part (an Eichler integral of a cusp form). Specifically, F⁻(τ) = Σ_{n<0} b(n) Γ(1−k, 4π|n|y) q^n + constant, where Γ(s, x) is the incomplete Gamma function. Zwegers's completion: for Ramanujan's mock theta function f(q), define the shadow g(τ) = q^{-1/24} η(τ) (the Dedekind eta function). Then the completion ̂f(τ) = q^{-1/24}f(q) + i√3 ∫_{−τ̄}^{i∞} g(z) (−i(z+τ))^{-1/2} dz is a harmonic Maass form of weight ½. The shadow operator ξ_k = 2i y^k ∂/∂τ̄ maps harmonic Maass forms of weight k to cusp forms of weight 2−k. The kernel is the space of mock modular forms. The Borcherds lift (Borcherds, 1995) relates harmonic Maass forms to infinite products and the Griffiths–Zagier theory. Indefinite theta series (Zwegers): Σ_{n∈ℤ^r} (−1)^{Q(n)} q^{Q(n)} where Q is an indefinite quadratic form — these give mock modular forms whose shadows are unary theta series.
Mock modular forms are the holomorphic parts of harmonic Maass forms; the non-holomorphic correction restores modularity, exactly as Zwegers discovered.
Part 4

🔗 Quantum Modular Forms and Moonshine

Quantum
Zagier's Quantum Modular Forms, Moonshine, and Partition Functions
Quantum modular forms (Zagier, 2010) are functions f: ℚ → ℂ (defined only at rational points) satisfying the modular transformation f(τ) − (cτ + d)^{-k} f((aτ + b)/(cτ + d)) = g(τ), where for each γ ∈ SL₂(ℤ), the error g(τ) extends to a real-analytic function on ℂ\ℙ¹(ℝ) with some continuity property. Ramanujan's mock theta functions, when restricted to roots of unity, give quantum modular forms. The Eichler integral ∫_τ^{i∞} f(z) (z−τ)^{-k} dz of a cusp form produces a quantum modular form. The Mathieu moonshine (Eguchi–Ooguri–Tachikawa, 2010): the elliptic genus of the K3 surface decomposes into characters of the Mathieu group M₂₄, with mock modular forms appearing as McKay–Thompson series. The Umbral moonshine (Cheng–Duncan–Harvey, 2012): for each of the 23 Niemeier lattices, there is a finite group and a vector-valued mock modular form whose coefficients encode group characters — a profound generalization of monstrous moonshine. The partition function p(n): the generating function Σ p(n) q^n = 1/∏_{n≥1} (1−q^n) = q^{1/24}/η(τ) is a modular form of weight −½ (essentially). The Hardy–Ramanujan circle method uses the modular transformation of η to derive p(n) ∼ e^{π√(2n/3)}/(4√3 n). Dyson's rank and the Andrews–Garvan crank give combinatorial interpretations of Ramanujan's congruences p(5k+4) ≡ 0 mod 5, p(7k+5) ≡ 0 mod 7, p(11k+6) ≡ 0 mod 11. The Kontsevich–Zagier period conjectures and WRT invariants of 3-manifolds (the volume conjecture relates the colored Jones polynomial of a knot to the hyperbolic volume of its complement via quantum modular forms).
Mock and quantum modular forms connect to moonshine (finite group theory), partition congruences, and invariants of 3-manifolds and knots.

The Hidden Geometry of the Shadow: Explicit Integrals and Eichler Series

To master Mock Modular Forms at an advanced level, one must move beyond the definition of Zwegers's completion and understand the explicit construction of the "shadow" term. In classical modular form theory, a cusp form f(τ) is often recovered via the Serre–Katz operator or by integrating against a kernel. For mock theta functions, the shadow g(τ) is not an arbitrary function; it is specifically an Eichler integral of a weight 1/2 modular form (or a linear combination thereof). Consider Ramanujan's function ω(q). Its shadow involves the non-holomorphic completion of the Dedekind eta function. The explicit integral representation for the shadow term F(τ) is given by:

F(τ) = ∫i∞τ f(z) (z-τ)-1/2 dz

This integral does not converge absolutely at infinity without regularization, but in the context of harmonic Maass forms, it is defined via analytic continuation. The crucial mechanism here is the modular transformation of the non-holomorphic part. When you apply γ = begin{pmatrix} a & b c & d end{pmatrix} to the completion f(τ) = f(τ) + F(τ), the holomorphic part f(τ) transforms with an error term. The non-holomorphic part F(τ) is constructed precisely so that this error cancels out. Specifically, if f has a shadow g, then under γ, the term F(γτ) picks up the factor (cτ+d)-k and an additive constant involving the integral of g. This cancellation is the essence of modularity restoration.

A landmark case in this explicit construction is the work of Zagier (1987), who identified that certain indefinite theta series, such as those associated with the quadratic form Q(x,y) = x2 + xy - y2, naturally appear as shadows. These forms are not positive definite; they have signature (2,1) or similar. The explicit evaluation of these integrals often leads to expressions involving logarithms and dilogarithms, which explains why the completions of mock theta functions involve log|q| terms in their asymptotic expansions near roots of unity. For instance, the asymptotic expansion of a mock modular form f at a cusp usually looks like:

f(e2π i τ) ∼ C0 + C1 log(1-e2π i τ) + Σn a_n e2π i n τ

The logarithmic singularity is the signature of the non-holomorphic shadow term. Understanding that the "error" in modularity is not random but structured as an Eichler integral allows one to reconstruct the full harmonic Maass form from just the holomorphic mock part and the knowledge of its shadow's weight and multiplier system.

Explicit Shadow Calculation for a Specific Mock Theta Function
Let f(τ) = Σn=1^∞ a(n) q^n be the mock theta function defined by Ramanujan. Suppose its shadow is known to be proportional to the derivative of the Dedekind eta function, specifically related to g(τ) = eta'(τ)/eta(τ) in a generalized sense. If the modular transformation law for the holomorphic part yields an error term Δ_γ f(τ) = (cτ+d)-1/2 Σn b_n e2π i n (aτ+b)/(cτ+d), construct the explicit form of the shadow integral F(τ) that cancels this error. Show how the weight k=1/2 affects the power in the kernel (z-τ)-k.
The shadow integral is defined as F(τ) = ∫i∞τ g(z) (z-τ)-1/2 dz. For weight k=1/2, the kernel is (z-τ)-1/2. Under a modular transformation γ, the term (cτ+d)-1/2 appears in the automorphy factor. The integral transforms as: F(γτ) = ∫i∞γτ g(z) (z-γτ)-1/2 dz. By changing variables and using the modular property of g, this splits into a term proportional to (cτ+d)-1/2 F(τ) plus an additive constant coming from the path deformation. This constant is exactly the negative of the error term generated by f(τ). Thus, f(γτ) = (cτ+d)-1/2 f(τ). The explicit calculation confirms that the weight 1/2 dictates the square root behavior in the kernel and the resulting automorphy factor.
Exam Strategy: Identifying the Shadow Weight
When asked to identify the shadow of a given mock theta function in an exam, do not guess. Look for the asymptotic behavior at roots of unity. If the expansion contains terms like e2π i n τ / s with specific coefficients, match them against known unary theta series (like eta(τ) or eta(2τ)). The weight of the shadow is almost always 1/2 for Ramanujan's original functions, but can be integer weights for later generalizations. Remember: Shadow Weight + Mock Weight = Harmonic Maass Weight.

From Mock to Quantum: The Zagier Connection and Rational Points

The transition from Mock Modular Forms to Quantum Modular Forms represents a profound shift in the domain of definition. While mock modular forms are defined on the upper half-plane H (holomorphic parts) or as harmonic Maass forms on H, quantum modular forms, introduced by Dominique Zagier in 2010, are functions f: Q/Z → C. They are not defined on the entire complex plane but only at rational points τ = a/c. The defining property is that for any γ ∈ SL2(Z), the difference:

f(γτ) - (cτ+d)-k f(τ)

is not zero, but rather extends to a real-analytic function on C ∖ R (often called the "error term" or "correction"). This concept is deeply tied to Ramanujan's mock theta functions. When one evaluates a mock theta function at roots of unity, it does not yield a modular form value but rather a value that satisfies a quantum modular transformation law. The error term in this case is often related to the incomplete gamma function or logarithmic terms.

A critical application of this theory lies in Witten's Reshetikhin-Turaev (RT) invariants and the volume conjecture for hyperbolic knots. The colored Jones polynomial J_N(K), when viewed as a function of the variable q = e2π i τ at roots of unity, behaves like a quantum modular form. Specifically, the limit of J_N(K) as N → ∞ relates to the hyperbolic volume of the knot complement. The "error" in the modular transformation captures the geometric volume. This connection was formalized by Bridgeman, Dunfield, and others around 2015-2017. They showed that the asymptotic expansion of these quantum modular forms encodes the Chern-Simons invariant and the hyperbolic volume.

The distinction is vital for competitive exams: Mock Modular Forms are about holomorphic parts of harmonic Maass forms on H, whereas Quantum Modular Forms are about functions defined strictly on Q that satisfy a "modularity up to error" condition.
