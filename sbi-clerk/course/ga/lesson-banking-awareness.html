Key Concepts

What is Banking Awareness?

Banking Awareness is a key section in IBPS PO, SBI Clerk, and RBI Grade B exams. It tests your knowledge of the Indian banking system, banking regulations, financial institutions, government schemes, and banking terminology. Questions range from history of banking to recent developments in the financial sector.

Key Topics in Banking Awareness

1. History of Banking in India: Bank of Hindustan (1770), Presidency Banks (1800s), Imperial Bank (1921), nationalization of 14 banks in 1969 and 6 more in 1980, establishment of RBI in 1935, SBI in 1955, NABARD in 1982.

2. Reserve Bank of India (RBI): Central bank of India. Key functions: monetary policy formulation, regulation of banking system, currency issuance, foreign exchange management. Important terms: Repo Rate, Reverse Repo Rate, CRR, SLR, MSF, Bank Rate, OMO, LAF.

3. Scheduled Commercial Banks: Public sector banks (SBI, PNB, BOB, Canara, etc.), private sector banks (HDFC, ICICI, Axis, Kotak), foreign banks, regional rural banks (RRBs), small finance banks, payment banks.

4. Financial Regulators: RBI (banking), SEBI (securities markets), IRDAI (insurance), PFRDA (pensions), NHB (housing finance), SIDBI (MSME finance), NABARD (agriculture and rural development).

5. Banking Terms: NPA (Non-Performing Asset), CAR (Capital Adequacy Ratio), Basel III norms, CASA ratio, Net Interest Margin (NIM), RTGS, NEFT, IMPS, UPI, CBS (Core Banking Solution), KYC, AML.

6. Government Schemes: Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana (PMJDY), Pradhan Mantri Mudra Yojana (PMMY), Stand-Up India, Startup India, Atal Pension Yojana (APY), Pradhan Mantri Jeevan Jyoti Bima Yojana (PMJJBY), Pradhan Mantri Suraksha Bima Yojana (PMSBY).

7. Financial Inclusion: Initiatives to provide banking services to unbanked population — BC/BF model, no-frills accounts, PMJDY, differentiated banks (payments banks, small finance banks), digital India.

Solved Examples

Example 1
What is the current Repo Rate as set by the RBI? (This changes with monetary policy reviews.)

a) It is the rate at which RBI lends to commercial banks b) It is the rate at which RBI borrows from commercial banks c) It is the rate on government securities d) It is the rate for inter-bank lending
Solution: a) Repo Rate is the rate at which RBI lends money to commercial banks against government securities. The reverse repo rate is the rate at which RBI borrows from banks.
Example 2
Which of the following is NOT a public sector bank in India?

a) Bank of Baroda b) Punjab National Bank c) HDFC Bank d) Canara Bank
Solution: c) HDFC Bank is a private sector bank, not a public sector bank.
Example 3
What does CRR stand for in banking?

a) Cash Reserve Ratio b) Capital Reserve Requirement c) Credit Risk Ratio d) Cash Requirement Regulation
Solution: a) Cash Reserve Ratio. It is the portion of deposits that banks must keep with RBI in the form of cash.

Shortcut Techniques

  • Keep a current affairs diary — note important banking appointments, mergers, schemes, and policy rate changes monthly.
  • Create mnemonics for lists — e.g., 'PPF CASH' for Basel III pillars: Pillar 1 (Minimum Capital), Pillar 2 (Supervisory Review), Pillar 3 (Market Discipline).
  • Focus on RBI functions, monetary policy instruments, and recent developments — these are high-yield topics.
  • Learn the headquarters, taglines, and founding years of major banks for 1-mark questions.
Pro Tip
Banking Awareness questions are very current-affairs driven. The best preparation is to read the monthly banking/economic capsules from trusted sources. Focus on the last 6 months of banking news before the exam date.
