Key Concepts

What are Blood Relations?

Blood relation questions test your ability to understand family relationships and genealogical connections. You are given descriptions of relationships between family members and must answer questions about how two individuals are related. These questions appear regularly in SSC CGL Tier 1 exams.

Key Relationships to Remember

  • Father/Mother: Parent of the person.
  • Brother/Sister: Sibling (same parents).
  • Husband/Wife: Spouse.
  • Son/Daughter: Child.
  • Grandfather/Grandmother: Parent's parent.
  • Uncle/Aunt: Parent's brother/sister or spouse's sibling.
  • Cousin: Child of uncle/aunt.
  • Nephew/Niece: Child of sibling.
  • Father-in-law/Mother-in-law: Spouse's parent.
  • Brother-in-law/Sister-in-law: Spouse's sibling or sibling's spouse.

Solved Examples

Example 1 (Direct Relation)
A is the father of B. C is the daughter of B. D is the brother of C. How is A related to D?

a) Grandfather b) Father c) Uncle d) Cousin
Solution: a) Grandfather. A â†’ B (father). B â†’ C (daughter), so B is C's parent. D is C's brother, so D is also B's child. Therefore, A is D's grandfather (parent's parent).
Example 2 (Coded Relation)
If 'P + Q' means P is the father of Q, 'P - Q' means P is the mother of Q, 'P Ã— Q' means P is the brother of Q, and 'P Ã· Q' means P is the sister of Q, then what does 'A + B - C' mean?

a) A is the grandfather of C b) A is the father of C c) A is the uncle of C d) Cannot be determined
Solution: a) A is the grandfather of C. A + B means A is father of B. B - C means B is mother of C. So A is the father of B, who is the mother of C. Hence A is the maternal grandfather of C.
Example 3 (Generation Gap)
A is the sister of B. B is the mother of C. C is the brother of D. D is the wife of E. How is A related to E?

a) Mother-in-law b) Sister-in-law c) Aunt d) Grandmother
Solution: b) Sister-in-law. A is B's sister. B is C's mother. C is D's brother, so C and D are siblings. D is E's wife. So A is the sister of B, who is the mother of C (and D). A is D's aunt. D is married to E. So A is the aunt of E's wife â€” in relationship terms, A is E's aunt by marriage = sister-in-law. Actually: A is the sister of B, B is mother of C and D (since C and D are siblings), so A is maternal aunt of D. D is married to E. So A is the aunt of E's wife = no direct relation. Actually: A is sister-in-law of E because A is the sister of B (who is the mother of D, who is E's wife). So A is the sister of B, who is D's mother. D is E's wife. A has no direct relation to E. But through D: A is D's maternal aunt, so A is the aunt of E's wife. In common terms, A is E's aunt. Among options: Sister-in-law (wife's aunt is not sister-in-law), Mother-in-law (no), Aunt (yes, by marriage), Grandmother (no). So answer: c) Aunt.

Shortcut Techniques

  • Always draw a family tree with horizontal lines for marriage (=) and vertical lines for parent-child (|). Write names and relationships as you read.
  • Use generation counting: assign +1 to each older generation, 0 to same generation, -1 to younger generation. Add the gaps.

Pro Tip
In coded relation problems, always start from the last expression. For 'A + B - C', first find B-C relationship, then A with that result. Also, gender matters â€” if A is father of B, A is male. Track genders carefully.
