Q1. English Language
Identify the segment in the sentence which contains a grammatical error. If there is no error, select 'No error'. "If the RBI would have raised interest rates last month, the inflation rate would have been controlled by now."
A. If the RBI would have raised
B. interest rates last month
C. the inflation rate would have been controlled
D. by now
Correct Answer: A
Shortcut: In Third Conditional (hypothetical past), the 'if' clause never uses 'would have'. It strictly uses 'had + V3'. Spot 'would have' in the 'if' clause → immediate error. Full Solution: The sentence describes an unreal past condition. The correct structure for Type 3 Conditional is: 'If + Past Perfect (had + V3), Subject + would have + V3'. Segment A incorrectly uses 'would have raised' instead of 'had raised'. Segments B, C, and D are grammatically sound.
Q2. English Language
Choose the option that best expresses the meaning of the idiom **'to keep the wolf from the door'**.
A. To protect oneself from a dangerous animal
B. To have just enough money to avert hunger or starvation
C. To avoid a difficult confrontation
D. To guard a secret fiercely
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: Idioms are fixed metaphors. 'Wolf' = Hunger/Poverty; 'Door' = Home/Survival. 'Keep away' = Earn subsistence. Full Solution: This idiom originates from the wolf being a symbol of famine and destitution. 'Keeping the wolf from the door' signifies earning a bare minimum livelihood to prevent starvation. Option A is the literal meaning. Options C and D are unrelated metaphorical interpretations.
Q3. English Language
In the following question, a word is given followed by four sentences. Choose the sentence in which the usage of the word is **INCORRECT** or **INAPPROPRIATE**. Word: **PERUSE**
A. He asked me to peruse the contract carefully before signing it.
B. She spent the afternoon perusing the shelves for a good novel.
C. The manager decided to peruse the employee's request for leave immediately.
D. Please peruse through this document and give your feedback by evening.
Correct Answer: D
Shortcut: 'Peruse' is a transitive verb meaning 'read thoroughly'. It inherently implies 'through'. Adding 'through' after 'peruse' is redundant (tautology). Scan for 'peruse + through'. Full Solution: 'Peruse' means to examine or read something in a detailed, careful way. It takes a direct object (e.g., 'peruse the document'). It does not require the preposition 'through'. Option D ('peruse through') is grammatically redundant. Options A, B, and C use 'peruse' correctly as a transitive verb with direct objects (contract, shelves, request).
Q4. Quantitative Aptitude
Two trains of lengths 150 m and 200 m run in opposite directions at speeds of 54 km/h and 72 km/h respectively. What is the time taken by them to cross each other completely?
A. 8 sec
B. 12 sec
C. 14 sec
D. 10 sec
Correct Answer: D
Shortcut: Relative speed = (54 + 72) km/h = 126 km/h = 126 × 5/18 = 35 m/s. Total length = 150 + 200 = 350 m. Time = 350 / 35 = 10 s. Full: Convert speeds: 54 × 5/18 = 15 m/s, 72 × 5/18 = 20 m/s. Relative speed = 35 m/s. Time = total length / relative speed = 350 / 35 = 10 s.
Q5. Quantitative Aptitude
A boat travels 36 km downstream in 2 hours and returns the same distance upstream in 3 hours. What is the speed of the boat in still water?
A. 13 km/h
B. 14 km/h
C. 15 km/h
D. 16 km/h
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut: Speed in still water = (Downstream speed + Upstream speed)/2. Downstream speed = 36/2 = 18 km/h, Upstream speed = 36/3 = 12 km/h. So still water speed = (18+12)/2 = 15 km/h. Full solution: Let boat speed = b, stream speed = s. Downstream: b+s = 18, Upstream: b-s = 12. Adding gives 2b = 30 => b = 15 km/h.
Q6. Reasoning Ability
Five friends – Karan, Leena, Mohit, Nisha and Omar – are sitting in a row facing north. The following conditions are true: 1. Karan is not at either end. 2. Leena sits immediately to the right of Mohit. 3. Nisha sits exactly two seats away from Omar. 4. Mohit does not sit next to Omar. If Leena occupies the third seat from the left, who sits at the far right end?
A. Karan
B. Leena
C. Nisha
D. Omar
Correct Answer: D
Shortcut: Fix the known seat (Leena at 3rd). Since Leena is immediately right of Mohit, Mohit must be at seat 2. Karan cannot be at an end, so he can be at seat 1 or 4 or 5. Nisha is two seats away from Omar, giving two possible pairs: (1,3), (2,4), (3,5). Seats 2 and 3 are already taken, so the only viable pair is Nisha at seat 1 and Omar at seat 5. This satisfies condition 4 (Mohit at seat 2 is not next to Omar at seat 5). Hence the far right end (seat 5) is occupied by Omar. Full solution: Seats 1‑5 left to right. Leena =3. Mohit =2 (Leena right of Mohit). Karan not at ends → cannot be 1 or 5. Nisha‑Omar distance 2 seats. Possible positions for the pair are (1,3), (2,4), (3,5). Seats 2 and 3 occupied, so (1,3) and (2,4) impossible. Thus Nisha=1, Omar=5. Condition 4 holds (Mohit at 2 not adjacent to Omar at5). Remaining seat 4 goes to Karan (not an end). Therefore far right seat is Omar.
Q7. Reasoning Ability
Eight persons A, B, C, D, E, F, G and H are sitting around a circular table facing the centre. A sits third to the right of B. C sits second to the left of A. D sits immediate right of B. E sits third to the left of D. F is not an immediate neighbour of C. G sits second to the right of H. Who sits immediate left of F?
A. A
B. C
C. E
D. G
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut: Fix B at a position, place A 3rd to right (anti-clockwise). Place D immediate right of B. Place C 2nd left of A (clockwise). Place E 3rd left of D. Use 'F not neighbor of C' and 'G 2nd right of H' to fill remaining. Full Solution: Let B be at position 1. A is 3rd to right → pos 4. D immediate right of B → pos 8. C 2nd left of A → pos 6. E 3rd left of D (pos 8) → pos 3. Vacant: 2, 5, 7. C at 6, so neighbors 5,7 blocked for F. F at 2. Remaining 5,7 for G,H. G 2nd right of H: if H at 5, G at 7 (via 6,7). Valid. Arrangement: 1-B, 2-F, 3-E, 4-A, 5-H, 6-C, 7-G, 8-D. Immediate left of F (pos 2) is pos 3 → E.
Q8. Quantitative Aptitude
A shopkeeper marks an article 40% above its cost price and then allows a discount of 15% on the marked price. What is his profit percent?
A. 19%
B. 20%
C. 18%
D. 21%
Correct Answer: A
Shortcut: Net% = Markup% + Discount% + (Markup%×Discount%)/100 = 40 - 15 - 6 = 19%. Full: Let CP = 100. MP = 140. SP after 15% discount = 140 × 0.85 = 119. Profit = 19 on 100 → 19%.
Q9. Quantitative Aptitude
A and B can complete a work in 12 days and 18 days respectively. They work together for 4 days, after which A leaves. In how many more days will B finish the remaining work?
A. 6 days
B. 8 days
C. 10 days
D. 12 days
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: Take LCM of 12 and 18 = 36 units. A does 3 units/day, B does 2 units/day. Together 5 units/day → in 4 days 20 units done. Remaining 16 units, B alone needs 16/2 = 8 days. Full: Work = 1 unit. A's rate = 1/12, B's = 1/18. Combined = 5/36 per day. In 4 days = 20/36 = 5/9. Remaining = 4/9. B's time = (4/9)/(1/18) = 8 days.

Choose the option that best replaces the underlined segment to make the sentence grammatically correct and structurally parallel. "The manager objected to the proposal being delayed and that it was revised without consultation."
A. to the proposal being delayed and its revision
B. to the proposal being delayed and revised
C. to the delay of the proposal and its revision
D. to the proposal's delay and revising it
Correct Answer: C

Option D mixes noun ('delay') and gerund ('revising').
Q11. Reasoning Ability
In the following question, a statement showing relationships between elements is given, followed by two conclusions. Assuming the given statement to be true, find which conclusion(s) is/are definitely true. Statement: K ≥ L > M = N ≤ O < P = Q Conclusions: I. K > N II. Q > O
A. Only Conclusion I is true
B. Only Conclusion II is true
C. Both Conclusions I and II are true
D. Neither Conclusion I nor II is true
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut: Chain the inequality signs from left to right for Conclusion I: K ≥ L > M = N ⇒ K > N (True). Chain from right to left for Conclusion II: Q = P > O ⇒ Q > O (True). Full Solution: For Conclusion I, the path from K to N contains a strict '>' sign (L > M), so K is definitely greater than N. For Conclusion II, the path from Q to O contains a strict '>' sign (P > O), and Q = P, so Q is definitely greater than O. Both conclusions are definitely true.
Q12. Quantitative Aptitude
The difference between compound interest and simple interest on a certain sum for 2 years at 10% per annum is Rs. 125. Find the principal.
A. 12000
B. 11500
C. 12500
D. 13000
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut: For 2 years, CI − SI = P × (R/100)². So P = Diff × 10000 / R² = 125 × 10000 / 100 = 12500. Full: SI = P × 10 × 2 / 100 = 0.2P. CI = P(1.1)² − P = 0.21P. Difference = 0.01P = 125 → P = 12500.

Q14. Reasoning Ability
Read the following information carefully and answer the question given below. 'A × B' means 'A is the father of B'. 'A + B' means 'A is the mother of B'. 'A ÷ B' means 'A is the brother of B'. 'A – B' means 'A is the sister of B'. If the expression 'P × Q + R ÷ S – T' is definitely true, then how is P related to T?
A. Grandfather
B. Grandmother
C. Father
D. Uncle
Correct Answer: A
Shortcut: Decode backwards from T. T is sibling of S (S–T). S is sibling of R (R÷S). R is child of Q (Q+R). Q is child of P (P×Q). So P is grandfather of T. Since P×Q means P is father of Q, P is male → Grandfather. Full Solution: P × Q ⇒ P is father of Q. Q + R ⇒ Q is mother of R. Thus P is maternal grandfather of R. R ÷ S ⇒ R is brother of S. S – T ⇒ S is sister of T. Thus R, S, T are siblings. Therefore, P is grandfather of T.
Q15. Reasoning Ability
In the following question, some statements are given followed by two conclusions. Assuming the statements to be true, decide which conclusion(s) logically follow(s). Statements: Only a few Pens are Pencils. All Pencils are Erasers. No Eraser is a Sharpener. Conclusions: I. Some Pens are not Sharpeners. II. All Pencils can never be Sharpeners.
A. Only I follows
B. Only II follows
C. Either I or II follows
D. Both I and II follow
Correct Answer: D
Shortcut: 'Only a few A are B' ⇒ Some A are B + Some A are not B. 'All B are C' + 'No C is D' ⇒ No B is D. Conclusion I: Some Pens are Pencils (from Stmt 1) → Those Pens are Erasers (Stmt 2) → Not Sharpeners (Stmt 3). True. Conclusion II: No Pencil is Sharpener (All Pencils are Erasers, No Eraser is Sharpener) ⇒ 'Can never be' is true. Both follow. Full Solution: From 'Only a few Pens are Pencils', we get 'Some Pens are Pencils'. From 'All Pencils are Erasers' and 'No Eraser is Sharpener', we get 'No Pencil is Sharpener'. For Conclusion I: The Pens which are Pencils are Erasers and hence not Sharpeners. So 'Some Pens are not Sharpeners' follows. For Conclusion II: Since No Pencil is Sharpener, it is definite that All Pencils can never be Sharpeners. Hence both conclusions follow.
