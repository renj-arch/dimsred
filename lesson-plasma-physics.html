Plasma Physics — Debye Shielding, MHD & Fusion Plasmas

Deep dive into Plasma Physics — the quasineutral ionized gas with collective behavior, the Debye length λ_D = √(ε₀ k_B T_e / (n_e e²)) characterizing the screening distance of electric fields in a plasma, the plasma parameter Λ = n λ_D³ counting the number of particles in a Debye sphere (Λ ≫ 1 for an ideal plasma), the single-particle motion: the gyromotion with Larmor radius r_L = m v_⟂/(qB) and gyrofrequency Ω = qB/m, the guiding center drifts (E×B drift v_E = E × B/B², ∇B drift v_∇B = (m v_⟂²/2q) (B × ∇B)/B³, curvature drift v_R = (m v_∥²/q) (R_c × B)/(R_c² B²)), the two-fluid equations for electrons and ions (continuity, momentum, energy with collisions and electromagnetic forces), magnetohydrodynamics (the single-fluid MHD equations: ∂ρ/∂t + ∇·(ρv) = 0, ρ dv/dt = −∇p + J × B, E + v × B = ηJ, ∂B/∂t = ∇ × (v × B) + (η/μ₀) ∇²B), the frozen-in flux theorem (Alfvén's theorem: magnetic field lines move with the fluid in ideal MHD), the plasma beta β = 2μ₀ p/B² (ratio of thermal to magnetic pressure), plasma waves: Langmuir waves ω² = ω_pe² + 3k² v_Te² (electron plasma waves), ion-acoustic waves ω² = k² c_s²/(1 + k² λ_D²) with sound speed c_s = √(k_B T_e/m_i), Alfvén waves ω² = k_∥² v_A² with Alfvén speed v_A = B/√(μ₀ ρ), electromagnetic waves in plasmas ω² = ω_pe² + k²c², the magnetosonic waves (fast and slow), Landau damping (the collisionless damping of plasma waves via resonant wave-particle interaction at v = ω/k), magnetic reconnection (the Sweet-Parker model L = (τ_A τ_R)^{1/2}, Petschek model with slow shocks), plasma instabilities (the Rayleigh-Taylor, Kelvin-Helmholtz, drift-wave, and Weibel instabilities), and the physics of fusion plasmas in toroidal confinement devices.

Part 1

⚡ Fundamentals: Debye Shielding and Single-Particle Motion

Basic Plasma Physics
Quasineutrality, Debye Length, Plasma Parameter, and Single-Particle Orbits
A plasma is a quasineutral gas of charged particles that exhibits collective behavior. The Debye length λ_D = √(ε₀ k_B T_e / (n_e e²)) is the distance over which electric fields are screened. Inserting a test charge Q into a plasma creates a screened Coulomb potential φ(r) = (Q/4π ε₀ r) e^{−r/λ_D}. The plasma parameter Λ = n λ_D³ is the number of particles in a Debye sphere. A plasma is ideal when Λ ≫ 1 (weak coupling) and collective effects dominate. The plasma frequency ω_pe = √(n_e e²/(ε₀ m_e)) is the natural oscillation frequency of electrons about the ion background. A single charged particle in a uniform magnetic field B = B ẑ gyrates with cyclotron (Larmor) frequency Ω = qB/m and Larmor radius r_L = m v_⟂/(|q|B). The magnetic moment μ = m v_⟂²/(2B) = (kinetic energy perpendicular to B)/B is an adiabatic invariant (conserved for slow variations of B). The guiding center motion consists of parallel motion along B and perpendicular drifts: the E×B drift v_E = (E × B)/B² (charge-independent, so no net current), the ∇B drift v_∇B = (m v_⟂²/2q) (B × ∇B)/B³ (charge-dependent, producing currents), the curvature drift v_R = (m v_∥²/q) (R_c × B)/(R_c² B²) (from centrifugal force along curved field lines), and the polarization drift v_p = (m/qB²) dE_⟂/dt (responsible for inertial effects). The conservation of the first adiabatic invariant μ leads to magnetic mirroring: as a particle moves into a stronger B field, v_⟂ increases and v_∥ decreases until the particle is reflected (the mirror point where v_∥ = 0). The loss cone angle sin²θ_L = B_min/B_max determines which particles are trapped.
The Debye length and plasma parameter define the collective nature of plasmas; single-particle drifts in magnetic fields are the foundation for understanding confinement and transport.
Part 2

🌀 Magnetohydrodynamics and the Frozen-In Flux

MHD
The MHD Equations, Magnetic Reynolds Number, Flux Freezing, and the Plasma Beta
The MHD equations treat the plasma as a single conducting fluid. The continuity equation ∂ρ/∂t + ∇·(ρv) = 0. The momentum equation ρ dv/dt = −∇p + J × B + ρ g, where J = (∇ × B)/μ₀ is the current density. The induction equation ∂B/∂t = ∇ × (v × B) + (η/μ₀) ∇²B, where η is the electrical resistivity. The Magnetic Reynolds number R_m = μ₀ L v / η compares advection to diffusion of the magnetic field. For R_m ≫ 1 (ideal MHD), the field is frozen into the plasma (Alfvén's theorem): the magnetic flux through any surface moving with the fluid is constant. The frozen-in condition implies that magnetic field lines are material lines — they move with the plasma and cannot break or reconnect in ideal MHD. The ideal MHD Ohm's law E + v × B = 0. The plasma beta β = 2μ₀ p / B² compares the thermal pressure to the magnetic pressure p_B = B²/(2μ₀). In low-β plasmas, magnetic fields dominate the dynamics; in high-β plasmas, pressure gradients drive flows. The ∇p force balance in static MHD: ∇p = J × B, requiring currents perpendicular to both ∇p and B. The Grad-Shafranov equation Δ^* ψ = −μ₀ R² p'(ψ) − FF'(ψ) describes axisymmetric MHD equilibrium in toroidal geometry (tokamaks). The safety factor q = (r/R) (B_φ/B_θ) measures the helical pitch of field lines in a torus; the Kruskal-Shafranov limit q > 1 for stability. The MHD wave modes: shear Alfvén waves (ω = k_∥ v_A), fast magnetosonic waves ω² = k² (v_A² + c_s²), and slow magnetosonic waves (ω² = k_∥² v_A² c_s² / (v_A² + c_s²)).
Ideal MHD treats the plasma as a conducting fluid with magnetic field lines frozen into the flow — a powerful approximation that breaks down only at small scales where resistivity allows reconnection.
Part 3

🌊 Waves, Instabilities, and Kinetic Theory

Waves and Instabilities
Langmuir Waves, Ion-Acoustic Waves, Landau Damping, and Macro-Instabilities
Electrostatic waves in plasmas derive from the Vlasov-Poisson system. The cold plasma dispersion for electron plasma waves (Langmuir waves): ω² = ω_pe² + 3k² v_Te², where v_Te = √(k_B T_e/m_e). Ion-acoustic waves have dispersion ω² = k² c_s² / (1 + k² λ_D²), with ion sound speed c_s = √(k_B T_e/m_i) — the ions provide the inertia and electrons provide the pressure. The Vlasov equation ∂f_s/∂t + v·∇f_s + (q_s/m_s)(E + v×B)·∂f_s/∂v = 0 describes the kinetic distribution function f_s(x, v, t) for species s. Landau damping (Landau 1946) is the collisionless damping of plasma waves due to resonant particles with velocity v ≈ ω/k (the phase velocity). The Landau damping rate for Langmuir waves: γ_L = ω √(π/8) (ω_pe/k v_Te)³ exp(−ω_pe²/(2k² v_Te²)). The Bump-on-tail instability occurs when a fast electron beam creates a positive slope in f(v) at v ≈ ω/k, leading to wave growth (inverse Landau damping). The Weibel instability grows from anisotropic velocity distributions. The MHD instabilities: the Rayleigh-Taylor instability occurs when a dense fluid is accelerated by a light fluid (or vice versa in a gravitational field), with growth rate γ = √(kg), where k is the wavenumber. The Kelvin-Helmholtz instability arises from velocity shear across a boundary, with growth rate γ = k Δv/√(ρ₁+ρ₂). The kink instability (m = 1 mode) in a plasma column occurs when the plasma current exceeds the Kruskal-Shafranov limit. The sausage instability (m = 0 mode) constricts the plasma column. The tearing mode is a resistive instability that drives magnetic reconnection by forming magnetic islands at rational surfaces where k·B = 0.
Landau damping is a quintessentially kinetic effect — wave-particle resonance transfers energy from the wave to particles or vice versa, impossible to capture in any fluid description.
Part 4

🔬 Fusion Plasmas and Toroidal Confinement

Fusion
The Lawson Criterion, Tokamak Confinement, H-Mode, and the ITER Design
The fusion reactions of interest for energy: D + T → ⁴He (3.5 MeV) + n (14.1 MeV), D + D → T (1.01 MeV) + p (3.03 MeV) and D + D → ³He (0.82 MeV) + n (2.45 MeV). The reactivity ⟨σv⟩ peaks at T ∼ 10–20 keV for D-T. The Lawson criterion (1957) for ignition: n τ_E ≥ 12 k_B T / ⟨σv⟩ E_α, where n is density, τ_E is energy confinement time, and E_α = 3.5 MeV is the alpha particle energy. For D-T at T = 20 keV, n τ_E ≥ 10^20 s/m³ (the fusion triple product n T τ_E ≥ 3 × 10^21 m⁻³ keV s). The tokamak confines plasma in a toroidal geometry with a strong toroidal field B_φ (created by external coils) and a poloidal field B_θ (from the plasma current induced by the central solenoid). The flux surfaces are nested tori labeled by the poloidal magnetic flux ψ. The safety factor q(r) = r B_φ/(R B_θ). The H-mode (high confinement mode) is characterized by a pedestal at the plasma edge with steep pressure gradients, forming an edge transport barrier (ETB) that suppresses turbulent transport. The L-H transition occurs above a threshold heating power P_th. The ELMs (edge localized modes) are periodic relaxation oscillations of the pedestal. The scrape-off layer (SOL) and divertor handle the exhaust power and impurities. The stellarator (e.g., Wendelstein 7-X) uses external 3D coils to create twist in the magnetic field without requiring a plasma current, avoiding disruptions and current drive. Microturbulence — driven by the ion temperature gradient (ITG) mode and the trapped electron mode (TEM) — determines the anomalous transport. The gyrokinetic equation describes turbulence at frequencies ω ≪ Ω_i with the gyro-averaged distribution function. ITER (the international tokamak under construction in France) aims for Q = P_fusion/P_heating ≥ 10, producing 500 MW of fusion power from 50 MW of heating. The DEMO design (post-ITER) aims to demonstrate net electric power generation.
Achieving controlled fusion requires confining a 150-million-degree plasma long enough for the fusion reactions to produce net energy — the grand challenge of plasma physics and fusion engineering.
