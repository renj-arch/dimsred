Key Concepts

Language Development and Acquisition

Language development is the process by which children acquire the ability to understand and use language. It is a remarkable human achievement that follows a predictable sequence across cultures. Understanding language development theories is essential for CTET teachers to support children's language learning in the classroom, particularly in multilingual contexts where children may be learning in a language different from their mother tongue.

Key Concepts

First Language (L1) Acquisition: Children acquire their mother tongue naturally through exposure and interaction, without formal instruction. Stages of L1 acquisition: Pre-linguistic stage (0-12 months - crying, cooing, babbling), Holophrastic stage (12-18 months - one-word utterances), Two-word stage (18-24 months - 'mama go', 'big ball'), Telegraphic stage (24-36 months - 'daddy go store', 'want cookie'), and later multi-word stage (3+ years - complex sentences, grammar refinement). By age 5, most children have acquired the basic grammatical structures of their native language.

Theories of Language Acquisition: Noam Chomsky proposed the Language Acquisition Device (LAD) - an innate biological capacity for language. He argued that children are born with Universal Grammar (UG), a set of grammatical principles common to all languages. This theory is called Nativism. B.F. Skinner argued that language is learned through operant conditioning (reinforcement, imitation, association) - the Behaviorist view. Lev Vygotsky emphasized social interaction - language develops through communication with more knowledgeable speakers (Sociocultural theory). Jean Piaget linked language to cognitive development - language depends on the development of symbolic thought. The Interactionist view (e.g., Bruner's Language Acquisition Support System - LASS) combines innate capacity with social interaction and environmental input.

Second Language (L2) Acquisition: Stephen Krashen's Monitor Model has five hypotheses: Acquisition-Learning Hypothesis (acquired vs learned language - acquisition is subconscious, learning is conscious), Monitor Hypothesis (learned language acts as an editor/monitor), Natural Order Hypothesis (grammatical structures are acquired in a predictable order), Input Hypothesis (comprehensible input i+1 - input slightly above current level promotes acquisition), and Affective Filter Hypothesis (anxiety, motivation, self-confidence affect acquisition; low filter = better acquisition).

L1 vs L2 Learning Differences: L1 acquisition is natural, subconscious, and universally successful. L2 learning is often conscious, influenced by age of onset, motivation, and exposure. Key concepts: Interlanguage (the developing linguistic system between L1 and L2, with its own rules), Fossilization (persistent L2 errors that become permanent), Language Transfer (positive when L1 and L2 structures are similar, negative/interference when they differ), and Critical Period Hypothesis (there is an optimal age window for native-like L2 proficiency).

Multilingualism in Indian Classrooms: India has 22 scheduled languages and hundreds of dialects. The Three-Language Formula (Hindi, English, and a regional language) is the official language education policy. Research shows that multilingualism is cognitively beneficial (enhances metalinguistic awareness, cognitive flexibility, creative thinking). The mother tongue should be used as a resource in teaching, not seen as a problem. Code-switching and code-mixing are natural features of multilingual communication.

Solved Examples

Example 1
According to Chomsky, children are born with an innate language faculty called:

a) Language Acquisition Support System b) Language Acquisition Device c) Zone of Proximal Development d) Cognitive Schema
Solution: b) Language Acquisition Device (LAD). Chomsky argued that humans are biologically programmed for language through the LAD, which contains Universal Grammar - the underlying principles common to all languages. This explains why children acquire language rapidly despite limited and often imperfect input. Bruner proposed LASS (Language Acquisition Support System), which emphasizes the role of social interaction and caregiver support in language development.
Example 2
A child learning English as a second language says, 'He go to school yesterday.' This error in past tense illustrates:

a) Language transfer b) Overgeneralization c) Fossilization d) Code-switching
Solution: b) Overgeneralization. The child has learned the rule for adding -ed to form past tense and is applying it to an irregular verb (go). This is a common developmental error called overgeneralization, showing that the child is actively constructing rules about language rather than just imitating. This is a normal part of both L1 and L2 acquisition and typically resolves with more exposure and practice.
Example 3
Krashen's Affective Filter Hypothesis suggests that effective language acquisition requires:

a) High anxiety and high motivation b) Low anxiety and low self-confidence c) Low anxiety and high motivation d) High self-confidence and high anxiety
Solution: c) Low anxiety and high motivation. Krashen's Affective Filter Hypothesis states that affective factors (anxiety, motivation, self-confidence) create a 'mental block' that can prevent comprehensible input from reaching the language acquisition device. A low affective filter (low anxiety, high motivation, high self-confidence) promotes acquisition. Creating a supportive, non-threatening classroom environment is therefore crucial for L2 learning.

Shortcut Techniques

  • L1 Acquisition Stages - P-H-T-T-M: Pre-linguistic, Holophrastic, Two-word, Telegraphic, Multi-word. Remember: Pre, Holo, Two, Tele, Multi - increasing complexity.
  • Chomsky = Nativist, Skinner = Behaviorist: Think: Chomsky = Child has innate gift (nature), Skinner = Shaped by environment (nurture). Vygotsky = Social interaction, Piaget = Cognitive development.
  • Krashen's 5 Hypotheses - MAIN IF: Monitor, Acquisition-Learning, Input (i+1), Natural Order, Filter (Affective). Remember the word 'MAIN' plus 'F' for Filter.
  • L1 vs L2: L1 = Natural, L2 = Learned. L1 = Subconscious, L2 = Conscious. L1 = From birth, L2 = After L1 is established. Think: L1 is like learning to walk; L2 is like learning to dance - one is natural, the other requires practice.
Pro Tip
For CTET, remember that the NCF 2005 and NEP 2020 strongly support multilingualism and the use of the mother tongue as a medium of instruction, especially at the primary level. The three-language formula is relevant for language policy questions. Know the key differences between acquisition and learning.

Key S-V Agreement Rules

  • With either-or/neither-nor, verb agrees with the nearer subject
  • Collective nouns can be singular or plural based on context
  • Mathematics, news, physics are singular despite ending in s
