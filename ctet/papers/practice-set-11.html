Q1. Language II
Choose the correct passive voice transformation of the interrogative sentence: "Who taught you English?"
A. By whom were you taught English?
B. By whom was English taught to you?
C. Who was English taught to you by?
D. By whom you were taught English?
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: In passive voice of 'Who + V2 + Object', the object becomes subject. Here, 'English' (object) becomes subject, requiring singular verb 'was'. Option A retains 'you' as subject (incorrect shift). Option C ends with preposition 'by' (informal/avoided in formal grammar). Option D misses auxiliary 'was' before subject. Option B correctly makes 'English' subject with 'was taught' and retains 'by whom'.
Q2. Child Development & Pedagogy
In the context of moral development according to Lawrence Kohlberg, what characterizes the 'Conventional Level'?
A. Moral reasoning based on avoiding punishment and seeking personal reward.
B. Moral reasoning based on adherence to social norms, rules, and the expectations of others.
C. Moral reasoning based on abstract principles of justice and universal ethical principles.
D. Moral reasoning that is entirely driven by biological instincts rather than societal influence.
Correct Answer: B
Option B accurately defines the Conventional Level, where individuals conform to rules to maintain social order and gain approval. Option A describes the Preconventional Level (obedience and self-interest). Option C describes the Postconventional Level (principled conscience). Option D is incorrect as Kohlberg's theory posits that moral reasoning develops through social interaction and cognitive maturation, not just biological instincts.
Q3. Language II
Which of the following is a core principle of Communicative Language Teaching (CLT)?
A. Primary focus on explicit grammar rules and structural accuracy through drills.
B. Memorization of scripted dialogues to achieve native-like pronunciation.
C. Interaction as both the means and the ultimate goal of language learning.
D. Immediate correction of all learner errors to prevent fossilization.
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut: Eliminate options reflecting Grammar-Translation (A), Audio-Lingual Method (B, D). CLT keywords: 'Communication', 'Interaction', 'Fluency', 'Meaning over Form'. Worked Solution: CLT emphasizes communicative competence. It posits that language is acquired through meaningful interaction (means) to enable real-life communication (goal). It tolerates errors as natural to interlanguage development and uses authentic materials rather than scripted drills.
Q4. Language I
Identify the figure of speech used in the following sentence: "Her voice was a soothing melody that calmed the restless crowd."
A. Simile
B. Personification
C. Metaphor
D. Hyperbole
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut: Eliminate Simile (requires 'like' or 'as'), Personification (attributes human traits to non-human), and Hyperbole (intentional exaggeration); the direct equation 'voice was a melody' signals Metaphor. Worked Solution: A Metaphor makes an implicit comparison between two unlike things by stating one *is* the other. Here, 'voice' is directly equated to 'a soothing melody' without comparative words, transferring the qualities of melody (soothing, musical) to the voice.
Q5. Mathematics
What is the unit digit of the number obtained by raising 7 to the power of 45 (7^45)?
A. 1
B. 3
C. 7
D. 9
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut: The unit digit of powers of 7 follows a cycle of 4: 7, 9, 3, 1. Divide the exponent 45 by 4; the remainder is 1. The 1st value in the cycle is 7. Step-by-step: 7^1=7, 7^2=49, 7^3=343, 7^4=2401. The cycle (7, 9, 3, 1) repeats every 4 powers. 45 = 4 × 11 + 1, so the unit digit corresponds to 7^1, which is 7.
Q6. Child Development & Pedagogy
In the context of emotional development, what is the primary function of 'empathy' in peer relationships among school-age children?
A. It allows a child to understand and share the feelings of another person
B. It enables a child to manipulate others for personal gain
C. It helps a child to ignore the emotions of others to focus on their own
D. It encourages a child to express anger towards peers
Correct Answer: A
Empathy is the ability to recognize, understand, and share the feelings of another, which is crucial for forming healthy peer relationships and prosocial behavior. Manipulating others involves social intelligence but not empathy, ignoring emotions indicates a lack of emotional awareness or suppression, and expressing anger does not define the function of empathy.
Q7. Language I
In Jean Piaget's theory of cognitive development, which specific term describes the child's inability to see that the quantity of liquid remains the same even when poured into a taller, thinner glass? A) Conservation B) Egocentrism C) Centration D) Reversibility
A. Conservation.
B. Egocentrism.
C. Centration.
D. Reversibility.
Correct Answer: C
Option C is correct because 'Centration' is the specific cognitive bias where a child focuses exclusively on one dimension (the height of the glass) and ignores others (width), leading to the incorrect conclusion that quantity has changed. Option A ('Conservation') is the ability to understand quantities remain constant, which the child lacks here. Option B ('Egocentrism') refers to difficulty taking another's perspective. Option D ('Reversibility') is the understanding that actions can be undone. The question asks for the specific error mechanism described.
Q8. Environmental Studies
Which of the following waste materials will decompose naturally through the action of microorganisms within a relatively short period?
A. Vegetable peels
B. Polythene bags
C. Aluminium foil
D. Glass bottles
Correct Answer: A
Shortcut: Organic origin (plant/animal) = Biodegradable; Synthetic/Metal/Glass = Non-biodegradable. Step-by-step: Vegetable peels are organic kitchen waste composed of cellulose and water, easily broken down by soil bacteria and fungi. Polythene (plastic), aluminium (metal), and glass (silica) are inorganic/synthetic materials that microorganisms cannot digest, persisting in the environment for hundreds of years.
Q9. Mathematics
A train is moving at a speed of 72 km/h. What is its speed in metres per second (m/s)?
A. 15 m/s
B. 20 m/s
C. 25 m/s
D. 30 m/s
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: To convert km/h to m/s, multiply by the factor 5/18. Calculation: 72 × (5/18) = (72/18) × 5 = 4 × 5 = 20 m/s. Step-by-step: 1 km = 1000 m and 1 hour = 3600 seconds. Speed in m/s = (72 × 1000) / 3600 = 72000 / 3600 = 720 / 36 = 20 m/s.
Q10. Language I
A primary school teacher uses 'exit tickets' where students write one thing they learned and one question they still have at the end of a lesson. The teacher uses this data to plan the next day's remedial activity. This practice is a classic example of:
A. Assessment for Learning (Formative Assessment)
B. Assessment of Learning (Summative Assessment)
C. Assessment as Learning (Metacognitive Assessment)
D. Standardized Achievement Testing
Correct Answer: A
Shortcut: Identify keywords 'uses data to plan next day's remedial activity' indicating immediate instructional adjustment; eliminate 'end-of-term' (Summative), 'external comparison' (Standardized), and 'self-regulation' (Metacognitive) options. Worked Solution: Formative Assessment (Assessment for Learning) is conducted during the instructional process to provide continuous feedback to both teacher and learner, enabling timely modification of teaching strategies to address learning gaps, unlike summative assessment which evaluates learning at the end.
Q11. Child Development & Pedagogy
A teacher observes that a 10-year-old child can logically solve conservation of volume tasks but struggles to solve abstract hypothetical problems like 'If all cats were blue, would the sky be green?'. According to Piaget, this child is most likely in which stage of cognitive development?
A. A. Sensorimotor Stage
B. B. Pre-operational Stage
C. C. Concrete Operational Stage
D. D. Formal Operational Stage
Correct Answer: C
The Concrete Operational Stage (approx. 7-11 years) is characterized by the mastery of conservation, reversibility, and classification, but thinking remains tied to concrete, tangible objects and events. Abstract hypothetical-deductive reasoning is the hallmark of the subsequent Formal Operational Stage. Elimination trick: Sensorimotor (0-2) and Pre-operational (2-7) fail conservation tasks. Formal Operational succeeds at the hypothetical task described.

A. Taking students on a nature walk to observe local plants and asking them to record the leaf shape, venation, and texture in their observation notebooks.
B. Asking students to write down the definitions of 'Photosynthesis' and 'Transpiration' from the textbook.
C. Conducting a multiple-choice test on the functions of root, stem, and leaf.
D. Asking students to draw and label the diagram of a 'Typical Flower' from memory.
Correct Answer: A
Shortcut: Eliminate options testing only memory/recall (B, C, D). Option A is the only one requiring real-time sensory engagement (observation) and simultaneous documentation (recording) in an authentic environment. Step-by-step: 'Observation and Recording' involves using senses to gather first-hand data and documenting it immediately. Option B tests rote memorization of definitions. Option C tests recognition/recall of theoretical knowledge. Option D tests reproduction of a memorized diagram. Option A requires students to observe live specimens, discriminate features (shape, venation, texture), and record findings directly, aligning perfectly with the skill.
Q13. Mathematics
Which of the following numbers is a prime number?
A. 9
B. 15
C. 17
D. 21
Correct Answer: C
A prime number has exactly two distinct positive divisors: 1 and itself. 9 is divisible by 3; 15 is divisible by 3 and 5; 21 is divisible by 3 and 7. Only 17 has no divisors other than 1 and 17, making it the only prime number in the list.

Q15. Language II
Choose the correct indirect speech transformation of the following direct speech: He said, "I am writing a letter."
A. He said that he is writing a letter.
B. He said that he was writing a letter.
C. He said that I was writing a letter.
D. He said that he will write a letter.
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: Eliminate options where the tense does not shift back (Present Continuous → Past Continuous) or the pronoun 'I' remains unchanged. Option A fails tense shift; Option C fails pronoun change; Option D changes meaning/tense incorrectly. Worked Solution: Reporting verb 'said' (past) triggers backshift. 'I' becomes 'he'. 'am writing' (Present Continuous) becomes 'was writing' (Past Continuous). Result: He said that he was writing a letter.
