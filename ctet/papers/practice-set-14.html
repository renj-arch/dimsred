A. Stage 1: Obedience and Punishment Orientation
B. Stage 2: Individualism and Exchange
C. Stage 3: Good Interpersonal Relationships
D. Stage 4: Maintaining Social Order
Correct Answer: A
Shortcut/Trick: Spot the keyword "punished" or "fear of authority". In Kohlberg's Pre-conventional level, Stage 1 is defined solely by avoiding punishment. Stage 2 involves self-interest ('what's in it for me?'), while Stages 3 and 4 belong to the Conventional level (peer approval/law & order). Eliminate options B, C, D based on level mismatch. Full Solution: Lawrence Kohlberg's Stage 1 (Obedience and Punishment Orientation) is the earliest stage of moral reasoning, typically found in young children. The child judges the morality of an action by its direct consequences—specifically, the fear of punishment from authority figures (police/teacher). The statement "I will be punished" is the definitive marker for Stage 1. Stage 2 focuses on instrumental exchange ('you scratch my back...'), Stage 3 on being a 'good person' to gain approval, and Stage 4 on upholding laws to maintain societal order.

Correct Answer: A

The main clause 'would have understood' is correctly formed.

B. 9
C. 3
D. 1
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut: The unit digits of powers of 7 repeat in a cycle of 4: 7, 9, 3, 1. Divide the exponent 23 by 4; remainder = 3. The third digit in the cycle is 3. Step‑by‑step: 7^1=7, 7^2=49→9, 7^3=343→3, 7^4=2401→1, cycle repeats. Since 23 = 4×5 + 3, the unit digit equals that of 7^3, which is 3.

Worked: LCM(12,15,18) = 2²×3²×5 = 180. Smallest 4-digit multiple: 1000 ÷ 180 = 5 remainder 100. Required number = 1000 + (180 - 100) = 1080.
