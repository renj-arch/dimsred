Key Concepts

What is Reading Comprehension?

Reading Comprehension (RC) passages test your ability to read, understand, and interpret written text. In banking exams like IBPS PO, SBI Clerk, and RBI Grade B, RC passages are typically 350-500 words long, covering topics like economics, banking, technology, social issues, and science. Questions test main idea, inference, vocabulary in context, and specific details.

Types of Questions in RC

1. Main Idea/Purpose: What is the central theme or primary purpose of the passage? Options may use phrases like 'to explain', 'to argue', 'to describe'.

2. Factual/Detail Questions: Specific information directly stated in the passage. These are the easiest � locate the relevant sentence and choose the matching option.

3. Inference Questions: Information not directly stated but implied. Use clues in the text to draw logical conclusions. These require deeper understanding.

4. Vocabulary in Context: A word from the passage is given; you must choose the option closest in meaning based on how it is used in the passage.

5. Tone of the Passage: Identify the author's attitude � critical, supportive, neutral, optimistic, skeptical, etc.

6. Title Selection: Choose the most appropriate title that captures the essence of the passage.

Reading Strategies

1. Skim First: Quickly read the first sentence of each paragraph to get the gist. Then read the questions before reading the passage in full. This helps you focus on relevant parts.

2. Identify the Structure: Is it problem-solution? Cause-effect? Compare-contrast? Descriptive? Recognizing structure helps predict content.

3. Spot Transition Words: Words like 'however', 'therefore', 'consequently', 'in contrast', 'furthermore' signal the relationship between ideas and are often tested in inference questions.

4. Read for Meaning, Not Words: Focus on understanding the idea rather than memorizing details. You can always refer back to the passage.

Solved Examples

Example 1 (Main Idea)
Passage: 'Digital banking has transformed the financial landscape. While it has increased accessibility and convenience, it has also raised concerns about cybersecurity and data privacy. Banks are investing heavily in encryption and multi-factor authentication to address these challenges.'

What is the main idea of the passage?

a) Digital banking is completely safe b) Digital banking has both benefits and challenges c) Banks should stop digital banking d) Encryption is expensive
Solution: b) The passage discusses both the positive (accessibility, convenience) and negative (cybersecurity, privacy) aspects, making option b the best choice.
Example 2 (Inference)
Passage: 'The central bank's decision to keep interest rates unchanged was unexpected, given the rising inflation. Analysts had widely predicted a rate hike.'

What can be inferred from the passage?

a) The central bank always follows analysts' predictions b) Inflation is under control c) Analysts expected a rate hike d) Interest rates were reduced
Solution: c) The passage says analysts 'widely predicted a rate hike' meaning they expected rates to increase. So option c is correct.
Example 3 (Vocabulary in Context)
Passage: 'The government's proactive fiscal measures helped mitigate the economic downturn.'

What does 'mitigate' mean in this context?

a) Worsen b) Cause c) Lessen d) Ignore
Solution: c) Lessen. 'Mitigate' means to make less severe or reduce the impact of something.

Shortcut Techniques

  • Read the questions before reading the passage to know what to look for.
  • For vocabulary questions, read the surrounding sentence � the context almost always gives the meaning.
  • Eliminate extreme options (words like 'always', 'never', 'completely') unless the passage clearly supports them.
  • For tone questions, look at the adjectives and verbs the author uses � positive, negative, or neutral words are giveaways.
Pro Tip
In RBI Grade B phase 2, RC passages are often from Economic Survey or RBI annual reports. Reading editorials from The Hindu and Business Standard regularly helps you get comfortable with the language and topics used in these exams.

Advanced Grammar

Conditional sentences, subject-verb agreement rules, and advanced vocabulary building techniques.

Key S-V Agreement Rules

  • Each/every/either/neither take singular verbs
  • With either-or/neither-nor, verb agrees with the nearer subject
  • Collective nouns can be singular or plural based on context
  • Mathematics, news, physics are singular despite ending in s
Pro Tip
For RC, read questions FIRST then scan passage. For error detection, read sentence from right to left.
