Choose the option indicating the starter(s) which can form a grammatically and contextually correct single sentence. **Sentence 1:** The company invested heavily in research and development. **Sentence 2:** It failed to launch a single viable product in five years. **Starters:** (i) Although the company invested heavily... (ii) Despite investing heavily... (iii) The company invested heavily; however,
A. Only (i)
B. Only (ii)
C. Both (i) and (ii)
D. All (i), (ii) and (iii)
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut: **Connector Grammar Classification**. (i) 'Although' = Subordinating Conjunction (joins clauses). Correct. (ii) 'Despite' = Preposition (takes Gerund 'investing'). Correct. (iii) 'However' = Conjunctive Adverb. It **cannot** join two independent clauses with just a comma/semicolon in a 'starter' format without creating a run-on or requiring specific punctuation (; however,). As a starter 'The company invested...; however,' it creates a compound sentence, but standard 'starter' questions expect a single complex sentence structure. 'However' fails the 'single sentence flow' test without punctuation change. Step-by-step: 1. Test (i): 'Although the company invested heavily in R&D, it failed to launch...' -> Grammatically correct complex sentence. 2. Test (ii): 'Despite investing heavily in R&D, the company failed to launch...' -> Correct participle phrase. 3. Test (iii): 'The company invested heavily; however, it failed...' -> This is two independent clauses joined by a conjunctive adverb. While punctuation allows it, 'starter' questions typically require Subordinators (Although) or Prepositions (Despite) to form *one* syntactic unit. (iii) is structurally distinct (Compound vs Complex). Thus, only (i) and (ii) are valid starters.
