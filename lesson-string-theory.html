String Theory — Bosonic Strings, Superstrings & M-Theory

Deep dive into String Theory — the Nambu-Goto action S_NG = −T ∫ d²σ √−h and its equivalent Polyakov action S_P = −(T/2) ∫ d²σ √−γ γ^{ab} ∂_a X^μ ∂_b X_μ, the worldsheet conformal symmetry and the Virasoro algebra [L_m, L_n] = (m−n) L_{m+n} + (c/12) m(m²−1) δ_{m+n,0}, the quantization of closed and open strings (the massless spectrum containing the graviton, dilaton, and Kalb-Ramond field for closed strings, and the photon for open strings), the critical dimension D = 26 (bosonic) and D = 10 (superstrings), the superstring actions with worldsheet supersymmetry (the RNS formalism with Neveu-Schwarz and Ramond sectors), the GSO projection (eliminating the tachyon and ensuring spacetime supersymmetry), the five consistent superstring theories: Type I (SO(32) with open and closed strings), Type IIA (non-chiral, D=10 N=2A), Type IIB (chiral, D=10 N=2B), heterotic E₈×E₈, and heterotic SO(32), the T-duality relating Type IIA and Type IIB on a circle (R ↔ α'/R), the S-duality relating Type I to heterotic SO(32) and Type IIB to itself, D-branes as hypersurfaces where open strings can end (carrying Ramond-Ramond charges), the Calabi-Yau compactifications (preserving N=1 supersymmetry in 4D with a holonomy group SU(3)), the AdS/CFT correspondence (the equivalence between Type IIB on AdS₅×S⁵ and N=4 super-Yang-Mills in 4D), and the eleven-dimensional M-theory (low-energy limit of which is 11D supergravity) unifying all five superstring theories via dualities.

Part 1

🌀 Bosonic Strings and the Polyakov Action

Classical Strings
The Nambu-Goto Action, Polyakov Action, and Conformal Symmetry
A relativistic string sweeps out a two-dimensional worldsheet X^μ(σ,τ) in D-dimensional spacetime. The Nambu-Goto action S_NG = −T ∫ d²σ √−h is proportional to the area of the worldsheet, where T = 1/(2πα') is the string tension, α' = ℓ_s² is the Regge slope parameter, and h_ab = ∂_a X^μ ∂_b X_μ is the induced metric. The Polyakov action S_P = −(T/2) ∫ d²σ √−γ γ^{ab} ∂_a X^μ ∂_b X_μ introduces an auxiliary worldsheet metric γ_{ab}. Varying γ_{ab} gives the constraint T_{ab} = (2/√−γ) δS_P/δγ^{ab} = 0 — the energy-momentum tensor vanishes, reproducing the Nambu-Goto equations. The Polyakov action has three symmetries: Poincaré invariance (global), reparametrization invariance (diffeomorphisms on the worldsheet), and Weyl invariance γ_{ab} → e^{2ω(σ)} γ_{ab} (local scale transformations). The conformal gauge fixes γ_{ab} = e^{2ω} η_{ab}, reducing the equations of motion to the free wave equation ∂_+ ∂_− X^μ = 0 in light-cone coordinates σ^± = τ ± σ. The general solution factorizes into left-moving and right-moving modes: X^μ = X_L^μ(σ^+) + X_R^μ(σ^−). The Virasoro constraints T_{++} = (∂_+ X)^2 = 0 and T_{−−} = (∂_− X)^2 = 0 follow from T_{ab} = 0. The mode expansion for closed strings: X^μ = x^μ + α' p^μ τ + i√(α'/2) Σ_{n≠0} (α_n^μ/n) e^{−inσ^+} + (α̃_n^μ/n) e^{−inσ^−}. The Virasoro generators L_m = (1/2) Σ_n :α_{m−n}·α_n: satisfy the Virasoro algebra [L_m, L_n] = (m−n) L_{m+n} + (c/12) m(m²−1) δ_{m+n,0} with central charge c = D. The critical dimension D = 26 arises from requiring the vanishing of the conformal anomaly (the Weyl symmetry is preserved at the quantum level only when c = 26 for the bosonic string).
The Polyakov action reveals that string theory is a two-dimensional conformal field theory coupled to D-dimensional spacetime — the critical dimension D = 26 emerges from anomaly cancellation.
Part 2

🌟 Superstrings and Spacetime Supersymmetry

Superstrings
The RNS Formalism, GSO Projection, and the Five Superstring Theories
The RNS (Ramond-Neveu-Schwarz) formalism adds worldsheet fermions ψ^μ(σ,τ) to the Polyakov action: S = −(T/2) ∫ d²σ (∂_a X^μ ∂^a X_μ + i ψ̄^μ ρ^a ∂_a ψ_μ). The superconformal gauge gives the equations of motion ∂_+ ψ_−^μ = 0 and ∂_− ψ_+^μ = 0, so left-moving and right-moving fermions decouple. The mode expansions differ for the two sectors: in the Neveu-Schwarz (NS) sector, fermions are half-integer moded (ψ_r^μ with r ∈ ℤ + 1/2, antiperiodic boundary conditions); in the Ramond (R) sector, fermions are integer moded (ψ_n^μ with n ∈ ℤ, periodic boundary conditions). The super-Virasoro algebra includes the bosonic generators L_m and fermionic generators G_r (NS) or F_n (R). The critical dimension for the superstring is D = 10 (from the conformal anomaly vanishing when c = 15 for the (1,1) superconformal field theory). The GSO projection (Gliozzi-Scherk-Olive) projects out the tachyon from the NS sector and ensures spacetime supersymmetry by keeping only states with (−1)^F = +1 in the NS sector and appropriate chirality projections in the R sector. The five consistent superstring theories in D = 10: Type IIA (non-chiral, left and right R sectors have opposite chirality, giving N=2A supersymmetry in 10D), Type IIB (chiral, same chirality in both R sectors, N=2B), Type I (open and closed strings with SO(32) gauge group, N=1), heterotic SO(32) (left-moving bosonic D=26 string with 16 compactified dimensions, right-moving superstring, N=1), and heterotic E₈×E₈ (same but with E₈×E₈ gauge group). The low-energy limits are various supergravity theories in 10D.
The GSO projection eliminates the tachyon and introduces spacetime supersymmetry; the five superstring theories are distinguished by their chirality, gauge groups, and open/closed string content.

🔷 Extra Dimensions, T-Duality, and D-Branes

Dualities
Compactification, T-Duality, D-Branes as Charged Hypersurfaces, and Calabi-Yau Spaces
Compactification on a circle of radius R yields a Kaluza-Klein tower of states with momentum p = n/R (n ∈ ℤ) and winding modes w = m R/α' (m ∈ ℤ) for closed strings. The mass spectrum: M² = n²/R² + m² R²/α'² + (oscillator contributions). T-duality exchanges momentum and winding modes under R ↔ α'/R, mapping M² invariant. T-duality maps Type IIA on a circle of radius R to Type IIB on a circle of radius α'/R, and vice versa. D-branes are dynamical hypersurfaces of Dp dimensions on which open strings can end. A Dp-brane has p spatial dimensions and carries Ramond-Ramond (R-R) charge under the (p+1)-form gauge potential C_{p+1}. The open-string spectrum on a Dp-brane contains a U(1) gauge field A_a (a = 0,…,p) and transverse scalars Φ^i (i = p+1,…,9) describing the brane's position. The Dirac-Born-Infeld (DBI) action S_DBI = −T_p ∫ d^{p+1}ξ √−det(G_{ab} + 2πα' F_{ab}) describes the dynamics of the gauge field on the brane. Brane stacks of N coincident D-branes yield a U(N) gauge theory via the Chan-Paton factors. Calabi-Yau manifolds are Ricci-flat Kähler manifolds with SU(3) holonomy in 6 real dimensions, preserving N=1 supersymmetry upon compactification from 10D to 4D. The Hodge numbers h^{1,1} and h^{2,1} parametrize the Kähler and complex structure moduli. Mirror symmetry exchanges the Hodge numbers h^{1,1} ↔ h^{2,1} between mirror Calabi-Yau pairs. Branes wrapping cycles in the Calabi-Yau give rise to gauge theories and chiral matter in 4D.
D-branes are not just defects where strings end — they are dynamical objects carrying R-R charge, enabling gauge theories, holography, and the geometric realization of particle physics.

♾️ M-Theory and the Web of Dualities

M-Theory
The Strong-Coupling Limit, S-Duality, Eleven-Dimensional Supergravity, and the Second Superstring Revolution
S-duality exchanges strong and weak coupling: Type IIB is self-dual under g_s ↔ 1/g_s, with the SL(2, ℤ) symmetry acting on τ = a + i/g_s (where a = C₀, the axion). Type I S-dualizes to heterotic SO(32). M-theory (Witten 1995) is the 11-dimensional theory whose low-energy limit is 11D supergravity with bosonic fields: the metric g_{MN}, the 3-form gauge potential C_{MNP}, and gravitino. The compactification of M-theory on a circle S¹ of radius R₁₁ gives Type IIA string theory with coupling g_s = (R₁₁/ℓ_Pl)^{3/2} and string scale ℓ_s = ℓ_Pl^{3/2} / R₁₁^{1/2}. As R₁₁ → ∞ (strong coupling of IIA), M-theory emerges in 11D. The M2-brane (2-brane) and M5-brane (5-brane) are the fundamental extended objects of M-theory. M-theory on S¹/ℤ₂ (an interval) yields the heterotic E₈×E₈ string. The web of dualities unifies all five superstring theories and 11D supergravity as limits of a single underlying theory — M-theory. The AdS/CFT correspondence (Maldacena 1997) posits the equivalence between Type IIB string theory on AdS₅×S⁵ and N=4 super-Yang-Mills theory in 4D. The duality is holographic: the bulk theory in AdS has one extra dimension representing the energy scale of the boundary CFT. The dictionary: the bulk partition function Z_bulk[φ] = ⟨exp(∫ φ₀ O)⟩_{CFT} equates the generating functional of boundary correlators with the bulk on-shell action. The Klebanov-Strassler solution, the Maldacena-Núñez solution, and holographic QCD are top-down models applying AdS/CFT to strongly coupled gauge theories. The swampland program (Vafa 2005) constrains which effective field theories can be UV-completed in string theory, with the distance conjecture and the weak gravity conjecture as key criteria.
M-theory unifies all superstring theories through dualities, revealing that they are different perturbative limits of a single 11-dimensional quantum theory of gravity and branes.
