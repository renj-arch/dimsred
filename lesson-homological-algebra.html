Homological Algebra — The Algebra of Long Exact Sequences

Homological algebra studies chain complexes and their homology — powerful algebraic invariants that measure the failure of exactness. It provides the language of derived functors (Ext, Tor), spectral sequences (computational machinery), derived categories (global homotopy theory), and homotopical algebra (model categories). It's the algebraic backbone of algebraic topology, algebraic geometry, and representation theory.

"Homological algebra is the art of computing invariants by resolving objects into simpler pieces."
— Henri Cartan / Samuel Eilenberg
Part 1

🔗 Chain Complexes and Homology

Complexes
Chain Complexes, Exact Sequences, and the Snake Lemma
A chain complex (C_•, d) is a sequence ... → C_n → C_{n-1} → ... with d_{n-1}∘d_n = 0. The homology H_n(C) = ker d_n / im d_{n+1} measures the failure of exactness. A short exact sequence 0 → A → B → C → 0 gives a long exact sequence in homology: ... → H_n(A) → H_n(B) → H_n(C) → H_{n-1}(A) → ... The connecting morphism ∂: H_n(C) → H_{n-1}(A) is constructed via the snake lemma — the fundamental diagram-chasing result. Projective objects lift maps through epimorphisms — used to construct projective resolutions. Injective objects extend maps through monomorphisms. Every R-module has a projective resolution P_• → M → 0 and an injective resolution 0 → M → I_•. The Horseshoe lemma combines resolutions of A and C to give a resolution of B.
Homology is the universal measure of inexactness — every short exact sequence spawns a long exact sequence in homology via the snake lemma.
Part 2

🧪 Derived Functors — Ext and Tor

Derived
Ext and Tor — Measuring Extension and Torsion
Derived functors measure the failure of a functor to be exact. For a right exact functor F, the left derived functors L_nF are computed by applying F to a projective resolution and taking homology. The Tor functor Tor_n^R(A,B) = L_n(−⊗_R B)(A) = H_n(P_• ⊗_R B). Tor_0(A,B) = A⊗B. Tor_1(A,B) = 0 if A is flat. For abelian groups, Tor_1(ℤ/m, ℤ/n) = ℤ/gcd(m,n). The Ext functor Ext^n_R(A,B) = R^n Hom_R(−,B)(A) = H^n Hom_R(P_•, B). Ext⁰(A,B) = Hom(A,B). Ext¹(ℤ/m, ℤ) = ℤ/m. Ext classifies extensions: Ext¹(A,B) is in bijection with isomorphism classes of extensions 0 → B → E → A → 0. The Yoneda product Extᵖ × Ext^q → Ext^{p+q} gives a graded ring structure on Ext*(A,A). Universal coefficient theorems relate homology with coefficients to Hom and Tor.
Tor measures torsion and common divisors; Ext measures extensions and obstructions — they're the fundamental derived functors of tensor product and Hom.
Part 3

📐 Spectral Sequences — The Computational Workhorse

Spectral
Spectral Sequences — Approximating Homology via Filtrations
A spectral sequence is a sequence of pages (E^r_{p,q}, d^r) converging to a target H_{p+q}. It's a computational tool that approximates the homology of a chain complex with a filtration. The Leray-Serre spectral sequence: for a fibration F → E → B, E²_{p,q} = H_p(B; H_q(F)) ⇒ H_{p+q}(E). The Grothendieck spectral sequence: for composite functors G∘F, E²_{p,q} = (R^p G)(R^q F)(A) ⇒ R^{p+q}(G∘F)(A). The Lyndon-Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence for group cohomology: E²_{p,q} = H_p(G/N; H_q(N, A)) ⇒ H_{p+q}(G; A). The Adams spectral sequence computes stable homotopy groups. The edge homomorphisms and differentials give relations between the input and output. A spectral sequence collapses at E^r if all differentials are zero from that page onward — common when there are only two rows or columns.
Spectral sequences are the universal approximation machine — they let you compute homology by first understanding a simpler (graded) version and correcting page by page.
Part 4

🔄 Derived Categories and Triangulated Structures

Derived
Derived Categories, Triangulated Categories, and Verdier Duality
The derived category D(A) of an abelian category A is obtained by formally inverting quasi-isomorphisms (maps inducing isomorphisms on homology). Objects are chain complexes; morphisms are roofs (zigzags). A triangulated category has a shift functor [1] and distinguished triangles X → Y → Z → X[1] that generalize short exact sequences. The Verdier octahedral axiom governs composition of triangles. The derived functor in the derived category sense is the extension of a functor to D(A) that preserves quasi-isomorphisms. The derived tensor product ⊗^L and derived Hom Rℋℴ𝓂 are the correct derived-category-level operations. Grothendieck duality: for a proper morphism f of schemes, the right adjoint f^! of Rf_* exists — the dualizing complex. The derived category D^b Coh(X) is the natural setting for Serre duality and the Riemann-Roch theorem. Koszul duality relates the derived category of a symmetric algebra to that of an exterior algebra.
Derived categories are the homotopy theory of homological algebra — they replace chain complexes up to quasi-isomorphism, making the derived functors exact.
