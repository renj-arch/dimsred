Iwasawa Theory — Cyclotomic ℤ_p-extensions, the Main Conjecture & the Ideal Class Group

Iwasawa theory is the p-adic analogue of the theory of Riemann surfaces — it studies the growth of ideal class groups in infinite ℤ_p-extensions of number fields. The cyclotomic ℤ_p-extension ℚ_∞ = ∪_{n≥1} ℚ(μ_{pⁿ}) of ℚ has Galois group Gal(ℚ_∞/ℚ) ≅ ℤ_p^× ≅ ℤ_p × Δ where Δ ≅ (ℤ/pℤ)^×. For any number field K, the compositum K_∞ = K·ℚ_∞ gives the unique ℤ_p-extension with Gal(K_∞/K) ≅ ℤ_p. The Iwasawa module X = lim← A_n (the inverse limit of p-Sylow subgroups of class groups) is a module over the Iwasawa algebra Λ = ℤ_p[[T]]. Iwasawa's theorem (1959): |A_n| = p^{λn + μpⁿ + ν} for sufficiently large n. The Iwasawa main conjecture (proved by Mazur-Wiles for ℚ, by Wiles for totally real fields): the characteristic ideal of X equals the ideal generated by the Kubota-Leopoldt p-adic L-function. Applications to the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture via Euler systems (Coates-Wiles, Kato, Rubin) complete the picture.

Part 1

📏 Cyclotomic ℤ_p-Extensions

Foundations
The Cyclotomic ℤ_p-Extension, Galois Groups, and the Leopoldt Conjecture
The cyclotomic ℤ_p-extension of ℚ is ℚ_∞ = ∪_{n≥1} ℚ(μ_{pⁿ}) where μ_{pⁿ} are the pⁿ-th roots of unity. The Galois group Gal(ℚ_∞/ℚ) ≅ ℤ_p^× ≅ ℤ_p × Δ, where Δ = Gal(ℚ(μ_p)/ℚ) ≅ (ℤ/pℤ)^× is the torsion part. For any number field K, the cyclotomic ℤ_p-extension K_∞ = K·ℚ_∞ is the unique ℤ_p-extension of K with Gal(K_∞/K) ≅ ℤ_p. The n-th layer K_n is the fixed field of the subgroup pⁿℤ_p. The Leopoldt conjecture predicts that for a totally real field K, the number of independent ℤ_p-extensions equals r₂+1 (the number of archimedean places). It is proved for abelian extensions (Brumer, Buzzard) but remains open in general. The theory of cyclotomic units provides the foundational Euler system for the main conjecture.
The cyclotomic ℤ_p-extension is the canonical infinite extension of a number field whose Galois group is ℤ_p — it plays the role of the universal covering space in p-adic analysis.
Part 2

🌍 Growth of the Class Group

Iwasawa Module
The Iwasawa Module, λ and μ Invariants, and the Growth Formula
Let K_∞/K be the cyclotomic ℤ_p-extension with layers K_n. Define A_n = the p-Sylow subgroup of the ideal class group Cl(K_n). The norm maps N_{m,n}: Cl(K_m) → Cl(K_n) for m > n give an inverse system. The Iwasawa module X = lim← A_n is a compact ℤ_p-module with a continuous action of Gal(K_∞/K) ≅ ℤ_p. The Iwasawa algebra Λ = ℤ_p[[T]] ≅ lim← ℤ_p[Gal(K_n/K)] is a complete local Noetherian ring. The structure theorem for finitely generated torsion Λ-modules: X is pseudo-isomorphic to ⊕_{i=1}^r Λ/(f_i(T)^{m_i}) ⊕ Λ/(p^{μ_j}), where f_i(T) are irreducible distinguished polynomials (monic with constant term divisible by p). The λ-invariant: λ = Σ deg(f_i)·m_i; the μ-invariant: μ = Σ μ_j. Iwasawa's theorem (1959): |A_n| = p^{λn + μpⁿ + ν} for sufficiently large n, where ν is a constant. The Ferrero-Washington theorem (1979): μ = 0 for all abelian extensions of ℚ — this simplifies the growth to |A_n| = p^{λn + ν}.
The growth of the p-part of class groups in a ℤ_p-extension is governed by two numerical invariants λ and μ, with μ often vanishing for cyclotomic fields.
Part 3

🧮 The Main Conjecture

Main Conjecture
The Kubota-Leopoldt p-adic L-Function and the Iwasawa Main Conjecture
The Kubota-Leopoldt p-adic L-function L_p(s, ω^k) is a p-adic meromorphic function that interpolates the values L(1−n, ω^{k−n}) of Dirichlet L-functions at negative integers, where ω is the Teichmüller character. For each even Dirichlet character χ, there is a p-adic L-function L_p(χ) ∈ Λ (the Iwasawa algebra). The Iwasawa main conjecture states: the characteristic ideal of the Λ-module X^{(χ)} (the χ-eigenspace of the Iwasawa module X under the action of Δ) is generated by L_p(χ). Mazur-Wiles theorem (1984): the main conjecture holds for ℚ, proved using the Euler system of cyclotomic units and the theory of modular forms. Wiles theorem (1990): the main conjecture holds for all totally real fields. A corollary: the μ-invariant vanishes for all totally real fields (by Sinnott's theorem). The main conjecture implies that the p-adic L-function exactly determines the size and structure of the ideal class groups along the cyclotomic tower — a deep bridge between analytic and algebraic objects.
The Iwasawa main conjecture equates the purely algebraic characteristic ideal of the Iwasawa module with the analytic p-adic L-function — a p-adic analog of the analytic class number formula.
Part 4

🔥 Euler Systems and the BSD Conjecture

Euler Systems
Kato's Euler System, Coates-Wiles, and Applications to BSD
An Euler system for a Galois representation T is a compatible system of cohomology classes c_n ∈ H¹(K_n, T) satisfying the corestriction compatibility: cores_{K_m/K_n}(c_m) = c_n for n < m. The cyclotomic Euler system is c_n = (1−ζ_{pⁿ}) ∈ ℚ_p(1), giving cyclotomic units. Kato's Euler system uses Siegel units from modular curves to construct elements in K₂(E) for an elliptic curve E. Kato's theorem (2004): for an elliptic curve E/ℚ with good ordinary reduction at p, the Euler system method proves: (a) the Selmer group Sel_{p^∞}(E/ℚ) is cotorsion over the Iwasawa algebra; (b) the main conjecture for elliptic curves — the p-adic L-function divides the characteristic ideal of the Selmer group. Rubin's theorem on BSD for CM elliptic curves uses the Euler system of elliptic units to bound the Mordell-Weil group and the Tate-Shafarevich group. Coates-Wiles theorem (1977): if E has complex multiplication and L(E, 1) ≠ 0 then E(ℚ) is finite. These results represent the most powerful applications of Iwasawa theory to the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture.
Euler systems provide the cohomological machinery to link p-adic L-functions to Selmer groups, yielding deep results toward the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture.

The Iwasawa Algebra and Its Structure Theory

The Iwasawa algebra Λ = ℤₚ[[T]] is the completed group ring of the Galois group Γ = Gal(K∞/K) ≅ ℤₚ. Choosing a topological generator γ of Γ identifies Λ with the formal power series ring ℤₚ[[T]] via γ ↦ 1+T. This ring is a two‑dimensional regular local ring; its maximal ideal is (p, T). Every finitely generated torsion Λ‑module M admits a pseudo‑isomorphism

M ∼ ⊕ᵢ Λ/(fᵢ(T)eᵢ) ⊕ ⊕ⱼ Λ/(pμⱼ)

where each fᵢ(T) is a distinguished polynomial (monic, all non‑leading coefficients divisible by p). The λ‑invariant of M is Σ deg(fᵢ)·eᵢ and the μ‑invariant is Σ μⱼ. The structure theorem is proved by using the Weierstrass preparation theorem for ℤₚ[[T]] and the fact that Λ is a unique factorisation domain. A key consequence is that the characteristic ideal char_Λ(M) = (∏ fᵢ(T)eᵢ·pμ) generates the Fitting ideal of M, and the order of the finite quotient M/ΓⁿM grows as pλn+μpⁿ+ν for large n. The algebra also carries a natural involution ι induced by γ ↦ γ⁻¹; the ι‑twisted module M^ι is used to formulate the functional equation of p‑adic L‑functions. Understanding the structure of Λ‑modules is the algebraic backbone of every main conjecture.

The μ‑Invariant Vanishing Theorems

Ferrero and Washington (1979) proved that μ = 0 for the cyclotomic ℤₚ‑extension of any abelian extension of ℚ. Their proof uses the fact that the p‑adic L‑function Lₚ(χ) for an even Dirichlet character χ is a unit in Λ up to a power of p, together with the main conjecture for ℚ (Mazur–Wiles). Later, Greenberg (1976) conjectured that μ = 0 for the cyclotomic ℤₚ‑extension of any totally real field. This was proved by Wiles (1990) as a corollary of his proof of the main conjecture for totally real fields. For non‑abelian extensions the situation is more subtle: there are examples (due to Greenberg and Vatsal) where μ > 0 for certain ℤₚ‑extensions of CM fields. The vanishing of μ is equivalent to the statement that the characteristic power series of the Iwasawa module is not divisible by p; geometrically it means that the associated p‑adic L‑function has no trivial zero at the trivial character. In practice, μ = 0 simplifies the growth formula to |Aₙ| = pλn+ν, making the λ‑invariant the sole measure of class‑group growth.

Worked Example: Computing λ from Class‑Group Data
Let K = ℚ(ζ₇)⁺ be the maximal real subfield of the 7th cyclotomic field, and p = 3. The 3‑part of the class group of the n‑th layer Kₙ has orders |A₀| = 1, |A₁| = 3, |A₂| = 3³, |A₃| = 3⁵. Assuming μ = 0, determine λ and ν.
With μ = 0 the growth formula is |Aₙ| = 3λn+ν. Taking logarithms base 3 gives log₃|Aₙ| = λn+ν. For n = 0: 0 = ν. For n = 1: 1 = λ·1 + 0 ⇒ λ = 1. Check n = 2: λ·2 = 2, but log₃|A₂| = 3, so the data are not yet in the stable range. Using n = 3: λ·3 = 3, log₃|A₃| = 5, still off. The stable range begins at n ≥ 4 for this field; the given data illustrate that the formula holds only for sufficiently large n. In the stable range one would obtain λ = 2 and ν = –1, giving |Aₙ| = 32n−1.
Exam‑Day Strategy
When a question asks for λ or μ from a table of class‑group orders, first verify whether the given layers are large enough for the Iwasawa formula to apply. If the problem states “for all sufficiently large n”, you may assume the formula holds and solve the linear system. Remember that μ = 0 for cyclotomic ℤₚ‑extensions of abelian fields; do not waste time proving it unless explicitly required.

The Greenberg Conjecture and Its Implications

Greenberg (1976) conjectured that for a totally real field K the Iwasawa module X = lim← Aₙ is a finitely generated torsion Λ‑module with μ = 0 and λ = 0. Equivalently, the p‑part of the class group remains bounded in the cyclotomic ℤₚ‑extension. This is known to hold for ℚ (trivially) and for many real quadratic fields; it fails for some real cubic fields where λ > 0. The conjecture is intimately linked to the non‑existence of non‑trivial unramified p‑extensions in the tower. If λ = 0, the characteristic ideal is generated by a unit times a power of p, and the main conjecture forces the p‑adic L‑function to be a unit in Λ. Greenberg also formulated a “generalised” conjecture for CM fields, predicting that the minus part of the Iwasawa module has μ = 0 and λ equal to the number of primes of K above p that split completely in the cyclotomic extension. Recent work of Fukaya–Kato and of Hida has connected these predictions to the structure of Selmer groups of modular forms. The conjecture remains open in general, but it provides a guiding principle: bounded class‑group growth is the “generic” behaviour, and any positive λ signals the presence of a non‑trivial p‑adic L‑function zero.

The Control Theorem for Selmer Groups

Let E/ℚ be an elliptic curve with good ordinary reduction at p. For each n, the p‑primary Selmer group Selp^∞(E/Kₙ) fits into an exact sequence involving the local conditions at primes above p. The control theorem of Mazur (1972) states that the natural restriction map Selp^∞(E/Kₙ) → Selp^∞(E/K∞)Γₙ has finite kernel and cokernel bounded independently of n. Consequently, the Pontryagin dual X_E = Selp^∞(E/K∞)^∨ is a finitely generated torsion Λ‑module. The theorem is proved by analysing the local cohomology groups H¹(Kₙ,ᵥ, E[p^∞]) at primes v|p and using the fact that the formal group of E gives a filtration with successive quotients isomorphic to the additive group of the residue field. The boundedness of the kernel and cokernel is the key input for formulating the Iwasawa main conjecture for elliptic curves: char_Λ(X_E) = (Lₚ(E)), where Lₚ(E) is the p‑adic L‑function constructed by Mazur–Swinnerton‑Dyer (and later by Perrin‑Riou). The control theorem also underlies the Euler system arguments of Kato and of Skinner–Urban, because it allows one to compare cohomology classes at finite levels with their limit in the tower.

The Iwasawa Main Conjecture for Elliptic Curves

The elliptic curve main conjecture asserts that for an elliptic curve E/ℚ with good ordinary reduction at p, the characteristic ideal of the dual Selmer module X_E over Λ equals the principal ideal generated by the p‑adic L‑function Lₚ(E) ∈ Λ. This was proved by Kato (2004) for the divisibility (Lₚ(E)) ⊂ char_Λ(X_E) using his Euler system of Siegel units on modular curves. The opposite divisibility was established by Skinner–Urban (2014) under the assumption that the residual representation is irreducible and p‑distinguished, using the method of Eisenstein congruences and the modularity theorem. Together they give the full equality for a large class of curves. The conjecture implies the p‑part of the Birch and Swinnerton‑Dyer formula: the order of the Tate–Shafarevich group Ш(E/ℚ)[p^∞] is finite and its size is predicted by the leading term of Lₚ(E) at the trivial character. Moreover, the λ‑invariant of X_E equals the analytic rank of E (the order of vanishing of L(E,s) at s = 1) when the μ‑invariant vanishes, which is known for all ordinary primes by a theorem of Pollack–Weston. The main conjecture for elliptic curves is thus the precise p‑adic incarnation of the BSD conjecture, linking algebraic invariants (Selmer rank, Ш) to analytic data (p‑adic L‑function).

The Iwasawa Main Conjecture for Hilbert Modular Forms

The Iwasawa main conjecture for Hilbert modular forms extends the elliptic curve case to totally real fields. Let F be a totally real number field of degree d and let f be a Hilbert cuspidal eigenform of parallel weight k ≥ 2, level 𝔫, and nebentypus χ. Fix a prime p unramified in F such that f is ordinary at all primes above p. The cyclotomic ℤp-extension F∞/F has Galois group Γ ≅ ℤp. The Iwasawa algebra Λ = ℤp[[Γ]] acts on the Pontryagin dual Xf of the Selmer group Selp∞(f/F∞). Hida (1986) constructed a p-adic L-function Lp(f) ∈ Λ interpolating critical values of the complex L-function L(f, s) twisted by finite-order characters of Γ. The main conjecture asserts that the characteristic ideal charΛ(Xf) equals the principal ideal generated by Lp(f).

Progress has been made in several directions. For F = ℚ the conjecture reduces to the elliptic curve case proved by Kato and Skinner–Urban. For general F, the divisibility ( Lp(f) ) ⊂ charΛ(Xf) was established by Hida (1993) using his ordinary p-adic families and the Euler system of Siegel units on Hilbert modular varieties. The opposite divisibility requires the non-vanishing of certain p-adic L-values and the validity of the p-adic local Langlands correspondence for GL2(Fv) at v|p. Recent work of Dimca–Kato (2020) and of Wan (2022) proves the full equality under the assumptions that the residual representation is irreducible, p-distinguished, and that the μ-invariant of Xf vanishes. The vanishing of μ for Hilbert modular forms is known for ordinary primes by a theorem of Hida–Tilouine (1995) using the control theorem for nearly ordinary Selmer groups.

The conjecture has deep arithmetic consequences. It implies the p-part of the Bloch–Kato conjecture for the motive attached to f, giving a precise formula for the order of the Tate–Shafarevich group Ш(f/F)[p∞] in terms of the leading term of Lp(f) at the trivial character. Moreover, the λ-invariant of Xf equals the analytic rank of f (the order of vanishing of L(f, s) at the central critical point) when μ = 0. This provides a p-adic incarnation of the Birch and Swinnerton‑Dyer conjecture for Hilbert modular forms.

Non-Commutative Iwasawa Theory and the Equivariant Main Conjecture

Classical Iwasawa theory studies modules over the commutative Iwasawa algebra Λ = ℤp[[Γ]] with Γ ≅ ℤp. In many arithmetic situations the natural Galois group is a compact p-adic Lie group G of dimension > 1, for example the Galois group of the maximal pro‑p extension of a number field unramified outside a finite set of primes. The completed group ring ℤp[[G]] is then a non‑commutative Noetherian Auslander regular ring. The equivariant main conjecture, formulated by Coates–Fukaya–Kato–Sujatha–Venjakob (2005), predicts an equality in the relative K-group K0(ℤp[[G]], ℚp[[G]]) between the class of the Selmer complex and the class of the p-adic L-function viewed as an element of the reduced norm group.

Let K be a number field and K∞ a p-adic Lie extension with Galois group G. For a motive M over K (e.g. an elliptic curve or a modular form) one constructs a Selmer complex C•(K∞, M) in the derived category of ℤp[[G]]-modules. Its homology groups are the Selmer groups Selp∞(M/Kn) at finite layers. The conjecture asserts that the characteristic element χ(C•) ∈ K0(ℤp[[G]], ℚp[[G]]) coincides with the image of the p-adic L-function Lp(M) under the reduced norm map Nrd: K1(ℚp[[G]]) → K0(ℤp[[G]], ℚp[[G]]). When G is commutative this reduces to the classical main conjecture.

Significant progress has been achieved for the false Tate curve extension (the maximal pro‑p extension of a p-adic field obtained by adjoining all p-power roots of unity and a fixed uniformizer). In this case G is a two‑dimensional p-adic Lie group isomorphic to a semidirect product ℤp ⋊ ℤp×. The equivariant main conjecture for the Tate motive over this extension was proved by Fukaya–Kato (2006) using the theory of (φ, Γ)-modules and the explicit reciprocity law of Colmez. For non‑commutative extensions of number fields, the conjecture is known in the case of the cyclotomic ℤp-extension of a CM field where G = Δ × Γ with Δ a finite abelian group; the result follows from the commutative main conjecture for each character of Δ. The general non‑commutative case remains open, but the framework has led to new insights such as the formulation of a non‑commutative µ‑invariant and the study of the structure of Selmer groups as modules over ℤp[[G]].

Worked Example: Computing the µ‑invariant in a non‑commutative tower
Let K = ℚ(μp) and let K∞ be the maximal pro‑p extension of K unramified outside p. Its Galois group G is a compact p-adic Lie group of dimension 2. Assume the Selmer module X = Selp∞(E/K∞)∨ for an elliptic curve E/ℚ with good ordinary reduction at p is a finitely generated torsion ℤp[[G]]-module. Explain how the non‑commutative µ‑invariant of X is defined and why its vanishing is expected.
The non‑commutative µ‑invariant is defined via the characteristic element in the relative K-group K0(ℤp[[G]], ℚp[[G]]). One chooses a resolution of X by finitely generated projective ℤp[[G]]-modules and forms the alternating product of their classes. The µ‑invariant is the coefficient of the augmentation ideal in the image of this class under the boundary map to the Grothendieck group of finite ℤp[[G]]-modules. Vanishing is expected because the p-adic L-function Lp(E) is known to be a unit in ℤp[[Γ]] for the cyclotomic direction, and the equivariant main conjecture predicts that the characteristic element of X matches the reduced norm of Lp(E). Since the reduced norm of a unit is a unit, the µ‑part must be zero. This has been verified for the false Tate curve extension by Fukaya–Kato.
Exam Strategy
When a question asks for the relationship between the commutative and non‑commutative main conjectures, state clearly that the commutative case is obtained by abelianising G and applying the reduced norm map. Remember that the non‑commutative µ‑invariant lives in a relative K-group, not in ℤ. Cite the Coates–Fukaya–Kato–Sujatha–Venjakob formulation (2005) and the false Tate curve theorem (Fukaya–Kato 2006) as the only fully proved non‑commutative cases.

The p-adic Local Langlands Correspondence and Iwasawa Modules

The p-adic local Langlands correspondence for GL2(ℚp) provides a bridge between p-adic Galois representations and p-adic Banach space representations of GL2(ℚp). Completed by Colmez (2010) and independently by Berger–Breuil (2010), the correspondence associates to a two‑dimensional p-adic representation V of Gal(ℚ̅p/ℚp) a unitary Banach space representation Π(V) of GL2(ℚp). When V is the p-adic Tate module of an elliptic curve with good ordinary reduction, Π(V) contains the locally analytic vectors of the principal series representation attached to the Hecke eigenform. This correspondence has profound implications for Iwasawa theory because it allows one to translate questions about the growth of Selmer groups into questions about the structure of (φ, Γ)-modules over the Robba ring.

In the cyclotomic setting, the Iwasawa module XE = Selp∞(E/ℚ∞)∨ can be identified with the Pontryagin dual of the p-adic Selmer group, which in turn is related to the cohomology of the (φ, Γ)-module D(V) attached to the Tate module V = Tp(E) ⊗ ℚp. The main conjecture for E is equivalent to the statement that the characteristic ideal of the Iwasawa cohomology H1Iw(ℚp, V) matches the p-adic L-function constructed via the Perrin‑Riou exponential map. The local Langlands correspondence provides a geometric interpretation of the Perrin‑Riou map as a period isomorphism between the (φ, Γ)-module and the locally analytic representation Π(V). This perspective was crucial in the work of Colmez (2016) on the p-adic Birch and Swinnerton‑Dyer conjecture for elliptic curves with split multiplicative reduction, where the μ-invariant is shown to be zero by analysing the triangulation of the (φ, Γ)-module.

Beyond GL2(ℚp), the correspondence for GL2(Fv) over a p-adic field Fv is still conjectural in general, but the work of Emerton (2011) on locally analytic vectors in completed cohomology of Shimura varieties gives a global-to-local compatibility that feeds into the Iwasawa main conjecture for Hilbert modular forms. In particular, the p-adic L-function Lp(f) can be expressed as a pairing between a global cohomology class and a local period coming from the local Langlands correspondence. This approach underlies the recent proofs of one divisibility in the main conjecture for Hilbert modular forms by Wan (2022) and by Liu–Zhang–Zhang (2023).

The Growth of Selmer Groups in Anticyclotomic Extensions

Let K be an imaginary quadratic field in which a prime p splits, and let K∞ac be the anticyclotomic ℤp-extension of K (the unique ℤp-extension on which complex conjugation acts by −1). The Galois group Γac ≅ ℤp acts on the Selmer group of an elliptic curve E/ℚ with good ordinary reduction at p. The Pontryagin dual XEac = Selp∞(E/K∞ac)∨ is a module over the Iwasawa algebra Λac = ℤp[[Γac]]. Unlike the cyclotomic case, the anticyclotomic Selmer group is often not torsion over Λac; its rank is related to the root number of E over K. The control theorem of Mazur–Rubin (2004) shows that the restriction maps Selp∞(E/Kn) → Selp∞(E/K∞ac)Γn have kernel and cokernel bounded independently of n, provided E has no p-isogeny defined over K.

The anticyclotomic main conjecture, formulated by Bertolini–Darmon (1995) and refined by Howard (2005), predicts an equality of characteristic ideals charΛac(XEac) = ( Lpac(E) ), where Lpac(E) is the anticyclotomic p-adic L-function constructed by Bertolini–Darmon using Heegner points. When the root number is −1, the p-adic L-function has a trivial zero at the central character, and the main conjecture predicts that the Selmer group has Λac-rank one. The work of Castella–Kim–Longhi (2021) proved the divisibility ( Lpac(E) ) ⊂ charΛac(XEac) for a large class of curves using the Euler system of Heegner points and the non‑vanishing of p-adic logarithms of Heegner points. The opposite divisibility remains open in general but is known when the residual representation is irreducible and p-distinguished, by adapting the Skinner–Urban method to the anticyclotomic setting.

The growth formula for the p-part of the Selmer group in the anticyclotomic tower takes the form |Selp∞(E/Kn)| = pλ n + μ pn + ν for sufficiently large n, where λ and μ are the Iwasawa invariants of XEac. In the rank‑one case (root number −1) one expects μ = 0 and λ = 1, reflecting the fact that the Heegner point generates a free rank‑one Λac-submodule of the Selmer group. This prediction is a p-adic analogue of the Birch and Swinnerton‑Dyer conjecture over the anticyclotomic tower and has been verified numerically for many curves of conductor < 1000.

The µ‑Invariant in Non‑Cyclotomic ℤp-Extensions

While the vanishing of the µ‑invariant for the cyclotomic ℤp-extension of an abelian field is a theorem of Ferrero–Washington (1979), the behaviour of µ in other ℤp-extensions is far less understood. Let K be a number field and K∞/K a ℤp-extension which is not the cyclotomic one (for example, the ℤp-extension obtained by adjoining the p-power torsion of a CM elliptic curve). The Iwasawa module X = Gal(M∞/K∞), where M∞ is the maximal unramified abelian pro‑p extension of K∞, is a finitely generated torsion Λ-module. Its µ‑invariant can be positive. Greenberg (1976) constructed examples of ℤp-extensions of real quadratic fields with µ > 0 by using the existence of a non‑trivial unramified p-extension in the tower. The µ‑invariant measures the growth of the p-rank of the class group: if µ > 0 then the p-rank grows like µ pn for large n.

A major open problem is the Greenberg conjecture for general ℤp-extensions, which predicts that µ = 0 for all ℤp-extensions of a totally real field. This is known for the cyclotomic extension and for certain “admissible” extensions where the Leopoldt conjecture holds and the p-adic regulator does not vanish. In the CM case, the µ‑invariant of the minus part of the class group in a non‑cyclotomic ℤp-extension can be related to the µ‑invariant of the p-adic L-function attached to a Hecke character. The work of Hida (1991) on the µ‑invariant of p-adic L-functions for CM fields shows that µ vanishes for the anticyclotomic extension of a CM field when the associated Hecke character is of infinite order. However, for non‑CM fields there are examples of ℤp-extensions with µ > 0 due to the presence of a non‑trivial p-adic Lie extension inside the maximal pro‑p extension unramified outside p.

Recent progress uses the theory of (φ, Γ)-modules over the Robba ring to translate the µ‑invariant problem into a question about the slopes of Frobenius on certain p-adic differential equations. The work of Berger (2004) and of Kedlaya (2008) on the slope filtration of (φ, Γ)-modules provides a criterion for µ = 0 in terms of the Hodge–Tate weights of the associated Galois representation. This approach has been applied by Ochiai (2015) to prove µ = 0 for the ℤp-extension generated by the p-power torsion of a modular abelian variety with good ordinary reduction at p, under the assumption that the residual representation is irreducible. The general conjecture that µ = 0 for all ℤp-extensions of a given number field remains one of the central open problems in Iwasawa theory.

  • Confusing the cyclotomic µ‑invariant (always zero for abelian fields) with the µ‑invariant in a non‑cyclotomic ℤp-extension, where µ can be positive.
  • Assuming the anticyclotomic Selmer group is torsion over Λac without checking the root number; it often has positive rank.
  • Stating that the non‑commutative main conjecture is proved for all compact p-adic Lie extensions; only the false Tate curve and abelian‑by‑cyclotomic cases are fully established.
  • Neglecting the hypothesis of good ordinary reduction when applying the control theorem; for supersingular primes the Selmer dual is not torsion and the growth formula changes.
  • Mixing up the p-adic L-function in the cyclotomic variable with the anticyclotomic p-adic L-function; they live in different Iwasawa algebras and satisfy different functional equations.
  • Overlooking the role of the reduced norm map in the equivariant main conjecture; the equality takes place in a relative K-group, not in the Iwasawa algebra itself.
  • Claiming that the µ‑invariant vanishes for every ℤp-extension of a totally real field; this is the Greenberg conjecture and is still open in general.

The Two-Variable Main Conjecture and Hida Families

The classical Iwasawa main conjecture relates the characteristic ideal of the Selmer group over the cyclotomic ℤp-extension to a p-adic L-function in one variable. When one varies the modular form in a Hida family, a second variable appears: the weight variable. Let f be a p-ordinary eigenform of tame level N and weight k ≥ 2. Hida theory constructs a universal ordinary Hecke algebra 𝐡 which is finite flat over the Iwasawa algebra Λ = ℤp[[1 + pℤp]] (the weight space). For each arithmetic point x ∈ Spec(𝐡)(ℚ̄p) of weight k ≥ 2, one obtains a p-ordinary eigenform fx of weight k. The two-variable p-adic L-function Lp(f, s, k) interpolates the critical values L(fx, χ, j) for Dirichlet characters χ of p-power conductor and integers j in the critical strip. The two-variable main conjecture, formulated by Greenberg and proved in many cases by Skinner–Urban (2014) for GL(2)/ℚ, asserts that the characteristic ideal of the two-variable Selmer group X𝐡 over the Hida family equals the ideal generated by Lp(f, s, k) in 𝐡[[Γ]], where Γ = Gal(ℚ∞/ℚ). The proof uses the Euler system of Beilinson–Kato elements varying in the family, combined with a control theorem that relates the Selmer group of the family to the Selmer groups of specialisations. A crucial technical point is the choice of the ordinary projector e = limn→∞ Upn! which isolates the ordinary part of the cohomology. The two-variable main conjecture implies the one-variable main conjecture for all classical specialisations simultaneously, and it also implies the non-vanishing of the μ-invariant for the family is equivalent to the μ-invariant of the two-variable p-adic L-function. When the residual representation is irreducible and p-distinguished, the two-variable main conjecture is known for all ordinary families. For non-ordinary families (e.g., the eigencurve at the boundary of weight space), the theory of (φ, Γ)-modules over the Robba ring replaces the ordinary projector, and the two-variable main conjecture is open in general.

The Iwasawa Theory for Symmetric Powers of Modular Forms

Let f be a normalised cuspidal eigenform of weight k ≥ 2, level N, and nebentypus χ. For an integer m ≥ 1, the m-th symmetric power Symm f is a motive of rank m+1 with Hodge–Tate weights {0, 1, …, m(k−1)}. The Iwasawa theory of Symm f over the cyclotomic ℤp-extension ℚ∞ is governed by a p-adic L-function Lp(Symm f, s) constructed by Coates–Schmidt (1987) for m=2 and by Urban (2006) for general m using the pullback of Eisenstein cohomology classes on unitary groups. The main conjecture predicts that the characteristic ideal of the Selmer group Selp∞(Symm f/ℚ∞) over Λ = ℤp[[Γ]] is generated by Lp(Symm f, s). For m=2 (the adjoint representation), the main conjecture is equivalent to the non-vanishing of the p-adic L-function of f twisted by the adjoint representation, and it is known when f is ordinary at p and the residual representation is irreducible (Hida, 1991; Urban, 2006). For m=3, the symmetric cube corresponds to the Rankin–Selberg convolution f × f twisted by the determinant, and the main conjecture is related to the non-vanishing of the triple product p-adic L-function. The Euler system for symmetric powers is constructed from the Eisenstein cohomology on GU(m, m) or GL2m, and the explicit reciprocity law relates the Euler system to the p-adic L-function. A major difficulty is that the symmetric power motive is not self-dual for odd m, so the Selmer group is not self-dual and the functional equation of the p-adic L-function involves a root number that depends on the parity of m. The μ-invariant of Lp(Symm f, s) is conjectured to vanish for all m when f is ordinary, but this is only known for m=1, 2. For non-ordinary f, the theory of (φ, Γ)-modules of rank m+1 is required, and the main conjecture is largely open. The growth of the Selmer group in the cyclotomic tower is given by |Selpn(Symm f/ℚn)| = pλn + μpn + ν for large n, where λ is the order of vanishing of Lp(Symm f, s) at s=0 and μ is the μ-invariant of the p-adic L-function.

Worked Example: Symmetric Square Main Conjecture for an Ordinary Form
Let f be a p-ordinary eigenform of weight 2, level 11, and trivial nebentypus. The symmetric square Sym2 f has Hodge–Tate weights {0, 1, 2}. Assume the residual representation ρ̄f is irreducible and p-distinguished. Explain why the μ-invariant of the p-adic L-function Lp(Sym2 f, s) vanishes, and compute the expected λ-invariant if L(Sym2 f, 1) ≠ 0.
The symmetric square motive Sym2 f is self-dual with a sign +1 in the functional equation. The p-adic L-function Lp(Sym2 f, s) is constructed by Hida (1991) using the pullback of the Siegel Eisenstein series on GSp(4). Since f is ordinary at p, the Galois representation ρf is ordinary, and the (φ, Γ)-module D(Sym2 ρf) has a triangulation with graded pieces corresponding to the Hodge–Tate weights. The μ-invariant vanishes because the p-adic L-function is a unit in the Iwasawa algebra up to a power of p times a distinguished polynomial; this follows from the non-vanishing of the μ-invariant for the Siegel Eisenstein series (Hida, 1991, Theorem 5.1) and the explicit reciprocity law. If L(Sym2 f, 1) ≠ 0, then the complex L-function does not vanish at the central critical point. By the interpolation property, Lp(Sym2 f, 0) ≠ 0, so the p-adic L-function does not vanish at s=0. Hence the λ-invariant, which is the order of vanishing at s=0, is λ = 0. The main conjecture then predicts that the Selmer group Selp∞(Sym2 f/ℚ∞) is finite, and its order is given by the p-adic valuation of Lp(Sym2 f, 0) up to a p-adic unit.
Exam Strategy: Symmetric Power L-functions
When a question asks about the symmetric power main conjecture, first identify the weight and level of the base form, then check ordinariness at p. The Hodge–Tate weights of Symm f are {0, k−1, 2(k−1), …, m(k−1)}. The μ-invariant vanishing is known for m=1,2 when f is ordinary and the residual representation is irreducible. For m≥3, it is open in general. The λ-invariant equals the order of vanishing of the p-adic L-function at s=0, which by the interpolation formula equals the order of vanishing of the complex L-function at the central critical point, provided the Euler factors at p do not introduce extra zeros. Remember that for odd m, the motive is not self-dual, so the functional equation relates L(Symm f, s) to L(Symm f, m(k−1)+1−s) with a root number that can be −1, forcing a zero at the central point.

The Iwasawa Theory for Rankin–Selberg Convolutions and Beilinson–Flach Elements

Let f and g be two normalised cuspidal eigenforms of weights kf, kg ≥ 2, levels Nf, Ng, and nebentypus χf, χg. The Rankin–Selberg convolution f × g is a motive of rank 4 with Hodge–Tate weights {0, kf−1, kg−1, kf+kg−2}. The p-adic L-function Lp(f × g, s) interpolates the critical values L(f × g, χ, j) for Dirichlet characters χ of p-power conductor and integers j in the critical strip. The Euler system for f × g is constructed from the Beilinson–Flach elements in the étale cohomology of the product of two modular curves Y1(N) × Y1(N), or more precisely from the cohomology of the Kuga–Sato varieties over the modular curve. Kings–Loeffler–Zerbes (2017) constructed the Beilinson–Flach Euler system as a collection of classes BFm ∈ H1(ℚ(μm), Vf ⊗ Vg(1)) for integers m coprime to pNfNg, where Vf, Vg are the p-adic Galois representations attached to f, g. The explicit reciprocity law relates the image of BFm under the Perrin-Riou logarithm map to the p-adic L-function Lp(f × g, s). The main conjecture for f × g asserts that the characteristic ideal of the Selmer group Selp∞(f × g/ℚ∞) over Λ is generated by Lp(f × g, s). This is known when both f and g are ordinary at p and the residual representations are irreducible and p-distinct (Kings–Loeffler–Zerbes, 2017; Lei–Loeffler–Zerbes, 2020). For the non-ordinary case, the theory of (φ, Γ)-modules of rank 4 is required, and the Euler system is constructed using the theory of overconvergent modular symbols. The growth formula for the Selmer group in the cyclotomic tower is |Selpn(f × g/ℚn)| = pλn + μpn + ν, where λ is the order of vanishing of Lp(f × g, s) at s=0. A key feature is that the Beilinson–Flach elements satisfy a norm-compatibility relation that involves the Hecke operators Tℓ for primes ℓ ∤ pNfNg, and the Euler system argument uses the fact that the Selmer group is controlled by the local conditions at p given by the ordinary filtration (or the Fontaine–Laffaille filtration in the non-ordinary case). The μ-invariant of Lp(f × g, s) is conjectured to vanish when both forms are ordinary, but this is only known when the residual representations are irreducible and the p-adic L-function is not identically zero.

The Iwasawa Theory for p-adic Families of Galois Representations and the Eigencurve

The eigencurve 𝒞 of Coleman–Mazur (1998) parametrises finite-slope p-adic families of eigenforms of tame level N. It is a rigid analytic curve equipped with a weight map κ: 𝒞 → 𝒲 to the weight space 𝒲 = Spf ℤp[[1+pℤp]]. Over the ordinary locus, the eigencurve is finite flat over the weight space and coincides with the Hida family. Over the non-ordinary locus, the slope of the Up-operator is positive, and the Galois representations are not ordinary. The Iwasawa theory over the eigencurve requires a two-variable p-adic L-function Lp(f, s, κ) that interpolates the critical values for all classical specialisations fx of weight k ≥ 2 and slope < k−1. The construction of this p-adic L-function uses the theory of overconvergent modular symbols (Pollack–Stevens, 2011) and the theory of (φ, Γ)-modules over the Robba ring (Berger, 2004). The two-variable Selmer group is defined as the Pontryagin dual of the Selmer group of the universal Galois representation ρ𝒞 over the cyclotomic tower. The main conjecture over the eigencurve asserts that the characteristic ideal of this Selmer group in the Iwasawa algebra of the eigencurve Λ𝒞 = O(𝒞)[[Γ]] is generated by Lp(f, s, κ). This is known in the ordinary case (Skinner–Urban, 2014) and for certain non-ordinary families where the slope is small (Loeffler–Zerbes, 2021). A major technical tool is the slope filtration of (φ, Γ)-modules (Kedlaya, 2008), which allows one to decompose the (φ, Γ)-module of the universal representation into pieces of increasing slope. The μ-invariant of the two-variable p-adic L-function on the eigencurve is related to the geometry of the eigencurve at the boundary of weight space: if the eigencurve is smooth at a classical point of weight 1, then the μ-invariant vanishes in a neighbourhood. The growth of the Selmer group in the cyclotomic direction for a fixed non-ordinary specialisation is given by the same formula pλn + μpn + ν, but the λ and μ invariants now depend on the slope of the form. For forms of slope k−1 (critical slope), the Selmer group is not torsion over Λ and the growth formula changes; the main conjecture in this case involves a p-adic L-function with a trivial zero at s=0, and the λ-invariant is increased by 1 (the Greenberg–Benois trivial zero conjecture).

The Equivariant Iwasawa Theory and the ETNC for Motives

The Equivariant Tamagawa Number Conjecture (ETNC) of Burns–Flach (2001) generalises the Bloch–Kato conjecture to the setting of motives with coefficients in a non-commutative ring. Let M be a pure motive over ℚ with coefficients in a number field E, and let G be a finite group acting on M. The ETNC predicts an equality in the relative algebraic K-group K0(ℤ[G], ℝ) between the equivariant Euler characteristic of the perfect complex RΓc(ℤ[1/S], M) and the equivariant L-value L*(M, 0). In the Iwasawa-theoretic setting, one replaces the finite group G by the Galois group Γ = Gal(ℚ∞/ℚ) ≅ ℤp and considers the Iwasawa algebra Λ = ℤp[[Γ]]. The equivariant main conjecture for the cyclotomic extension asserts that the characteristic element of the Selmer complex RΓIw(ℚ∞, T) in K1(ΛS) (where ΛS is the localisation at the set of non-zero divisors) equals the p-adic L-function Lp(M, s) under the boundary map K1(ΛS) → K0(Λ, ΛS). This is the formulation of the main conjecture in the language of the ETNC. The work of Fukaya–Kato (2006) and Burns–Kurihara–Sano (2016) establishes the equivariant main conjecture for Dirichlet motives (i.e., the cyclotomic main conjecture for abelian fields) and for the symmetric square of an ordinary modular form. The key advantage of the equivariant formulation is that it captures the functional equation of the p-adic L-function as an equality in the relative K-group, and it allows one to descend the main conjecture from a larger extension to a subextension using the functoriality of K-theory. For non-commutative p-adic Lie extensions, the ETNC predicts an equality in K0(Λ(G), ℚp) where G is a compact p-adic Lie group. The non-commutative main conjecture of Coates–Fukaya–Kato–Sujatha–Venjakob (2005) is a special case of the ETNC for the motive h1(E) of an elliptic curve. The reduced norm map Nrd: K1(Λ(G)S) → Z(Λ(G)S)× plays a crucial role: the equality in the non-commutative main conjecture takes place in the centre of the localised Iwasawa algebra, not in the algebra itself. The μ-invariant in the equivariant setting is defined as the length of the μ-part of the Selmer module as a Λ(G)-module, and its vanishing is related to the smoothness of the p-adic L-function as an element of the centre.

  • Confusing the two-variable main conjecture for Hida families (weight + cyclotomic variable) with the two-variable main conjecture for the anticyclotomic extension (anticyclotomic + cyclotomic variable); they live in different Iwasawa algebras and have different Euler systems.
  • Assuming the symmetric power main conjecture is known for all m; it is only proved for m=1,2 in the ordinary case, and open for m≥3 even for ordinary forms.
  • Thinking the Beilinson–Flach Euler system works for all Rankin–Selberg convolutions; it requires the forms to have distinct weights or distinct nebentypus to avoid a trivial zero, and the local condition at p must be the ordinary one (or the Fontaine–Laffaille one in the non-ordinary case).
  • Believing the eigencurve main conjecture is proved for all finite-slope families; it is only known for ordinary families and for certain small-slope non-ordinary families, not for critical-slope families where the Selmer group is not torsion.
  • Mixing up the ETNC for motives (which involves K0(ℤ[G], ℝ)) with the non-commutative Iwasawa main conjecture (which involves K1(Λ(G)S) and the reduced norm); the ETNC is a conjecture about special values of L-functions at integers, while the Iwasawa main conjecture is about p-adic L-functions as elements of Iwasawa algebras.
  • Overlooking the role of the slope filtration in non-ordinary Iwasawa theory; the μ-invariant of a (φ, Γ)-module is not the same as the μ-invariant of its graded pieces, and the growth formula for the Selmer group involves the slopes of the Frobenius.
  • Assuming the μ-invariant vanishes for all symmetric power p-adic L-functions of an ordinary form; this is only known for m=1,2, and for m≥3 it is an open problem even when the residual representation is irreducible.

Iwasawa Theory for Elliptic Curves with Supersingular Reduction

When an elliptic curve E/ℚ has supersingular reduction at p ≥ 5, the classical ordinary Selmer group over the cyclotomic ℤp-extension ℚ∞ is not cotorsion over the Iwasawa algebra Λ = ℤp[[Γ]]; its Pontryagin dual has positive Λ-rank. Kobayashi (2003) introduced the ±-Selmer groups Sel±(E/ℚ∞) by imposing local conditions at p defined via the Kobayashi projector on the formal group. Let F be the formal group of E at p and let logE be the formal logarithm. For n ≥ 1, define the subgroups E±(ℚp,n) ⊂ E(ℚp,n) as the kernels of the maps Trn/m ∘ logE for appropriate m of opposite parity. The ±-Selmer groups are then defined by replacing the usual local condition at p with the image of E±(ℚp,n) in H1(ℚp,n, E[p∞]).

The Pontryagin duals X± = Sel±(E/ℚ∞)∨ are torsion Λ-modules. Pollack (2003) constructed the ±-p-adic L-functions Lp±(E, s) ∈ Λ by interpolating the complex L-values L(E, χ, 1) for Dirichlet characters χ of p-power conductor, using the logarithmic derivative of the Coleman map. The supersingular main conjecture asserts that the characteristic ideals satisfy charΛ(X±) = (Lp±). This was proved by Kobayashi (2003) for ap = 0 and by Pollack–Weston (2011) and Sprung (2012) for general supersingular E under mild hypotheses (irreducible residual representation, p ≥ 5). The ±-theory extends to the two-variable setting over the ℤp2-extension of an imaginary quadratic field where p splits, yielding ±±-Selmer groups and a two-variable main conjecture (Kobayashi, Pollack, Wan). A crucial technical point: the ±-local conditions are not the kernel or image of the global-to-local map on H1; they are defined via the Coleman map Col±: H1Iw(ℚp,∞, TpE) → Λ which is an isomorphism of Λ-modules. The Selmer group is then the kernel of the global-to-local map followed by Col±. The μ-invariant of X± vanishes (Kobayashi, Pollack), and the λ-invariant is given by the order of vanishing of Lp± at the trivial character.

Iwasawa Theory for Modular Forms with Non-ordinary Reduction

Let f = ∑n≥1 an qn ∈ Sk(Γ0(N)) be a normalised Hecke eigenform of weight k ≥ 2, and let p ∤ N be a prime where f has non-ordinary reduction, i.e., ap ≡ 0 (mod p). The Galois representation ρf: Gℚ → GL2(Ep) (where Ep is the completion of the Hecke field at p) is crystalline at p with Hodge–Tate weights {0, k−1} and Frobenius eigenvalues α, β satisfying α + β = ap, αβ = pk−1. Non-ordinarity means vp(α) = vp(β) = (k−1)/2 (the supersingular case for k = 2) or more generally that neither eigenvalue is a p-adic unit. The Kato Euler system (2004) constructs classes zm ∈ H1(ℚ(μm), T(f)(1)) for a Gℚ-stable lattice T(f) in the Deligne representation V(f), using the p-adic Rankin–Selberg convolution of f with an Eisenstein series. These classes satisfy norm-compatibility relations and bound the Selmer group.

For non-ordinary f, the Greenberg Selmer group SelGr(V(f)/ℚ∞) defined using the Fontaine–Laffaille or Breuil–Kisin local condition at p (the Dcris-condition) is conjectured to be cotorsion over Λ. Perrin-Riou (1994) defined the p-adic L-function Lp(f, s) ∈ Λ ⊗ ℚp by interpolating the critical values L(f, χ, j) for 1 ≤ j ≤ k−1 and Dirichlet characters χ of p-power conductor, using the exponential map expV: Dcris(V) → H1(ℚp, V) and its dual. The main conjecture states that the characteristic ideal of the Pontryagin dual of SelGr(V(f)/ℚ∞) is generated by Lp(f, s) up to a unit in Λ. This is known in many cases: Kato (2004) proved one divisibility (char | Lp) for all f (ordinary or not) under the hypothesis that the residual representation is irreducible and p-distinguished. The converse divisibility (Lp | char) is much deeper; it was established by Skinner–Urban (2014) for ordinary f and by Wan (2015), Lei–Loeffler–Zerbes (2017) for non-ordinary f using the Euler system of Beilinson–Flach elements for the Rankin–Selberg convolution f ⊗ fc (where fc is the dual form). The μ-invariant of the Selmer group is conjectured to vanish; this is known when the residual representation is irreducible and p ≥ 5 (Kato, Ochiai). For k > 2, the critical slope case (where vp(ap) = k−1) requires the overconvergent theory of Coleman families and the eigencurve; the Selmer group is not torsion over the weight-variable Iwasawa algebra, and the main conjecture involves a two-variable p-adic L-function with a trivial zero.

Worked Comparison: Ordinary vs Supersingular Local Conditions
Let E/ℚ be an elliptic curve with good reduction at p ≥ 5. Compare the structure of the local condition at p defining the Greenberg Selmer group in the ordinary case (a_p ∈ ℤ_p^×) versus the ±-Selmer group in the supersingular case (a_p = 0). Explain why the ordinary Selmer group is cotorsion over Λ while the naive Selmer group in the supersingular case is not, and how the ±-conditions restore cotorsion.
Ordinary case (a_p ∈ ℤ_p^×): The Galois representation T_pE restricted to Gℚ_p has a one-dimensional unramified quotient. The ordinary local condition is H1_ord(ℚp,n, E[p^∞]) = ker(H1(ℚp,n, E[p^∞]) → H1(Ip,n, E[p^∞])Gℚp,n/Ip,n). Equivalently, it is the image of the formal group E1(ℚp,n) ⊗ ℚ_p/ℤ_p under the Kummer map. The Pontryagin dual of the local condition is a free Λ-module of rank 1. By the control theorem, the global Selmer group Sel_ord(E/ℚ_∞) is cotorsion over Λ; its characteristic ideal is generated by the p-adic L-function L_p(E,s) (Mazur–Wiles, Kato, Skinner–Urban). Supersingular case (a_p = 0): T_pE|Gℚ_p is irreducible (no unramified quotient). The naive local condition H1_f(ℚp,n, E[p^∞]) = ker(H1 → H1(ℚp,n, E[p^∞] ⊗ Bcris)) has Pontryagin dual of Λ-rank 1 (it is "too large"). The global Selmer group with this local condition has Λ-rank ≥ 1 (in fact rank 1 for analytic rank 0), so it is not cotorsion. The ±-conditions of Kobayashi replace H1_f with the subgroups E^±(ℚp,n) defined via the formal logarithm: E+(ℚp,n) = {P ∈ E(ℚp,n) : Trn/2m(log_E(P)) = 0 for all m}, E-(ℚp,n) = {P : Trn/2m+1(log_E(P)) = 0}. The Coleman maps Col^±: H1Iw(ℚp,∞, T_pE) → Λ are isomorphisms. The ±-Selmer groups are defined as the kernel of the global-to-local map followed by Col^±. Their Pontryagin duals X^± are torsion Λ-modules, and the main conjecture char_Λ(X^±) = (L_p^±) holds (Kobayashi, Pollack–Weston).
Exam Strategy: Distinguishing Local Conditions
In questions comparing ordinary and supersingular Iwasawa theory, always identify the local Galois representation structure first: ordinary ⇒ split (unramified quotient exists) ⇒ Greenberg condition = kernel of restriction to inertia on the quotient. Supersingular ⇒ irreducible ⇒ no Greenberg condition; must use Kobayashi's ±-conditions defined via the formal group logarithm and trace maps. Remember: the ±-Selmer groups are two distinct groups (not one group with a ±-superscript on the characteristic ideal); their characteristic ideals are generated by the two Pollack p-adic L-functions L_p^±. The μ-invariant vanishes for both X+ and X- (Kobayashi, Pollack). For non-ordinary modular forms of weight k>2, the local condition is the Fontaine–Laffaille/Breuil–Kisin condition (D_cris-condition), not the ±-condition; the ±-theory is specific to weight 2 supersingular (or more generally when the two Frobenius eigenvalues have equal valuation).

Iwasawa Theory for CM Fields and the Coates–Wiles Theorem

Let K be an imaginary quadratic field in which p splits, and let E/K be an elliptic curve with complex multiplication by the ring of integers 𝒪K. The cyclotomic ℤp-extension K∞/K decomposes as K∞ = Kcyc Kanti where Kcyc is the cyclotomic ℤp-extension of ℚ and Kanti is the anticyclotomic ℤp-extension. The Coates–Wiles theorem (1977) was the first major result towards the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture for CM elliptic curves: if E has complex multiplication and L(E/K, 1) ≠ 0, then the Mordell–Weil group E(K) is finite and the Tate–Shafarevich group Ш(E/K) is finite. The proof uses the Euler system of elliptic units (the p-adic analogue of circular units) in the tower of ray class fields of K. Let K(p∞) be the union of ray class fields of K of p-power conductor; its Galois group over K is isomorphic to 𝒪Kp× ≅ ℤp2 × (finite). The elliptic units generate a subgroup of finite index in the global units of K(p∞), and their norm-compatibility yields an Euler system for the Tate module TpE ≅ 𝒪Kp(1) (the Tate twist of the CM character).

Rubin (1991) refined this into the main conjecture for CM elliptic curves: the characteristic ideal of the Pontryagin dual of the Selmer group Sel(E/K∞) over the cyclotomic ℤp-extension K∞ equals the ideal generated by the Katz p-adic L-function Lp(E/K, s) ∈ Λ. This was proved by Rubin using the Euler system of elliptic units and the Kolyvagin derivative construction. The Katz p-adic L-function interpolates the Hecke L-values L(ψ, 1) for Hecke characters ψ of K of infinite type (1,0) and p-power conductor. The μ-invariant vanishes (Ferrero–Washington for the cyclotomic extension of K, extended by Rubin to the Selmer module). The anticyclotomic main conjecture for CM elliptic curves (Bertolini–Darmon, 2005; Castella–Kim, 2018) relates the Selmer group over the anticyclotomic ℤp-extension to the Bertolini–Darmon p-adic L-function constructed from Heegner points. When E has analytic rank 1, the anticyclotomic Selmer group has Λ-rank 1, and the main conjecture involves a derived p-adic L-function (the derivative of the two-variable p-adic L-function along the anticyclotomic direction). The two-variable main conjecture over K(p∞) (the ℤp2-extension) was formulated by Coates–Sujatha and proved by Rubin (1991) for the cyclotomic variable and by Hsieh (2014) for the full two-variable case using the Euler system of elliptic units in the GL2-extension.

Iwasawa Theory for the Adjoint Representation of a Modular Form

Let f ∈ Sk(Γ0(N)) be a p-ordinary Hecke eigenform with p ∤ N, and let ρf: Gℚ → GL2(Ep) be the associated Galois representation. The adjoint representation Ad0ρf is the three-dimensional representation of Gℚ on the trace-zero endomorphisms of ρf, with the action g ⋅ X = ρf(g) X ρf(g)−1. Its Hodge–Tate weights are {0, k−1, 1−k}. The adjoint Selmer group Sel(Ad0ρf/ℚ∞) controls the deformation theory of ρf in Hida families: its tangent space is the space of infinitesimal deformations of ρf that are unramified outside Np and ordinary at p. The adjoint p-adic L-function Lp(Ad0f, s) ∈ Λ was constructed by Hida (1991) and Panchishkin (1994) by interpolating the critical values L(Ad0f, χ, 1) for Dirichlet characters χ of p-power conductor, using the Rankin–Selberg method on GL2 × GL2 (the convolution of f with its dual fc).

The adjoint main conjecture asserts that the characteristic ideal of the Pontryagin dual of Sel(Ad0ρf/ℚ∞) is generated by Lp(Ad0f, s) up to a unit in Λ. This was proved by Hida (1991) for k = 2 (using the Eisenstein ideal) and by Urban (2006) for general k ≥ 2 using the Euler system of Kato for the Rankin–Selberg convolution f ⊗ fc. The μ-invariant of the adjoint Selmer group is related to the congruence ideal of f in the Hida family: μ = 0 if and only if f is not congruent to another eigenform modulo p (Hida, Ohta). The adjoint main conjecture is a key input in the proof of the Iwasawa main conjecture for the symmetric square Sym2ρf (which is Ad0ρf ⊕ det ρf), and in the study of Selmer groups of Hida families (the two-variable main conjecture). For non-ordinary f, the adjoint Selmer group is defined using the Fontaine–Laffaille local condition at p, and the main conjecture is open in general; partial results exist for k = 2 supersingular (Kobayashi's ±-theory for the adjoint) and for small slope (Liu–Zhang–Zhang). The Bloch–Kato conjecture for the adjoint motive predicts the order of the Selmer group over ℚ in terms of the special value L(Ad0f, 1); the Iwasawa main conjecture is its p-adic analogue.

Iwasawa Theory over Function Fields: Drinfeld Modules and the Carlitz Module

The function field analogue of Iwasawa theory replaces ℚ with Fq(T) (the rational function field over a finite field) and ℤp with Fq[[T]]. Let A = Fq[T], K = Fq(T), and let ∞ be the place at infinity. The Carlitz module C is the rank-1 Drinfeld A-module defined by CT = T + τ (where τ is the Frobenius endomorphism x ↦ xq). Its p-power torsion points generate the cyclotomic function field K∞ = K(C[p∞]), whose Galois group Γ = Gal(K∞/K) ≅ Ap× ≅ ℤp × (finite). The Iwasawa algebra is Λ = Fq[[Γ]] ≅ Fq[[T]]. The class group in this setting is the p-part of the divisor class group of degree 0 of the constant field extension; its growth in the cyclotomic tower is governed by the Carlitz module analogue of the Iwasawa invariants.

Hayes (1979) constructed the Carlitz cyclotomic units (the function field analogue of circular units) and proved the function field main conjecture for the Carlitz module: the characteristic ideal of the p-part of the class group in the cyclotomic ℤp-extension is generated by the Goss p-adic L-function (the p-adic interpolation of the Carlitz–Goss zeta values). The μ-invariant vanishes (a theorem of Taelman, 2009, using the theory of t-motives). For higher-rank Drinfeld modules, the Drinfeld modular curves provide the analogue of modular curves, and the Drinfeld modular forms give rise to Galois representations. The main conjecture for Drinfeld modules (Taguchi, 1993; Böckle–Pink, 2009) relates the Selmer group of the t-adic Tate module of a Drinfeld module to the Goss L-function. The Euler system of Drinfeld modular units (the function field analogue of Siegel units) was constructed by Hayes and used by Taelman to prove one divisibility. The non-commutative function field Iwasawa theory (for the full GLr(Ap)-extension) was developed by Pink and others, with the main conjecture formulated in terms of the reduced norm in the non-commutative Iwasawa algebra. A key difference from the number field case: the function field Iwasawa algebra Fq[[T]] is a principal ideal domain (since Fq is a field), so every finitely generated torsion module is pseudo-isomorphic to a direct sum of cyclic modules Λ/(fi), and the characteristic ideal is simply the product of the fi. The μ-invariant is always zero in equal characteristic (Taelman), but can be non-zero in mixed characteristic (the classical number field case).

  • Assuming the non-ordinary main conjecture for modular forms of weight k>2 is proved in the same generality as the ordinary case; the converse divisibility requires the Beilinson–Flach Euler system and is only known under restrictive hypotheses (distinct weights, ordinary local condition at p).
  • Thinking the Coates–Wiles theorem applies to all elliptic curves; it is specific to CM curves and uses the Euler system of elliptic units, which does not exist for non-CM curves.
  • Mixing up the adjoint representation Ad0ρf (dimension 3) with the symmetric square Sym2ρf (dimension 3 but with different Hodge–Tate weights: {0, k−1, 2k−2} vs {0, k−1, 1−k}); their p-adic L-functions and main conjectures are related but distinct.
  • Believing the function field Iwasawa algebra Fq[[T]] has the same structure as ℤp[[T]]; Fq[[T]] is a PID (since Fq is a field), so the structure theory of torsion modules is simpler (no μ-invariant in equal characteristic).

Iwasawa Theory for p‑adic L‑functions of Motives and the Bloch–Kato Conjecture

The Bloch–Kato conjecture (1990) predicts that the order of the Selmer group of a motive M over a number field K, twisted by a Tate twist, is governed by the special value of the complex L‑function L(M, s) at the critical point. In the p‑adic setting one replaces the complex L‑function by a p‑adic L‑function Lp(M, s) constructed via p‑adic Hodge theory (Fontaine–Messing, Fontaine–Perrin‑Riou). The Iwasawa main conjecture for motives asserts that the characteristic ideal of the Pontryagin dual of the Selmer group over the cyclotomic ℤp‑extension K∞/K coincides with the principal ideal generated by Lp(M, s) in the Iwasawa algebra Λ = ℤp[[Gal(K∞/K)]]. When M is the Tate motive ℚ(1) this recovers the classical Iwasawa main conjecture for the p‑part of the class group. For a modular form f of weight k ≥ 2, the motive Mf has rank 2 and the conjecture becomes the main conjecture for the Galois representation ρf. The work of Kato (2004) on Euler systems of Beilinson–Kato elements proves one divisibility (the characteristic ideal divides the p‑adic L‑function) for all ordinary modular forms. The converse divisibility, which would give equality, is known only under additional hypotheses such as the non‑vanishing of the μ‑invariant or the existence of a suitable Euler system of Rankin–Selberg type. For motives with higher rank, such as symmetric powers Symm ρf, the construction of p‑adic L‑functions relies on the work of Coates–Perrin‑Riou and the theory of (φ, Γ)‑modules. The Bloch–Kato conjecture also predicts the structure of the local condition at p: the Bloch–Kato exponential map identifies the local Selmer condition with the de Rham cohomology H¹dR(Kp, M). In the Iwasawa setting this local condition varies in a p‑adic family, leading to the notion of a Selmer complex whose cohomology groups are Λ‑modules. The main conjecture then becomes an equality of characteristic ideals in the derived category of Λ‑modules, a formulation due to Nekovář and later refined by Fukaya–Kato. Recent progress by Skinner–Urban (2014) on the main conjecture for GL₂ over ℚ uses the Eisenstein congruence method to establish the converse divisibility for a wide class of ordinary representations, thereby proving the full main conjecture for many motives of rank 2.

Iwasawa Theory for the Selmer Groups of p‑adic Representations with Potentially Semistable Reduction

Let V be a p‑adic Galois representation of GK = Gal(ℚ̄/ℚ) which is potentially semistable at p in the sense of Fontaine. The local Galois group Gℚp acts on V through a filtered (φ, N)‑module Dst(V). The Bloch–Kato local condition H¹f(ℚp, V) is defined as the kernel of the map H¹(ℚp, V) → H¹(ℚp, V ⊗ Bcrys). When V comes from a modular form f of weight k ≥ 2 with p‑adic valuation of the Hecke eigenvalue ap(f) satisfying 0 < vp(ap) < k−1, the representation is non‑ordinary but potentially semistable. In this case the Selmer group Sel(K∞, V) over the cyclotomic tower is not Λ‑cotorsion in general; its Λ‑rank can be positive, reflecting the presence of a non‑trivial p‑adic L‑function with a zero at the central point. The work of Berger (2004) and Colmez (2008) on the p‑adic local Langlands correspondence for GL₂(ℚp) provides a classification of such representations and a construction of the associated (φ, Γ)‑modules. The Iwasawa main conjecture for potentially semistable representations was formulated by Perrin‑Riou (1995) using her theory of p‑adic heights and the exponential map. She defined a p‑adic L‑function Lp(V, s) as an element of the fraction field of Λ and conjectured that the characteristic ideal of the dual Selmer group X(K∞, V) equals the principal ideal generated by Lp(V, s). For elliptic curves with supersingular reduction at p, the representation Vp(E) is potentially semistable with Hodge–Tate weights {0,1}. Kobayashi (2003) introduced the ±‑Selmer groups Sel±(K∞, E) by imposing a finer local condition using the Coleman map. The ±‑main conjectures, proved by Pollack–Weston (2011) and later by Sprung (2012) for a large class of curves, state that the characteristic ideals of X±(K∞, E) are generated by the p‑adic L‑functions Lp±(E, s) constructed via the ±‑logarithm of the formal group. In the potentially semistable but non‑crystalline case (e.g. modular forms of weight k > 2 with vp(ap) = (k−1)/2), the local condition is more subtle: one must use the trianguline condition of Colmez or the (φ, Γ)‑module approach of Pottharst. The main conjecture in this generality remains open; partial results exist for small slope (Liu–Zhang–Zhang, 2020) using the eigenvariety machinery.

Worked Example: ±‑Selmer Groups for a Supersingular Elliptic Curve
Let E/ℚ be the elliptic curve y² = x³ + x with conductor 32, which has supersingular reduction at p = 3. The 3‑adic Tate module V₃(E) has Hodge–Tate weights {0,1} and ap(E) = 0. Describe the construction of the ±‑Selmer groups Sel±(ℚ∞, E) over the cyclotomic ℤ₃‑extension ℚ∞/ℚ and state the ±‑main conjecture.
The formal group of E at 3 has height 2. The Coleman map Col±: H¹(ℚ₃,∞, E[3∞]) → Λ sends a cohomology class to its ±‑logarithm. Define the local condition H¹±(ℚ₃,∞, E[3∞]) = ker(Col±). The ±‑Selmer group Sel±(ℚ∞, E) is the kernel of the global-to-local map H¹(ℚΣ/ℚ∞, E[3∞]) → ∏v H¹(ℚ∞,v, E[3∞]) / H¹±(ℚ₃,∞, E[3∞]) for v = 3 and the unramified condition elsewhere. Its Pontryagin dual X±(ℚ∞, E) is a finitely generated torsion Λ‑module. The ±‑main conjecture (Kobayashi, Pollack–Weston) asserts that the characteristic ideal charΛ(X±(ℚ∞, E)) equals the principal ideal generated by the p‑adic L‑function Lp±(E, s) ∈ Λ constructed by interpolating the algebraic parts of L(E, χ, 1) for even/odd Dirichlet characters χ of 3‑power conductor. For this curve the μ‑invariant vanishes and the λ‑invariant equals the order of vanishing of Lp±(E, s) at the trivial character.
Exam Strategy
When a question asks for the difference between the ordinary and supersingular main conjectures, focus on the local condition: ordinary uses the Greenberg condition (unramified quotient), supersingular uses the Coleman map to split the local cohomology into two ±‑parts. Remember that the ±‑Selmer groups are defined only for the cyclotomic ℤp‑extension; for anticyclotomic or GL₂‑extensions the local condition is different.

Iwasawa Theory for the p‑adic L‑functions of Siegel Modular Forms of Genus 2

Siegel modular forms of genus 2 are holomorphic functions on the Siegel upper half‑space ℌ₂ satisfying transformation laws under Sp₄(ℤ). Their associated Galois representations, constructed by Weissauer (2005) and Laumon (2005), are 4‑dimensional p‑adic representations ρF of Gℚ with Hodge–Tate weights {0, k−1, k−2, 2k−3} for a form of weight (k, k). When F is a non‑CAP (non‑cuspidal associated to a parabolic) eigenform, ρF is irreducible and expected to be motivic, arising from the middle cohomology of a Siegel threefold. The p‑adic L‑function Lp(F, s) interpolates the critical values of the spinor L‑function L(F, spin, s) twisted by Dirichlet characters. The construction of Lp(F, s) uses the doubling method of Piatetski‑Shapiro–Rallis and the p‑adic interpolation of Eisenstein series on Sp₄, carried out by Böcherer–Schmidt (2000) and later by Urban (2006) for ordinary forms. The Iwasawa main conjecture for Siegel modular forms predicts that the characteristic ideal of the dual Selmer group X(ℚ∞, ρF) over the cyclotomic ℤp‑extension equals the ideal generated by Lp(F, s) in Λ. For ordinary F (i.e. the Hecke eigenvalues at p satisfy certain non‑vanishing conditions), the local condition at p is given by the ordinary filtration on Dcris(ρF), and the Selmer group is Λ‑cotorsion. The work of Hida (2004) on p‑adic families of Siegel modular forms provides a Λ‑adic Eisenstein measure whose characteristic ideal matches the p‑adic L‑function. The converse divisibility, i.e. the inclusion of the p‑adic L‑function in the characteristic ideal, was proved by Skinner–Urban (2014) for a class of ordinary Siegel forms using the Eisenstein congruence method on the unitary group U(2,2). For non‑ordinary forms, the local condition is more complicated; the representation may be potentially semistable with non‑trivial monodromy, and the ±‑theory of Kobayashi does not directly generalize. Recent work of Liu–Zhang (2022) on the eigenvariety for GSp₄ suggests a possible approach via trianguline deformations. The μ‑invariant is expected to vanish for non‑CAP forms, but this is known only in special cases (e.g. when F is a Yoshida lift). The main conjecture for the adjoint representation Ad ρF, which has dimension 10, is related to the Bloch–Kato conjecture for the motive of F and remains largely open.

Iwasawa Theory for the p‑adic L‑functions of Automorphic Forms on Unitary Groups and the Gan–Gross–Prasad Conjecture

Let U(n,n) be a quasi‑split unitary group over ℚ associated to an imaginary quadratic field K. Automorphic forms on U(n,n) give rise to Galois representations of dimension 2n via the work of Harris–Taylor (2001) and Shin (2011). For a cuspidal automorphic representation π of U(n,n) which is cohomological and regular algebraic, the associated p‑adic representation ρπ is potentially semistable at all places above p. The Gan–Gross–Prasad (GGP) conjecture predicts that the central value of the Rankin–Selberg L‑function L(π × τ, 1/2) for a pair of representations (π, τ) on U(n,n) × U(n−1,n−1) is non‑zero if and only if a certain period integral is non‑zero. In the p‑adic setting, the GGP conjecture has an Iwasawa‑theoretic formulation due to Hsieh (2014) and Wan (2017): the characteristic ideal of the Selmer group of the tensor product representation ρπ ⊗ ρτ over the cyclotomic ℤp‑extension of the reflex field should be generated by a p‑adic L‑function Lp(π × τ, s) constructed via the doubling method on the unitary group. The Euler system of diagonal cycles, built by Liu–Zhang–Zhang (2020) using the geometry of unitary Shimura varieties, provides one divisibility in the main conjecture for the Rankin–Selberg convolution. The local condition at p is given by the Panchishkin condition, which selects a sub‑representation of ρπ ⊗ ρτ of Hodge–Tate weight 0. When π and τ are ordinary at p, the Selmer group is Λ‑cotorsion and the main conjecture predicts an equality of characteristic ideals. The work of Skinner–Urban (2014) on the Eisenstein congruence for U(2,2) proves the converse divisibility for n = 2 under a non‑vanishing hypothesis on the p‑adic L‑function. For higher n, the main conjecture is open; the difficulty lies in constructing a suitable Euler system and controlling the μ‑invariant. The μ‑invariant is expected to vanish when the automorphic forms are not congruent to an Eisenstein series modulo p, a condition related to the non‑vanishing of the Hida congruence ideal. The equivariant main conjecture for the GGP setting, formulated in the non‑commutative Iwasawa algebra of the Galois group of the maximal pro‑p extension unramified outside p, predicts a refined equality involving the reduced norm. This equivariant conjecture is known only in the function field analogue (Pink, 2009) and in the case n = 1 (the classical Heegner point setting).

Iwasawa Theory for the p‑adic L‑functions of p‑adic Families of Motives and the Eigencurve

The eigencurve, constructed by Coleman–Mazur (1998) and Buzzard (2007), parametrizes p‑adic families of finite‑slope overconvergent modular forms. Each point on the eigencurve corresponds to a p‑adic Galois representation ρx of dimension 2, varying analytically with the weight parameter κ ∈ W (the weight space). The Iwasawa theory of such families was developed by Hida (1986) for ordinary families and extended by Emerton (2011) to the whole eigencurve using completed cohomology. For a family ρ over an affinoid disc U ⊂ W, one can define a ΛU‑adic Selmer group Sel(U, ρ) where ΛU = O(U)[[Gal(ℚ∞/ℚ)]]. The main conjecture in families asserts that the characteristic ideal of the dual Selmer group X(U, ρ) in ΛU is generated by a two‑variable p‑adic L‑function Lp(ρ, s, κ) interpolating the critical values L(ρx, χ, 1) for classical specializations x ∈ U and Dirichlet characters χ. The construction of Lp(ρ, s, κ) uses the p‑adic Rankin–Selberg method of Hida and the p‑adic interpolation of Eisenstein series on the Hida family. The work of Ochiai (2006) and later of Wan (2015) proves one divisibility (the characteristic ideal divides the p‑adic L‑function) for Hida families using the Euler system of Beilinson–Kato elements in families. The converse divisibility is known for ordinary families under the non‑vanishing of the μ‑invariant (Skinner–Urban, 2014). For non‑ordinary families (e.g. the supersingular locus of the eigencurve), the local condition at p is given by the trianguline variety of Bellaïche–Chenevier (2009). The ±‑main conjecture in families, formulated by Lei–Loeffler–Zerbes (2015), replaces the single Selmer group by a pair of Selmer groups Sel±(U, ρ) defined via the Coleman map in families. The characteristic ideals of X±(U, ρ) are conjectured to be generated by the ±‑p‑adic L‑functions Lp±(ρ, s, κ). Recent progress by Liu–Zhang–Zhang (2020) on the eigenvariety for GL₂ over imaginary quadratic fields provides a framework for the anticyclotomic main conjecture in families. The μ‑invariant in families is a function on the weight space; its vanishing on a Zariski‑open set is equivalent to the non‑existence of congruences between the family and an Eisenstein family, a result of Hida (1993) for ordinary families and of Bellaïche (2012) for the whole eigencurve.

  • Thinking the μ‑invariant vanishes for all p‑adic families on the eigencurve; it vanishes on the ordinary locus but can be non‑zero on the supersingular locus where congruences with Eisenstein families occur.
  • Mixing up the Gan–Gross–Prasad period integral with the p‑adic height pairing; the GGP conjecture relates a complex period to an L‑value, while the p‑adic main conjecture relates a Selmer group to a p‑adic L‑function.
  • Believing the main conjecture for the adjoint representation of a Siegel modular form follows from the main conjecture for the standard representation; the adjoint has dimension 10 and its p‑adic L‑function is constructed via the doubling method on Sp₄ × Sp₄, a different Euler system.

Iwasawa Theory for the p-adic L-functions of the Asai Representation

The Asai representation arises from the restriction of scalars of a Hilbert modular form over a totally real field F to ℚ. Let f be a Hilbert modular form of parallel weight k ≥ 2 over F, and let ρf : GF → GL2(E) be the associated p-adic Galois representation. The Asai representation As(ρf) is the representation of Gℚ on the tensor product ρf ⊗ ρfc where c is the non-trivial automorphism of F/ℚ when F is quadratic, or more generally the induction from GF to Gℚ of ρf ⊗ ρfc. This yields a representation of dimension 4 (or 2[F:ℚ] in general) whose L-function is the Asai L-function L(As(f), s). The Iwasawa theory for this setting was initiated by Hida (1994) for quadratic F and extended by Dimitrov (2013) and Loeffler–Zerbes (2016) to general totally real fields. The cyclotomic ℤp-extension F∞/F gives rise to a Λ-adic Selmer group Sel(F∞, As(f)) where Λ = O[[Gal(F∞/F)]]. The main conjecture asserts that the characteristic ideal of the Pontryagin dual X(F∞, As(f)) is generated by a p-adic L-function Lp(As(f)) ∈ Λ interpolating the critical values L(As(f), χ, k) for finite-order characters χ of Gal(F∞/F). The construction of Lp(As(f)) uses the p-adic Rankin–Selberg method of Hida for the Asai L-function, which involves the pullback of an Eisenstein series on GSp(4) to GL(2) × GL(2) via the doubling method. The Euler system is provided by the Asai–Flach elements constructed by Loeffler–Zerbes (2016) using the geometry of the Siegel modular variety of genus 2 and the pullback of the Klingen Eisenstein class. These elements live in the Galois cohomology of the Asai representation twisted by characters, and their norm-compatibility relations are governed by the explicit reciprocity law of Kings–Loeffler–Zerbes (2017). The one divisibility (characteristic ideal divides the p-adic L-function) was proved by Loeffler–Zerbes (2016) for ordinary f at all primes above p under the non-vanishing of the μ-invariant. The converse divisibility remains open in general but is known for CM Hilbert modular forms by the work of Hsieh (2014) using the Eisenstein congruence method. A key technical point is the local condition at primes above p: for the Asai representation, the local Galois representation at a prime v|p is the tensor product of two 2-dimensional representations, and the ordinary filtration induces a 2-step filtration on the 4-dimensional space. The Greenberg local condition selects the unramified quotient of the tensor product, which corresponds to the p-stabilisation of f at v. When f is non-ordinary at some v|p, the local condition must be replaced by the trianguline condition of Bellaïche–Chenevier, and the ±-main conjecture of Lei–Loeffler–Zerbes (2015) generalises to this setting with four Selmer groups corresponding to the four possible choices of ± at each prime above p.

The Iwasawa Main Conjecture for p-adic L-functions of the Exterior Square Representation

Let f be a cuspidal eigenform of weight k ≥ 2 for GL(2)/ℚ with associated p-adic Galois representation ρf : Gℚ → GL2(E). The exterior square representation ∧2ρf is a 1-dimensional representation isomorphic to the determinant det(ρf) = εcyck-1χf where χf is the nebentypus character. However, for GL(4) or GSp(4), the exterior square of the standard 4-dimensional representation is 6-dimensional and yields a non-trivial L-function. The Iwasawa theory for the exterior square L-function of a Siegel modular form of genus 2 was developed by Urban (2006) and extended by Liu–Zhang–Zhang (2020). Let F be a Siegel modular form of genus 2, weight (k1, k2) with k1 ≥ k2 ≥ 3, and let ρF : Gℚ → GSp(4)(E) be the associated p-adic Galois representation (constructed by Weissauer (2005) for holomorphic F and by Pilloni–Stroh (2016) for overconvergent F). The exterior square representation ∧2ρF is a 6-dimensional representation of Gℚ whose L-function L(∧2F, s) is the spin L-function of F twisted by the inverse of the similitude character. The cyclotomic ℤp-extension ℚ∞/ℚ gives a Λ-adic Selmer group Sel(ℚ∞, ∧2ρF). The main conjecture predicts that the characteristic ideal of the dual Selmer group is generated by a p-adic L-function Lp(∧2F) ∈ Λ interpolating the critical values L(∧2F, χ, j) for j in the critical range. The construction of Lp(∧2F) uses the doubling method on GSp(4) × GSp(4) and the pullback of the Siegel Eisenstein series to the diagonal, as developed by Böcherer (1985) and refined by Furusawa–Morimoto (2016) for the p-adic interpolation. The Euler system is provided by the Beilinson–Flach elements for GSp(4) constructed by Lei–Loeffler–Zerbes (2018) using the geometry of the Siegel modular variety of genus 2 and the pullback of the Klingen Eisenstein class along the diagonal embedding GSp(4) → GSp(4) × GSp(4). These elements satisfy norm-compatibility relations governed by the explicit reciprocity law of Kings–Loeffler–Zerbes (2017) for the exterior square. The one divisibility was proved by Lei–Loeffler–Zerbes (2018) for ordinary F at p (i.e., the Up-eigenvalue is a p-adic unit) under the non-vanishing of the μ-invariant. The ordinary condition at p for GSp(4) means that the local Galois representation ρF|Gℚp admits a full flag of Gℚp-stable subspaces with graded pieces given by characters ψ1, ψ2, ψ3, ψ4 satisfying ψ1ψ4 = ψ2ψ3 = χcyck1+k2-3. The Greenberg local condition for the exterior square selects the subquotient corresponding to ψ1ψ2 and ψ3ψ4. When F is non-ordinary at p (e.g., the Up-eigenvalue has positive valuation), the local condition must be replaced by the trianguline condition, and the ±-main conjecture involves four Selmer groups corresponding to the choices of ± for the two Hodge–Tate weights. The μ-invariant in this setting is related to congruences between F and an Eisenstein series on GSp(4), and its vanishing is equivalent to the non-existence of such congruences, a result of Urban (2006) for ordinary F.

Worked Example: Local Condition for Asai Representation
Let F = ℚ(√5) and p = 5, which splits as p = v1v2 in F. Let f be a Hilbert modular form over F of parallel weight 2, ordinary at both v1 and v2. The local Galois representation ρf|GFv1 has Hodge–Tate weights {0, 1} and an ordinary filtration 0 ⊂ Fil1 ⊂ ρf with Fil1 unramified. Describe the Greenberg local condition for the Asai representation As(ρf) at v1 and compute its Hodge–Tate weights.
The Asai representation at v1 is As(ρf)|GFv1 ≅ ρf|GFv1 ⊗ ρfc|GFv1. Since c swaps v1 and v2, the representation ρfc|GFv1 is the local representation at v2. Both are ordinary with Hodge–Tate weights {0, 1}. The tensor product has Hodge–Tate weights {0, 1, 1, 2} (sums of pairs). The ordinary filtration on ρf gives 0 ⊂ Fil1 ⊂ ρf with Fil1 of HT weight 0 and quotient of HT weight 1. Similarly for ρfc. The tensor product filtration has graded pieces: Fil1 ⊗ Fil1c (HT 0), Fil1 ⊗ ρfc/Fil1c (HT 1), ρf/Fil1 ⊗ Fil1c (HT 1), ρf/Fil1 ⊗ ρfc/Fil1c (HT 2). The Greenberg local condition selects the unramified quotient of the tensor product, which is the quotient by the maximal submodule on which GFv1 acts via the cyclotomic character. This corresponds to the quotient (ρf/Fil1) ⊗ (ρfc/Fil1c) of HT weight 2, which is 1-dimensional and unramified. The local Selmer condition is the kernel of the localisation map H1(Fv1, As(ρf)) → H1(Fv1, As(ρf)/Fil1As(ρf)) where Fil1As(ρf) is the preimage of the unramified quotient.
Exam Strategy: Distinguishing Asai from Rankin–Selberg
The Asai L-function of a Hilbert modular form over a quadratic field F is the L-function of the tensor product of f with its Galois conjugate. The Rankin–Selberg L-function of two Hilbert modular forms f and g over F is the L-function of ρf ⊗ ρg. In the Asai case, the two factors are conjugate under Gal(F/ℚ), so the L-function is invariant under the Galois action and descends to ℚ. In the Rankin–Selberg case, f and g are independent. The Euler systems differ: Asai–Flach elements come from the Siegel modular variety of genus 2 via the Klingen Eisenstein class, while Beilinson–Flach elements for Rankin–Selberg come from the product of two modular curves. The local conditions at p also differ: Asai uses the tensor product of two ordinary filtrations, Rankin–Selberg uses the tensor product of two ordinary filtrations but with independent p-stabilisations. On exams, check whether the two forms are Galois conjugates (Asai) or independent (Rankin–Selberg).

Iwasawa Theory for p-adic L-functions of Automorphic Forms on GL(3) and the Standard L-function

Let π be a cuspidal automorphic representation of GL(3)/ℚ which is cohomological (i.e., contributes to the cohomology of the locally symmetric space for GL(3)) and regular algebraic. By the work of Scholze (2015) and Caraiani–Scholze (2017), there exists a continuous p-adic Galois representation ρπ : Gℚ → GL(3)(E) associated to π, satisfying the expected local-global compatibility at all primes. The standard L-function L(π, s) is the L-function of the standard 3-dimensional representation of the L-group GL(3, ℂ). The Iwasawa theory for this setting was initiated by Hida (1993) for ordinary π and extended by Loeffler–Zerbes (2020) and Kings–Loeffler–Zerbes (2021) to the non-ordinary case. The cyclotomic ℤp-extension ℚ∞/ℚ gives a Λ-adic Selmer group Sel(ℚ∞, ρπ) where Λ = O[[Gal(ℚ∞/ℚ)]]. The main conjecture asserts that the characteristic ideal of the dual Selmer group X(ℚ∞, ρπ) is generated by a p-adic L-function Lp(π) ∈ Λ interpolating the critical values L(π, χ, j) for finite-order characters χ and integers j in the critical range. The construction of Lp(π) uses the p-adic Rankin–Selberg method of Jacquet–Piatetski-Shapiro–Shalika (1983) for the standard L-function of GL(3), which involves the integral of an Eisenstein series on GL(3) × GL(2) against a cusp form on GL(2). The p-adic interpolation was developed by Hida (1993) for ordinary π using the p-adic interpolation of Eisenstein series on GL(3). For non-ordinary π, the construction uses the p-adic interpolation of the Shalika germ expansion, as developed by Loeffler–Zerbes (2020) using the geometry of the p-adic local Langlands correspondence for GL(3) (which is not fully known, but the trianguline variety provides a substitute). The Euler system is provided by the Beilinson–Flach elements for GL(3) × GL(2) constructed by Kings–Loeffler–Zerbes (2021) using the geometry of the locally symmetric space for GL(3) × GL(2) and the pullback of the Eisenstein cohomology class. These elements live in the Galois cohomology of ρπ ⊗ ρf for a GL(2) form f, and by varying f in a Hida family, one obtains an Euler system for ρπ itself via the Rankin–Selberg method. The norm-compatibility relations are governed by the explicit reciprocity law of Kings–Loeffler–Zerbes (2021). The one divisibility was proved by Loeffler–Zerbes (2020) for ordinary π at p (i.e., the Up-eigenvalues are p-adic units) under the non-vanishing of the μ-invariant. The ordinary condition for GL(3) means that ρπ|Gℚp admits a full flag of Gℚp-stable subspaces with graded pieces given by characters ψ1, ψ2, ψ3 with distinct Hodge–Tate weights. The Greenberg local condition selects the unramified quotient, which corresponds to the quotient by the maximal submodule on which Gℚp acts via the cyclotomic character. When π is non-ordinary at p, the local condition must be replaced by the trianguline condition, and the ±-main conjecture involves three Selmer groups corresponding to the choices of ± for the three Hodge–Tate weights. The μ-invariant is related to congruences between π and an Eisenstein series on GL(3), and its vanishing is equivalent to the non-existence of such congruences, a result of Hida (1993) for ordinary π.

The Iwasawa Theory for p-adic L-functions of the Tensor Product of Two Hilbert Modular Forms

Let F be a totally real field of degree d, and let f, g be Hilbert modular forms over F of parallel weights kf, kg ≥ 2 with associated p-adic Galois representations ρf, ρg : GF → GL(2)(E). The Rankin–Selberg L-function L(f × g, s) is the L-function of the tensor product representation ρf ⊗ ρg of dimension 4. The Iwasawa theory for this setting over totally real fields was developed by Hida (1994) for ordinary f, g and extended by Loeffler–Zerbes (2016) and Kings–Loeffler–Zerbes (2017) to the non-ordinary case. The cyclotomic ℤp-extension F∞/F gives a Λ-adic Selmer group Sel(F∞, ρf ⊗ ρg) where Λ = O[[Gal(F∞/F)]]. The main conjecture asserts that the characteristic ideal of the dual Selmer group is generated by a p-adic L-function Lp(f × g) ∈ Λ interpolating the critical values L(f × g, χ, j) for finite-order characters χ of Gal(F∞/F) and integers j in the critical range. The construction of Lp(f × g) uses the p-adic Rankin–Selberg method of Hida (1994) for Hilbert modular forms, which involves the pullback of a Siegel Eisenstein series on GSp(4) to GL(2) × GL(2) over F via the doubling method. The p-adic interpolation uses the p-adic interpolation of Eisenstein series on the Hilbert–Siegel modular variety. The Euler system is provided by the Beilinson–Flach elements for Hilbert modular forms constructed by Kings–Loeffler–Zerbes (2017) using the geometry of the product of two Hilbert modular varieties and the pullback of the Eisenstein cohomology class along the diagonal embedding. These elements satisfy norm-compatibility relations governed by the explicit reciprocity law of Kings–Loeffler–Zerbes (2017). The one divisibility was proved by Loeffler–Zerbes (2016) for ordinary f, g at all primes above p under the non-vanishing of the μ-invariant. The ordinary condition at a prime v|p means that both ρf|GFv and ρg|GFv admit ordinary filtrations. The tensor product then has a 2-step filtration with graded pieces of Hodge–Tate weights {0, 1, 1, 2}. The Greenberg local condition selects the unramified quotient, which corresponds to the quotient by the maximal submodule on which GFv acts via the cyclotomic character. When f or g is non-ordinary at some v|p, the local condition must be replaced by the trianguline condition, and the ±-main conjecture involves four Selmer groups corresponding to the choices of ± for the two Hodge–Tate weights at each prime. The μ-invariant is related to congruences between the Rankin–Selberg convolution and an Eisenstein series on GSp(4) over F, and its vanishing on a Zariski-open set of the weight space is equivalent to the non-existence of such congruences, a result of Hida (1994) for ordinary families and Bellaïche (2012) for the whole eigenvariety.

Iwasawa Theory for the p-adic L-functions of the Symmetric Square of Hilbert Modular Forms

Let F be a totally real field of degree d, and let f be a Hilbert modular form over F of parallel weight k ≥ 2 with associated p-adic Galois representation ρf : GF → GL(2)(E). The symmetric square representation Sym2ρf is a 3-dimensional representation of GF whose L-function L(Sym2f, s) is the symmetric square L-function of f. The Iwasawa theory for this setting was initiated by Hida (1994) for ordinary f and extended by Loeffler–Zerbes (2018) and Kings–Loeffler–Zerbes (2021) to the non-ordinary case. The cyclotomic ℤp-extension F∞/F gives a Λ-adic Selmer group Sel(F∞, Sym2ρf) where Λ = O[[Gal(F∞/F)]]. The main conjecture asserts that the characteristic ideal of the dual Selmer group is generated by a p-adic L-function Lp(Sym2f) ∈ Λ interpolating the critical values L(Sym2f, χ, j) for finite-order characters χ and integers j in the critical range. The construction of Lp(Sym2f) uses the p-adic Rankin–Selberg method of Hida (1994) for the symmetric square L-function, which involves the pullback of an Eisenstein series on GL(3) to GL(2) via the diagonal embedding. The p-adic interpolation uses the p-adic interpolation of Eisenstein series on the Hilbert modular variety for GL(3). The Euler system is provided by the Beilinson–Flach elements for the symmetric square constructed by Kings–Loeffler–Zerbes (2021) using the geometry of the Hilbert modular variety for GL(3) and the pullback of the Eisenstein cohomology class along the diagonal embedding GL(2) → GL(3). These elements satisfy norm-compatibility relations governed by the explicit reciprocity law of Kings–Loeffler–Zerbes (2021). The one divisibility was proved by Loeffler–Zerbes (2018) for ordinary f at all primes above p under the non-vanishing of the μ-invariant. The ordinary condition at a prime v|p means that ρf|GFv admits an ordinary filtration 0 ⊂ Fil1 ⊂ ρf with Fil1 of Hodge–Tate weight 0 and quotient of weight 1. The symmetric square then has a filtration with graded pieces of Hodge–Tate weights {0, 1, 2}. The Greenberg local condition selects the unramified quotient, which corresponds to the quotient of Hodge–Tate weight 2. When f is non-ordinary at some v|p, the local condition must be replaced by the trianguline condition, and the ±-main conjecture involves three Selmer groups corresponding to the choices of ± for the three Hodge–Tate weights. The μ-invariant is related to congruences between f and an Eisenstein series on GL(2) over F (which induce congruences on the symmetric square), and its vanishing on a Zariski-open set of the weight space is equivalent to the non-existence of such congruences, a result of Hida (1994) for ordinary families and Bellaïche (2012) for the whole eigenvariety. A key application is the proof of the Bloch–Kato conjecture for the symmetric square motive of f at critical values, which follows from the main conjecture and the explicit reciprocity law.

  • Assuming the exterior square of a GL(2) representation is non-trivial; it is 1-dimensional (the determinant), so the exterior square L-function for GL(2) is just a twist of the Riemann zeta function.
  • Thinking the standard L-function of a GL(3) automorphic form is the same as the Rankin–Selberg L-function of a GL(2) form and a GL(1) form; the standard L-function of GL(3) is degree 3, while Rankin–Selberg of GL(2) × GL(1) is degree 2.

Iwasawa Theory for the p-adic L-functions of the Symmetric Cube of a Modular Form

The symmetric cube L‑function of a cuspidal eigenform f of weight k ≥ 2 and level N is a degree‑4 motivic L‑function attached to the representation Sym³ ρ_f. Iwasawa theory for this object was initiated by Urban (2006) in the ordinary case and later extended to the non‑ordinary setting by Loeffler–Zerbes (2020) using the theory of (φ,Γ)‑modules. Let F be a number field containing the coefficients of f and let F_∞/F be the cyclotomic ℤ_p‑extension. The Λ‑adic Selmer group Sel(F_∞, Sym³ ρ_f) is defined via Greenberg local conditions at primes above p: the ordinary filtration on ρ_f induces a filtration on Sym³ ρ_f with Hodge–Tate weights {0,1,2,3}, and the local condition selects the unramified quotient corresponding to weight 3. The main conjecture asserts that the characteristic ideal of the Pontryagin dual of this Selmer group coincides with the principal ideal generated by a p‑adic L‑function L_p(Sym³ f) ∈ Λ = O[[Gal(F_∞/F)]]. This p‑adic L‑function interpolates the critical values L(Sym³ f, χ, j) for finite‑order characters χ and integers j in the critical strip 1 ≤ j ≤ k‑1. Its construction uses the pullback of a Siegel Eisenstein series on GSp(4) to GL(2) via the diagonal embedding, following the method of Böcherer and Schmidt. The Euler system is supplied by the Beilinson–Flach elements for the symmetric cube, constructed by Loeffler–Zerbes (2020) from the geometry of the Siegel modular threefold and the pullback of the Eisenstein cohomology class. These elements satisfy explicit reciprocity laws relating their images under the Perrin‑Riou regulator to the p‑adic L‑function. The one‑divisibility (the Selmer group is controlled by the p‑adic L‑function) was proved by Loeffler–Zerbes under the assumption that the μ‑invariant vanishes, which is known on a Zariski‑open subset of the weight space by Hida theory. The μ‑invariant itself is linked to congruences between f and a Saito–Kurokawa lift on GSp(4); its vanishing is equivalent to the non‑existence of such congruences. A major application is the proof of the Bloch–Kato conjecture for the symmetric cube motive at critical values, conditional on the main conjecture and the non‑vanishing of the μ‑invariant.

Iwasawa Theory for the p-adic L-functions of the Adjoint Representation of a Hilbert Modular Form

Let F be a totally real field and f a Hilbert modular eigenform over F of parallel weight k ≥ 2, ordinary at all primes above p. The adjoint representation Ad⁰ ρ_f is the trace‑zero subspace of ρ_f ⊗ ρ_f^∨, a three‑dimensional p‑adic Galois representation with Hodge–Tate weights {0,1,2} at each v|p. Iwasawa theory for Ad⁰ ρ_f was developed by Hida (1994) for ordinary f and by Bellaïche–Dimitrov (2016) for the non‑ordinary case via the eigenvariety. For the cyclotomic ℤ_p‑extension F_∞/F, the Λ‑adic Selmer group Sel(F_∞, Ad⁰ ρ_f) uses the Greenberg local condition: at each v|p the ordinary filtration on ρ_f gives a filtration on Ad⁰ ρ_f with graded pieces of weights 0,1,2, and the local condition selects the unramified quotient of weight 2. The main conjecture predicts that the characteristic ideal of the dual Selmer group is generated by a p‑adic L‑function L_p(Ad⁰ f) ∈ Λ interpolating the critical values L(Ad⁰ f, χ, j) for finite‑order characters χ and j = 1,…,k‑1. The construction of L_p(Ad⁰ f) uses the p‑adic Rankin–Selberg method of Hida, integrating f against a p‑adic family of Eisenstein series on the Hilbert modular variety for GL(2)_F. The Euler system is provided by the Kato–Hida zeta elements, which arise from the Eisenstein cohomology on the Hilbert modular variety and satisfy norm‑compatibility relations governed by the explicit reciprocity law of Hida. The one‑divisibility was established by Hida (1994) under the vanishing of the μ‑invariant, which holds on a Zariski‑open set of the weight space by the work of Bellaïche (2012) on the eigenvariety. The μ‑invariant is related to congruences between f and an Eisenstein series on GL(2)_F; its vanishing is equivalent to the non‑existence of such congruences. An important consequence is the proof of the Bloch–Kato conjecture for the adjoint motive of f at critical values, which follows from the main conjecture and the explicit reciprocity law. In the non‑ordinary setting, the local condition is replaced by the trianguline condition, leading to a ±‑main conjecture with three Selmer groups corresponding to the three Hodge–Tate weights.

Worked Example: Identifying the Correct Local Condition
Let f be a Hilbert modular form over a totally real field F, ordinary at all v|p. The adjoint representation Ad⁰ ρ_f has Hodge–Tate weights {0,1,2} at each v|p. Which quotient of the filtration on Ad⁰ ρ_f does the Greenberg local condition select for the definition of the Selmer group Sel(F_∞, Ad⁰ ρ_f)?
The ordinary filtration on ρ_f gives 0 ⊂ Fil¹ ⊂ ρ_f with Hodge–Tate weights 0 on Fil¹ and 1 on the quotient. On Ad⁰ ρ_f = (ρ_f ⊗ ρ_f^∨)⁰, the induced filtration has graded pieces of weights 0, 1, 2. The Greenberg local condition for a representation with non‑negative Hodge–Tate weights selects the maximal unramified quotient, which corresponds to the highest weight piece. Here the highest weight is 2, so the local condition selects the unramified quotient of Hodge–Tate weight 2. This is the quotient of Ad⁰ ρ_f by the submodule generated by the weight 0 and weight 1 pieces. Therefore the Selmer group is defined using this quotient as the local condition at v|p.
Exam‑Day Strategy
When a question asks for the local condition in an Iwasawa‑theoretic Selmer group, first list the Hodge–Tate weights of the representation. The Greenberg condition always picks the unramified quotient of the highest weight. For trianguline (±) conditions, the number of Selmer groups equals the number of distinct Hodge–Tate weights. Memorise this rule to avoid confusing the tensor product of conditions with the condition on the tensor product.

Technique: Distinguishing Greenberg Conditions
Explain why the Greenberg local condition for the standard representation of GSp(6) selects the unramified quotient of weight k₁+k₂+k₃‑3, whereas for the adjoint representation of a Siegel modular form of genus 2 the selected weight is k₁+k₂‑2.
The standard representation of GSp(6) is seven‑dimensional with Hodge–Tate weights {0, k₃‑1, k₂‑1, k₁‑1, k₁+k₂‑2, k₁+k₃‑2, k₁+k₂+k₃‑3}. The ordinary filtration yields a one‑dimensional graded piece for each weight; the Greenberg condition picks the quotient corresponding to the highest weight because the local Galois representation at p is ordinary and the unramified quotient is the one on which the inertia acts trivially. For the adjoint representation of a genus‑2 Siegel modular form, the representation is ten‑dimensional with highest weight k₁+k₂‑2 (the similitude weight of GSp(4)). The ordinary filtration again isolates the highest weight piece, which is k₁+k₂‑2, not k₁+k₂+k₃‑3 because there is no third weight k₃ in genus 2. Thus the selected weight differs by the presence or absence of the third weight parameter.

Iwasawa Theory for the p-adic L-functions of the Exterior Square of a GL(5) Automorphic Form

Let π be a cuspidal automorphic representation of GL₅(𝔸_ℚ), regular algebraic and ordinary at p in the sense of Hida. The associated p‑adic Galois representation ρ_π : G_ℚ → GL₅(ℚ_p) has Hodge–Tate weights {0, a₁, a₂, a₃, a₄} with 0 < a₁ < a₂ < a₃ < a₄. The exterior square representation ∧² ρ_π is a ten‑dimensional representation of G_ℚ (since ∧² of a 5‑dimensional space has dimension 10). Its Hodge–Tate weights are the pairwise sums a_i + a_j for 1 ≤ i < j ≤ 5, together with 0 from the trivial summand if the determinant is factored out. The L‑function L(∧² π, s) is expected to be automorphic, corresponding to a representation of GL₁₀(𝔸_ℚ). The p‑adic L‑function L_p(∧² π) ∈ Λ is constructed by p‑adic interpolation of the critical values L(∧² π, χ, j) using the pullback of an Eisenstein series on the split group of type D₆ (the simply connected cover of SO₁₂) to a Shimura variety of Hodge type attached to GL₅ × GL₅.

The Λ‑adic Selmer group Sel(ℚ_∞, ∧² ρ_π) is defined via the Greenberg local condition: the ordinary filtration on ∧² ρ_π yields a one‑dimensional unramified quotient corresponding to the highest weight a₄ + a₃, and the local condition at p selects its preimage. The main conjecture asserts that the characteristic ideal of the dual Selmer group X(∧² π) is generated by L_p(∧² π). The Euler system for ∧² π is expected to arise from cycles on the product of two copies of the Shimura variety for GL₅ (or from Eisenstein cohomology on the split group of type D₆). These cycles are constructed by pulling back the Eisenstein class along the diagonal embedding GL₅ → GL₅ × GL₅ and then projecting to the ∧²‑isotypic component. The reciprocity law relates the Coleman map of the Euler system classes to L_p(∧² π). The μ‑invariant of L_p(∧² π) measures congruences between π and an Eisenstein series on GL₁₀ whose constant term contains ∧² ρ_π. Its vanishing on a Zariski‑open set of the weight space would follow from the geometry of the GL₅ eigenvariety, which is still under development. Partial results exist when π is a functorial lift from a product of smaller groups, for instance from GL₂ × GL₃, where the main conjecture reduces to the known conjectures for the factors.

Technique: Identifying the Correct Hodge–Tate Weights
Let π be a regular algebraic cuspidal automorphic representation of GL₅(𝔸_ℚ) with Hodge–Tate weights {0, 1, 3, 6, 10} for ρ_π. List the Hodge–Tate weights of the exterior square representation ∧² ρ_π, and indicate which weight corresponds to the one‑dimensional unramified quotient used in the Greenberg local condition.
The Hodge–Tate weights of ∧² ρ_π are all pairwise sums a_i + a_j for 1 ≤ i < j ≤ 5, where {a_i} = {0,1,3,6,10}. Computing the sums: 0+1=1, 0+3=3, 0+6=6, 0+10=10, 1+3=4, 1+6=7, 1+10=11, 3+6=9, 3+10=13, 6+10=16. Thus the multiset of weights is {1,3,4,6,7,9,10,11,13,16}. The highest weight is 16 = 10+6, which corresponds to the one‑dimensional unramified quotient in the ordinary filtration. The Greenberg local condition at p selects the preimage of this quotient.
Exam‑Day Strategy
When a question asks for the dimension of a symmetric or exterior power representation, compute it combinatorially: Symⁿ of a 2‑dimensional representation has dimension n+1; ∧² of a d‑dimensional representation has dimension d(d−1)/2. For Hodge–Tate weights, remember that the weights of a tensor product are sums of the original weights, and the weights of a symmetric power are n‑fold sums with repetitions allowed. Write down the weight set explicitly before answering; this avoids off‑by‑one errors that examiners frequently test.

Iwasawa Theory for the p-adic L-functions of the Standard L-function of a GSp(10) Automorphic Form

Worked Comparison: Adjoint GL(3) vs Symmetric Cube Hilbert
Compare the dimensions, Hodge–Tate weight structures, and Euler system geometries for the adjoint representation of a GL₃/ℚ automorphic form and the symmetric cube of a Hilbert modular form over a totally real field F of degree d. Explain why the adjoint case yields an 8‑dimensional representation while the symmetric cube yields a 4‑dimensional representation, and how the number of Selmer groups in the ±‑main conjecture differs between the two settings when p is non‑ordinary.
Dimensions: For GL₃, Ad(ρ_π) = End⁰(ρ_π) has dimension 3² − 1 = 8. For GL₂/F, Sym³(ρ_f) has dimension 4 (the symmetric cube of a 2‑dimensional space).

Hodge–Tate weights: Ad(ρ_π) weights are differences a_i − a_j (i ≠ j), giving 6 distinct weights (pairs (i,j) with i≠j, but a_i−a_j = −(a_j−a_i)). Sym³(ρ_f) weights are 3a_τ, a_τ+2b_τ, 2a_τ+b_τ, 3b_τ for each τ, giving 4d weights total.

Euler system geometry: Adjoint GL₃ uses cycles on GL₃ × GL₃ Shimura variety (diagonal embedding). Symmetric cube Hilbert uses cycles on Hilbert modular variety × Siegel threefold over F (pullback of Eisenstein on GL₂ × GSp₄).

±‑main conjecture Selmer groups: For non‑ordinary p, the number of ±‑Selmer groups equals the number of Hodge–Tate weights in the critical range. Adjoint GL₃ has 6 weights → 6 Selmer groups. Symmetric cube Hilbert has 4d weights → 4d Selmer groups. The difference arises because adjoint weights are differences (symmetric pairs) while symmetric cube weights are all positive combinations.
Exam Strategy: Tracking Dimensions and Weights
When comparing Iwasawa theories for different representations, always compute: (1) dimension of the representation, (2) number of distinct Hodge–Tate weights, (3) rank of the Selmer group (equals number of critical weights), (4) degree of the p‑adic L‑function (equals dimension of the representation for self‑dual cases). A frequent examiner trap is to confuse the dimension of Symⁿ (which is n+1 for GL₂) with the dimension of ∧² (which is n(n−1)/2 for GLₙ). Write a quick table: GL₂ Sym³ → dim 4; GL₃ Ad → dim 8; GL₄ ∧² → dim 6; GL₅ Ad → dim 24. Memorise these four numbers — they appear in multiple choice questions asking which representation matches a given Selmer rank or p‑adic L‑function degree.

Iwasawa Theory for the p-adic L-functions of the Symmetric Square of a GL(3) Automorphic Form

Let π be a cuspidal automorphic representation of GL₃(𝔸_ℚ), regular algebraic and ordinary at a fixed prime p. The symmetric square representation Sym²(ρ_π) is a 6‑dimensional continuous representation of G_ℚ with Hodge–Tate weights given by the pairwise sums a_i + a_j (1 ≤ i ≤ j ≤ 3) where {a₁,a₂,a₃} are the Hodge–Tate weights of ρ_π ordered increasingly. The complex L‑function L(Sym² π, s) is the symmetric square L‑function of degree 6. Its critical values lie at integers j with 1 ≤ j ≤ 3 (the centre of symmetry is at s = 2). The p‑adic L‑function L_p(Sym² π) ∈ Λ = ℤ_p[[Γ]] (Γ ≅ ℤ_p) is conjectured to exist and to interpolate the algebraic parts of L(Sym² π, χ, j) for finite‑order characters χ of Γ and critical j. The construction is expected to follow the doubling method: one pulls back an Eisenstein cohomology class on the Shimura variety for GSp(6) (or the appropriate covering group) along the embedding of the GL₃ Shimura variety induced by the symmetric square map. The Λ‑adic Selmer group Sel(ℚ_∞, Sym² ρ_π) is defined via the Greenberg local condition at p: the ordinary filtration on Sym² ρ_π yields a one‑dimensional unramified quotient corresponding to the highest weight a₃ + a₃, and the local condition selects its preimage. The Iwasawa main conjecture asserts that the characteristic ideal of the Pontryagin dual X(Sym² π) = Sel(ℚ_∞, Sym² ρ_π)^∨ is generated by L_p(Sym² π). The μ‑invariant of L_p(Sym² π) measures congruences between π and an Eisenstein series on GSp(6) whose constant term involves the symmetric square L‑function. Vanishing of μ on a Zariski‑open subset of the weight space would follow from the geometry of the GL₃ eigenvariety and the non‑vanishing of certain Hida families. Partial results are available when π is a functorial lift from GL₂ × GL₁ (so that Sym² π decomposes as a product of a symmetric square of a GL₂ form and a GL₂ L‑function), where the theory reduces to the known Iwasawa theory for symmetric squares of modular forms (Hida, Urban). For general π, the Euler system is expected to arise from cycles on the GL₃ Shimura variety constructed via the pullback of the Eisenstein class on the GSp(6) Shimura variety. The reciprocity law relating the Coleman map of these classes to L_p(Sym² π) remains a central open problem.

Iwasawa Theory for the p-adic L-functions of the Exterior Square of a GL(3) Automorphic Form

Let π be as above. The exterior square representation ∧² ρ_π is a 3‑dimensional representation of G_ℚ, isomorphic to ρ_π^∨ ⊗ det(ρ_π). Its Hodge–Tate weights are the sums a_i + a_j for i < j, i.e. a₁+a₂, a₁+a₃, a₂+a₃. The L‑function L(∧² π, s) coincides with the adjoint L‑function of π twisted by the central character, and is of degree 3. The critical strip is symmetric about s = 1. The p‑adic L‑function L_p(∧² π) ∈ Λ is expected to interpolate the critical values L(∧² π, χ, j) for j = 1,2. Because ∧² ρ_π is essentially the dual of ρ_π twisted by a character, its Iwasawa theory is closely related to the Iwasawa theory of the standard representation ρ_π itself. The Λ‑adic Selmer group Sel(ℚ_∞, ∧² ρ_π) is defined by the Greenberg condition at p: the ordinary filtration on ∧² ρ_π gives a one‑dimensional unramified quotient corresponding to the highest weight a₂+a₃, and the local condition is its preimage. The main conjecture predicts that the characteristic ideal of the dual Selmer group X(∧² π) is generated by L_p(∧² π). The μ‑invariant of L_p(∧² π) measures congruences between π and an Eisenstein series on GL₃ whose constant term involves the exterior square L‑function. When π is ordinary, the control theorem for Selmer groups identifies the Selmer group over ℚ_∞ with the inverse limit of Selmer groups over finite layers, and the characteristic ideal can be studied via the p‑adic L‑function of the adjoint representation (which is known in many cases thanks to the work of Urban on GL₃ adjoint p‑adic L‑functions). The Euler system for ∧² π is expected to come from the same cycles that give the Euler system for the standard representation, via the exterior square map on the GL₃ Shimura variety. In the case where π is a functorial lift from GL₂ × GL₁, the exterior square decomposes as a product of the standard L‑function of the GL₂ form and a Dirichlet L‑function, reducing the main conjecture to the classical Iwasawa theory for modular forms and Dirichlet characters.

Worked Example: Identifying the Hodge–Tate weights of Sym² and ∧² for a GL(3) form

Iwasawa Theory for the p-adic L-functions of the Standard L-function of a GL(4) Automorphic Form

Let π be a cuspidal automorphic representation of GL₄(𝔸_ℚ), regular algebraic and ordinary at p. The standard representation ρ_π is a 4‑dimensional continuous representation of G_ℚ with Hodge–Tate weights {a₁,a₂,a₃,a₄} (increasing). The L‑function L(π, s) is the standard L‑function of degree 4. The GL₄ Shimura variety is not of PEL type, which complicates the geometry of the Eisenstein class and the definition of the Coleman map. Iwasawa Theory for the p-adic L-functions of the Adjoint Representation of a GL(4) Automorphic Form

Let π be a cuspidal automorphic representation of GL₄(𝔸_ℚ), regular algebraic and ordinary at p. The adjoint representation Ad(ρ_π) is the 15‑dimensional representation of G_ℚ given by the trace‑zero endomorphisms of ρ_π. Its Hodge–Tate weights are the differences a_i - a_j for i ≠ j (with multiplicities). The adjoint L‑function L(Ad π, s) is of degree 15 and is self‑dual with sign +1. The critical strip is symmetric about s = 1. The p‑adic L‑function L_p(Ad π) ∈ Λ is expected to interpolate the critical values L(Ad π, χ, j) for j in the critical range. Construction follows the doubling method for the adjoint representation: one uses the embedding of the GL₄ Shimura variety into the Shimura variety for the split group of type D₄ (or the appropriate covering group) induced by the adjoint map. The μ‑invariant of L_p(Ad π) measures congruences between π and an Eisenstein series on the split group of type D₄ whose constant term involves the adjoint L‑function. When π is a functorial lift from GL₂ × GL₂, the adjoint representation decomposes as a direct sum of adjoint representations of the two GL₂ factors and their tensor product, reducing the main conjecture to the known Iwasawa theories for adjoint of GL₂ (Hida, Urban) and Rankin–Selberg convolutions. For general π, the Euler system is expected to come from cycles on the GL₄ Shimura variety constructed via the pullback of the Eisenstein class on the D₄ Shimura variety. The reciprocity law and the control theorem for the adjoint Selmer group are active areas of research.

Iwasawa Theory for the p-adic L-functions of the Symmetric Power of a GL(2) Automorphic Form: The General Theory

Let π be a cuspidal automorphic representation of GL₂(𝔸_ℚ) that is regular algebraic and ordinary at a prime p. For each integer k ≥ 1, the k-th symmetric power representation Symᵏ ρ_π is a (k+1)-dimensional representation of G_ℚ with Hodge–Tate weights {0, 1, …, k} (after a suitable twist). The L-function L(Symᵏ π, s) is the symmetric power L-function of degree k+1. The p-adic L-function L_p(Symᵏ π) ∈ Λ = ℤ_p[[Γ]] (where Γ ≅ ℤ_p is the Galois group of the cyclotomic ℤ_p-extension) is conjectured to interpolate the critical values L(Symᵏ π, χ, j) for Dirichlet characters χ of p-power conductor and integers j in the critical range. The construction of L_p(Symᵏ π) for general k relies on the pullback of an Eisenstein cohomology class on the Shimura variety for the split group of type B⌊(k+1)/2⌋ or C⌊(k+1)/2⌋ to the modular curve, following the method of Urban for k=2 and Loeffler–Zerbes for higher k using the theory of Eisenstein classes for symplectic groups. The Λ-adic Selmer group Sel(ℚ_∞, Symᵏ ρ_π) is defined via the Greenberg local condition at p: the ordinary filtration on Symᵏ ρ_π yields a one-dimensional unramified quotient corresponding to the highest Hodge–Tate weight k, and the local condition selects its preimage. The main conjecture asserts that the characteristic ideal of the dual Selmer group X(Symᵏ π) is generated by L_p(Symᵏ π). The μ-invariant of L_p(Symᵏ π) measures congruences between π and an Eisenstein series on the relevant symplectic group whose constant term involves the symmetric power L-function. Vanishing of μ on a Zariski-open set of the weight space follows from the geometry of the eigencurve and the fact that the symmetric power map from the GL₂ eigencurve to the weight space is finite flat away from the boundary. When k is even, the symmetric power representation is orthogonal and the functional equation of L(Symᵏ π, s) has sign +1; when k is odd, it is symplectic and the sign is −1. This parity affects the structure of the Selmer group and the order of vanishing of L_p(Symᵏ π) at the trivial character. The Euler system is expected to come from cycles on the modular curve constructed by pulling back the Eisenstein class on the symplectic Shimura variety. The reciprocity law relating the Coleman map to L_p(Symᵏ π) has been established for k=2 (Urban) and k=3 (Loeffler–Zerbes) and is conjectural for k ≥ 4. The control theorem for the symmetric power Selmer group holds under the assumption that the residual representation is irreducible and p-distinguished.

Iwasawa Theory for the p-adic L-functions of the Standard L-function of a GL(n) Automorphic Form: The General Linear Group

Let π be a cuspidal automorphic representation of GL_n(𝔸_ℚ) that is regular algebraic and ordinary at a prime p, with n ≥ 2. The standard representation ρ_π is an n-dimensional representation of G_ℚ with distinct Hodge–Tate weights a₁ < a₂ < … < a_n. The L-function L(π, s) is the standard L-function of degree n. The p-adic L-function L_p(π) ∈ Λ is conjectured to interpolate the critical values L(π, χ, j) using the pullback of an Eisenstein cohomology class on the Shimura variety for the group GL_n (or its appropriate covering) to the GL_n Shimura variety itself, which is not of PEL type for n ≥ 3. The construction of L_p(π) for general n was initiated by Harris for n=3 using the geometry of the Drinfeld upper half-plane and extended by Ming-Lun Hsieh and others using the theory of Eisenstein classes on the Borel–Serre compactification of the GL_n Shimura variety. The Λ-adic Selmer group Sel(ℚ_∞, ρ_π) is defined via the Greenberg local condition at p: the ordinary filtration on ρ_π yields a one-dimensional unramified quotient corresponding to the highest Hodge–Tate weight a_n, and the local condition selects its preimage. The main conjecture asserts that the characteristic ideal of the dual Selmer group X(π) is generated by L_p(π). The μ-invariant of L_p(π) measures congruences between π and an Eisenstein series on GL_n whose constant term involves the standard L-function. Vanishing of μ on a Zariski-open set of the n-variable weight space (the weights of π) would follow from the geometry of the GL_n eigenvariety constructed by Emerton and the fact that the weight map is finite flat away from the boundary. The Euler system is expected to arise from cycles on the GL_n Shimura variety constructed by pulling back the Eisenstein cohomology class along the diagonal embedding. The reciprocity law relating the Coleman map to L_p(π) is a key open problem for n ≥ 3; for n=2 it is the work of Kato, and for n=3 partial results exist using the geometry of the Drinfeld upper half-plane. The control theorem for the standard Selmer group holds under the assumption that the residual representation is irreducible and p-distinguished. When π is a functorial lift from a smaller group (e.g., a symmetric power of a GL₂ form, or a tensor product of GL₂ forms), the main conjecture reduces to the known Iwasawa theories for those smaller groups. The case n=3 is the most developed beyond n=2, with results of Hsieh, Loeffler–Zerbes, and others on the construction of p-adic L-functions and the formulation of the main conjecture.

Iwasawa Theory for the p-adic L-functions of the Exterior Power Representations of GL(n): General Theory

For each integer k with 2 ≤ k ≤ n−1, the k-th exterior power representation ∧ᵏ ρ_π is a (n choose k)-dimensional representation of G_ℚ. Iwasawa Theory for the p-adic L-functions of the Standard L-function of a GSp(2n) Automorphic Form: General Symplectic Group

Let π be a cuspidal automorphic representation of GSp2n(𝔸_ℚ) that is regular algebraic and ordinary at a prime p, with n ≥ 2. The standard representation ρ_π is a (2n+1)-dimensional representation of G_ℚ (the standard representation of the dual group GSpin2n+1) with Hodge–Tate weights a₁ < a₂ < … < a_n < an+1 < … < a2n < a2n+1 satisfying the symmetry a_i + a2n+2-i = constant (the similitude weight). The L-function L(π, s) is the standard L-function of degree 2n+1 (including the similitude character). The p-adic L-function L_p(π) ∈ Λ is conjectured to interpolate the critical values L(π, χ, j) using the pullback of an Eisenstein cohomology class on the Shimura variety for the split group of type Bn+1 or Cn+1 to the Siegel modular variety of genus n (the Shimura variety for GSp2n). For n=2 this is the work of Urban, Skinner–Urban, and Loeffler–Zerbes using the pullback of the Siegel Eisenstein class on GSp₄ to the Siegel modular threefold. For general n, the construction uses the theory of Eisenstein classes for orthogonal groups developed by Ginzburg–Rallis–Soudry and the pullback to the Siegel modular variety via the embedding GSp2n → SO2n+1 (or the appropriate covering). The Λ-adic Selmer group Sel(ℚ_∞, ρ_π) is defined via the Greenberg local condition at p: the ordinary filtration on ρ_π yields a one-dimensional unramified quotient corresponding to the highest weight a2n+1, and the local condition selects its preimage. The main conjecture asserts that the characteristic ideal of the dual Selmer group X(π) is generated by L_p(π). The μ-invariant of L_p(π) measures congruences between π and an Eisenstein series on the relevant orthogonal group whose constant term involves the standard L-function. Vanishing of μ on a Zariski-open set of the (n+1)-variable weight space (the weights of π and the similitude character) would follow from the geometry of the GSp2n eigenvariety constructed by Pilloni and others. The Euler system is expected to arise from cycles on the Siegel modular variety constructed by pulling back the Eisenstein cohomology class on the orthogonal Shimura variety. The reciprocity law relating the Coleman map to L_p(π) is known for n=2 (Urban, Loeffler–Zerbes) and is conjectural for n ≥ 3. The control theorem for the standard Selmer group holds under the assumption that the residual representation is irreducible and p-distinguished. When π is a functorial lift from a smaller group (e.g., a Saito–Kurokawa lift from GL₂, or a Yoshida lift from GL₂ × GL₂), the standard representation decomposes as a direct sum of representations of smaller groups, reducing the main conjecture to known Iwasawa theories. The spinor L-function of π (degree 2ⁿ) has a separate Iwasawa theory using the pullback of an Eisenstein class on the exceptional group of type En+1 for n ≤ 7.

  • Confusing the Euler system for the symmetric square of GL₂ (Kato's Euler system on the modular curve) with the Euler system for the standard L-function of GL₃ (expected to come from cycles on the GL₃ Shimura variety, not yet constructed).

Iwasawa Theory for the p-adic L-functions of the Standard L-function of an SO(2n) Automorphic Form

The split even orthogonal group SO(2n) has a standard representation of dimension 2n. For a cuspidal automorphic representation π of SO(2n)(𝔸_ℚ) that is regular algebraic and ordinary at a prime p, the standard L-function L(π, s) is of degree 2n and satisfies a functional equation relating s to 1−s. The p-adic L-function L_p(π) is conjectured to interpolate the critical values L(π, χ, j) for finite-order characters χ of the cyclotomic ℤ_p-extension and integers j in the critical strip. The construction follows the doubling method: one embeds SO(2n) × SO(2n) into a larger symplectic or orthogonal group, pulls back a Siegel Eisenstein cohomology class on the Shimura variety for the larger group, and then restricts to the diagonal. For n=2, SO(4) is isogenous to SL₂ × SL₂, and the theory reduces to the Rankin–Selberg convolution of two Hilbert modular forms, which is already treated in the general GL₂ × GL₂ Iwasawa theory. For n≥3, the Shimura variety for SO(2n) is of Hodge type but not of PEL type; the Borel–Serre compactification and the theory of Eisenstein cohomology for non-PEL Shimura varieties (developed by Harder, Nair, and others) are required to define the Eisenstein class. The Λ-adic Selmer group Sel(ℚ_∞, ρ_π) is defined via the Greenberg ordinary condition at p: the ordinary filtration on the p-adic Galois representation ρ_π attached to π yields a one-dimensional unramified quotient corresponding to the highest Hodge–Tate weight, and the local condition selects its preimage. The main conjecture asserts that the characteristic ideal of the Pontryagin dual X(π) is generated by L_p(π). The μ-invariant of L_p(π) measures congruences between π and an Eisenstein series on the relevant orthogonal group whose constant term involves the standard L-function. Vanishing of μ on a Zariski-open set of the weight space would follow from the geometry of the SO(2n) eigenvariety, which is still under construction. The Euler system is expected to arise from cycles on the SO(2n) Shimura variety obtained by pulling back the Eisenstein class. The reciprocity law relating the Coleman map to L_p(π) is known for n=2 (via the GL₂ × GL₂ theory) and is conjectural for n≥3. The control theorem for the standard Selmer group holds under the assumption that the residual representation is irreducible and p-distinguished.

Iwasawa Theory for the p-adic L-functions of the Standard L-function of a G₂ Automorphic Form

The exceptional group G₂ of dimension 14 has a 7-dimensional standard representation. A cuspidal automorphic representation π of G₂(𝔸_ℚ) that is regular algebraic and ordinary at p gives rise to a standard L-function L(π, s) of degree 7. Unlike the classical groups, G₂ does not admit a Shimura variety because it has no Hermitian symmetric domain; consequently, there is no natural geometric setting for constructing p-adic L-functions via Eisenstein cohomology on a Shimura variety attached to G₂ itself. The prevailing strategy is to realize the standard representation of G₂ as a functorial transfer from a larger group that does have a Shimura variety. For instance, the embedding G₂ SO(7) (or the spin double cover Spin(7)) allows one to pull back an Eisenstein class from the Shimura variety for SO(7) (which is of Hodge type) to a suitable cycle on a G₂-variety, such as a moduli space of octonion algebras or a compactification of a G₂-period domain. The p-adic L-function L_p(π) is then defined as the image of this pullback under a suitable p-adic integration map. The Λ-adic Selmer group Sel(ℚ_∞, ρ_π) is defined using the ordinary filtration on the 7-dimensional Galois representation ρ_π; the highest Hodge–Tate weight gives a one-dimensional unramified quotient, and the Greenberg local condition selects its preimage. The main conjecture predicts that the characteristic ideal of the dual Selmer group X(π) is generated by L_p(π). At present, the construction of L_p(π) is entirely conjectural; no p-adic L-function for a G₂ standard L-function has been unconditionally constructed. The μ-invariant is completely unknown, and the Euler system has not been defined. The control theorem would require a detailed study of the ordinary filtration on ρ_π and the local Galois cohomology at p, which is complicated by the fact that the Hodge–Tate weights are not all distinct (the standard representation of G₂ has weights 0, ±1, ±2, ±3 with multiplicities). The reciprocity law relating a hypothetical Coleman map to L_p(π) is open. Progress on this front is tied to the development of the Langlands program for exceptional groups and the construction of p-adic families of automorphic forms on G₂, which is an active area of research.

Worked Example: Obstacle for G₂ p-adic L-function
Explain why the construction of a p-adic L-function for the standard representation of G₂ faces the obstacle of the absence of a Shimura variety, and describe the proposed strategy using Eisenstein cohomology on a larger group.
The group G₂ is an exceptional algebraic group of dimension 14. It does not possess a Hermitian symmetric domain, hence there is no Shimura variety attached to G₂ in the classical sense. The standard method for constructing p-adic L-functions (via pullback of Eisenstein cohomology classes on Shimura varieties) therefore cannot be applied directly. The proposed strategy uses the embedding of G₂ into a larger group that does have a Shimura variety, for example the inclusion G₂ SO(7) (or Spin(7)). One constructs a Siegel Eisenstein cohomology class on the Shimura variety for SO(7), which is of Hodge type, and then pulls it back to a suitable cycle on a G₂-variety (such as a moduli space of octonion algebras or a compactified period domain). The p-adic L-function is defined as the image of this pullback under a p-adic integration map. This approach reduces the problem to the better-understood theory for orthogonal groups, but it requires a precise description of the cycle and the pullback map, which remains conjectural.
Exam-Day Strategy
When a question asks about exceptional groups (G₂, F₄, E₆, E₇, E₈), always mention the lack of Shimura varieties and the reliance on functorial embeddings into classical groups. Cite the doubling method or pullback from a larger group as the standard conjectural construction. Contrast this with the situation for classical groups where Shimura varieties exist and Eisenstein cohomology is directly available.

Iwasawa Theory for the p-adic L-functions of the Standard L-function of an E₇ Automorphic Form

The exceptional group E₇ of dimension 133 has a 56-dimensional standard representation (the minimal representation). For a cuspidal automorphic representation π of E₇(𝔸_ℚ) that is regular algebraic and ordinary at p, the standard L-function L(π, s) has degree 56. E₇ also admits a Shimura variety of Hermitian type (associated to the Hermitian symmetric domain of type E₇). The p-adic L-function L_p(π) can be constructed by pulling back an Eisenstein cohomology class on the Shimura variety for E₇, or by using the embedding E₇ E₈ and pulling back from the E₈ Shimura variety (which is not of Hermitian type but can be treated via the theory of Eisenstein cohomology for non-Hermitian Shimura varieties). The Λ-adic Selmer group Sel(ℚ_∞, ρ_π) is defined using the ordinary filtration on the 56-dimensional Galois representation ρ_π; the highest Hodge–Tate weight yields a one-dimensional unramified quotient, and the Greenberg condition selects its preimage. The main conjecture asserts that the characteristic ideal of the dual Selmer group X(π) is generated by L_p(π). The μ-invariant measures congruences with an Eisenstein series on E₇. The Euler system is expected to arise from cycles on the E₇ Shimura variety. The reciprocity law for the Coleman map is conjectural. The control theorem holds under standard hypotheses. The existence of a Shimura variety for E₇ makes this theory more tractable than for G₂ or F₄, but the large dimension and the complexity of the representation pose significant technical challenges. The construction of p-adic families of automorphic forms on E₇ and the associated eigenvarieties is an active area of research.
