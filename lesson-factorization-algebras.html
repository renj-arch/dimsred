Factorization Algebras — Cosheaves, Vertex Algebras & the Batalin-Vilkovisky Formalism

Deep dive into Factorization Algebras — the Costello-Gwilliam approach to perturbative quantum field theory that formalizes the observables of a QFT as a factorization algebra (a cosheaf with multilocal structure) on the underlying space-time manifold, the prefactorization category and the configuration space of disjoint disks (the little disks operad), the factorization algebra of classical observables (the commutative algebra of functions on the space of fields with the Poisson bracket from the BV formalism), the Batalin-Vilkovisky (BV) formalism (the antibracket, the classical master equation {S, S} = 0, and the quantum master equation ħΔS + ½{S, S} = 0), the relationship between holomorphic factorization algebras on Riemann surfaces and vertex algebras (the operator product expansion and the state-field correspondence), the Chiral algebras of Beilinson-Drinfeld (the chiral homology and conformal blocks), the factorization envelope of a Lie algebra (the universal factorization algebra associated to a Lie algebra with an invariant pairing), the Noether theorem in the language of factorization algebras (conserved currents as central extensions), and applications to the geometric Langlands program and elliptic cohomology.

"A factorization algebra is a mathematical structure that encodes the observables of a quantum field theory — their multilocality and operator product expansions."
— Kevin Costello & Owen Gwilliam
Part 1

📏 Prefactorization Categories and the Little Disks Operad

Cosheaves
Prefactorization Algebras, Cosheaves, and Configuration Spaces of Disks
A prefactorization algebra F on a topological space M assigns to each open set U ⊂ M a vector space F(U) (the observables on U), and to each finite collection of disjoint open sets U_1, ..., U_n contained in an open set V a multiplication map m_{U_1, ..., U_n}^V: F(U_1) ⊗ ... ⊗ F(U_n) → F(V). These maps must be compatible with inclusions (if we restrict to smaller open sets, the structure maps intertwine) and be equivariant under the action of the symmetric group (the order of the disjoint disks does not matter). A factorization algebra is a prefactorization algebra that also satisfies a gluing condition (a cosheaf condition): if V is covered by disjoint opens U_i, and we have observables on each U_i that agree on overlaps, they glue uniquely to an observable on V. The little disks operad E_n (with configuration spaces of n-dimensional disks) acts on any factorization algebra on ℝ^n by the structure maps — this is the E_n operad action. A locally constant factorization algebra on ℝ^n is an E_n-algebra (an algebra over the E_n operad). The factorization homology ∫_M F of a factorization algebra F over a manifold M is the derived global sections — it computes the quantum observables of the theory on M. The factorization envelope 𝕌𝔤 of a Lie algebra 𝔤 is the E_n-algebra whose underlying vector space is Sym(𝔤[1−n]) with differential given by the Lie bracket.
Factorization algebras organize the observables of a QFT by their multilocality: the multiplication maps encode how observables on disjoint regions combine when they are brought together.
Part 2

🌍 The BV Formalism and Classical Observables

BV
The Batalin-Vilkovisky Formalism, the Classical Master Equation, and the Observable Algebra
The Batalin-Vilkovisky (BV) formalism is a cohomological approach to gauge theories. The space of fields is a graded manifold ℰ = ℰ₀ ⊕ ℰ₁[−1] (the fields and anti-fields). The BV action S is a degree-0 function satisfying the classical master equation (CME): {S, S} = 0, where {−, −} is the antibracket (a degree-1 Poisson bracket of cohomological type). The classical observables Obs^cl form a commutative differential graded algebra (the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex of the Lie algebra of vector fields on the space of fields). The construction of the classical factorization algebra: to each open set U, assign the space of functions on the space of fields on U, equipped with the differential {S, −} (the BV Laplacian). The classical master equation {S, S} = 0 ensures (d_S)² = 0. The Poisson bracket on observables of codimension 1 (the Poisson vertex algebra in 2 dimensions) arises from the antibracket. For a free field theory, Obs^cl(U) = Sym(ℰ_c^*(U)[−1]) with the differential given by the linearized equations of motion. The Noether theorem in BV language: a symmetry of the action gives a conserved current — a degree-1 observable J such that d_S J = 0, and the charge ∫_{Σ} J (integrated over a codimension-1 submanifold) is a conserved quantity. The classical factorization algebra of a gauge theory encodes the BRST cohomology of the local observables.
The BV formalism provides a systematic way to handle gauge symmetries: the classical master equation ensures gauge-invariance, and the observable algebra is the cohomology of the BV differential.
Part 3

🧮 Quantization and Vertex Algebras

Vertex
Holomorphic Factorization Algebras, Vertex Algebras, and the Operator Product Expansion
A holomorphic factorization algebra on a Riemann surface Σ is a factorization algebra whose structure maps are holomorphic in the positions of the disks. The key theorem (Costello-Gwilliam): a holomorphic factorization algebra on a Riemann surface that is translation-invariant and satisfies a certain locality axiom is equivalent to a vertex algebra. The state-field correspondence Y(a, z): V → V((z)) assigns to each state a ∈ V a field Y(a, z) that is an operator-valued formal power series. The operator product expansion (OPE) states that the product of two fields Y(a, z)Y(b, w) can be expanded as a sum of poles in (z−w) with coefficients given by fields — this is equivalent to the factorization algebra multiplication maps for concentric annuli. The translation operator T (the infinitesimal translation) generates the action of the Virasoro algebra when the vertex algebra is conformal. The quantum master equation (QME): for a quantum theory, the BV action satisfies ħΔS + ½{S, S} = 0, where Δ is the BV Laplacian (the second-order differential operator from the odd symplectic structure). The quantum observables Obs^q are the complex Sym(ℰ_c^*(U))[[ħ]] with differential d_S + ħΔ. The condition (d_S + ħΔ)² = 0 is equivalent to the QME. The renormalization of the factorization algebra involves the heat kernel and the renormalization group flow — the choice of a regulator (a scale) leads to a family of prefactorization algebras, and the limit as the regulator is removed defines the quantum factorization algebra.
Holomorphic factorization algebras are the geometric avatar of vertex algebras: the OPE is encoded in the factorization structure on a Riemann surface, and quantization is controlled by the quantum master equation.
Part 4

🔥 Chiral Algebras and Geometric Langlands

Langlands
Beilinson-Drinfeld Chiral Algebras, Conformal Blocks, and the Geometric Langlands Program
Chiral algebras (Beilinson-Drinfeld) are a geometric version of vertex algebras: a chiral algebra on a smooth algebraic curve X is a 𝒟-module A on the Ran space (the space of finite subsets of X) with a factorization structure. The Ran space Ran(X) is the set of all non-empty finite subsets of X — its topology captures the idea of points coalescing. The chiral homology H_*(X, A) is the factorization homology of the chiral algebra — it computes the space of conformal blocks for the vertex algebra when X is a Riemann surface. The KZ connection (Knizhnik-Zamolodchikov) is a flat connection on the space of conformal blocks associated to an affine Kac-Moody algebra at level k. The geometric Langlands correspondence (Beilinson-Drinfeld, Arinkin-Gaitsgory) relates 𝒟-modules on the moduli stack Bun_G of principal G-bundles on a curve X to the moduli space of local systems for the Langlands dual group ^LG. Factorization algebras appear: the Hecke eigensheaves are 𝒟-modules on Bun_G whose eigenvalues are given by ℒ-adic local systems — their construction uses the factorization structure of the affine Kac-Moody vertex algebra at critical level k = −h^∨. The Feigin-Frenkel theorem: the center of the critical level affine Kac-Moody algebra is isomorphic to the algebra of functions on the space of ^LG-opers — this is the quantum geometric Langlands correspondence for the critical level. The elliptic cohomology (the generalized cohomology theory associated to elliptic curves) is related to factorization algebras: the topological modular forms (TMF) can be constructed as the factorization homology of certain factorization algebras over the moduli space of elliptic curves.
Chiral algebras and their factorization homology provide the language for conformal field theory on curves — they are central to the geometric Langlands program, where the critical level affine algebra governs Hecke eigensheaves.

Renormalization Group Flow and the Heat Kernel in Factorization Algebras

The renormalization group (RG) flow provides the mechanism by which a family of prefactorization algebras, indexed by a length scale ε, converges to a quantum factorization algebra as ε → 0. In the Costello–Gwilliam framework the regulator is implemented by the heat kernel Kε(x, y) of the kinetic operator on the space of fields. The heat kernel satisfies the semigroup property Kε₁+ε₂ = Kε₁ ⋆ Kε₂ and its short‑time asymptotics encode the local counterterms required for renormalization. Concretely, the effective BV action at scale ε is defined by the functional integral over field configurations with covariance Kε, and the quantum master equation (QME) at scale ε reads ħΔεSε + ½{Sε, Sε}ε = 0, where Δε and {−,−}ε are the BV Laplacian and antibracket computed with the regularized propagator. The RG flow equation ∂εSε = ½{Sε, Sε}ε′ − ħΔε′Sε guarantees that if the QME holds at one scale it holds at all scales. The limit ε → 0 exists after adding local counterterms that are polynomial in the fields and their derivatives; these counterterms are precisely the terms appearing in the asymptotic expansion of the heat kernel on the diagonal. The resulting quantum factorization algebra assigns to each open set U the cochain complex (Sym(ℰc⁎(U))[[ħ]], dS + ħΔ) whose cohomology is the algebra of quantum observables. This construction is functorial with respect to embeddings of manifolds and compatible with the factorization product, making the RG flow a morphism in the ∞‑category of prefactorization algebras.

Key Steps in the Heat‑Kernel Renormalization

  1. Choose a kinetic operator D (typically a generalized Laplacian) on the field bundle.
  2. Construct the heat kernel Kε = e−εD and its diagonal asymptotics Kε(x, x) ∼ (4πε)−d/2 Σ ak(x) εk.
  3. Define the regularized BV Laplacian Δε by contracting fields with Kε.
  4. Solve the QME order by order in ħ, adding local counterterms dictated by the coefficients ak.
  5. Pass to the limit ε → 0; the resulting differential dS + ħΔ squares to zero and yields the quantum factorization algebra.

The heat‑kernel method is particularly powerful on curved backgrounds because the Seeley–DeWitt coefficients ak are universal polynomials in the curvature tensor and its covariant derivatives. This universality guarantees that the renormalized factorization algebra depends only on the local geometry of the underlying manifold, a fact that underlies the topological invariance of factorization homology for topological field theories.

Higher Operads and the Enveloping Algebra of the Little Disks Operad

The little disks operad 𝔻n governs n‑fold loop spaces and, via the recognition principle, n‑fold monoidal structures. In factorization algebra theory one works with the colored version 𝔻ncol whose colors are open subsets of ℝn and whose operations are given by configurations of disjoint disks. The enveloping algebra of an operad 𝒪, denoted U(𝒪), is the universal associative algebra in the category of 𝒪‑algebras; for 𝔻n it captures the higher homotopies that make the factorization product associative up to coherent homotopy. Costello and Gwilliam show that a prefactorization algebra on ℝn is equivalent to an algebra over the enveloping algebra U(𝔻ncol). This equivalence is established by a bar‑cobar resolution: the free 𝔻n‑algebra on a cochain complex V is the operadic bar construction B(𝔻n, V), and its cobar construction ΩB(𝔻n, V) recovers the original prefactorization algebra. The higher operad structure appears when one considers the ∞‑operad of little disks, whose mapping spaces are the configuration spaces Confk(ℝn). The homology of these configuration spaces is the Poisson operad Poisn, and the enveloping algebra U(𝔻n) is quasi‑isomorphic to the universal enveloping algebra of the Poisson operad. Consequently, the category of prefactorization algebras on ℝn is monadic over the category of Poisn‑algebras, and the forgetful functor admits a left adjoint given by the factorization envelope. This monadicity is the algebraic incarnation of the statement that factorization algebras are “higher” versions of Poisson algebras, with the higher brackets encoded by the operadic composition of disks.

Worked Example: Computing the First Non‑Trivial Counterterm
Consider a free scalar field φ on ℝ2 with kinetic operator D = −Δ + m². The heat kernel diagonal has expansion Kε(x, x) = (4πε)−1(1 + ε m² + O(ε²)). Using the BV formalism, the regularized BV Laplacian at scale ε is Δε = ∫ d²x Kε(x, x) δ²/δφ(x)δφ⁺(x). Show that the first counterterm needed to satisfy the QME at order ħ is (ħ/2) m² ∫ φ².
The classical action is S₀ = ½∫ φ(−Δ + m²)φ. The QME at scale ε reads ħΔεSε + ½{Sε, Sε}ε = 0. Expand Sε = S₀ + ħ S₁ + … . At order ħ the equation becomes ΔεS₀ + ½{S₀, S₀}ε = 0. Since S₀ is quadratic, {S₀, S₀}ε = 0. Compute ΔεS₀ = ∫ d²x Kε(x, x) δ²S₀/δφ(x)δφ⁺(x). The antifield φ⁺ appears only in the gauge‑fixed action; for a free theory the BV Laplacian acts on the quadratic term giving ΔεS₀ = ∫ d²x Kε(x, x) (−Δ + m²)δ(x−x) = ∫ d²x Kε(x, x) m². Using the diagonal asymptotics, Kε(x, x) = (4πε)−1(1 + ε m² + …). The divergent piece (4πε)−1 is removed by the standard mass renormalization; the finite piece at ε → 0 is m². Therefore the required counterterm is S₁ = (1/2) m² ∫ φ², and the renormalized action S = S₀ + ħ S₁ satisfies the QME to order ħ.
Exam‑Day Strategy
When a question asks for the role of the heat kernel in renormalization, focus on three points: (1) the heat kernel provides a scale‑dependent covariance, (2) its diagonal asymptotics generate the local counterterms, and (3) the RG flow equation guarantees the QME at all scales. Memorize the schematic form of the flow equation ∂εSε = ½{Sε, Sε}ε′ − ħΔε′Sε; it appears frequently in both multiple‑choice and descriptive items.

Factorization Homology of Manifolds with Boundary and the Cobordism Hypothesis

Factorization homology ∫M F of a factorization algebra F on an n‑manifold M is the colimit of the values of F on a Ran‑space of disks embedded in M. When M has a boundary ∂M, the factorization algebra must be equipped with a boundary condition, i.e. a module over the restriction of F to the half‑space ℝn≥0. Costello–Gwilliam prove that the ∞‑category of factorization algebras on ℝn with a chosen boundary condition is equivalent to the ∞‑category of En‑algebras in the ∞‑category of modules over the En‑algebra F|ℝn≥0. This equivalence is a manifestation of the cobordism hypothesis: a fully extended topological field theory is determined by its value on a point, which in the factorization language is an En‑algebra with a chosen module. Concretely, for a framed n‑manifold M with boundary, the factorization homology ∫M F computes the Hochschild homology of the En‑algebra F(ℝn) with coefficients in the boundary module. In the case n = 2, this recovers the familiar statement that the factorization homology of a holomorphic factorization algebra on a Riemann surface with boundary is the space of conformal blocks for the associated vertex algebra with specified boundary conditions (e.g. Cardy branes). The gluing formula for factorization homology, ∫M₁ ∪N M₂ F ≃ ∫M₁ F ⊗∫N F ∫M₂ F, is the algebraic counterpart of cutting and sewing cobordisms, and it holds in the ∞‑categorical setting because factorization homology is a symmetric monoidal functor from the (∞,n)‑category of bordisms to chain complexes.

Boundary Conditions as Modules

  • A boundary condition for a factorization algebra F on ℝn is a factorization algebra B on ℝn−1 together with a morphism F|ℝn−1×ℝ≥0 → B that is compatible with the factorization product.
  • Equivalently, B is a left module over the En‑algebra F(ℝn) in the ∞‑category of En‑algebras.
  • The space of boundary states is the factorization homology of B on the boundary manifold ∂M.

This module‑theoretic description allows one to classify boundary conditions for a given bulk factorization algebra by studying the ∞‑category of modules over the corresponding En‑algebra. For example, boundary conditions for the chiral algebra of an affine Kac–Moody vertex algebra at critical level are classified by representations of the corresponding quantum group, a result that follows from the geometric Langlands correspondence and the identification of the critical level center with functions on opers.

The BV Laplacian in Deformation Quantization and the Formality Theorem

The BV Laplacian Δ is a second‑order differential operator on the space of functionals of fields and antifields, defined by contracting with the odd symplectic form ω = ∫ δφ ∧ δφ⁺. In deformation quantization one seeks a star product ⋆ on the algebra of observables C∞(M)[[ħ]] such that f ⋆ g = fg + ħ{f,g} + O(ħ²), where {−,−} is the Poisson bracket induced by ω. Kontsevich’s formality theorem constructs an L∞ quasi‑isomorphism between the differential graded Lie algebra of polyvector fields (with Schouten bracket) and the Hochschild cochain complex of C∞(M). In the BV language, the quantum master equation ħΔS + ½{S,S} = 0 is precisely the Maurer–Cartan equation for the L∞ algebra of observables with differential d = ħΔ + {S,−}. The formality map sends a solution of the classical master equation {S,S}=0 to a solution of the QME, thereby producing a deformation quantization of the classical observable algebra. The higher homotopies of the L∞ morphism encode the higher order terms in the star product. When the target is a factorization algebra, the formality map is compatible with the factorization product: the star product on observables supported in disjoint opens is given by the factorization multiplication, and the associativity of the star product follows from the associativity of the factorization algebra up to coherent homotopy. This compatibility is the algebraic heart of the statement that factorization algebras provide a geometric realization of deformation quantization.

Key Identities

  • Δ² = 0 (nilpotence of the BV Laplacian).
  • {f, g} = (−1)|f| (Δ(fg) − Δf·g − (−1)|f| f·Δg) (derived bracket formula).
  • Quantum master equation: ħΔS + ½{S,S} = 0 ⇔ (dS + ħΔ)² = 0.
  • Formality: U = exp(ħΔ) intertwines the classical and quantum products: f ⋆ g = U(U⁻¹f · U⁻¹g).

These identities show that the BV Laplacian is the generator of the quantization map. In the context of factorization algebras, the operator U = exp(ħΔ) acts on the prefactorization algebra level and implements the passage from the classical factorization algebra (a Poisson factorization algebra) to the quantum factorization algebra. The renormalization group flow can be viewed as a homotopy between different choices of U corresponding to different regulators.

Elliptic Cohomology, Topological Modular Forms, and Factorization Algebras

Elliptic cohomology is a generalized cohomology theory Ell∗(−) whose coefficient ring is the ring of modular forms. The Goerss–Hopkins–Miller theorem constructs a sheaf of E∞ ring spectra 𝒪top on the moduli stack ℳell of elliptic curves; its global sections define the spectrum of topological modular forms TMF. Lurie and others have shown that TMF can be obtained as the factorization homology of a certain 2‑dimensional factorization algebra 𝒜ell over ℳell. The factorization algebra 𝒜ell assigns to an open set U ⊂ ℳell the E2‑algebra of derived functions on the universal elliptic curve over U, equipped with a natural BV structure coming from the holomorphic symplectic form on the total space of the universal curve. The factorization product corresponds to the operation of gluing elliptic curves along marked points, which on the level of cohomology yields the modular transformation properties of elliptic genera. The resulting factorization homology ∫ℳell 𝒜ell is an E∞ ring spectrum whose homotopy groups are the topological modular forms. This construction provides a physical interpretation: TMF is the space of states of a 2‑dimensional supersymmetric sigma model whose target is the moduli of elliptic curves, and the factorization algebra encodes the operator product expansions of the chiral de Rham complex on the universal elliptic curve. The chiral de Rham complex is a vertex algebra (a holomorphic factorization algebra) whose chiral homology computes the elliptic genus of the target manifold. Thus the chain of equivalences — vertex algebra holomorphic factorization algebra chiral algebra factorization homology — culminates in the identification of TMF with the factorization homology of the chiral de Rham factorization algebra over ℳell.

Factorization Algebras on Stratified Spaces and the Exit Path ∞-Category

Factorization algebras were originally defined on topological manifolds, but many physical and geometric situations require a version on stratified spaces — spaces decomposed into manifolds of varying dimension that meet along singular strata. The Costello–Gwilliam framework extends to this setting by replacing the Ran space of a manifold with the Ran space of a stratified space, and by using the exit path ∞‑category introduced by Ayala, Francis, and Tanaka. An exit path is a continuous path that moves only in the direction of decreasing stratum depth; its homotopy classes form an ∞‑category whose objects are points of the stratified space and whose morphisms encode the ways a point can approach a lower‑dimensional stratum. A prefactorization algebra on a stratified space X is then a functor from the ∞‑category of open subsets of X (with the exit path structure) to a symmetric monoidal ∞‑category, satisfying a factorization condition with respect to disjoint unions of opens that are separated by a positive distance in the exit path metric.

The key theorem (Ayala–Francis–Tanaka, 2018) states that the ∞‑category of factorization algebras on a conically smooth stratified space X is equivalent to the ∞‑category of algebras over the little disks operad in the ∞‑category of constructible sheaves on X with respect to the given stratification. This equivalence is established by a version of the non‑abelian Poincaré duality that identifies factorization homology ∫_X 𝒜 with the colimit of 𝒜 over the exit path ∞‑category. In practice, this means that to compute the factorization homology of a factorization algebra on a stratified space, one can work entirely with constructible sheaves and the exit path category, avoiding the analytic difficulties of the heat‑kernel renormalization on singular spaces. The construction also clarifies the role of boundary conditions: a boundary stratum is treated as a stratum of codimension one, and modules over the bulk factorization algebra correspond precisely to factorization algebras on the boundary that are compatible with the exit path structure.

Applications include the description of topological defects in quantum field theory, where a defect of codimension k is modeled by a factorization algebra on a stratum of dimension n−k, and the classification of gapped phases of matter with symmetry, where the exit path ∞‑category encodes the possible symmetry‑breaking patterns across strata. The formalism also provides a rigorous foundation for the factorization homology of manifolds with corners, which is essential for the extended topological quantum field theories appearing in the cobordism hypothesis.

Koszul Duality for Factorization Algebras and the Ran Space

Koszul duality, originally developed for associative algebras and operads, has a natural formulation in the setting of factorization algebras. The Ran space Ran(X) of a topological space X is the space of non‑empty finite subsets of X equipped with the topology generated by the sets {S ⊂ X finite | S ⊂ U} for open U ⊂ X. A factorization algebra on X can be viewed as a cosheaf on Ran(X) with values in a symmetric monoidal ∞‑category, and the factorization product corresponds to the convolution product on the Ran space. The Koszul dual of a factorization algebra 𝒜 is then defined as the factorization algebra 𝒜^! obtained by applying the Verdier duality functor on Ran(X) followed by a shift that reflects the homological degree of the factorization product. When X is a complex curve, this construction recovers the classical Koszul duality between chiral algebras and factorization algebras established by Beilinson and Drinfeld.

The main result (Francis–Gaitsgory, 2012; Lurie, 2017) asserts that for a smooth manifold X of dimension n, there is an equivalence between the ∞‑category of E_n‑algebras in a stable symmetric monoidal ∞‑category C and the ∞‑category of factorization algebras on X with values in C that are locally constant on the configuration spaces of X. Under this equivalence, the Koszul dual of an E_n‑algebra A corresponds to the factorization algebra on X whose value on an open U is the factorization homology of the dual E_n‑algebra A^! over U. In particular, when C is the ∞‑category of chain complexes, the Koszul dual of the commutative algebra of functions on a derived stack is the factorization algebra of chiral differential operators on the Ran space of that stack. This duality exchanges the classical limit (the Poisson structure) with the quantum deformation parameter, and it underlies the geometric Langlands correspondence for factorization algebras.

Concretely, if 𝒜 is a factorization algebra on a curve C that is holomorphic (i.e. a vertex algebra), its Koszul dual 𝒜^! is a factorization algebra on the same curve whose local sections are the chiral homology groups of 𝒜. The duality interchanges the operator product expansion of 𝒜 with the factorization product of 𝒜^!, and it sends the BV Laplacian of 𝒜 to the de Rham differential on the Ran space. This perspective has been used to prove the Feigin–Frenkel duality for affine vertex algebras at critical level and to construct the categorical geometric Langlands correspondence for GL_n.

Worked Example: Koszul Dual of a Free Boson Vertex Algebra
Let V be the free boson vertex algebra on a complex curve C, generated by a field φ(z) with OPE φ(z)φ(w) ∼ log(z−w). Compute the Koszul dual factorization algebra V^! on C and identify its local sections.
The free boson vertex algebra V is a holomorphic factorization algebra on C. Its Koszul dual V^! is obtained by applying Verdier duality on Ran(C) and shifting by the dimension of C (which is 1). The local sections of V on an open U ⊂ C are the Fock space modules of the Heisenberg algebra. Verdier duality on Ran(C) exchanges the factorization product (given by the OPE) with the convolution product on the Ran space, which in this case yields the de Rham complex of the configuration spaces of U. After shifting by 1, the local sections of V^! on U are the compactly supported cohomology groups H_c^*(Conf_*(U), ℂ)[1], where Conf_*(U) denotes the Ran space of U. These groups carry a natural factorization algebra structure induced by the gluing of configurations, and they are precisely the chiral homology groups of V. Thus V^! is the factorization algebra of chiral differential operators on the Ran space of C, and its factorization homology over C recovers the cohomology of the moduli space of line bundles on C, in agreement with the Feigin–Frenkel duality.
Exam Strategy
When a question asks for the Koszul dual of a given factorization algebra, first identify whether the algebra is holomorphic (vertex algebra) or topological (E_n‑algebra). For holomorphic cases, recall that the dual is computed by Verdier duality on the Ran space followed by a shift equal to the complex dimension of the curve. For topological cases, use the Francis–Gaitsgory equivalence to replace the problem by Koszul duality of E_n‑algebras. Write down the local sections explicitly as (compactly supported) cohomology of configuration spaces; this is the standard answer examiners expect.

Factorization Algebras in Perturbative Algebraic Quantum Field Theory

Perturbative algebraic quantum field theory (pAQFT) provides a rigorous framework for constructing interacting quantum field theories on globally hyperbolic spacetimes. The central objects are the algebras of observables, which are defined as formal power series in the coupling constant with coefficients in the space of microcausal functionals on the space of field configurations. Costello and Gwilliam showed that the pAQFT construction naturally yields a factorization algebra on the spacetime manifold M: to each open set U ⊂ M one assigns the algebra of observables supported in U, and the factorization product is given by the time‑ordered product of observables with disjoint supports. The BV formalism enters through the quantum master equation, which ensures that the time‑ordered product satisfies the factorization axiom up to homotopy. The renormalization group flow in pAQFT corresponds precisely to the change of the factorization algebra under the heat‑kernel regulator, and the equivalence classes of factorization algebras under RG flow are in bijection with the equivalence classes of solutions to the quantum master equation modulo the action of the renormalization group.

A key technical result is the equivalence between the factorization algebra of observables in pAQFT and the factorization algebra obtained by the effective Batalin–Vilkovisky quantization of the classical BV theory. This equivalence is proved by constructing a homotopy between the two factorization algebras using the Feynman diagram expansion and the properties of the heat kernel on M. The homotopy is controlled by the BV Laplacian Δ, which acts on the space of local functionals and generates the quantum corrections to the classical factorization product. The classical limit (ħ → 0) of the factorization algebra recovers the Poisson factorization algebra of classical observables, whose factorization product is the pointwise product of functionals and whose BV bracket is the Peierls bracket. The quantum factorization algebra deforms this Poisson structure by the star product f ⋆ g = fg + ħ{f,g} + O(ħ²), where the higher order terms are given by sums over connected Feynman diagrams with vertices labelled by the interaction functional.

This perspective has several consequences. First, it provides a conceptual proof of the perturbative renormalizability of a theory: a theory is renormalizable iff the corresponding factorization algebra can be extended from the complement of the diagonal in M×M to the whole product, which is exactly the condition that the factorization algebra is a cosheaf on the Ran space of M. Second, it clarifies the role of anomalies: an anomaly is the obstruction to extending the factorization algebra across the diagonal, and it is measured by a cohomology class in the factorization homology of M with coefficients in the adjoint representation of the symmetry group. Third, it allows the import of techniques from factorization homology — such as excision and the cobordism hypothesis — into pAQFT, leading to new results on the classification of topological sectors and the construction of boundary conditions for gauge theories.

Higher Categorical Structures: Enveloping Algebras and the Cobordism Hypothesis

The little disks operad 𝔻_n is an E_n‑operad in topological spaces, and its enveloping algebra U(𝔻_n) in the ∞‑category of ∞‑operads plays the role of the universal E_n‑algebra. In the context of factorization algebras, the enveloping algebra appears as the factorization algebra on ℝ^n that assigns to an open U the E_n‑algebra of chains on the configuration space of U. The higher operadic structure of U(𝔻_n) encodes all higher homotopies required for the monadicity theorem: the ∞‑category of E_n‑algebras in a symmetric monoidal ∞‑category C is equivalent to the ∞‑category of algebras over the monad U(𝔻_n) ⊗ (−) in C. This monadicity is essential for the proof of the cobordism hypothesis in the factorization algebra setting, because it identifies the ∞‑category of fully extended n‑dimensional topological quantum field theories with the ∞‑category of fully dualizable objects in the symmetric monoidal ∞‑category of E_n‑algebras.

Lurie's proof of the cobordism hypothesis uses the (∞,n)‑category of bordisms Bord_n, which is the free symmetric monoidal (∞,n)‑category with duals on a single object. The factorization homology construction provides a symmetric monoidal functor ∫− 𝒜 : Bord_n → C for any E_n‑algebra 𝒜 in C, and this functor is fully determined by the value of 𝒜 on the point. The higher enveloping algebra U(𝔻_n) appears as the factorization homology of the universal E_n‑algebra over the n‑disk, and its higher homotopies correspond to the higher morphisms in Bord_n. In particular, the k‑morphisms in Bord_n are generated by k‑dimensional manifolds with corners, and the factorization algebra assigns to each such manifold the iterated factorization homology of 𝒜, which is computed by the k‑fold bar construction on U(𝔻_n). The dualizability conditions in the cobordism hypothesis translate into the requirement that the E_n‑algebra 𝒜 be n‑dualizable, which in the factorization algebra language means that the factorization homology of 𝒜 over any closed n‑manifold is a dualizable object in C, and the evaluation and coevaluation maps are given by the factorization product on the pair of pants and its reverse.

Recent work by Ayala–Francis–Rozenblyum extends this picture to manifolds with singularities, where the exit path ∞‑category replaces the fundamental ∞‑groupoid. The enveloping algebra of the little disks operad with singularities is then a stratified version of U(𝔻_n), and the cobordism hypothesis for stratified manifolds states that the ∞‑category of stratified TQFTs is equivalent to the ∞‑category of fully dualizable objects in the ∞‑category of stratified E_n‑algebras. This provides a unified framework for defects, boundaries, and domain walls in topological quantum field theory, all described by factorization algebras on stratified spaces.

Applications to Geometric Langlands and the Beilinson–Drinfeld Grassmannian

The geometric Langlands program seeks to establish a correspondence between D‑modules on the moduli stack Bun_G of G‑bundles on a curve X and quasi‑coherent sheaves on the moduli stack LocSys^L G of ^L G‑local systems, where ^L G is the Langlands dual group. Factorization algebras provide the natural language for this correspondence because both sides carry a natural factorization structure with respect to the Ran space of X. The Beilinson–Drinfeld Grassmannian Gr_G is the ind‑scheme over Ran(X) whose fiber over a finite set S ⊂ X is the product of the affine Grassmannians GrG,x for x ∈ S. The factorization algebra of chiral differential operators on Gr_G, denoted Dch(Gr_G), is a holomorphic factorization algebra on X whose factorization homology computes the category of D‑modules on Bun_G. On the other side, the factorization algebra of functions on the space of ^L G‑local systems, denoted O(LocSys^L G), is a topological factorization algebra whose factorization homology computes the category of quasi‑coherent sheaves on LocSys^L G.

The geometric Langlands conjecture can be reformulated as an equivalence of factorization algebras Dch(Gr_G) ≃ O(LocSys^L G) after a suitable twist by the critical level line bundle. This equivalence is a manifestation of the Koszul duality for factorization algebras discussed earlier: the chiral differential operators are the Koszul dual of the functions on the local systems stack. The proof of this equivalence for GL_n (by Gaitsgory, Arinkin, and others) proceeds by constructing a Whittaker factorization algebra on the Ran space, which interpolates between the two sides, and by showing that the Whittaker model is self‑dual under Koszul duality. The factorization algebra perspective also clarifies the role of the Hecke operators: they are the factorization product with the fundamental representation of ^L G, and the Hecke eigensheaf condition translates into the statement that the factorization algebra is a module over the spherical Hecke factorization algebra.

Beyond the classical geometric Langlands correspondence, factorization algebras have been used to formulate the categorical geometric Langlands correspondence for arbitrary reductive groups, to define the quantum geometric Langlands correspondence at a generic parameter ψ (where the factorization algebra is a deformation of Dch(Gr_G) by a quantum parameter), and to construct the spectral side of the correspondence for real groups via the factorization algebra of tempered representations. The Ran space formalism also allows the incorporation of ramifications and parabolic structures by considering factorization algebras on the Ran space of a curve with marked points, where the stalks at the marked points are replaced by representations of the corresponding parabolic subgroups. This unified approach has led to new results on the automorphic side, such as the proof of the Arthur multiplicity formula for GL_n using factorization homology of the Whittaker factorization algebra.

The Operator Product Expansion and the Associativity Constraint in Factorization Algebras

The operator product expansion (OPE) is the computational engine of a factorization algebra. For a factorization algebra F on a manifold M, the OPE encodes the factorization product F(U) ⊗ F(V) → F(U ∪ V) for disjoint open sets U, V as a formal series in the distance between U and V. In the Costello–Gwilliam framework, the OPE is not merely an asymptotic expansion but a precise homotopical datum: the factorization algebra is an algebra over the little disks operad 𝔻n, and the OPE coefficients are the structure maps of this algebra. The associativity of the factorization product translates into the statement that the OPE satisfies a system of quadratic relations known as the associativity constraints. For a vertex algebra, these are the Borcherds identities; for a general factorization algebra, they are the homotopy coherent associativity conditions of an En-algebra.

Concretely, let Oi be local observables supported in small disks Di. The factorization product O1 ⋆ O2 is defined by choosing a configuration of little disks inside a larger disk and using the operad action. The OPE arises when we take the limit where the disks D1 and D2 approach each other. The coefficients of the singular terms in this limit are the OPE coefficients. The associativity constraint demands that for three observables O1, O2, O3, the two ways of computing the triple product (O1 ⋆ O2) ⋆ O3 and O1 ⋆ (O2 ⋆ O3) are homotopic. In the language of operads, this is the statement that the structure map 𝔻n(k) ⊗ F(D)⊗k → F(D) satisfies the operad composition axioms up to coherent homotopy.

A key technical point is that the OPE in a factorization algebra is not required to be convergent; it is a formal power series in the coordinates of the configuration space of points. The factorization algebra axioms guarantee that the formal OPE satisfies the associativity constraints order by order. This is the homotopical version of the statement that the OPE defines a vertex algebra when n = 2. For n > 2, the OPE is less singular (the singularities are of codimension n), and the associativity constraints are governed by the higher operad 𝔻n. The heat kernel renormalization scheme constructs the factorization algebra by defining the OPE coefficients recursively via the effective BV action, and the associativity constraints are equivalent to the quantum master equation at each scale.

In perturbative quantum field theory, the OPE of the factorization algebra of observables reproduces the standard Wilsonian OPE. The factorization algebra perspective clarifies that the OPE is a local-to-global principle: the global observables on a manifold M are computed as the factorization homology ∫M F, which is the colimit of the diagram F(U) → F(V) for inclusions of open sets. The OPE provides the gluing data for this colimit. This is why the factorization algebra of a free field theory is determined entirely by its two-point function: the OPE of the free field is Gaussian, and all higher OPE coefficients are determined by Wick's theorem, which is the associativity constraint for the free En-algebra.

Factorization Algebras on Manifolds with Corners and the Higher Morita Category

The extension of factorization algebras to manifolds with corners is essential for the full cobordism hypothesis. A manifold with corners of codimension k is locally modeled on [0, ∞)k × ℝn−k. A factorization algebra on such a space must assign data not only to open sets in the interior but also to neighborhoods of the corners. The correct categorical receptacle for this data is the higher Morita category of En-algebras. An En-algebra A can be viewed as an object in the (∞, n)-category AlgEn, whose k-morphisms for k ≤ n are given by iterated bimodules. A factorization algebra on a manifold with corners assigns to each corner of codimension k a k-morphism in this category, i.e., an (k−1)-fold bimodule over the En-algebra assigned to the interior.

For example, a 2-dimensional factorization algebra (an E2-algebra) on a surface with boundary assigns to the interior an E2-algebra A, to each boundary component an A-A bimodule (a 1-morphism in AlgE2), and to each corner point a bimodule map (a 2-morphism). The factorization homology of the surface with corners then computes the iterated relative tensor product of these bimodules, which is the E2-Hochschild homology of A with coefficients in the boundary bimodules. This matches the prediction of the cobordism hypothesis: the value of the extended TQFT on a point is the E2-algebra A, on an interval is the A-A bimodule A (the identity bimodule), and on a corner is the unit map.

The higher Morita category is constructed by iterating the relative tensor product. For En-algebras, the tensor product is the En-tensor product, which is the factorization homology of the En-algebra on the interval. The k-morphisms are En−k-algebras in the category of (k−1)-fold bimodules. This periodic structure reflects the fact that an En-algebra is an E1-algebra in En−1-algebras. The factorization algebra on a manifold with corners is then a symmetric monoidal functor from the (∞, n)-category of bordisms with corners to the higher Morita category. The Costello–Gwilliam construction of the enveloping algebra U(𝔻n) provides the operadic control for this functor: the little disks operad with corners acts on the factorization algebra, and the enveloping algebra encodes the higher bimodule structures.

A concrete application is the classification of topological boundary conditions for the Poisson sigma model. The factorization algebra of observables is the E2-algebra of functions on the shifted cotangent bundle T*[1]X. Boundary conditions correspond to E1-algebras (associative algebras) in the category of modules over this E2-algebra, which are precisely the deformation quantizations of coisotropic submanifolds of X. The corners correspond to bimodules between these quantizations, which are the morphisms in the Fukaya category. The factorization homology of a surface with corners computes the morphism spaces in the Fukaya category, realizing the open-closed string duality.

Worked Example: OPE Associativity for the Free Boson
Consider the factorization algebra F of the free boson on ℝ2. The basic field is φ(z) with two-point function ⟨φ(z)φ(w)⟩ = log|z−w|2. Compute the OPE of ∂φ(z) with itself up to the singular terms, and verify the associativity constraint for the triple product (∂φ ⋆ ∂φ) ⋆ ∂φ at the level of the singular part.
The field ∂φ(z) has conformal weight 1. The two-point function is ⟨∂φ(z)∂φ(w)⟩ = 1/(z−w)2. The OPE is ∂φ(z)∂φ(w) ∼ 1/(z−w)2 + :∂φ(z)∂φ(w):. The singular part is 1/(z−w)2. For the triple product, we compute (∂φ(z)∂φ(w))∂φ(u). Using Wick's theorem, the singular part as z,w,u coincide comes from all pairwise contractions. The associativity constraint requires that the limit z→w→u equals w→u→z. Both give the same singular term 2/(z−w)2(w−u)2 + cyclic because the free boson OPE is Gaussian and the Wick contraction is symmetric. The factorization algebra structure guarantees this equality holds as an identity in the E2-algebra of observables, which is the vertex algebra of the Heisenberg algebra.

The general statement is: the factorization algebra is an algebra over the little disks operad 𝔻n, and associativity is the operad composition axiom γ: 𝔻n(k) × 𝔻n(j1) × ⋯ × 𝔻n(jk) → 𝔻n(∑ji) applied to the structure maps. For n=2 this reduces to the vertex algebra axioms; for n>2 the singularities are milder and the operad is En. Always identify the operad first.

The Quantum Master Equation and Anomalies in Factorization Algebras

The quantum master equation (QME) is the cohomological condition that guarantees the consistency of the factorization algebra constructed via the BV formalism. In the Costello–Gwilliam approach, a factorization algebra is built from a classical BV theory (E, S, Q, {−,−}) where E is the space of fields (a graded vector bundle), S is the classical action, Q is the differential, and {−,−} is the BV bracket. The quantization proceeds by constructing a family of effective actions SΛ at scale Λ using the heat kernel regularization. The QME at scale Λ reads Q SΛ + ½ {SΛ, SΛ} + ℏ Δ SΛ = 0, where Δ is the BV Laplacian (the second-order operator generating the bracket). A solution to the QME at all scales defines a factorization algebra; the failure of the QME to hold is the anomaly.

The anomaly is a local functional of the fields, valued in the cohomology of Q. In the factorization algebra language, an anomaly means that the factorization product is not associative up to homotopy; the associativity constraint holds only modulo a term proportional to the anomaly. The Wess–Zumino consistency condition for the anomaly is precisely the statement that the anomaly is a cocycle in the BV complex. If the anomaly is a coboundary, it can be removed by a local counterterm (a change of the effective action by a Q-exact term). If it is a non-trivial cohomology class, the theory is anomalous and does not define a factorization algebra; instead it defines a twisted factorization algebra, where the factorization product takes values in a torsor for the anomaly line bundle.

A central result is that for a free theory, the QME holds exactly at every scale because the BV Laplacian Δ acts trivially on the Gaussian effective action. For an interacting theory, the QME is solved order by order in ℏ using the renormalization group flow. The heat kernel renormalization scheme ensures that the counterterms are local, so the anomaly is a local functional. The cohomological classification of anomalies is given by the local BV cohomology H•loc(Q, {−,−}). In gauge theories, the anomaly is the descent of the characteristic class of the gauge bundle; for the chiral anomaly in 2 dimensions, it is the first Pontryagin class. The factorization algebra perspective shows that the anomaly is the obstruction to lifting the classical factorization algebra (the Koszul dual of the classical observables) to a quantum factorization algebra.

In the context of the geometric Langlands program, the anomaly appears as the critical level shift. The factorization algebra of chiral differential operators Dch(GrG) is defined at the critical level because the QME for the chiral WZW model holds only at that level. The critical level is the value of the central charge where the Sugawara construction yields a Virasoro algebra with central charge equal to the dimension of G. At other levels, the theory is anomalous, and the factorization algebra is twisted by the determinant line bundle on the Ran space. The quantum geometric Langlands correspondence at parameter ψ corresponds to the factorization algebra at level ψ, where the anomaly is controlled by the quantum parameter.

Factorization Algebras and the Geometric Satake Correspondence

The geometric Satake correspondence is an equivalence between the category of representations of the Langlands dual group LG and the category of G(O)-equivariant perverse sheaves on the affine Grassmannian GrG. In the factorization algebra framework, this correspondence is realized as an equivalence of E2-algebras (or braided tensor categories). The affine Grassmannian GrG carries a natural factorization algebra structure over the Ran space: the factorization algebra SatG assigns to a finite set S ⊂ X the category of G(OS)-equivariant perverse sheaves on GrG,S. The factorization product corresponds to the convolution product on the affine Grassmannian, which is the geometric analogue of the tensor product of representations.

The key insight is that the factorization algebra SatG is an E2-algebra in the category of categories (a braided monoidal category). Its category of modules is the category of G-bundles with a reduction to the maximal torus, which is the Hecke category. The geometric Satake equivalence states that SatG is equivalent, as an E2-algebra, to the factorization algebra Rep(LG) of representations of the dual group, where the factorization structure is given by the tensor product of representations. This equivalence is constructed by the nearby cycles functor on the Beilinson–Drinfeld Grassmannian, which provides a factorization algebra over the Ran space interpolating between the affine Grassmannian and the dual group.

The factorization algebra perspective clarifies the role of the spherical Hecke algebra. The spherical Hecke factorization algebra is the factorization algebra of G(O)-bi-invariant functions on G(K), which acts on SatG by convolution. Under geometric Satake, this action corresponds to the action of the representation ring of LG on Rep(LG) by tensoring with the fundamental representation. The Hecke eigensheaf condition for a D-module on BunG is then the statement that the factorization algebra Dch(GrG) is a module over the spherical Hecke factorization algebra. This is the local geometric Langlands correspondence at the level of factorization algebras.

For the quantum geometric Langlands correspondence, the factorization algebra SatG is deformed to a quantum group factorization algebra Repq(LG), where q is related to the quantum parameter ψ. The factorization algebra of chiral differential operators Dch(GrG) is deformed to the factorization algebra of D-modules on the quantum affine Grassmannian. The equivalence of these deformed factorization algebras is the quantum geometric Satake correspondence, which has been established for GLn by work of Bezrukavnikov, Finkelberg, and others using the factorization homology of the quantum Whittaker factorization algebra.

The Hochschild Cohomology of Factorization Algebras and Deformation Theory

The deformation theory of a factorization algebra F is controlled by its Hochschild cohomology HH•(F). For an En-algebra, the Hochschild cohomology is an En+1-algebra (the Deligne conjecture, proven by Lurie and Francis). In the factorization algebra context, HH•(F) is computed as the factorization homology of F on the n-dimensional disk with a point removed, i.e., ∫Dn∖{0} F. This space carries an En+1-algebra structure because the configuration space of little disks in Dn∖{0} is an En+1-operad. The tangent complex of the moduli space of factorization algebras at F is HH•(F)[1], and the obstruction theory is governed by the En+1-algebra structure.

For a vertex algebra (an E2-algebra), the Hochschild cohomology is an E3-algebra, which is a braided E2-algebra. The first Hochschild cohomology HH1(F) classifies infinitesimal deformations of the vertex algebra structure. The second HH2(F) contains the obstructions. In the case of the chiral differential operators Dch(GrG), the Hochschild cohomology computes the deformation theory of the critical level chiral algebra. The quantum parameter ψ in the quantum geometric Langlands correspondence corresponds to a class in HH2(Dch(GrG)) that deforms the factorization algebra to the quantum group factorization algebra.

The BV formalism provides a concrete model for the Hochschild cohomology. For a factorization algebra arising from a BV theory, the Hochschild cohomology is the cohomology of the BV complex of local functionals with the quantum BV differential Q + ℏΔ + {S,−}. The En+1-structure comes from the higher derived brackets of the BV bracket. This identifies the deformation theory of the factorization algebra with the deformation theory of the quantum BV action. In particular, the quantum master equation is the Maurer–Cartan equation in the L∞-algebra controlling the deformations, and the anomaly is the obstruction class in HH2.

A powerful application is the classification of topological defects in a TQFT. A topological defect of codimension k is a module over the factorization algebra on a k-dimensional sphere. The space of such defects is the factorization homology of the factorization algebra on Sk, which is the k-fold Hochschild homology. The Hochschild cohomology HH•(F) acts on this space by the cap product, giving the operator product of defects. For the 2-dimensional TQFT associated to a modular tensor category, the Hochschild cohomology of the factorization algebra is the Drinfeld center of the category, which classifies the topological line defects. The E3-structure on the Hochschild cohomology corresponds to the braiding of line defects in 3 dimensions.

Factorization Algebras and the Local-to-Global Principle on the Ran Space

The Ran space of a manifold X, denoted Ran(X), is the topological space whose points are non‑empty finite subsets of X. A factorization algebra F on X is precisely a cosheaf on Ran(X) with respect to the natural topology generated by disjoint unions of open subsets. This equivalence, proved by Beilinson and Drinfeld and later refined by Costello–Gwilliam, turns the local-to-global principle into a statement about colimits: the value of F on an open set U is the colimit of its values on finite configurations of disjoint disks inside U. The factorization product maps F(D₁) ⊗ … ⊗ F(Dₙ) → F(D) for disjoint disks Dᵢ ⊂ D encode the operator product expansion in a purely algebraic way. Because Ran(X) is contractible for a connected X, the global sections Γ(Ran(X), F) compute the factorization homology ∫ₓ F, which is the natural target for topological field theories. The cosheaf condition guarantees that gluing of local data along overlaps is homotopically coherent, and the factorization structure ensures that the gluing respects the Eₙ‑operad action. In practice, one constructs a factorization algebra by specifying its values on small disks and the factorization maps, then uses the left Kan extension along the inclusion of the disk category into Ran(X) to obtain the full cosheaf. This procedure is the higher‑categorical analogue of sheafification and is essential for passing from perturbative BV data to a fully fledged factorization algebra.

The Role of the Factorization Envelope in the Construction of Vertex Algebras

Given a prefactorization algebra A on the complex plane, its factorization envelope Env(A) is the universal factorization algebra receiving a map from A. Concretely, Env(A) is obtained by freely adding factorization products for all configurations of disjoint disks and then imposing the associativity and symmetry constraints of the E₂‑operad. When A is the prefactorization algebra of a free field theory — for instance the βγ‑system or the Heisenberg algebra — Env(A) recovers the corresponding vertex algebra. The envelope construction makes precise the physics intuition that a vertex algebra is the “completion” of a local quantum field theory under operator product expansions. In the language of operads, Env(A) is the left adjoint to the forgetful functor from E₂‑algebras to prefactorization algebras; it can be computed as a bar‑cobar resolution involving the little disks operad D₂. The resulting vertex algebra carries a conformal vector if the original prefactorization algebra is equipped with a compatible dilation action. This framework explains why the chiral de Rham complex on a smooth variety, built as the factorization envelope of the free βγ‑system, automatically satisfies the vertex algebra axioms without any ad hoc normal‑ordering prescriptions.

Higher Genus Conformal Blocks from Factorization Homology

Factorization homology ∫_Σ F of a factorization algebra F on a surface Σ computes the space of conformal blocks of the associated chiral CFT on Σ. For a modular tensor category C, the factorization algebra F_C constructed from the Reshetikhin–Turaev TQFT satisfies ∫_Σ F_C ≅ Hom(1, ⊗p∈Σ V_p) where V_p are the objects labeling punctures. The genus‑g conformal blocks are obtained by evaluating ∫_Σ F_C on a closed surface of genus g. The mapping class group action on these blocks arises from the functoriality of factorization homology with respect to diffeomorphisms of Σ. In the holomorphic setting, the chiral algebra of a vertex operator algebra V gives a factorization algebra F_V on ℂ; its factorization homology on a compact Riemann surface Σ with marked points reproduces the sheaf of conformal blocks constructed by Beilinson–Drinfeld. The factorization product corresponds to sewing of surfaces, and the associativity constraint of the E₂‑algebra structure on F_V guarantees the sewing axioms. This perspective also clarifies the Verlinde formula: the dimension of ∫_Σ F_C is the trace of the identity on the modular functor, which factorization homology computes as the Hochschild homology of the E₂‑algebra F_C on the circle. The higher genus generalization of the Zhu algebra is then the factorization homology of F_V on a genus‑g surface with one puncture, providing a natural algebraic model for the space of genus‑g characters.

The Factorization Algebra of the Free Boson and its Deformation Quantization

The free boson on a Riemann surface is described by the factorization algebra Ffree that assigns to a disk D the completed symmetric algebra on the space of harmonic one‑forms on D with values in a lattice Λ. The factorization product is induced by the orthogonal decomposition of harmonic forms under disjoint union. The BV formalism for the free boson starts with the classical action S = ½ ∫_Σ dφ ∧ *dφ, where φ is a map Σ → Λ ⊗ ℝ. The Factorization Algebra of the Chiral de Rham Complex

The chiral de Rham complex, introduced by Malikov, Schechtman, and Vaintrob, is a sheaf of vertex algebras on any smooth complex manifold X. The construction uses the free βγ-system for each local coordinate and the free bc-system for each differential, together with the OPE relations that encode the de Rham differential. The resulting factorization algebra is a cosheaf on the Ran space of X with values in the category of vertex algebras.

The factorization product is determined by the operator product expansion of the βγ and bc fields. This map is compatible with the OPE associativity and the translation operator, making FcdR a factorization algebra in the sense of Costello–Gwilliam.

In factorization homology terms, the factorization homology of FcdR over a compact Riemann surface Σ computes the chiral de Rham cohomology of the mapping space Map(Σ, X). An important subtlety is that the chiral de Rham complex is not a factorization algebra on the whole Ran space unless one works with the completed tensor product of vertex algebras. The resulting completed factorization algebra is an object in the symmetric monoidal ∞‑category of factorization algebras on the Ran space, and its factorization envelope recovers the original vertex algebra sheaf. Factorization Algebras and the BV‑BFV Formalism on Manifolds with Boundary

The Batalin–Vilkovisky (BV) formalism extends naturally to manifolds with boundary via the BV‑BFV (Batalin–Fradkin–Vilkovisky) construction of Cattaneo, Mnev, and Reshetikhin (2014). In this setting, a classical BV theory on a manifold M with boundary ∂M consists of a BV action S on the space of fields F_M and a BFV action S^∂ on the space of boundary fields F_∂M, together with a surjective map π: F_M → F_∂M that is a morphism of BV‑BFV manifolds. The quantum theory is obtained by quantizing the bulk BV theory with a heat‑kernel regularization that respects the boundary conditions. The resulting factorization algebra F on M assigns to an open set U ⊂ M the cochain complex of observables supported in U, with the factorization product given by the BV pushforward along the diagonal. For a boundary open set V ⊂ ∂M, the value F(V) carries a natural module structure over the factorization algebra F^∂ on ∂M obtained by restricting the bulk theory to the boundary. The boundary factorization algebra F^∂ is itself a factorization algebra on the (n−1)‑dimensional manifold ∂M, and its category of modules classifies admissible boundary conditions. The quantum master equation in the bulk, Δ eS/ħ = 0, together with the boundary quantum master equation (Δ_∂ + {S^∂, −}) eS^∂/ħ = 0, ensures that the bulk‑boundary system is anomaly‑free. The central charge of a two‑dimensional theory appears as the obstruction to extending the bulk BV Laplacian to a boundary‑compatible operator; it is computed as the trace anomaly of the BV Laplacian on the space of boundary observables. In the factorization algebra language, the bulk‑boundary correspondence is the statement that the factorization algebra on M is a left module over the factorization algebra on ∂M in the Morita (∞,2)‑category of E_n‑algebras.

Factorization Algebras and the Factorization Homology of 3‑Manifolds and Reshetikhin–Turaev Invariants

Factorization homology ∫_M A of a 3‑manifold M with coefficients in an E₂‑algebra A (or more generally an E₃‑algebra) provides a rigorous mathematical construction of the Reshetikhin–Turaev topological quantum field theory. When A is the E₂‑algebra associated to a modular tensor category C (via the Drinfeld center construction), the factorization homology of a closed 3‑manifold M computes the Reshetikhin–Turaev invariant RT_C(M). The proof, due to Ben‑Zvi, Francis, and Nadler (2010) and later extended by Ayala, Francis, and Tanaka, proceeds by showing that the factorization homology of M is equivalent to the state‑sum invariant obtained from a triangulation of M labeled by objects of C. The key technical tool is the excision property of factorization homology: for a decomposition M = M₁ ∪_N M₂ along a surface N, there is a canonical equivalence ∫_M A ≃ ∫M₁ A ⊗∫_N A ∫M₂ A, where the tensor product is taken in the Morita (∞,2)‑category of E₂‑algebras. The mapping class group of N acts on ∫_N A, and this action coincides with the modular functor action on the space of conformal blocks. For a rational vertex algebra V, the associated modular tensor category is Rep(V), and the factorization homology of a genus‑g surface Σ_g with coefficients in the factorization envelope of V yields the space of conformal blocks of V on Σ_g. The non‑degeneracy of the pairing on conformal blocks follows from the Calabi–Yau property of the factorization envelope in the Morita category. The central charge of V appears as the framing anomaly of the 3‑dimensional TQFT; it shifts the invariant by a factor exp(2πi c/24 σ(M)), where σ(M) is the signature of a 4‑manifold bounding M.

The higher categorical center Z(k)(A) of an E_n‑algebra A in a symmetric monoidal ∞‑category C is defined as the factorization homology of the k‑sphere S^k with coefficients in A, regarded as an En−k‑algebra via the natural action of the little (n−k)‑disks operad on the sphere. Factorization Algebras on the Moduli Space of Curves and the Geometry of Chiral Homology

The moduli stack Mg,n of stable n‑pointed curves of genus g carries a natural factorization algebra structure when one considers the chiral homology of a vertex algebra V. For each open subset U ⊂ Mg,n the factorization algebra assigns the space of conformal blocks of V on the universal curve over U. The factorization product corresponds to gluing curves at marked points, which on the level of conformal blocks is implemented by the sewing isomorphism of the vertex algebra. Costello and Gwilliam proved that the resulting factorization algebra is locally constant with respect to the natural topology on the moduli stack, and its global sections recover the chiral homology of V on the curve. The key technical input is the identification of the Ran space of a curve with the moduli of effective divisors, which allows one to transport the factorization algebra from the Ran space to the moduli stack via the Abel–Jacobi map. This construction yields a rigorous mathematical formulation of the Verlinde formula: the dimension of the space of conformal blocks on a genus‑g surface equals the trace of the modular transformation on the vacuum module, and the factorization algebra encodes the modular functor as a symmetric monoidal functor from the category of decorated surfaces to vector spaces.

Sewing and the Factorization Product

Given two stable curves C₁, C₂ with marked points p₁, p₂, the factorization product on the associated factorization algebra is defined by the composition
H⁰(C₁, V) ⊗ H⁰(C₂, V) → H⁰(C₁ ∪p₁∼p₂ C₂, V),
where the map is induced by the operator product expansion of V at the identified points. The associativity of this product follows from the associativity of the OPE and the fact that the gluing of three curves can be performed in two equivalent orders. The unit is provided by the conformal block on the three‑punctured sphere, which is canonically isomorphic to the ground field.

Local Constancy and the Mapping Class Group Action

The factorization algebra on Mg,n is locally constant because the conformal blocks vary holomorphically with the complex structure of the curve. The monodromy of this local system is precisely the action of the mapping class group Γg,n on conformal blocks. In the language of factorization algebras, this monodromy is encoded by the fact that the factorization algebra is a module over the little disks operad, and the mapping class group acts by operadic automorphisms. The resulting projective representation of Γg,n coincides with the modular functor constructed by Tsuchiya, Ueno, and Yamada, and later by Bakalov and Kirillov.

Factorization Algebras and the Algebraic Structure of Chiral Differential Operators

Chiral differential operators (CDOs) on a smooth algebraic variety X form a sheaf of vertex algebras that quantizes the classical algebra of differential operators. In the factorization algebra framework, a CDO is a factorization algebra on the Ran space Ran(X) whose value on a finite set S ⊂ X is the completed tensor product of the CDO stalks at the points of S. The factorization product corresponds to the operator product expansion of the chiral fields, and the locality axiom of the vertex algebra translates into the factorization axiom on disjoint subsets. Malikov, Schechtman, and Vaintrob showed that the obstruction to the existence of a CDO on X is a class in H²(X, Ω2,cl), and when this class vanishes the set of isomorphism classes of CDOs is a torsor over H¹(X, Ω2,cl). In the factorization language, this obstruction appears as the failure of the factorization algebra to be strictly unital; the anomaly is measured by the central charge of the associated Virasoro element.

Relation to the Chiral de Rham Complex

The chiral de Rham complex ΩchX is a canonical CDO on any smooth X, constructed by taking the semi‑infinite cohomology of the βγ‑system. As a factorization algebra, ΩchX assigns to a finite set S the tensor product of the chiral de Rham complexes at the points of S, equipped with the natural factorization product coming from the OPE of the βγ‑fields. The global sections of ΩchX on a curve compute the chiral homology of the βγ‑system, which is known to be isomorphic to the cohomology of the loop space of X. This identification provides a geometric realization of the elliptic genus of X as the super‑trace of the Virasoro operator on the chiral homology.

Twisted CDOs and the Factorization Envelope

When the obstruction class does not vanish, one can still construct a twisted CDO by choosing a gerbe with connection whose curvature represents the obstruction. In the factorization algebra picture, this corresponds to a factorization algebra with a non‑trivial central extension of the little disks operad. The factorization envelope of a vertex algebra V, introduced by Beilinson and Drinfeld, is the universal factorization algebra generated by V; for a CDO it coincides with the sheaf of chiral differential operators itself. The envelope construction is functorial and respects the factorization product, making it a powerful tool for comparing different CDOs on the same variety.

Factorization Algebras and the Non‑Perturbative Conformal Bootstrap

The conformal bootstrap program seeks to classify all consistent conformal field theories by imposing crossing symmetry and unitarity on the operator product expansion. In the factorization algebra language, a CFT is a factorization algebra on the category of Riemann surfaces with boundaries, and the bootstrap equations are precisely the associativity and commutativity constraints of the factorization product. The non‑perturbative bootstrap can be formulated as the problem of finding all E₂‑algebras in the category of Hilbert spaces that satisfy the modular invariance condition on the torus. Recent work by Gaiotto, Mazac, and others shows that the space of such E₂‑algebras is discrete and can be explored numerically by truncating the operator spectrum. The factorization algebra perspective clarifies why the bootstrap equations are sufficient: the factorization homology of a genus‑g surface with coefficients in the E₂‑algebra computes the space of conformal blocks, and modular invariance is exactly the statement that the mapping class group acts projectively on this space. The central charge appears as the anomaly of the factorization algebra under the framing of the surface, and the Verlinde formula emerges from the factorization product on the pair of pants.

Crossing Symmetry as Factorization Associativity

Consider four punctured spheres with operator insertions a, b, c, d. The two ways of factorizing the sphere into three‑punctured spheres correspond to the s‑channel and t‑channel conformal blocks. The equality of the resulting correlation functions is the crossing equation. In the factorization algebra, this equality is the associativity isomorphism
(Fa,b ⊗ id) ∘ α = (id ⊗ Fc,d) ∘ α⁻¹,
where F denotes the factorization product and α the associator of the E₂‑algebra. The pentagon identity for α guarantees that all higher crossing equations are automatically satisfied once the basic four‑point crossing holds.

Modular Invariance and the Torus Partition Function

The torus partition function Z(τ) = TrH(qL₀−c/24 q̄L̄₀−c/24) is the factorization homology of the torus with coefficients in the factorization algebra. The action of SL(2,ℤ) on the modular parameter τ is induced by the mapping class group action on the torus, which in the factorization algebra is encoded by the E₂‑structure. Modular invariance Z(γ·τ) = Z(τ) for all γ ∈ SL(2,ℤ) is therefore a direct consequence of the fact that the factorization algebra defines a modular functor. The discreteness of the spectrum of scaling dimensions follows from the compactness of the moduli space of E₂‑algebras with fixed central charge, a result proved using the factorization algebra formalism by Carpi, Kawahigashi, and Longo.

Factorization Algebras and the Categorical Trace in Higher Morita Categories

The categorical trace of an Eₙ‑algebra A in a symmetric monoidal (∞,n)‑category C is defined as the factorization homology of the n‑sphere Sⁿ with coefficients in A, regarded as an object of C via the natural action of the little n‑disks operad on Sⁿ. For n = 2 this trace recovers the Hochschild homology of A as an E₂‑algebra, which is known to be equivalent to the Drinfeld center of the braided tensor category of A‑modules. In the higher Morita category Morn(C) whose objects are Eₙ‑algebras, 1‑morphisms are bimodules, and higher morphisms are intertwiners, the categorical trace provides a symmetric monoidal functor Tr: Morn(C) → C. This functor is the universal recipient of a cyclotomic structure on A, and it satisfies a higher version of the Hattori–Stallings trace formula: for any A‑bimodule M, the trace of M is equivalent to the factorization homology of the circle with coefficients in M. The trace functor plays a central role in the classification of topological defects in factorization homology theories: a k‑dimensional defect is precisely a module over the (n−k)‑fold categorical trace of A.

Trace and the Cobordism Hypothesis

The cobordism hypothesis states that the (∞,n)‑category of fully extended topological field theories with values in C is equivalent to the (∞,n)‑category of fully dualizable objects of C. An Eₙ‑algebra A determines a fully extended TFT ZA whose value on a closed n‑manifold M is the factorization homology ∫M A. The categorical trace of A is exactly the value of ZA on the n‑sphere, and the higher traces ZA(Sk) for k < n give the higher centers Z(k)(A). This identification shows that the categorical trace is not merely an algebraic invariant but the physical partition function of the theory on the sphere, and the higher centers are the partition functions on lower‑dimensional spheres.

Applications to Gapped Boundaries of 3‑D Topological Orders

For a modular tensor category C, the associated E₂‑algebra A = Z(C) (the Drinfeld center) defines a 3‑dimensional TFT. The categorical trace of A is the ground field, reflecting the fact that the theory has no local operators on the sphere. The first higher center Z(1)(A) is equivalent to C itself, and its module category classifies gapped boundary conditions of the 3‑D topological order. The second higher center Z(2)(A) is the ground field again, corresponding to the absence of point‑like defects in the bulk. This hierarchy of centers, obtained entirely from factorization homology of spheres, provides a complete algebraic classification of all topological defects in the theory.

Factorization Algebras and the Formalism of Factorization Modules over the Ran Space

A factorization module over a factorization algebra F on a space X is a sheaf M on the Ran space Ran(X) equipped with a factorization product M(U) ⊗ F(V) → M(U ∪ V) for disjoint finite subsets U, V ⊂ X, satisfying associativity and unit axioms. This notion generalizes the concept of a module over an algebra to the factorization setting and is essential for describing boundary conditions and defects in factorization homology. Beilinson and Drinfeld introduced factorization modules to formulate the geometric Langlands correspondence: a Hecke eigensheaf on the moduli stack of G‑bundles is a factorization module over the factorization algebra of chiral differential operators on the curve. The category of factorization modules over F carries a natural monoidal structure given by the factorization product, and when F is the factorization envelope of a vertex algebra V, this monoidal category is equivalent to the category of V‑modules with the fusion product.

Construction via the Ran Space

The Ran space Ran(X) is the colimit of the symmetric powers X(n) with the natural maps induced by adding points. A factorization algebra F on X is a cosheaf on Ran(X) with a factorization isomorphism F(U ⊔ V) ≃ F(U) ⊗ F(V). A factorization module M is a cosheaf on Ran(X) with a compatible action F(U) ⊗ M(V) → M(U ⊔ V). The factorization product on M is required to be associative with respect to the factorization product on F, and the unit of F acts as the identity on M. This definition makes sense in any symmetric monoidal ∞‑category, allowing one to consider factorization modules in chain complexes, spectra, or higher categories.

Relation to the Local‑to‑Global Principle

The local‑to‑global principle for factorization algebras states that the global sections of a factorization algebra on X can be computed as the factorization homology of X with coefficients in the algebra. For a factorization module M, the analogous statement is that the global sections of M on X are equivalent to the factorization homology of X with coefficients in M, where M is regarded as a module over the factorization algebra F. This principle is the key technical tool in the proof of the geometric Langlands conjecture for GL(n) by Gaitsgory and Lurie: they show that the category of factorization modules over the chiral differential operators on a curve is equivalent to the category of D‑modules on the moduli stack of local systems, and the equivalence respects the factorization structures on both sides.

In a perturbative quantum field theory, a boundary condition corresponds to a factorization module over the factorization algebra of bulk observables. The factorization product encodes the operator product expansion of bulk fields with boundary fields. Similarly, a topological defect of codimension k is a factorization module over the k‑fold categorical trace of the bulk factorization algebra. For the free boson, the factorization algebra is the chiral de Rham complex on the formal disk, and its factorization modules are classified by the modules over the Heisenberg vertex algebra, which are parametrized by the momentum lattice. This classification matches the known boundary conditions of the free boson CFT.

Factorization Algebras and the Higher Enveloping Algebras of Lie Algebroids

The higher enveloping algebra of a Lie algebroid provides a systematic way to produce an En-algebra in chain complexes from geometric data. Given a Lie algebroid A → M equipped with a compatible Pn-algebra structure on its Chevalley–Eilenberg complex, the construction of Calaque, Grivaux, and collaborators yields an En-algebra whose underlying complex is the symmetric algebra on the shifted sections of A. The factorization algebra associated to this En-algebra on ℝn is locally constant and its factorization homology over a framed n-manifold X computes the derived global sections of the corresponding sheaf of Pn-algebras on X. In particular, when A is the tangent Lie algebroid of a Poisson manifold, the higher enveloping algebra recovers the deformation quantization algebra of Kontsevich, and the factorization algebra encodes the perturbative Poisson sigma model. The construction is functorial with respect to Lie algebroid morphisms and respects the En-operadic composition, making it a powerful bridge between derived geometry and factorization homology.

Technically, the higher enveloping algebra Un(A) is defined as the left Kan extension of the inclusion of the category of Pn-algebras into En-algebras along the forgetful functor. This universal property implies that any Pn-algebra map from the Chevalley–Eilenberg complex of A to an En-algebra B factors uniquely through Un(A). The factorization algebra FA on ℝn is then obtained by applying the factorization homology functor to the constant En-algebra Un(A). The local constancy of FA follows from the fact that Un(A) is an En-algebra in the ∞-category of chain complexes, and the factorization homology of a constant En-algebra on a framed manifold is invariant under diffeomorphisms preserving the framing.

Applications include the computation of the factorization homology of the Poisson sigma model on surfaces, where the higher enveloping algebra of the cotangent Lie algebroid of a Poisson manifold reproduces the BV-BFV complex of Cattaneo–Felder. Moreover, the higher enveloping algebra construction provides a conceptual explanation for the appearance of shifted symplectic structures in derived symplectic geometry: the Pn-algebra structure on the Chevalley–Eilenberg complex corresponds to a n-shifted Poisson structure, and its higher enveloping algebra is the corresponding n-shifted symplectic algebra. This perspective has been used by Safronov and others to relate factorization algebras to the derived moduli stack of local systems.

Factorization Algebras and the Quantization of AKSZ Sigma Models

The AKSZ formalism, introduced by Alexandrov, Kontsevich, Schwarz, and Zaboronsky in 1997, constructs topological sigma models from a source manifold Σ of dimension n and a target Q-manifold equipped with a Hamiltonian function of degree n+1. Costello and Gwilliam showed that the perturbative quantization of an AKSZ theory in the BV framework yields a factorization algebra on the source manifold Σ. The key observation is that the space of fields of the AKSZ theory is a mapping space Map(T[1]Σ, X), where X is the target Q-manifold. The BV action is the transgression of the Hamiltonian function, and the BV Laplacian is defined using a choice of a volume form on Σ. The renormalization of the theory is performed using the heat kernel on Σ, which provides a regularized propagator compatible with the factorization algebra structure.

In the factorization algebra language, the classical observables form a prefactorization algebra whose values on open subsets U ⊂ Σ are the functions on the derived critical locus of the action restricted to U. The quantum master equation is equivalent to the statement that the renormalized BV operator squares to zero, which in turn is equivalent to the associativity of the factorization product. The resulting factorization algebra is locally constant when the target Q-manifold is symplectic and the Hamiltonian is non-degenerate; this includes the Poisson sigma model (where X = T*[1]M for a Poisson manifold M) and the Chern–Simons theory (where X = 𝔤[1] for a Lie algebra 𝔤). For the Poisson sigma model, the factorization algebra on a surface is equivalent to the factorization algebra obtained from the higher enveloping algebra of the cotangent Lie algebroid, providing a rigorous derivation of the Cattaneo–Felder star product.

The factorization algebra perspective also clarifies the role of boundary conditions in AKSZ theories. A boundary condition corresponds to a Lagrangian Q-submanifold of the target, and the induced factorization module over the bulk factorization algebra encodes the boundary operator product expansions. This matches the BV-BFV formalism of Cattaneo–Mnev–Reshetikhin, where the boundary theory is a BFV theory whose observables form a module over the bulk BV algebra. The factorization algebra framework thus unifies the bulk and boundary quantization and makes the gluing of manifolds with boundary manifest through the factorization product.

Factorization Algebras and the Formality of the Little Disks Operad

The formality of the little n-disks operad Dn is the statement that the operad of chains C•(Dn) is quasi‑isomorphic to its homology operad H•(Dn), which is the Pn-operad (the operad governing n-shifted Poisson algebras). Kontsevich proved formality for n = 2 in 1999 using configuration space integrals, and Tamarkin gave an independent proof in 2003 using the Deligne conjecture and the formality of the operad of little intervals. For general n, formality was established by Fresse and Willwacher (2015) using the theory of operadic twisting and the formality of the Fulton–MacPherson compactification. In the factorization algebra framework, formality implies that any En-algebra in chain complexes can be strictified to a Pn-algebra up to quasi‑isomorphism, and conversely any Pn-algebra can be lifted to an En-algebra. This equivalence is crucial for the construction of factorization algebras from classical field theories: the classical observables form a Pn-algebra (the derived critical locus carries a shifted Poisson structure), and formality guarantees the existence of a quantization to an En-algebra, i.e. a factorization algebra.

Concretely, the formality quasi‑isomorphism C•(Dn) → H•(Dn) is constructed by integrating propagators over the configuration spaces of points in ℝn. These integrals are exactly the Feynman diagrams of the perturbative Chern–Simons theory in dimension n = 3, and more generally of the AKSZ theories with target a shifted symplectic manifold. The factorization algebra of the free boson on ℝn is the constant factorization algebra associated to the En-algebra C•(Dn) acting on the symmetric algebra of a vector space. Formality then implies that this factorization algebra is equivalent to the one obtained from the Pn-algebra structure, which is the classical limit. This equivalence underlies the statement that the perturbative quantization of a free theory is trivial, while for interacting theories the formality map encodes the higher homotopies that correct the classical product to a quantum product.

In the context of the Costello–Gwilliam renormalization group flow, formality of Dn ensures that the space of En-algebras is formal as a moduli problem, so the RG flow on the space of theories can be studied using the simpler Pn-algebra models. This has been exploited by Costello and Gwilliam to prove that the RG flow preserves the class of locally constant factorization algebras and to construct the effective BV theory at each scale. Factorization Algebras and Extended Topological Field Theories

An extended topological field theory (TFT) in the sense of Baez–Dolan and Lurie is a symmetric monoidal functor Z : Bordn → C from the (∞,n)-category of n-dimensional cobordisms to a symmetric monoidal (∞,n)-category C. The cobordism hypothesis states that such functors are classified by fully dualizable objects of C. Factorization algebras provide a rich source of extended TFTs when C is the (∞,n)-category of Ek-algebras in a symmetric monoidal ∞-category V (for k ≤ n). Specifically, a locally constant factorization algebra F on ℝk with values in V determines an Ek-algebra A = F(ℝk). The factorization homology functor ∫− F then defines a symmetric monoidal functor from the (∞,k)-category of framed k-manifolds to V. By the cobordism hypothesis, this functor extends uniquely to an n-dimensional extended TFT if A is fully dualizable as an Ek-algebra in V.

The classification of fully dualizable Ek-algebras is known in several cases. For k = 1, an E1-algebra (i.e. an associative algebra) is fully dualizable in the Morita 2‑category of algebras, bimodules, and intertwiners iff it is separable (i.e. finite‑dimensional and semisimple over a field of characteristic zero). For k = 2, an E2-algebra (i.e. a braided monoidal category) is fully dualizable in the Morita 3‑category iff it is a non‑degenerate braided fusion category. For k = 3, the classification involves braided monoidal 2‑categories with additional non‑degeneracy conditions. In the factorization algebra setting, the Ek-algebra A is often the algebra of observables of a topological or holomorphic field theory. For example, the factorization algebra of the chiral de Rham complex on a Calabi–Yau manifold yields an E2-algebra that is expected to be fully dualizable, giving rise to a 3‑dimensional extended TFT related to the B‑model. Similarly, the factorization algebra of the free boson on ℝ2 gives an E2-algebra that is the enveloping algebra of the Heisenberg vertex algebra; its full dualizability is equivalent to the rationality of the corresponding vertex operator algebra.

The extended TFT perspective also clarifies the role of defects and boundaries. A codimension‑j defect in an n-dimensional extended TFT corresponds to a j-morphism in the target (∞,n)-category. In the factorization algebra language, a defect of codimension j is a factorization module over the j-fold categorical trace of the bulk Ek-algebra. The categorical trace of an Ek-algebra is the factorization homology of the k-sphere, which for k = 1 is Hochschild homology and for k = 2 is the Drinfeld center. This matches the physical expectation that a line defect in a 3‑d TFT is labeled by an object of the Drinfeld center of the bulk braided category. The factorization algebra framework thus provides a concrete algebraic model for the higher categorical structures appearing in extended TFTs.

Factorization Algebras and the Classification of Fully Dualizable Objects in Higher Morita Categories

The higher Morita category Morn(V) of En-algebras in a symmetric monoidal ∞-category V has objects En-algebras, 1‑morphisms bimodules, 2‑morphisms bimodule maps, up to n-morphisms. Factorization Algebras and the Derived Category of D‑modules on the Ran Space

The Ran space Ran(X) of a smooth curve X parametrizes finite non‑empty subsets of X. Costello and Gwilliam show that a factorization algebra on X is equivalent to a commutative algebra object in the symmetric monoidal ∞‑category of D‑modules on Ran(X) equipped with the factorization product. The derived category D(Ran(X)) carries a natural t‑structure whose heart consists of constructible sheaves; the factorization product is defined by the push‑forward along the union map Ran(X) × Ran(X) → Ran(X) and the !‑pullback along the diagonal. This construction yields a fully faithful embedding of the ∞‑category of factorization algebras into the ∞‑category of commutative algebras in D(Ran(X)). The derived nature is essential: the classical (underived) category of D‑modules does not capture the higher homotopies required by the factorization associativity constraints. In particular, the factorization envelope of a Lie* algebra is obtained by taking the universal enveloping algebra in this derived setting, which automatically incorporates the higher brackets coming from the operadic structure of the little disks operad. The equivalence also explains why the Ran space appears naturally in the geometric Langlands program: the Hecke eigensheaf condition translates into a factorization property of the corresponding D‑module on Ran(X).

Technically, one works with the ind‑completion of the category of constructible D‑modules to allow infinite colimits needed for the factorization product. The factorization product is compatible with Verdier duality, and the resulting commutative algebra objects are automatically equipped with a compatible co‑unit, making them factorization cosheaves. The derived category perspective also clarifies the relationship with chiral algebras: a chiral algebra on X is precisely a factorization algebra whose underlying D‑module on Ran(X) is supported on the diagonal, i.e. it satisfies the locality condition that the factorization product is given by the usual operator product expansion. This viewpoint has been used by Beilinson and Drinfeld to construct the chiral algebra of the WZW model at critical level as a factorization algebra on the Ran space of the curve.

From a computational standpoint, the derived category of D‑modules on Ran(X) can be approximated by a cosimplicial diagram of D‑modules on configuration spaces Conf_n(X). The factorization product then corresponds to the simplicial structure maps, and the homotopy limit of this diagram recovers the factorization algebra. This description is particularly useful for explicit calculations in perturbative quantum field theory, where the factorization algebra of a free field theory is built from the propagator as a distribution on Conf2(X) and extended to higher configuration spaces by the factorization product.

Factorization Algebras and the Higher Categorical Structure of Topological Defects in Two‑Dimensional Conformal Field Theory

In a two‑dimensional conformal field theory, topological defects of codimension one are described by module categories over the vertex operator algebra (VOA) of the bulk theory. The factorization algebra framework lifts this statement to the (∞,2)‑category of E₂‑algebras: a bulk factorization algebra F on a surface Σ determines an E₂‑algebra A = ∫_Σ F, and a topological line defect corresponds to an (A,A)‑bimodule in the Morita (∞,2)‑category of E₂‑algebras. The mapping class group of Σ acts on the factorization homology ∫_Σ F, and this action factors through the action on the higher Morita category. For a rational VOA, the category of modules is a modular tensor category, and the factorization homology on a genus‑g surface with n marked points computes the space of conformal blocks, which carries a projective representation of the mapping class group. The factorization algebra perspective shows that this representation arises from the functoriality of factorization homology with respect to diffeomorphisms of Σ, and the projective anomaly is captured by the central charge of the VOA, which appears as the curvature of the determinant line bundle on the moduli space of curves.

Recent work by Johnson‑Freyd and others extends this structure to non‑topological (conformal) defects by equipping the factorization algebra with a Virasoro action. The Virasoro algebra acts on the factorization algebra via the stress‑energy tensor, which is a local operator of conformal weight two. In the factorization algebra language, the stress‑energy tensor corresponds to a degree‑zero element in the Hochschild cohomology of the E₂‑algebra A, and its Ward identities are encoded by the BV master equation for the factorization algebra. This provides a uniform description of both topological and conformal defects within a single higher categorical framework.

Factorization Algebras and the BV Formalism for the Rozansky–Witten Model

The Rozansky–Witten model is a three‑dimensional topological sigma model whose target is a hyperkähler manifold X. Its classical observables form a factorization algebra on the source three‑manifold M, which can be constructed via the BV formalism. The fields are maps φ : M → X together with odd‑degree antifields and ghosts encoding the gauge symmetry of the model. The BV action is the integral over M of the pullback of the holomorphic symplectic form on X, written in terms of the superfields. The BV differential squares to zero because the target is hyperkähler, which implies the holomorphic symplectic form is closed and the Schouten bracket of the corresponding Poisson bivector vanishes.

Quantization proceeds by choosing a BV‑exact gauge fixing fermion, which leads to a heat‑kernel regularization of the propagator on M. The renormalized BV operator Δ acts on the space of local functionals, and the quantum master equation Δ(eS/ħ) = 0 is satisfied order by order in ħ because the Rozansky–Witten model is one‑loop exact: all higher loop diagrams vanish due to the topological nature of the theory and the supersymmetry Ward identities. The resulting factorization algebra is locally constant on M and its factorization homology computes the Rozansky–Witten invariants of M, which are known to coincide with the cohomology of the Hilbert scheme of points on X for certain M.

The factorization algebra of the Rozansky–Witten model carries an action of the little 3‑disks operad, making it an E₃‑algebra. By the cobordism hypothesis, this E₃‑algebra determines a fully extended 3‑dimensional TQFT. The boundary conditions of this TQFT correspond to modules over the E₃‑algebra, which are identified with categories of coherent sheaves on X equipped with a suitable holomorphic symplectic structure. This provides a physical realization of the derived geometric Satake correspondence for hyperkähler targets.

This identification has several consequences. Second, the HKR (Hochschild–Kostant–Rosenberg) isomorphism for smooth algebras generalizes to an equivalence between higher Hochschild homology and the de Rham complex of the mapping space Map(S^n, Spec A) in derived algebraic geometry.
