Factorization Algebras — Cosheaves, Vertex Algebras & the Batalin-Vilkovisky Formalism

Deep dive into Factorization Algebras — the Costello-Gwilliam approach to perturbative quantum field theory that formalizes the observables of a QFT as a factorization algebra (a cosheaf with multilocal structure) on the underlying space-time manifold, the prefactorization category and the configuration space of disjoint disks (the little disks operad), the factorization algebra of classical observables (the commutative algebra of functions on the space of fields with the Poisson bracket from the BV formalism), the Batalin-Vilkovisky (BV) formalism (the antibracket, the classical master equation {S, S} = 0, and the quantum master equation ħΔS + ½{S, S} = 0), the relationship between holomorphic factorization algebras on Riemann surfaces and vertex algebras (the operator product expansion and the state-field correspondence), the Chiral algebras of Beilinson-Drinfeld (the chiral homology and conformal blocks), the factorization envelope of a Lie algebra (the universal factorization algebra associated to a Lie algebra with an invariant pairing), the Noether theorem in the language of factorization algebras (conserved currents as central extensions), and applications to the geometric Langlands program and elliptic cohomology.

"A factorization algebra is a mathematical structure that encodes the observables of a quantum field theory — their multilocality and operator product expansions."
— Kevin Costello & Owen Gwilliam
Part 1

📏 Prefactorization Categories and the Little Disks Operad

Cosheaves
Prefactorization Algebras, Cosheaves, and Configuration Spaces of Disks
A prefactorization algebra F on a topological space M assigns to each open set U ⊂ M a vector space F(U) (the observables on U), and to each finite collection of disjoint open sets U_1, ..., U_n contained in an open set V a multiplication map m_{U_1, ..., U_n}^V: F(U_1) ⊗ ... ⊗ F(U_n) → F(V). These maps must be compatible with inclusions (if we restrict to smaller open sets, the structure maps intertwine) and be equivariant under the action of the symmetric group (the order of the disjoint disks does not matter). A factorization algebra is a prefactorization algebra that also satisfies a gluing condition (a cosheaf condition): if V is covered by disjoint opens U_i, and we have observables on each U_i that agree on overlaps, they glue uniquely to an observable on V. The little disks operad E_n (with configuration spaces of n-dimensional disks) acts on any factorization algebra on ℝ^n by the structure maps — this is the E_n operad action. A locally constant factorization algebra on ℝ^n is an E_n-algebra (an algebra over the E_n operad). The factorization homology ∫_M F of a factorization algebra F over a manifold M is the derived global sections — it computes the quantum observables of the theory on M. The factorization envelope 𝕌𝔤 of a Lie algebra 𝔤 is the E_n-algebra whose underlying vector space is Sym(𝔤[1−n]) with differential given by the Lie bracket.
Factorization algebras organize the observables of a QFT by their multilocality: the multiplication maps encode how observables on disjoint regions combine when they are brought together.
Part 2

🌍 The BV Formalism and Classical Observables

BV
The Batalin-Vilkovisky Formalism, the Classical Master Equation, and the Observable Algebra
The Batalin-Vilkovisky (BV) formalism is a cohomological approach to gauge theories. The space of fields is a graded manifold ℰ = ℰ₀ ⊕ ℰ₁[−1] (the fields and anti-fields). The BV action S is a degree-0 function satisfying the classical master equation (CME): {S, S} = 0, where {−, −} is the antibracket (a degree-1 Poisson bracket of cohomological type). The classical observables Obs^cl form a commutative differential graded algebra (the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex of the Lie algebra of vector fields on the space of fields). The construction of the classical factorization algebra: to each open set U, assign the space of functions on the space of fields on U, equipped with the differential {S, −} (the BV Laplacian). The classical master equation {S, S} = 0 ensures (d_S)² = 0. The Poisson bracket on observables of codimension 1 (the Poisson vertex algebra in 2 dimensions) arises from the antibracket. For a free field theory, Obs^cl(U) = Sym(ℰ_c^*(U)[−1]) with the differential given by the linearized equations of motion. The Noether theorem in BV language: a symmetry of the action gives a conserved current — a degree-1 observable J such that d_S J = 0, and the charge ∫_{Σ} J (integrated over a codimension-1 submanifold) is a conserved quantity. The classical factorization algebra of a gauge theory encodes the BRST cohomology of the local observables.
The BV formalism provides a systematic way to handle gauge symmetries: the classical master equation ensures gauge-invariance, and the observable algebra is the cohomology of the BV differential.
Part 3

🧮 Quantization and Vertex Algebras

Vertex
Holomorphic Factorization Algebras, Vertex Algebras, and the Operator Product Expansion
A holomorphic factorization algebra on a Riemann surface Σ is a factorization algebra whose structure maps are holomorphic in the positions of the disks. The key theorem (Costello-Gwilliam): a holomorphic factorization algebra on a Riemann surface that is translation-invariant and satisfies a certain locality axiom is equivalent to a vertex algebra. The state-field correspondence Y(a, z): V → V((z)) assigns to each state a ∈ V a field Y(a, z) that is an operator-valued formal power series. The operator product expansion (OPE) states that the product of two fields Y(a, z)Y(b, w) can be expanded as a sum of poles in (z−w) with coefficients given by fields — this is equivalent to the factorization algebra multiplication maps for concentric annuli. The translation operator T (the infinitesimal translation) generates the action of the Virasoro algebra when the vertex algebra is conformal. The quantum master equation (QME): for a quantum theory, the BV action satisfies ħΔS + ½{S, S} = 0, where Δ is the BV Laplacian (the second-order differential operator from the odd symplectic structure). The quantum observables Obs^q are the complex Sym(ℰ_c^*(U))[[ħ]] with differential d_S + ħΔ. The condition (d_S + ħΔ)² = 0 is equivalent to the QME. The renormalization of the factorization algebra involves the heat kernel and the renormalization group flow — the choice of a regulator (a scale) leads to a family of prefactorization algebras, and the limit as the regulator is removed defines the quantum factorization algebra.
Holomorphic factorization algebras are the geometric avatar of vertex algebras: the OPE is encoded in the factorization structure on a Riemann surface, and quantization is controlled by the quantum master equation.
Part 4

🔥 Chiral Algebras and Geometric Langlands

Langlands
Beilinson-Drinfeld Chiral Algebras, Conformal Blocks, and the Geometric Langlands Program
Chiral algebras (Beilinson-Drinfeld) are a geometric version of vertex algebras: a chiral algebra on a smooth algebraic curve X is a 𝒟-module A on the Ran space (the space of finite subsets of X) with a factorization structure. The Ran space Ran(X) is the set of all non-empty finite subsets of X — its topology captures the idea of points coalescing. The chiral homology H_*(X, A) is the factorization homology of the chiral algebra — it computes the space of conformal blocks for the vertex algebra when X is a Riemann surface. The KZ connection (Knizhnik-Zamolodchikov) is a flat connection on the space of conformal blocks associated to an affine Kac-Moody algebra at level k. The geometric Langlands correspondence (Beilinson-Drinfeld, Arinkin-Gaitsgory) relates 𝒟-modules on the moduli stack Bun_G of principal G-bundles on a curve X to the moduli space of local systems for the Langlands dual group ^LG. Factorization algebras appear: the Hecke eigensheaves are 𝒟-modules on Bun_G whose eigenvalues are given by ℒ-adic local systems — their construction uses the factorization structure of the affine Kac-Moody vertex algebra at critical level k = −h^∨. The Feigin-Frenkel theorem: the center of the critical level affine Kac-Moody algebra is isomorphic to the algebra of functions on the space of ^LG-opers — this is the quantum geometric Langlands correspondence for the critical level. The elliptic cohomology (the generalized cohomology theory associated to elliptic curves) is related to factorization algebras: the topological modular forms (TMF) can be constructed as the factorization homology of certain factorization algebras over the moduli space of elliptic curves.
Chiral algebras and their factorization homology provide the language for conformal field theory on curves — they are central to the geometric Langlands program, where the critical level affine algebra governs Hecke eigensheaves.
