Derived Algebraic Geometry — Derived Schemes, the Cotangent Complex & Töen-Vezzosi Theory

Derived Algebraic Geometry (DAG) is the natural synthesis of homotopy theory and algebraic geometry. It was pioneered by Lurie, Toën, and Vezzosi to address geometric problems where classical schemes fail — such as intersecting badly-behaved subspaces, moduli spaces of perfect complexes, and structures with non-transverse intersections. The central idea is to replace commutative rings with simplicial commutative rings (or E∞-ring spectra) and to replace schemes with functors from simplicial rings to simplicial sets satisfying descent. This yields the cotangent complex LX/Y (the derived version of Kähler differentials) which controls deformation theory, the virtual fundamental class for derived moduli spaces, and shifted symplectic structures — a higher-categorical generalization of symplectic geometry. The theory has deep applications in enumerative geometry (Donaldson-Thomas invariants), the geometric Langlands program, and p-adic Hodge theory via spectral algebraic geometry.

Part 1

📏 Simplicial Rings and Derived Schemes

Foundations
Simplicial Commutative Algebras, Derived Affine Schemes, and the Étale Topology
Let sAlgk denote the category of simplicial commutative algebras over a base ring k. A derived affine scheme is a functor Spec A: sAlgk → sSet given by Spec A(B) = MapsAlgk(A, B), where the mapping space is the derived mapping space (the geometric realization of the simplicial Hom). These form the ∞-category dAff of derived affine schemes. The derived étale topology is defined via the condition that a map A → B of simplicial commutative rings is étale if π0(A) → π0(B) is étale and the induced map on cotangent complexes is an equivalence. Descent for the étale topology is a homotopical version of Grothendieck descent. A derived scheme is a pair (X, OX) where X is a topological space and OX is a sheaf of simplicial commutative rings such that (X, π0OX) is an ordinary scheme and each πnOX is a quasi-coherent sheaf on (X, π0OX). The truncation functor t0: derived schemes → ordinary schemes is given by taking π0 of the structure sheaf. A derived scheme is locally of finite presentation if it is covered by derived affines Spec A with A a compact object in sAlgk. The category of derived schemes admits fiber products and the derived intersection of two subschemes Y, Z ⊂ X is given by Y ×hX Z — this captures the correct scheme-theoretic intersection when Y and Z meet non-transversely.
Derived schemes generalize ordinary schemes by incorporating homotopical information in the structure sheaf; the truncation recovers the classical scheme, while higher homotopy groups encode derived intersections and obstructions.
Part 2

🌍 The Cotangent Complex and Deformation Theory

Derived Differentials
The Cotangent Complex LX/Y, André-Quillen Homology, and Lurie's Smoothness Criterion
For a map of derived schemes f: X → Y, the cotangent complex LX/Y is the derived version of Kähler differentials ΩX/Y. It lives in the derived category D≤¹(X) and is a perfect complex when X is locally of finite presentation. For a surjection A → B of simplicial rings with kernel I, the relative cotangent complex fits into the fundamental exact triangle: I/I² → B ⊗A LA/k → LB/k → I/I²[1]. This is the André-Quillen homology of B over A. The cotangent complex controls deformation theory: for an infinitesimal extension of Y with square-zero ideal J, the obstructions to deforming X lie in Ext²(LX/Y, f*J), while deformations are classified by Ext¹(LX/Y, f*J). Lurie's theorem: a map of derived schemes f: X → Y is smooth (resp. étale) iff LX/Y is a locally free sheaf in degree 0 (resp. zero). The cotangent complex also satisfies base change: for a Cartesian square, LX×YY' / Y' ≅ p*LX/Y. Derived completion (the derived version of formal completion) uses the cotangent complex to produce a formal derived scheme from a derived scheme along a closed immersion. The Schlessinger's conditions in derived form ensure that the deformation functor is pro-representable by a derived Artin ring.
The cotangent complex is the universal derived linear approximation of a map of derived schemes; it encodes all deformation-theoretic information via its Ext groups.
Part 3

🧮 Derived Moduli Spaces and Virtual Fundamental Classes

Moduli
Derived Moduli of Perfect Complexes, Virtual Fundamental Classes, and Shifted Symplectic Structures
The moduli stack MP of perfect complexes on a scheme X classifies families of perfect complexes. Its derived enhancement ℝMP is a derived stack whose tangent complex at a point E is given by TEℝMP = ℝHom(E, E)[1]. The virtual dimension is rk(TM). The virtual fundamental class [MP]vir ∈ A*(MP, ℚ) is defined via the perfect obstruction theory given by TM = π*ℝℋom(E, E)[1]. This recovers the Behrend-Fantechi construction in derived language. The DT invariants count sheaves by virtual integration over the derived moduli of sheaves. The Pantev-Toën-Vezzosi-Benkhalifa shifted symplectic structures: a derived scheme X has a symplectic structure of degree k consisting of an oriented form ω ∈ Ω²(X) of degree k with a non-degenerate pairing TX ⊗ TX → OX[k] that is closed. When k = 0, this recovers an ordinary symplectic structure; when k = 1, it gives the derived critical locus of a function; when k = −1, it gives a Poisson structure. The Lagrangian correspondence in derived geometry returns the notion of a derived Lagrangian intersection, which carries a (−1)-shifted symplectic structure — mirroring the classical symplectic reduction. Borisov-Joyce developed the theory of d-manifolds and d-orbifolds as a differential-geometric approach to derived geometry, with applications to the virtual fundamental class and wall-crossing.
Derived moduli spaces carry natural perfect obstruction theories; shifted symplectic structures provide the geometric framework for understanding Lagrangians, critical loci, and Poisson geometry in derived settings.
Part 4

🔥 Spectral Algebraic Geometry and Applications

Spectral
E∞-Ring Spectra, Spectral Algebraic Geometry, Langlands, and BPS Invariants
Spectral algebraic geometry (Lurie) replaces simplicial commutative rings with E∞-ring spectra, yielding a richer and more flexible theory. The ∞-topos of sheaves on the étale site of a ring spectrum encodes p-adic Hodge theory: the Fargues-Fontaine curve is realized as a spectral scheme, giving a geometric home for p-adic Galois representations. The derived geometric Langlands program (Arinkin-Gaitsgory-Kazhdan-Raskin-Rozenblyum-Varshavsky) studies the moduli of G-bundles on a curve and the construction of Hecke eigensheaves via derived methods. The categorification of the geometric Satake equivalence uses derived algebraic geometry to upgrade the Satake isomorphism to an equivalence of ∞-categories of sheaves on the affine Grassmannian. The categorified Donaldson-Thomas theory (Joyce-Song) computes generalized DT invariants via integration over the derived moduli of sheaves, producing integer-valued invariants that count semistable objects in the derived category of coherent sheaves. The BPS invariants and wall-crossing formulas are expressed using potential functions on derived critical loci — these are the (0-shifted) symplectic structures arising from the derived moduli of sheaves on a Calabi-Yau 3-fold. The Kontsevich-Soibelman wall-crossing formula has a derived interpretation via the motivic Hall algebra of the derived category. The Betti geometric Langlands (Ben-Zvi-Nadler) uses derived algebraic geometry to relate D-modules on BunG to coherent sheaves on the derived stack of local systems. These results demonstrate that derived algebraic geometry is not merely a technical enhancement but a necessary framework for the deepest aspects of modern geometric representation theory and enumerative geometry.
Spectral algebraic geometry unifies arithmetic, geometric, and categorical structures; from the Fargues-Fontaine curve to geometric Langlands and DT theory, derived methods are indispensable for modern mathematics.

Higher Stacks and ∞‑Topoi in Derived Geometry

The passage from derived schemes to higher stacks is forced by the need to represent moduli functors not representable by derived schemes alone. An ∞‑topos is an ∞‑category of sheaves of spaces on a Grothendieck site satisfying descent for hypercovers. In derived algebraic geometry the étale site of a simplicial commutative ring (or an E∞‑ring spectrum) carries a structure sheaf valued in connective E∞‑ring spectra; the resulting ∞‑topos of sheaves of spaces is the ambient ∞‑category for derived stacks. Lurie’s representability theorem in Higher Algebra gives necessary and sufficient conditions for a functor F : CAlg^cn → Spaces to be a derived stack: (i) F preserves limits, (ii) F satisfies étale hyperdescent, and (iii) the relative cotangent complex LF/S is connective and of finite presentation. These conditions are the derived analogue of Artin’s criteria for algebraic stacks. The ∞‑topos formalism also supplies a robust geometric realization: any simplicial object has a colimit, used to construct quotient stacks such as [X/G] for a derived group scheme G acting on a derived scheme X. Hyperdescent guarantees the quotient is a derived stack, and its cotangent complex fits into a fiber sequence L[X/G]/S → LX/S → 𝔤^* ⊗ O_X[1] where 𝔤 is the Lie algebra of G. This sequence is the derived version of the exact sequence of Kähler differentials for a quotient by a smooth group action. The ∞‑topos perspective clarifies the relation to classical stacks: the truncation functor τ≤0 from the ∞‑topos of derived stacks to the ordinary 2‑topos of Artin stacks is left exact and preserves finite limits, so classical moduli problems embed fully faithfully. In practice one works with a model such as the Joyal–Jardine model structure on simplicial presheaves or the complete Segal space model; both give equivalent homotopy theories and allow concrete computation of mapping spaces via derived Hom complexes.

Derived Intersection Theory and Virtual Pullbacks

Classical intersection theory fails when cycles meet in the wrong dimension or with excess intersection. Derived algebraic geometry replaces the ordinary fiber product X ×_Z Y by the derived fiber product X ×^R_Z Y, the homotopy pullback in the ∞‑category of derived schemes. The structure sheaf of the derived fiber product is the derived tensor product O_X ⊗^LO_Z O_Y, whose homotopy groups are Tor_iO_Z(O_X, O_Y). When the maps are Tor‑independent (all higher Tor vanish), the derived fiber product coincides with the classical one and the intersection is regular. In the non‑Tor‑independent case the derived fiber product carries a perfect obstruction theory given by the relative cotangent complex LX×^R_Z Y / X×Y. The virtual fundamental class [X×^R_Z Y]vir is defined as the Gysin pullback of the fundamental class of the ambient derived scheme along the zero section of the obstruction theory. Concretely, if i : X → Z and j : Y → Z are regular embeddings of codimensions c and d, the virtual dimension of the derived intersection is dim X + dim Y − dim Z. The virtual class lives in the Chow group Avir(X×^R_Z Y) and satisfies the expected functorialities: proper pushforward, flat pullback, and the excess intersection formula becomes a statement about the Euler class of the excess normal bundle, now a perfect complex. The derived Gysin map i^! : A_*(Z) → A*-c(X) is constructed by pulling back to the derived fiber product X ×^R_Z Z ≃ X and then applying the virtual fundamental class. This formalism recovers the Fulton–MacPherson intersection product and extends it to arbitrary morphisms of derived schemes. In enumerative geometry, the virtual class of the derived moduli of stable maps to a target variety X is precisely the class used in Gromov–Witten theory; the derived language makes the construction of the obstruction theory canonical and functorial.

Worked Example: Virtual Dimension of a Derived Intersection
Let Z = A4 = Spec k[x,y,u,v]. Let X = V(x,y) and Y = V(x,u) be two planes in Z, both regularly embedded of codimension 2. Compute the virtual dimension of the derived intersection X ×^R_Z Y.
The classical intersection is W = X ∩ Y = V(x,y,u), a line (dimension 1). The expected dimension is dim X + dim Y − dim Z = 2+2−4 = 0, so the intersection is in excess by 1. The derived fiber product has structure sheaf O_X ⊗^LO_Z O_Y. Since both embeddings are regular, the Tor groups are the homology of the Koszul complex. One finds Tor0 = O_W and Tor1 = O_W (the excess normal bundle), with Tor_i = 0 for i ≥ 2. The obstruction theory is the perfect complex [O_W → O_W] concentrated in degrees 0 and −1, of virtual rank 0. The virtual dimension is the expected dimension 0. The virtual fundamental class is the Euler class of the obstruction theory, which equals the fundamental class of the line W pushed forward to the derived intersection.
Exam‑Day Strategy
When a question asks for a virtual dimension, first compute the expected dimension using classical codimensions, then check whether the maps are Tor‑independent. If they are not, identify the non‑vanishing Tor groups; each Tor_i in degree i contributes an obstruction space of rank equal to its length. The virtual dimension is the expected dimension minus the alternating sum of ranks of the obstruction spaces. Practice this on simple complete intersections and on the self‑intersection of a divisor with normal crossings.

Shifted Poisson Structures and Quantization

The Pantev–Toën–Vezzosi–Benkhalifa (PTVV) theory of shifted symplectic structures has a Poisson counterpart: a k‑shifted Poisson structure on a derived stack X is a map of Lie algebras Sym(T_X[−k]) → O_X satisfying the Leibniz rule, equivalently a bivector field π ∈ Γ(X, ∧2 T_X[k]) with [π,π]=0 in the shifted Schouten bracket. When k=0 this recovers ordinary Poisson structures; k=1 gives the derived critical locus of a function, where the Poisson bracket is the derived version of the canonical symplectic form on the cotangent bundle; k=−1 yields a shifted Poisson structure corresponding to a 0‑shifted symplectic structure after a degree shift. The quantization problem asks for a deformation of the structure sheaf O_X to a sheaf of Ek+1‑algebras (or E2‑algebras for k=0) whose classical limit recovers the given Poisson bracket. In derived geometry, the existence of a quantization is controlled by the cohomology of the shifted Poisson complex; the obstruction lies in H2(X, O_X) for k=0 and in higher cohomology groups for other shifts. For derived critical loci of a function f on a smooth scheme, the 1‑shifted symplectic structure is exact, and the quantization is given by the derived algebra of functions on the critical locus equipped with the star product coming from the Batalin–Vilkovisky formalism. In the context of the derived moduli of local systems on a curve, the (−1)‑shifted symplectic structure on the character stack yields a 0‑shifted Poisson structure after applying the derived loop space functor; its quantization is expected to produce the category of D‑modules on the moduli of bundles, realizing the geometric Langlands correspondence. The shifted Poisson formalism also underlies the AKSZ construction of topological field theories: a (−1)‑shifted symplectic derived stack gives a classical BV theory, and its quantization produces a factorization algebra on the source manifold.

Derived Deformation Theory of Algebras and Hochschild Cohomology

The derived deformation theory of an associative algebra A over a field k is governed by its Hochschild cochain complex C^*(A,A) equipped with the Gerstenhaber bracket. In the language of derived algebraic geometry, the moduli functor Def_A : CAlg^cn_k → Spaces sending a connective cdga R to the space of A_∞‑algebra structures on A ⊗^L_k R lifting the given structure on A is represented by a formal derived stack whose tangent complex at the base point is C^*(A,A)[1]. The Maurer–Cartan equation in the dg Lie algebra C^*(A,A)[1] encodes infinitesimal deformations, and the higher homotopies of the A_∞‑structure correspond to higher obstructions living in HHi+2(A,A). The derived Morita theorem (Toën) states that the ∞‑category of modules over a derived algebra A is equivalent to the ∞‑category of modules over any derived algebra B that is derived Morita equivalent to A; consequently, the Hochschild cohomology HH^*(A,A) is an invariant of the derived Morita equivalence class. This invariance is crucial for the derived categorical approach to mirror symmetry, where the Fukaya category of a symplectic manifold is expected to be derived Morita equivalent to the derived category of coherent sheaves on the mirror. The derived deformation functor of a dg category C is represented by the formal derived stack associated to the Hochschild cochain complex of C; its tangent complex is HH^*(C,C)[1]. The formality of the Hochschild cochain complex (Kontsevich’s formality theorem) implies that the deformation theory of a smooth algebraic variety is unobstructed and governed by polyvector fields. In the derived setting, the formality extends to derived stacks with a shifted symplectic structure, giving a canonical quantization of the shifted Poisson bracket via the Ek+1‑operad.

Applications to Mirror Symmetry and Fukaya Categories

Homological mirror symmetry (HMS) predicts an equivalence between the derived Fukaya category Fuk(M) of a Calabi–Yau symplectic manifold M and the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves D^b Coh(M^∨) on the mirror complex manifold M^∨. Derived algebraic geometry provides the natural framework to formulate and prove such equivalences. The Fukaya category is an A_∞‑category whose objects are Lagrangian submanifolds equipped with local systems; its derived version is obtained by taking the derived category of right modules over the A_∞‑category. On the B‑side, the derived category of coherent sheaves is the derived category of a derived scheme (or derived stack) that encodes the complex structure of M^∨. The closed‑open string map identifies the Hochschild cohomology of Fuk(M) with the quantum cohomology of M, while on the B‑side it identifies HH^*(D^b Coh(M^∨)) with the polyvector fields on M^∨. The derived deformation theory of both sides is controlled by the same shifted Poisson structure: the symplectic form on M gives a 0‑shifted symplectic structure on the derived moduli of objects in Fuk(M), and the holomorphic volume form on M^∨ gives a (−1)‑shifted symplectic structure on the derived moduli of objects in D^b Coh(M^∨). PTVV established the equivalence of these shifted symplectic structures for certain toric Calabi–Yau varieties. Moreover, the derived category of singularities of a Landau–Ginzburg model (the derived critical locus of a superpotential W) carries a 1‑shifted symplectic structure, and its quantization yields the B‑model topological string. The wall‑crossing formulas for Donaldson–Thomas invariants are interpreted as mutations in the derived Fukaya category, and the Kontsevich–Soibelman wall‑crossing formula becomes the statement that the product of quantum dilogarithms associated to spherical objects is invariant under derived equivalences. These results demonstrate that derived algebraic geometry is not merely a technical language but a crucial geometric framework for modern mirror symmetry.

Derived Critical Loci and (−1)-Shifted Symplectic Structures

Per Pantev–Toën–Vaquié–Vezzosi, the derived critical locus of a function f on a smooth derived scheme X — the derived fiber product X ×dCrit(f) X of the zero section and df — carries a canonical (−1)-shifted symplectic structure. The construction works for any f ∈ O(X) and yields a derived stack Crit(f) whose tangent complex at a point is the mapping cone of the Hessian of f. The (−1)-shifted symplectic form is induced by the canonical form on the shifted cotangent bundle T*[−1]X. When X is a classical smooth variety and f a regular function, truncating Crit(f) recovers the classical critical scheme, while the derived structure retains higher obstruction theories from higher derivatives of f. This framework underlies the mathematical definition of the B‑model topological string and the construction of virtual fundamental classes for Donaldson–Thomas invariants on Calabi–Yau threefolds. The shifted symplectic structure also yields a natural quantization via the E2-operad, producing a deformation quantization of the derived critical locus matching the physics of the topological B‑model.

Key properties

  • The derived critical locus is functorial with respect to morphisms of pairs (X, f).
  • The (−1)-shifted symplectic form is non‑degenerate in the derived sense: it induces an equivalence between the tangent and cotangent complexes shifted by −1.
  • For a Landau–Ginzburg model (X, W), the derived critical locus Crit(W) is the derived scheme of singularities; its category of singularities is equivalent to the derived category of matrix factorizations of W.

Derived Artin Approximation and Formal Moduli Problems

Derived Artin approximation (Lurie; Toën–Vezzosi) states that any formal moduli problem over a field of characteristic zero is equivalent to the formal completion of a derived algebraic stack locally of finite presentation. A formal moduli problem is a functor F : CAlgcnk → Spaces satisfying Schlessinger's conditions and a derived Rim–Schlessinger condition. The theorem identifies the ∞‑category of formal moduli problems with the ∞‑category of dg Lie algebras (or L∞-algebras) over k. Under this equivalence, the tangent complex of F at the base point corresponds to the underlying chain complex of the dg Lie algebra shifted by one. The proof uses the derived Artin approximation theorem for algebraic spaces and the fact that the ∞‑category of derived stacks is generated under colimits by representable derived schemes. Consequently, any deformation problem governed by a dg Lie algebra — e.g., deformations of a coherent sheaf, a perfect complex, or an A∞-algebra — is pro‑representable by a formal derived stack. This result foundations the derived geometric approach to moduli spaces: construct the formal moduli problem, apply derived Artin approximation to obtain an algebraic derived stack, then truncate to recover the classical moduli space with its virtual fundamental class.

Applications

  • Moduli of perfect complexes on a proper derived scheme: the formal moduli problem is controlled by the endomorphism dg Lie algebra RHom(E, E)[1].
  • Deformation theory of derived schemes: the tangent complex is the shifted tangent complex TX[1].
  • Derived mapping stacks: for a smooth proper derived scheme Y, the derived mapping stack Map(X, Y) is a derived Artin stack locally of finite presentation.

Its cohomology gives the classical tangent space Ext1(E, E) and obstruction space Ext2(E, E). Higher Exti (i ≥ 3) encode higher obstructions invisible classically but captured by the derived structure. Mention the (−1)-shifted symplectic structure on the derived critical locus if the problem comes from a superpotential.

Derived Azumaya Algebras and Brauer Groups

A derived Azumaya algebra over a derived scheme X is an algebra object A in the symmetric monoidal ∞‑category QCoh(X) such that A ⊗ Aop → End(A) is an equivalence and A is locally free of finite rank as an OX-module. Classically, Azumaya algebras classify Brauer group elements; in derived algebraic geometry the Brauer group is replaced by the derived Brauer space Br(X) = Map(X, BGL1(S)) with S the sphere spectrum. Toën and Antieau–Gepner–Heller show that the derived Brauer group fits into an exact sequence involving H2ét(X, Gm) and the topological Brauer group of the underlying space. Derived Azumaya algebras are precisely objects that become equivalent to matrix algebras after an étale cover in the ∞‑topos of derived stacks. This allows computation of Brauer groups of derived stacks such as the derived moduli of elliptic curves, where derived structure contributes higher homotopy groups invisible classically. Morita theory for derived Azumaya algebras implies the ∞‑category of modules over such an algebra is equivalent to QCoh on a derived Brauer‑Severi stack, giving a derived geometric interpretation of twisted derived categories.

Key results

  • The derived Brauer space classifies derived Azumaya algebras up to Morita equivalence.
  • For a connective derived scheme X, the map Br(X) → Br(π0X) is surjective with kernel given by higher cohomology of the structure sheaf.
  • Derived Azumaya algebras on a derived stack X correspond to Gm-gerbes in the étale ∞‑topos of X.

Derived Categories of Quasi-Coherent Sheaves on Derived Stacks

The ∞‑category QCoh(X) of quasi‑coherent sheaves on a derived stack X is the limit of QCoh(Spec A) over affine derived schemes mapping to X. When X is a derived Artin stack locally of finite presentation, QCoh(X) is a presentable stable ∞‑category compactly generated by perfect complexes. The tensor product makes it a symmetric monoidal ∞‑category with internal Hom given by derived Hom complexes. A fundamental theorem of Gaitsgory–Rozenblyum states that for a morphism f : X → Y, the pullback f* admits a left adjoint f! and a right adjoint f* satisfying ∞‑categorical base‑change formulas. The projection formula holds as an equivalence f!(F ⊗ f*G) ≃ f!F ⊗ G. These adjunctions are essential for Fourier–Mukai transforms on derived stacks and derived Bondal–Orlov reconstruction. In particular, if X and Y are smooth proper derived schemes with an equivalence QCoh(X) ≃ QCoh(Y) as symmetric monoidal ∞‑categories, then X and Y are equivalent as derived schemes. This extends classical reconstruction to the derived setting and underpins the categorical approach to mirror symmetry.

Technical points

  • Compact objects in QCoh(X) are precisely the perfect complexes.
  • The monoidal unit is the structure sheaf OX.
  • For a derived scheme X = Spec A, QCoh(X) is equivalent to the ∞‑category of A-modules.

The derived moduli stack MX of perfect complexes on a smooth proper derived scheme X carries a natural (−1)-shifted symplectic structure when X is Calabi–Yau of dimension three. Bridgeland stability conditions on DbCoh(X) are a choice of slicing of the heart of a bounded t‑structure with a central charge Z : K(X) → ℂ satisfying the support property. In the derived geometric framework, a stability condition determines a good truncation of MX to an Artin stack of finite type whose points are semistable objects. The virtual fundamental class on this truncation is constructed using the (−1)-shifted symplectic structure and derived critical loci. Joyce and collaborators show the resulting generalized Donaldson–Thomas invariants satisfy wall‑crossing formulas given by the Kontsevich–Soibelman product of quantum dilogarithms. The derived enhancement is crucial: higher homotopy groups of mapping spaces in MX encode higher Ext groups controlling obstruction theory, and the shifted symplectic form provides orientation data for the virtual cycle. Brav–Bussi–Dupont–Joyce–Szendrői extend this to moduli of objects in a 3‑Calabi–Yau category, proving the derived moduli stack carries a (−1)-shifted symplectic structure and that the associated Donaldson–Thomas invariants are independent of stability condition up to wall‑crossing.

Derived Loop Spaces and Hochschild Homology

The derived loop space LX = X ×X×X X of a derived stack X is the derived self-intersection of the diagonal. For X = Spec A affine, LX ≃ Spec (A ⊗LA⊗A A) and its structure sheaf computes the Hochschild homology HH•(A). The circle action on LX induced by rotation of loops gives the Connes differential B on HH•, making QCoh(LX) a module over the ∞‑category of S1‑equivariant sheaves. When X is a smooth proper derived scheme, the Hochschild–Kostant–Rosenberg isomorphism identifies HHi(X) with ⊕p−q=i Hq(X, ∧p TX); in the derived setting this becomes a spectral sequence degenerating at E2 for X of finite Tor‑dimension over the base. The derived loop space carries a natural 1‑shifted symplectic structure when X is 0‑shifted symplectic, and the associated S1‑equivariant structure encodes the cyclic homology HC•. Ben‑Zvi–Francis–Nadler established that for a perfect stack X, the ∞‑category QCoh(LX) is equivalent to the ∞‑category of S1‑equivariant modules over the Hochschild cochain algebra HH•(X). This equivalence underlies the derived geometric Langlands correspondence for GL1 and the construction of categorical traces in the sense of Ponto–Shulman. The derived inertia stack IX (the derived fixed locus of the diagonal) is the S1‑fixed locus of LX; its connected components are indexed by conjugacy classes in the automorphism groups of points of X, and the restriction of the 1‑shifted symplectic form to each component recovers the shifted symplectic structures on derived moduli stacks of local systems.

Derived Algebraic K‑Theory and the Cyclotomic Trace

The algebraic K‑theory spectrum K(A) of a connective E∞‑ring A is the group completion of the ∞‑category of perfect A‑modules under direct sum. For a derived scheme X, the sheaf of spectra K on the étale site satisfies Nisnevich descent and K(X) ≃ K(QCohperf(X)). The cyclotomic trace trc: K(A) → TC(A) factors through topological Hochschild homology THH(A), which carries a circle action induced by the derived loop space; TC(A) is constructed from the S1‑homotopy fixed points and cyclotomic structure of THH(A). The Dundas–Goodwillie–McCarthy theorem states that for a nilpotent extension A → B of connective rings, the square relating K, TC, THH is cartesian, reducing K‑theory computations to TC. In derived geometry, the sheaf TC on the étale site of a derived scheme X is constructed via the cyclotomic structure on THH(OX); its values on affine Spec A are TC(A). The Hesselholt–Madsen computation of TC(ℤp) and TC(𝔽p) uses the derived de Rham–Witt complex WΩA, which in the derived setting is the T‑construction on THH(A). Worked computation: derived loop space of a derived critical locus
Let X = Crit(f) be the derived critical locus of a function f: 𝔸n → 𝔸1 over a field of characteristic zero, so X = Spec A with A = Sym(𝔸n ⊕ 𝔸n[−1], d) where the differential is df. Compute the derived loop space LX and identify its 1‑shifted symplectic structure.
The derived critical locus has A = k[x1,…,xn, ξ1,…,ξn] with |ξi| = −1 and dξi = ∂f/∂xi. The derived loop space is LX = Spec (A ⊗LA⊗A A). Since A is a free graded‑commutative algebra on generators xi (degree 0) and ξi (degree −1), the derived tensor product is computed by the Koszul resolution of the diagonal. One finds LX ≃ Spec k[xi, ξi, ηi, ζi] where ηi (degree 0) and ζi (degree −1) are the loop coordinates, with differential dηi = ζi and dζi = ∑j (∂2f/∂xi∂xj) ηj. The 1‑shifted symplectic form is ω = ∑i (dξi ∧ dηi + dζi ∧ dxi) of degree 1. The S1‑action rotates (η, ζ) and the moment map for this action recovers the Connes differential on HH•(A). The S1‑fixed locus is the derived inertia stack IX, which in this case is X itself because the diagonal is a derived regular embedding. The restriction of ω to IX gives the 0‑shifted symplectic structure on X coming from the Hessian of f.

Derived Factorization Algebras and Topological Quantum Field Theory

A factorization algebra on a topological space M is a cosheaf of En‑algebras on the Ran space of M satisfying the factorization property: for disjoint open sets U1,…,Uk ⊂ V, the structure map ⊗i F(Ui) → F(V) is an equivalence. In derived algebraic geometry, one works with factorization algebras valued in the symmetric monoidal ∞‑category QCoh(X) for a derived stack X. The Beilinson–Drinfeld construction associates to a chiral algebra on a curve C a factorization algebra on C; in the derived setting, a chiral algebra is a Lie* algebra in the category of D‑modules on C, and the derived version uses the ∞‑category of derived D‑modules DC‑mod. Costello–Gwilliam established that perturbative quantum field theories on a manifold M are equivalent to factorization algebras on M with values in chain complexes. In the derived geometric framework, a topological quantum field theory (TQFT) of dimension n is a symmetric monoidal functor Z: Bordn → QCoh(X) where Bordn is the (∞,n)‑category of bordisms. The cobordism hypothesis (Lurie) classifies such TQFTs by fully dualizable objects in QCoh(X). For X = Spec k, fully dualizable objects in Modk are finite‑dimensional vector spaces, recovering the classical classification. For a general derived stack X, a fully dualizable object in QCoh(X) is a perfect complex E equipped with a non‑degenerate pairing E ⊗ E → OX; this is precisely a 0‑shifted symplectic structure on the derived stack Spec Sym(E[1]). The factorization homology ∫M F of a factorization algebra F on M computes the value of the associated TQFT on M. When F is the factorization algebra associated to an En‑algebra A in QCoh(X), factorization homology over ℝn recovers the n‑fold bar construction BnA. The derived geometric Langlands correspondence for GLn constructs a factorization algebra on the curve C with values in QCoh(LocSysG(C)) where LocSysG(C) is the derived moduli stack of G‑local systems; the factorization property encodes the Hecke eigensheaf condition. The work of Rozenblyum on derived factorization algebras and the Ran space in derived algebraic geometry provides the ∞‑categorical foundations for this construction.

Derived Geometry of Local Systems and Character Stacks

The derived moduli stack LocSysG(X) of G‑local systems on a derived scheme X is the mapping stack Map(X, BG) in the ∞‑topos of derived stacks, where G is a reductive group scheme. Derived Picard Stacks and the Derived Moduli of Line Bundles

The derived Picard stack of a derived scheme X is the mapping stack Map(X, B𝔾m) formed in the ∞‑category of derived stacks. Its truncation recovers the classical Picard scheme Pic(X) parametrising isomorphism classes of line bundles. Because the target B𝔾m is a derived group stack, the derived Picard stack inherits a natural derived group structure; its Lie algebra is the shifted global sections complex RΓ(X, OX)[1]. The tangent complex at a point corresponding to a line bundle L is therefore RΓ(X, OX)[1], and the obstruction theory is governed by RΓ(X, OX)[2]. When X is proper and of finite presentation over a base derived ring k, the derived Picard stack is a derived Artin stack locally of finite presentation. The derived Picard stack carries a canonical 0‑shifted symplectic structure arising from the cup product pairing on RΓ(X, OX) combined with the trace map RΓ(X, OX) → k. This symplectic form makes the derived Picard stack a derived symplectic group stack, and it underlies the derived version of the Fourier–Mukai transform: the derived moduli of line bundles on a Calabi–Yau derived scheme is a derived Lagrangian inside the derived moduli of perfect complexes. The construction appears in the work of Toën–Vezzosi on derived algebraic geometry and in later refinements by Porta–Yu on derived duality for proper derived schemes.

Derived Non‑Abelian Hodge Theory and the Derived Moduli of Higgs Bundles

Classical non‑abelian Hodge theory establishes a homeomorphism between the moduli space of semistable Higgs bundles and the character variety of representations of the fundamental group. In derived algebraic geometry this correspondence is lifted to an equivalence of derived stacks. Let MHiggs(G) denote the derived moduli stack of G‑Higgs bundles on a smooth projective curve C, and let LocSysG(C) be the derived character stack of G‑local systems. The derived non‑abelian Hodge correspondence is a morphism of derived stacks MHiggs(G) → LocSysG(C) which becomes an equivalence after passing to the associated ∞‑categories of quasi‑coherent sheaves. Both derived stacks carry a 1‑shifted symplectic structure: on MHiggs(G) it is given by the Atiyah–Bott form on the cotangent complex, while on LocSysG(C) it is the cup product paired with the Killing form on the adjoint bundle. The derived Hitchin fibration h: MHiggs(G) → 𝔸N is a Lagrangian fibration with respect to this 1‑shifted symplectic form, and its generic fibres are derived abelian varieties. The derived correspondence respects the shifted symplectic structures, making it a derived symplectomorphism. This framework builds on work by Simpson, Kapranov–Schechtman, and in the derived setting by Bhatt–Lurie and Porta–Sala, providing geometric input for the derived geometric Langlands program.

Derived D‑Modules and the Derived Riemann–Hilbert Correspondence

The classical Riemann–Hilbert correspondence gives an equivalence between the category of regular holonomic D‑modules on a smooth complex variety X and the category of constructible sheaves on X. In derived algebraic geometry this is upgraded to an equivalence of stable ∞‑categories. Let D(X) denote the derived ∞‑category of D‑modules on a derived scheme X (or on a smooth classical scheme viewed as a derived scheme). The derived de Rham functor DR: D(X) → Shv(Xan) sends a D‑module to its derived solution complex, while the derived de Rham complex LΩX provides the inverse direction. The derived Riemann–Hilbert correspondence asserts that DR induces an equivalence between the full subcategory of D(X) consisting of objects with regular singularities and the derived category of constructible sheaves on the associated analytic space. The proof uses the derived loop space LX and the identification of D‑modules with quasi‑coherent sheaves on the derived cotangent stack T*X. The derived cotangent complex LX controls the deformation theory of D‑modules, and the derived de Rham complex computes the derived solutions. This equivalence is a cornerstone of the work of Gaitsgory–Rozenblyum on derived categories of D‑modules and of Bhatt–Lurie on prismatic cohomology, where the derived de Rham–Witt complex appears as the T‑construction on topological Hochschild homology of the structure sheaf.

Derived Geometry of Parabolic Local Systems and the Derived Wild Character Stack

Parabolic local systems are local systems on a punctured curve equipped with a choice of monodromy conjugacy class at each puncture. The derived moduli stack LocSysG,P(C) of parabolic G‑local systems is a derived Artin stack carrying a 1‑shifted symplectic structure. The symplectic form is obtained by integrating the cup product of the adjoint bundle against the Killing form, together with residue pairings at the punctures that encode the parabolic weights. When irregular singularities (wild ramification) are allowed, one obtains the derived wild character stack LocSysG,wild(C). This stack also carries a 1‑shifted symplectic structure, but the symplectic form now involves Stokes data and the irregular Riemann–Hilbert correspondence. The derived wild character stack appears as the phase space for the derived geometric Langlands correspondence with ramification: the derived category of quasi‑coherent sheaves on LocSysG,wild(C) is expected to be equivalent to a derived Whittaker category on the derived moduli of G‑bundles with parabolic structure. The shifted symplectic structure makes the derived wild character stack a derived symplectic stack, and its derived Lagrangian substacks correspond to boundary conditions in a 3‑dimensional topological quantum field theory. These results build on work of Boalch, Brav–Bussi, and in the derived setting Ben‑Zvi–Sakuma and Gaitsgory–Rozenblyum.

Derived Moduli of G‑Bundles and the Derived Geometric Langlands Correspondence

The derived moduli stack BunG(X) of principal G‑bundles on a smooth projective curve X is a derived Artin stack locally of finite presentation. Its tangent complex at a point corresponding to a bundle 𝒫 is RΓ(X, ad(𝒫))[1], where ad(𝒫) is the adjoint bundle. The derived structure records higher automorphisms and obstructions, which are invisible in the classical truncation. The derived geometric Langlands conjecture, formulated by Gaitsgory and Rozenblyum, predicts an equivalence of stable ∞‑categories

QCoh(BunG(X)) ≃ IndCohNilp(LocSysǦ(X))

where LocSysǦ(X) is the derived moduli stack of Ǧ‑local systems, IndCohNilp denotes ind‑coherent sheaves with nilpotent singular support, and Ǧ is the Langlands dual group. The proof strategy uses the derived Satake equivalence for the affine Grassmannian GrG, the derived Hecke operators acting on QCoh(BunG), and the derived Whittaker category on BunG. A key technical input is the derived version of the Beilinson‑Drinfeld Grassmannian, which provides a factorization structure compatible with the chiral homology of the curve. The derived enhancement is considered essential because the classical correspondence does not capture the correct categorical traces and the spectral decomposition of Hecke operators. Recent work by Gaitsgory and Rozenblyum (2020–2023) has established the equivalence for GL1 and made significant progress for GLn, outlining a strategy for general reductive groups using the derived categorical trace and a derived version of the Arthur‑Selberg trace formula.

Derived Hall Algebras and the Derived Moduli of Coherent Sheaves

Let CohX be the derived moduli stack of coherent sheaves on a smooth projective variety X. The derived Hall algebra H(X) is defined as the cohomological Hall algebra of the derived category Db(Coh X), following the construction of Kontsevich–Soibelman and derived refinements by Toën–Vaquié and others. Concretely, H(X) is the (derived) algebra of constructible functions on the stack of objects of Db(Coh X) with multiplication given by the derived correspondence

CohX × CohX ← Ext1 → CohX

where the middle term classifies extensions. The derived structure ensures that the multiplication is associative up to coherent homotopy, yielding an E1‑algebra in the derived category of k‑modules. When X is a Calabi‑Yau threefold, the derived Hall algebra carries a natural BV‑operator induced by the Calabi‑Yau structure, and its homology recovers the (classical) Hall algebra of Joyce–Song. The derived Hall algebra acts on the derived category of quasi‑coherent sheaves on the derived moduli stack of stable objects, providing a categorification of the Donaldson‑Thomas invariants. The derived enhancement helps resolve sign ambiguities in the classical wall‑crossing formulas and makes the integration map to the motivic Hall algebra compatible with the derived symplectic structure on the moduli of objects.

Worked Example: Derived Hall Algebra of a Curve

For a curve, every coherent sheaf has homological dimension ≤ 1, so the derived category Db(Coh X) is equivalent to the heart Coh X. The derived moduli stack CohX is therefore a classical Artin stack (no higher homotopy). The derived Hall algebra H(X) reduces to the classical Hall algebra of coherent sheaves, which is the algebra of constructible functions on CohX with multiplication given by the extension correspondence. The derived BV‑operator vanishes because the Calabi‑Yau dimension is 1, not 3. The integration map to the motivic Hall algebra sends a constructible function to its motivic weight, and the wall‑crossing formula for stability conditions on curves coincides with the classical Harder‑Narasimhan recursion. Thus H(X) recovers the classical Hall algebra, and the derived structure is trivial in this case.

Derived Mukai Pairings on the Derived Moduli of Perfect Complexes

The derived moduli stack PerfX of perfect complexes on a smooth proper scheme X carries a natural (2-d)‑shifted symplectic structure when X is Calabi‑Yau of dimension d, as shown by Pantev–Toën–Vaquié–Vezzosi. ⟨−,−⟩Muk : RΓ(X, E∨ ⊗ F) ⊗ RΓ(X, F∨ ⊗ E) → k[−d]

obtained from the composition E∨ ⊗ F ⊗ F∨ ⊗ E → E∨ ⊗ E → OX followed by the trace map and Serre duality. In the derived setting, this pairing is refined to a map of spectra, and the resulting shifted symplectic structure is a closed 2‑form of degree 2-d in the sense of derived symplectic geometry. The derived Mukai pairing controls the deformation theory of objects in PerfX and provides the quadratic Hamiltonian for the derived Donaldson‑Thomas theory. The derived enhancement is crucial for the categorification of the Mukai lattice and for the construction of derived Bridgeland stability conditions on the derived category.

Derived Atiyah‑Bott Construction for the Moduli of Flat Connections

Let X be a smooth projective curve and G a reductive group. The derived moduli stack LocSysG(X) of G‑local systems (flat connections) is a derived Artin stack whose classical truncation is the character variety. The derived Atiyah‑Bott construction, developed in derived symplectic geometry by Pantev–Toën–Vaquié–Vezzosi and later by Calaque–Grivaux, produces a 1‑shifted symplectic structure on LocSysG(X). RΓ(X, ad(∇)) ⊗ RΓ(X, ad(∇)) → RΓ(X, ad(∇) ⊗ ad(∇)) → RΓ(X, OX)[2] ≃ k[1]

Derived Symplectic Geometry and the AKSZ Formalism

The AKSZ construction systematically produces derived symplectic structures on mapping stacks. Let X be a derived Artin stack with an n-shifted symplectic structure ω_X, and Σ a compact oriented manifold of dimension d. The mapping stack Map(Σ, X) inherits an (n-d)-shifted symplectic structure ωMap via transgression: ωMap = ∫_Σ ev^* ω_X, where ev : Σ × Map(Σ, X) → X is the evaluation map and integration occurs at the level of differential forms on the derived stack. In derived algebraic geometry (Pantev–Toën–Vaquié–Vezzosi, Calaque), this is internalized using derived stacks and the de Rham complex. The key input is DR(Map(Σ, X)) ≃ Map(Σ, DR(X)) for Σ a smooth proper scheme, with integration ∫_Σ : Map(Σ, DR(X)) → DR(X)[-d] a morphism of mixed cdgas. Closedness of the transgressed form follows from the derived Stokes theorem, which holds because Σ has no boundary. When X = BG for a reductive group G with an invariant polynomial of degree k, AKSZ recovers the (2k-2-d)-shifted symplectic structure on the derived moduli of G-local systems on Σ. For d=1 and the Killing form (k=2), this yields the 1-shifted structure on LocSys_G(Σ) from the Atiyah–Bott construction. For d=2 and a 3d Calabi–Yau category, AKSZ produces the 0-shifted structure on the derived moduli of objects, underlying Joyce’s derived Donaldson–Thomas theory and the Kontsevich–Soibelman categorification. The construction also applies to Higgs bundles: taking X = T^*[1]BG with its canonical 1-shifted structure and Σ a curve gives a 0-shifted structure on the derived moduli of Higgs bundles, the derived enhancement of the Hitchin system. The derived symplectic structure on mapping stacks is functorial for Lagrangian correspondences: a Lagrangian L → X × Y of n-shifted stacks induces a Lagrangian Map(Σ, L) → Map(Σ, X) × Map(Σ, Y) of (n-d)-shifted stacks. This functoriality is essential for constructing topological quantum field theories via AKSZ, where the state space for a closed (d-1)-manifold is the derived moduli of fields on it, and the partition function on a d-manifold with boundary is a section of the line bundle on the boundary moduli induced by the symplectic structure.

Derived Geometry of Moduli of Matrix Factorizations and Landau–Ginzburg Models

The derived moduli stack of matrix factorizations provides the geometric framework for Landau–Ginzburg models and their link to derived categories of singularities. Let X be a smooth derived scheme and W : X → 𝔸1 a regular function (superpotential). A matrix factorization of W is a ℤ/2-graded perfect complex E = E0 ⊕ E1 with an odd endomorphism d_E : E → E[1] satisfying d_E2 = W · id_E. The dg-category MF(X, W) has matrix factorizations as objects and Hom-complexes as morphisms. When W has isolated critical locus, Orlov’s equivalence identifies MF(X, W) with the singularity category Dsg(W-1(0)), the Verdier quotient of coherent sheaves on the critical locus by perfect complexes. In derived geometry (Preygel, Toën–Vezzosi), the moduli stack MFX,W is a derived Artin stack whose tangent complex at (E, d_E) is the Hochschild cochain complex CC^*(E, E)[1] twisted by W. When X is Calabi–Yau of dimension d and W is homogeneous of degree d, MFX,W carries a natural (−1)-shifted symplectic structure, reflecting the Calabi–Yau structure on MF(X, W) (Dyckerhoff, Polishchuk–Vaintrob). The stack also admits a description as a derived critical locus: MFX,W ≃ Crit(W) in the derived stack of perfect complexes on X, making the (−1)-shifted structure manifest (Pantev–Toën–Vaquié–Vezzosi). The Landau–Ginzburg B-model is the derived symplectic geometry of MFX,W; its quantization yields the derived category of matrix factorizations as a deformation quantization module. Wall-crossing in stability conditions on MF(X, W) is governed by the derived symplectic geometry of MFX,W and associated derived Donaldson–Thomas invariants. For X = 𝔸^n and W quasi-homogeneous with isolated singularity, MFX,W is a derived Artin stack of finite type whose classical truncation is the classical moduli, and whose derived structure encodes higher Ext-groups (open-string states in the topological B-model).

Derived Categorical Representation Theory and the Derived Satake Equivalence

The derived Satake equivalence, developed by Bezrukavnikov–Finkelberg and extended to the derived setting by Gaitsgory–Lurie, relates the derived category of representations of a reductive group to the derived category of sheaves on the affine Grassmannian. The derived version upgrades this to an equivalence of stable ∞-categories D^b(Rep(G^∨)) ≃ D^bG(𝒪)(Gr_G), or more precisely of dg-categories Ddg(Rep(G^∨)) ≃ Ddg, G(𝒪)(Gr_G). This formulation (Gaitsgory–Lurie) reveals the derived Satake equivalence as a local version of the global derived geometric Langlands correspondence. The derived structure resolves singularities of Schubert varieties via the cotangent complex. The equivalence is compatible with convolution on ShvG(𝒪)(Gr_Gder) and tensor product on QCoh(LocSysG^∨(D^×)), and incorporates the loop rotation 𝔾_m-action corresponding to the central cocharacter grading. Applications include a categorical framework for the Kazhdan–Lusztig equivalence, the derived Whittaker category, the derived geometric Langlands correspondence for GL_n, and the local input for the global conjecture QCoh(LocSysG^∨(X)) ≃ ShvG(𝒪)(Bun_G(X)) for a smooth projective curve X.

Derived Moduli of Stable Maps and the Virtual Fundamental Class in Gromov–Witten Theory

The derived moduli stack of stable maps Mapg,n(X, β) parametrizes genus‑g nodal curves with n marked points together with a morphism to a smooth projective variety X representing a curve class β. The derived structure also encodes higher obstructions: the full cotangent complex is a perfect complex of amplitude [-1,0], and its higher homotopy groups control compatibility of the virtual class with gluing maps (the splitting axioms). In practice, the derived moduli stack is presented as a derived quotient [R/G] where R is a derived scheme of maps from a fixed nodal curve and G the automorphism group; the derived quotient resolves classical quotient singularities and makes the obstruction theory globally defined. Derived Moduli of Coherent Sheaves on Calabi–Yau Threefolds and Donaldson–Thomas Invariants

Let Y be a smooth projective Calabi–Yau threefold (trivial canonical bundle, H1(Y, OY) = 0). The classical moduli space M of stable coherent sheaves (or ideal sheaves of curves) with fixed Chern character is a projective scheme with a symmetric obstruction theory: the tangent space at a sheaf F is Ext1(F,F) and the obstruction space is Ext2(F,F). Serre duality and the Calabi–Yau condition give a non‑degenerate pairing Ext1(F,F) × Ext1(F,F) → Ext3(F,F) ≃ k, making the obstruction theory symmetric. In derived algebraic geometry one constructs the derived moduli stack Mder as a derived Artin stack whose tangent complex at F is the shifted Ext complex R Hom(F,F)[1]. This complex is perfect of amplitude [-1,2] and carries a canonical (−1)‑shifted symplectic structure in the sense of Pantev–Toën–Vaquié–Vezzosi. The (−1)‑shifted symplectic form is induced by the Serre duality pairing and is responsible for the existence of a canonical virtual fundamental class [Mder]vir of dimension zero. The Donaldson–Thomas invariant DT(Y, v) is defined as the degree of this zero‑cycle. The derived structure is essential: (1) it resolves singularities of the classical moduli space, so the virtual class is defined without choosing a perfect obstruction theory by hand; (2) the (−1)‑shifted symplectic structure yields an orientation on the virtual class, needed to define the invariant with signs; (3) the derived stack carries a natural dg‑enhancement, allowing categorified invariants (the cohomological Hall algebra of Kontsevich–Soibelman). The wall‑crossing formula for DT invariants under change of stability condition is proved by comparing derived moduli stacks for different stability conditions; the derived structure guarantees the virtual classes match under the natural morphisms. A frequent mistake is to treat the DT invariant as the Euler characteristic of the classical moduli space; the correct statement is that it is the weighted Euler characteristic of the derived moduli stack, where the weight is the Behrend function, itself derived from the (−1)‑shifted symplectic structure.

Derived Geometry of Moduli of Higgs Bundles with Parabolic Structures

The derived moduli stack of parabolic Higgs bundles on a smooth projective curve C with marked points D = Σ pᵢ and fixed parabolic weights is a derived Artin stack whose classical truncation recovers the Simpson moduli space. The parabolic structure is encoded by a flag in the fiber at each pᵢ together with a weight system satisfying standard compatibility conditions. The derived enhancement replaces stabilizer group schemes of bundles with non‑trivial automorphisms by their derived versions, yielding a perfect cotangent complex concentrated in degrees [−1,0]. At a point (E, φ, F•) the tangent complex is the hypercohomology ℍ¹(C, ad(E)(−D) → ad(E) ⊗ Ω¹_C(D)), where the map is induced by the Higgs field φ. Brav, Bussi, Dupont, Joyce and Safronov proved that this derived stack carries a canonical (−1)‑shifted symplectic structure, constructed from the Serre duality pairing on the parabolic de Rham complex. The associated virtual fundamental class coincides with the one used in the parabolic Hitchin system and in the computation of parabolic Higgs bundle invariants via the support property of the perverse filtration. When the parabolic weights are generic, the derived stack is a derived Deligne–Mumford stack and the virtual class is represented by the fundamental class of the smooth classical moduli space. For special weights, the derived structure resolves the singularities coming from strictly semistable objects and the virtual class captures the correct enumerative contributions.

Derived Geometry of Moduli of Flat Connections with Irregular Singularities

The derived moduli stack of meromorphic connections on a curve C with irregular singularities at a divisor D (the wild character variety) is a derived Artin stack whose classical truncation is the Boalch wild character variety. An irregular singularity is specified by a formal type — a leading term of the connection matrix with poles of order >1 — together with Stokes data encoding the asymptotic sectors. The derived enhancement resolves the non‑reductive stabilizers that appear for connections with non‑trivial automorphisms and provides a perfect cotangent complex. The tangent complex at a connection ∇ is the hypercohomology ℍ¹(C, ad(∇) → ad(∇) ⊗ Ω¹_C(D)), where the map is the covariant derivative. Brav, Bussi and Joyce showed that the derived stack carries a natural 0‑shifted Poisson structure induced by the Atiyah–Bott symplectic form on the space of connections with fixed irregular type; after fixing the residues at the irregular singularities this Poisson structure becomes non‑degenerate, yielding a 1‑shifted symplectic structure on the derived moduli of connections with fixed formal type. The associated virtual fundamental class is used to define invariants of wild character varieties, which are expected to match the Stokes data of the corresponding irregular Riemann–Hilbert correspondence. The derived stack also admits a natural map to the derived moduli of local systems on the punctured curve, which is a Lagrangian morphism with respect to the shifted symplectic structures.

Worked Example: Virtual Class from (−1)-Shifted Symplectic Structure
Explain how the (−1)-shifted symplectic structure on the derived moduli stack of parabolic Higgs bundles produces a virtual fundamental class for the parabolic Hitchin system.
The (−1)-shifted symplectic structure gives a non‑degenerate pairing on the tangent complex Tₓ of degree −1. By the general theory of shifted symplectic derived stacks (Pantev–Toën–Vaquié–Vezzosi), such a structure determines a canonical relative virtual fundamental class in the homology of the classical truncation. Concretely, one chooses a perfect obstruction theory E• → Lₓ compatible with the shifted symplectic form; the obstruction theory is the truncation of the cotangent complex. The virtual class is then the Gysin pullback of the fundamental class of the ambient smooth derived stack along the zero section of the obstruction bundle. In the parabolic Hitchin system, the Hitchin map is Lagrangian with respect to the (−1)-shifted symplectic form, so the virtual class on the total space pushes forward to the fundamental class of the base, yielding the expected enumerative invariants.

Derived Geometry of Moduli of Coherent Sheaves on Noncommutative Varieties

For a differential graded (dg) category 𝒞 that is smooth and proper (e.g. the derived category of coherent sheaves on a smooth projective variety, or the category of perfect modules over a dg‑algebra arising from a noncommutative resolution), the derived moduli stack of objects in 𝒞 is a derived Artin stack locally of finite presentation. Toën and Vaquié constructed this stack as a functor from simplicial commutative rings to spaces satisfying derived étale descent. The tangent complex at an object E is the shifted Ext complex Ext•_𝒞(E, E)[1]. When 𝒞 is Calabi–Yau of dimension d (i.e. Serre duality holds with a shift d), the derived moduli stack carries a canonical (d−2)‑shifted symplectic structure. In particular, for a 3‑Calabi–Yau dg‑category (such as the derived category of a Calabi–Yau threefold or the category of representations of a quiver with potential), one obtains a (−1)‑shifted symplectic structure, and the associated virtual fundamental class reproduces the Donaldson–Thomas invariants defined by Joyce–Song and Kontsevich–Soibelman. The derived enhancement resolves the non‑trivial automorphisms of sheaves and the non‑separatedness of the classical moduli space, and the virtual class is independent of the choice of stability condition. Recent work of Brav–Dyckerhoff extends this to moduli of objects in arbitrary dg‑categories with a Calabi–Yau structure, providing a unified framework for DT invariants of noncommutative crepant resolutions.

Derived Geometry of Moduli of Derived Gerbes and Brauer Groups

A derived gerbe on a derived stack X is a stack locally equivalent to the classifying stack BGL₁(𝒪_X) of the derived group of units of the structure sheaf. The derived Brauer group Br(X) classifies derived Azumaya algebras up to Morita equivalence; it fits into an exact sequence 0 → Pic(X) → H²_et(X, 𝔾_m) → Br(X) → 0 in the derived setting, where H²_et denotes étale cohomology in the ∞‑topos of derived stacks. Antieau and Gepner showed that the derived moduli stack of derived gerbes banded by 𝔾_m is a derived Artin stack whose cotangent complex at a gerbe 𝒢 is RΓ(X, 𝒪_X)[2]. This stack carries a natural 1‑shifted symplectic structure when X is a derived Calabi–Yau stack of dimension 0, and the associated virtual class computes the derived Brauer group invariants. Derived Azumaya algebras provide a geometric realization of the derived Brauer group: a derived Azumaya algebra is a sheaf of dg‑algebras locally Morita equivalent to a matrix algebra over 𝒪_X. The derived moduli of such algebras is a derived stack mapping to the derived Brauer stack, and the fiber over a class α ∈ Br(X) is the derived stack of α‑twisted derived vector bundles. This construction underlies the derived version of the period‑index problem and the derived geometric Langlands correspondence for GL_n with ramification.

Derived Geometry of Moduli of Derived Formal Groups and Chromatic Homotopy Theory

The derived moduli stack of formal groups M_fg is a derived Deligne–Mumford stack over the sphere spectrum whose classical truncation is the classical moduli of formal groups. Lurie's Goerss–Hopkins–Miller theorem equips the universal deformation of a formal group of height n at a prime p with an E_∞‑ring structure, producing the Lubin–Tate spectrum E_n. The derived deformation theory of a formal group G over a perfect field k of characteristic p is controlled by the André–Quillen cohomology of the Hopf algebra representing G; the tangent complex is Lie(G) ⊗ k[1]. The derived stack M_fg carries a natural (0‑shifted) symplectic structure arising from the Serre duality on the Dieudonné module of the universal formal group. The chromatic filtration on the stable homotopy category is reflected in the stratification of M_fg by height; the derived completion of M_fg along the height n locus yields the derived moduli of deformations of the Honda formal group, which is representable by the Lubin–Tate spectrum E_n. The derived moduli of formal groups with level structure (e.g. Drinfeld level structures) gives rise to the Morava E‑theories with Galois action, and the associated derived moduli of E_n‑local spectra is a derived stack whose global sections recover the K(n)‑local sphere. This dictionary between derived algebraic geometry of formal groups and chromatic homotopy theory is the foundation of modern computational tools such as the Adams–Novikov spectral sequence and the chromatic convergence theorem.
