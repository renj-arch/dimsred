Tannakian Categories — Neutral Tannakian Categories, the Reconstruction Theorem & Motives

A Tannakian category is a rigid abelian tensor category over a field k equipped with a faithful exact fiber functor ω: C → Vectk — a tensor-preserving functor to finite-dimensional vector spaces. The Tannaka-Krein reconstruction theorem states that the group G = Aut^⊗(ω) of tensor automorphisms of ω is an affine group scheme, and the category C is equivalent to Repk(G), the category of finite-dimensional representations of G. This powerful formalism, developed by Saavedra Rivano and Deligne, shows that every neutral Tannakian category arises as the representation category of some pro-algebraic group. The theory has deep applications to Hodge theory (the Mumford-Tate group encodes all Hodge symmetries of a variety), to motives (the category of pure motives is Tannakian, with the motivic Galois group governing periods and algebraic cycles), and to the Langlands program (the Tannakian Langlands correspondence views automorphic representations as the representation theory of the Langlands group).

"Tannakian categories are the categorical essence of group representations."
— Pierre Deligne
Part 1

📏 Rigid Abelian Tensor Categories

Foundations
Symmetric Monoidal Categories, Rigidity, and Internal Hom
A symmetric monoidal category (C, ⊗, 1) is equipped with an associativity constraint αX,Y,Z: (X⊗Y)⊗Z → X⊗(Y⊗Z), a commutativity constraint σX,Y: X⊗Y → Y⊗X, and a unit object 1 satisfying Mac Lane's coherence conditions (pentagon and hexagon axioms). An abelian tensor category is an abelian category with a bilinear tensor product that is exact in each variable and compatible with the abelian structure. Rigidity: every object X has a dual X∨ equipped with evaluation ev: X∨⊗X → 1 and coevaluation coev: 1 → X⊗X∨ satisfying the zigzag identities: (idX⊗ev)∘(coev⊗idX) = idX and (ev⊗idX∨)∘(idX∨⊗coev) = idX∨. The internal Hom is defined as Hom̲(X, Y) = X∨⊗Y. The dimension of an object is the trace of the identity: dim(X) = Tr(idX) = ev ∘ σX,X∨ ∘ coev (regarded as an element of End(1) = k). The rank of an object is this categorical dimension. Examples: the category Repk(G) of finite-dimensional representations of an affine group scheme G over k; the category VecΓk of finite-dimensional ℤ-graded vector spaces (with the Koszul sign constraint); Vectk itself (trivial Tannakian category with G trivial); and the category of ℚ-Hodge structures (polarizable and of finite weight).
A rigid abelian tensor category is the minimal categorical structure needed to do representation theory — it axiomatizes the essential properties of the category of vector spaces.
Part 2

🌍 The Reconstruction Theorem

Reconstruction
Fiber Functors, the Tannaka-Krein Theorem, and the Group of Tensor Automorphisms
Let C be a ℂ-linear abelian category. A fiber functor on C is a faithful exact tensor functor ω: C → Vectk that is k-linear, additive, and preserves the tensor product (ω(X⊗Y) ≅ ω(X)⊗ω(Y)) and the unit. The group of tensor automorphisms G = Aut⊗(ω) consists of natural automorphisms g: ω → ω such that gX⊗Y = gX⊗gY and g1 = idω(1). The Tannaka-Krein reconstruction theorem: if C is a neutral Tannakian category (a rigid abelian tensor category with a fiber functor ω), then the natural functor C → Repk(G) given by X ↦ ω(X) (with the G-action via the natural transformation) is an equivalence of tensor categories. The reconstruction via Hopf algebras: the coalgebra A = Nat⊗(ω, ω) of tensor-preserving natural endomorphisms of ω is a commutative Hopf algebra, and G = Spec(A*) is the associated affine group scheme. The fundamental theorem of Tannakian categories (Deligne, 1990): if C is a neutral Tannakian category, then there exists an affine group scheme G over k such that C ≅ Repk(G). Moreover, if ω is a fiber functor, then G = Aut⊗(ω) is unique up to isomorphism. The group G is pro-algebraic — an inverse limit of algebraic groups — and its representation theory recovers the full tensor category structure.
The Tannaka-Krein theorem reconstructs the group from its representation category via the automorphisms of the fiber functor — groups and their representations are categorically equivalent.
Part 3

🧮 The Tannakian Philosophy in Hodge Theory

Hodge
Mumford-Tate Groups, Hodge Structures, and the Tannakian Formalism
The category of polarizable ℚ-Hodge structures is a neutral Tannakian category with fiber functor ω(V) = Vℚ (the underlying ℚ-vector space forgetting the Hodge filtration). For a Hodge structure V of weight n, the Mumford-Tate group MT(V) is defined as the Tannaka group of the Tannakian subcategory generated by V — i.e., the affine group scheme whose representations are exactly the subquotients of tensor powers V⊗a⊗(V∨)⊗b. For a smooth projective variety X, the Mumford-Tate group of Hk(X, ℚ) encodes all Hodge-theoretic symmetries of X. The Hodge conjecture can be reformulated: the space of Hodge classes on Xn is the space of invariants of the Mumford-Tate group acting on the cohomology. The Griffiths transversality condition for the variation of Hodge structures on a family of varieties has a Tannakian interpretation: the universal family of Hodge structures gives a representation of the fundamental group of the base into the Mumford-Tate group, and the Tannakian formalism explains how the Hodge filtration varies holomorphically. The Mumford-Tate conjecture relates the ℓ-adic Galois representation associated to an algebraic variety to the Mumford-Tate group: the image of the Galois group in the ℓ-adic cohomology is an open subgroup of the ℓ-adic points of the Mumford-Tate group.
The Mumford-Tate group is the Tannaka group of a Hodge structure — it is a reductive group whose invariants on tensor powers are exactly the Hodge classes.
Part 4

🔥 Motives and the Motivic Galois Group

Motives
Pure Motives, the Motivic Galois Group, and the Langlands-Tannakian Philosophy
The category of pure motives Mk (due to Klemm-Kahn-Murre and Deligne) has smooth projective varieties as objects and morphisms given by algebraic correspondences modulo rational equivalence, using the Chow ring. The Künneth projectors give a Lefschetz decomposition of the cohomology and define a fiber functor: ω(M) = H*(M, ℚ) (Betti cohomology) for a chosen embedding k ↪ ℂ. The category of pure motives with respect to ℚ-linear equivalence is a neutral Tannakian category. The motivic Galois group Gmot = Aut⊗(ω) is a pro-algebraic group that acts on the cohomology of all algebraic varieties simultaneously. Grothendieck's period conjecture: the transcendence degree of the field generated by the periods of a smooth projective variety X equals the dimension of the motivic Galois group of its motive — a profound link between algebraic geometry and transcendental number theory. The Langlands-Tannakian philosophy (the Tannakian Langlands correspondence) posits that the motivic Galois group is a universal symmetry group for arithmetic objects, and the Langlands program is about the representation theory of Gmot and its relationship to automorphic forms via the Langlands dual group LG. Applications: the Tate conjecture (the Galois invariants of ℓ-adic étale cohomology are spanned by algebraic cycle classes — a Tannakian statement about the Galois group acting on the cohomology of varieties), the Fontaine-Mazur conjecture (geometric ℓ-adic Galois representations come from algebraic geometry), and the Sato-Tate conjecture (the distribution of Frobenius eigenvalues is governed by the Sato-Tate group, which is the Tannaka group of the motive of an elliptic curve).
The category of pure motives is Tannakian; its Tannaka group — the motivic Galois group — controls periods, algebraic cycles, and the deep arithmetic symmetries of algebraic varieties.
