FILMS

Short, sourced histories. One object at a time.

These are not summaries with music. Each film starts from a thing you can see — salt, a notice, a wall, a watch, a blank column, a ledger — and follows it into the political fight that made it matter. Every number is sourced. Every argument has a map.

The Salt March

In 1930, the Raj taxed the one thing every Indian could gather for free. One man picked up a handful of salt, and the Empire discovered a weapon it could not arrest.

Built from ten sets of paper-cutout rooms and objects: the tax ledger, the salt crystal, the map, the gate and the clenched fist at Dharasana. The hosts argue; the narration carries the sources.
