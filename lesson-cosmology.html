Cosmology — Big Bang, CMB & the ΛCDM Model

Deep dive into Cosmology — the observational foundations of the expanding universe (Hubble's law, the cosmic distance ladder, and the Hubble tension), the Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker metric ds² = −dt² + a(t)² [dr²/(1−kr²) + r² dΩ²] and the Friedmann equations H² = (8πG/3)ρ − k/a² + Λ/3 and ä/a = −(4πG/3)(ρ + 3p) + Λ/3, the thermal history from the Planck epoch through grand unification, the electroweak phase transition, and quark-hadron transition, Big Bang nucleosynthesis (the abundances of ²H, ³He, ⁴He, ⁷Li as probes of baryon density), the cosmic microwave background (the recombination epoch, the surface of last scattering, the dipole anisotropy, and the Planck satellite measurements of the temperature and polarization power spectra), the dark matter evidence from rotation curves, gravitational lensing, and the Bullet Cluster, the dark energy from Type Ia supernovae (the Riess and Perlmutter discoveries), the cosmological constant problem (the 120 orders of magnitude discrepancy), cosmic inflation (the slow-roll conditions, the inflaton field, the generation of primordial perturbations, and the BICEP/Keck constraints on tensor modes), the ΛCDM concordance model parameters (Ω_m ≈ 0.315, Ω_Λ ≈ 0.685, H₀ ≈ 67.4 km/s/Mpc), and the growth of large-scale structure from the primordial power spectrum to the cosmic web.

Part 1

🌌 The Expanding Universe

Cosmic Expansion
The FLRW Metric, Friedmann Equations, and Hubble's Law
The cosmological principle — the universe is homogeneous and isotropic on large scales — leads to the FLRW metric ds² = −dt² + a(t)² [dr²/(1−kr²) + r² (dθ² + sin²θ dφ²)], where a(t) is the scale factor and k = {−1, 0, +1} describes open, flat, or closed spatial geometry. The Friedmann equations follow from the Einstein field equations: H² = (ȧ/a)² = (8πG/3) ρ − k/a² + Λ/3 (first equation) and ä/a = −(4πG/3)(ρ + 3p) + Λ/3 (second/acceleration equation). The Hubble parameter H(t) = ȧ/a defines the expansion rate; its present value H₀ ≈ 67–74 km/s/Mpc is the Hubble constant. The Hubble law v = H₀ d (observed by Hubble in 1929) relates recessional velocity to distance. The deceleration parameter q₀ = −(ä a / ȧ²)_t₀. The critical density ρ_c = 3H²/(8πG) defines the density of a flat universe. The density parameters Ω_m = ρ_m/ρ_c, Ω_r = ρ_r/ρ_c, Ω_Λ = Λ/(3H²), and Ω_k = −k/(a²H²) satisfy Ω_m + Ω_r + Ω_Λ + Ω_k = 1. The redshift z is related to the scale factor by 1 + z = a₀/a. The comoving distance d_C = c ∫₀ᶻ dz/H(z). The proper distance d = a(t) d_C. The particle horizon d_H = c ∫₀ᵗ dt/a(t) limits causal contact. The cosmic distance ladder calibrates distances from parallax to Cepheids to Type Ia supernovae. The Hubble tension — the discrepancy between early-universe (CMB-based) H₀ ≈ 67.4 km/s/Mpc and late-universe (distance ladder) H₀ ≈ 73 km/s/Mpc — is a major open problem in cosmology.
The Friedmann equations govern the expansion history of the universe, linking the geometry, matter content, and dynamics through a single parameter — the scale factor a(t).
Part 2

🔥 The Hot Big Bang: Nucleosynthesis and the CMB

Thermal History
BBN, Recombination, the CMB Power Spectrum, and the Silk Damping Scale
Big Bang nucleosynthesis (BBN) occurred between t ≈ 1 s and t ≈ 3 min at T ≈ 10^9 K. The neutron-to-proton ratio n/p = exp(−Δm/kT) freezes out at ∼ 1/6, then neutrons decay (n → p + e⁻ + ν̄_e) until deuterium bottleneck at T ≈ 0.07 MeV, when D formation (p + n → D + γ) opens the path to ⁴He via D(p,γ)³He and D(D,n)³He reactions. The primordial ⁴He mass fraction Y_p ≈ 0.25 is determined by the baryon-to-photon ratio η = n_b/n_γ. Recombination at z ≈ 1100 (t ≈ 380,000 yr, T ≈ 3000 K) occurs when free electrons combine with protons to form neutral hydrogen: p + e⁻ → H + γ. The Saha equation for hydrogen ionization x_e = n_e/n_p satisfies x_e²/(1−x_e) = 1/(η n_γ) (m_e kT/2πℏ²)^{3/2} e^{−I_H/kT}. The visibility function peaks at z ≈ 1100 — the surface of last scattering. The CMB temperature today is T₀ = 2.725 K, with a perfect blackbody spectrum (COBE FIRAS measured the deviation < 10⁻⁵). The CMB anisotropies are expanded in spherical harmonics: ΔT/T = Σ_{ℓ,m} a_{ℓm} Y_{ℓm}(θ,φ). The angular power spectrum C_ℓ = ⟨|a_{ℓm}|²⟩ shows acoustic peaks from sound waves in the photon-baryon plasma before recombination. The first peak at ℓ ≈ 200 corresponds to the angular size of the sound horizon at last scattering, measuring the spatial curvature (Ω_k). The Sakharov oscillations — the compression and rarefaction of the plasma — produce alternating peaks. The Silk damping scale (photon diffusion) exponentially suppresses power at ℓ > 2000. The polarization E-modes and B-modes (from gravitational lensing and potentially primordial gravitational waves) provide additional probes. Sunyaev-Zel'dovich clusters and the integrated Sachs-Wolfe effect (ISW) probe the growth of structure and dark energy.
The CMB is the oldest light in the universe, encoding a snapshot of the universe at age 380,000 years — its anisotropies contain a wealth of cosmological parameters.
Part 3

🌑 Dark Matter and Dark Energy

Dark Components
Evidence for Dark Matter, the Cosmological Constant, and the Hubble Tension
Dark matter evidence spans multiple scales. On galactic scales, flat rotation curves (Rubin, Ford) require an extended dark halo with M/L >> 1. Gravitational lensing — both strong (Einstein rings, multiple images) and weak (statistical shear) — maps the total mass distribution. The Bullet Cluster provides direct evidence: the X-ray gas lags behind the collisionless dark matter (traced by weak lensing), separating baryonic from dark mass. The ΛCDM model uses cold dark matter (CDM) — non-relativistic, collisionless particles interacting only gravitationally. Candidates include WIMPs (weakly interacting massive particles at the electroweak scale predicted by supersymmetry), axions (from the Peccei-Quinn solution to the strong CP problem), and sterile neutrinos. Direct detection experiments (LZ, XENONnT, DarkSide) search for nuclear recoils from WIMP-nucleus scattering. Dark energy was discovered via Type Ia supernovae (Perlmutter 1998, Riess 1998): high-z SNe Ia are dimmer than expected in a matter-dominated universe, implying accelerated expansion (q₀ < 0). The simplest explanation is the cosmological constant Λ: a vacuum energy density ρ_Λ = Λ/(8πG). The equation of state w = p/ρ characterizes dark energy: w = −1 for a cosmological constant, w > −1 for quintessence (a scalar field slowly rolling down a potential). The cosmological constant problem: quantum field theory predicts a vacuum energy ∼ (10^19 GeV)^4 / (8π) ≈ 10^74 GeV^4, while the observed dark energy is ∼ 10^{−47} GeV^4 — a discrepancy of 120 orders of magnitude. The coincidence problem asks why Ω_m ∼ Ω_Λ at the present epoch when they scale so differently (ρ_m ∝ a⁻³, ρ_Λ ∝ const). Future probes (Euclid, Roman, Rubin LSST, DESI) will constrain w and its time evolution w(a) = w₀ + w_a(1−a).
Ordinary baryonic matter constitutes only ∼5% of the cosmic energy budget — the universe is dominated by dark matter (∼27%) and dark energy (∼68%), two components whose fundamental nature remains unknown.
Part 4

💥 Cosmic Inflation and the ΛCDM Concordance Model

Early Universe
The Inflationary Paradigm, Primordial Perturbations, and the Standard Model of Cosmology
Cosmic inflation (Guth 1981, Linde 1982, Albrecht-Steinhardt 1982) postulates a period of exponential expansion driven by a scalar field φ (the inflaton) with potential V(φ). The slow-roll conditions: ε = M_Pl² (V'/V)² / (16π) << 1 and η = M_Pl² V''/(8π V) << 1. During slow-roll, H² ≈ (8πG/3) V ≈ const, so a(t) ∝ exp(Ht). Inflation solves the horizon problem (the CMB is uniform across causally disconnected regions) and the flatness problem (Ω₀ is very close to 1). Quantum fluctuations in the inflaton field generate primordial curvature perturbations with nearly scale-invariant power spectrum P_ζ(k) = A_s (k/k_*) ^{n_s−1}. The scalar spectral index n_s ≈ 0.965 (Planck 2018) deviates from exact scale invariance, supporting inflation. Tensor modes (primordial gravitational waves) produce B-mode polarization in the CMB, parameterized by the tensor-to-scalar ratio r < 0.036 (BICEP/Keck 2021). The ΛCDM concordance model parameters from Planck 2018: H₀ = 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc, Ω_m = 0.315 ± 0.007, Ω_Λ = 0.685 ± 0.007, Ω_b = 0.0493 ± 0.0003, n_s = 0.965 ± 0.004, σ₈ = 0.811 ± 0.006 (amplitude of matter fluctuations). The matter power spectrum P(k) grows from the primordial spectrum via the transfer function T(k) (which encodes the matter-radiation equality scale k_eq). Baryon acoustic oscillations (BAO) in the galaxy distribution provide a standard ruler at the sound horizon scale ∼ 150 Mpc. The cosmic web of voids, filaments, and clusters emerges from gravitational collapse of the primordial density field, described by the Zel'dovich approximation and N-body simulations. The end of the dark ages (z ∼ 6–15) witnessed the formation of the first stars (Population III) and galaxies during the Epoch of Reionization.
Inflation provides a causal mechanism for the origin of structure in the universe, generating the primordial seeds for all cosmic structures from quantum fluctuations stretched to cosmological scales.
