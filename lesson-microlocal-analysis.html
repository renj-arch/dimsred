Microlocal Analysis — Pseudodifferential Operators, Wavefront Sets & the Fourier Integral

Microlocal analysis studies linear partial differential equations at the level of "directions" in phase space — the cotangent bundle T*X of a manifold. By refining the concept of singularities from points in space to points in phase space, it reveals the fine structure of solutions to PDEs. Central objects include pseudodifferential operators (ΨDOs), which generalize differential operators with symbol calculus; the wavefront set of a distribution, which captures both the location and the direction of singularities; and Fourier integral operators (FIOs), which move singularities along canonical transformations. Landmark results include the Duistermaat-Hörmander propagation of singularities theorem and the Melrose b-calculus for manifolds with boundary, culminating in the Atiyah-Patodi-Singer index theorem.

"To understand a singularity, we must look in all directions."
— Lars Hörmander
Part 1

📏 Pseudodifferential Operators and Symbols

ΨDOs
Symbol Classes, Quantization, and the Calculus of Pseudodifferential Operators
A pseudodifferential operator a(x,D) acts on functions by the oscillatory integral a(x,D)f(x) = (2π)^{-n} ∫ e^{i⟨x,ξ⟩} a(x,ξ) ̂f(ξ) dξ, where a(x,ξ) is the symbol. The Hörmander symbol classes S^m_{ρ,δ} consist of a ∈ C^∞(Ω×ℝⁿ) satisfying |∂^β_x ∂^α_ξ a(x,ξ)| ≤ C_{αβ} (1+|ξ|)^{m−ρ|α|+δ|β|} for all multi-indices α,β. Here 0 ≤ δ < ρ ≤ 1, and m is the order. The principal symbol σ_m(a)(x,ξ) = lim_{t→∞} t^{-m} a(x,tξ) captures the leading-order homogenous behavior. The composition formula: the symbol of a(x,D)∘b(x,D) is given by the Leibniz product a♯b ∼ Σ_α (∂^α_ξ a)(D^α_x b)/α! (the Kohn-Nirenberg product). An operator is elliptic of order m if its principal symbol satisfies |a(x,ξ)| ≥ c|ξ|^m for large ξ. An elliptic operator admits a parametrix — a two-sided inverse modulo smoothing operators (those with symbols in S^{-∞}). This is the foundation of elliptic regularity: if P is elliptic and Pu is smooth, then u is smooth.
Pseudodifferential operators extend differential operators by allowing symbols that are not polynomials in ξ; their symbol calculus mirrors the algebra of differential operators with powerful asymptotic control.
Part 2

🌍 Wavefront Sets and Microlocal Regularity

Wavefront
The Wavefront Set, Microlocal Smoothness, and Propagation of Singularities
The wavefront set WF(u) ⊂ T^*X\0 of a distribution u refines the singular support: (x₀,ξ₀) ∉ WF(u) iff there exists a cutoff φ ∈ C^∞_c with φ(x₀)≠0 and a conic neighborhood Γ of ξ₀ such that |(φu)^(ξ)| ≤ C_N(1+|ξ|)^{-N} for all N in Γ. In other words, u is microlocally smooth at (x₀,ξ₀) if the Fourier transform decays rapidly in a cone of directions. The microlocal smoothing property: if WF(u) ∩ ell(a) = ∅ (where ell(a) = {(x,ξ) : σ(a) ≠ 0} is the elliptic set), then a(x,D)u is smooth. The propagation of singularities theorem (Duistermaat-Hörmander, 1972): let P be a real principal type operator with principal symbol p (real, homogeneous of degree m) and characteristic set Σ = {p = 0}. For Pu = f, we have WF(u)\WF(f) ⊂ Σ, and moreover WF(u) is a union of bicharacteristic strips — the integral curves of the Hamilton vector field H_p = Σ (∂p/∂ξ_i ∂/∂x_i − ∂p/∂x_i ∂/∂ξ_i) on T^*X\0. This means singularities travel along null bicharacteristics of P. The microlocal Holmgren uniqueness theorem says that if Pu = 0 and u vanishes on one side of a non-characteristic hypersurface, then u vanishes microlocally near the hypersurface.
The wavefront set records both where singularities occur and in which directions they oscillate; the propagation theorem shows that singularities flow along Hamiltonian trajectories in phase space.
Part 3

🧮 Fourier Integral Operators

FIOs
Canonical Relations, the Hörmander-Egorov Theorem, and Parametrices
A Fourier integral operator (FIO) A: C^∞(Y) → D'(X) of order m with canonical relation Λ ⊂ T^*X × T^*Y is locally given by A f(x) = ∫ e^{iφ(x,y,θ)} a(x,y,θ) f(y) dy dθ, where φ is a non-degenerate phase function (homogeneous of degree 1 in θ and with dφ non-vanishing on the critical set C_φ = {(x,y,θ): d_θ φ = 0}). The canonical relation Λ = {(x,d_x φ; y,−d_y φ) : (x,y,θ) ∈ C_φ}. The Hörmander-Egorov theorem: if F is an FIO whose canonical relation is the graph of a canonical transformation χ: T^*Y → T^*X, then F^* P F is a ΨDO on Y with principal symbol p∘χ. This conjugates pseudodifferential operators by canonical transformations, a cornerstone of microlocal reduction. The composition of FIOs corresponds to composition of canonical relations under a clean intersection condition. The solution operator to the wave equation ∂²_t u − Δ u = 0 is a FIO: the fundamental solution E(t) has WF(E(t)) = {(x,ξ; y,−η)} where (x,ξ) and (y,η) are related by geodesic flow. The L²-boundedness of FIOs holds under the graph condition: the canonical relation is a local canonical graph.
Fourier integral operators generalize ΨDOs by allowing phase functions beyond linear ones; they transport singularities along canonical transformations, making them the natural tool for solving hyperbolic equations.
Part 4

🔥 b-Calculus and Index Theory

b-Calculus
Melrose b-Calculus, the APS Index Theorem, and Analytic Torsion
The Melrose b-calculus provides a pseudodifferential framework for manifolds with boundary. A b-vector field on a manifold X with boundary ∂X is a vector field tangent to the boundary. The b-cotangent bundle ^bT^*X is the dual of the b-tangent bundle, where boundary-defining functions x introduce a degeneracy. The b-pseudodifferential operators form a calculus closed under composition, with a b-wavefront set that respects the boundary structure. The b-index theorem: for Dirac-type operators on manifolds with boundary, the index is given by the Atiyah-Patodi-Singer (APS) index theorem: ind(D) = ∫_M ̂A(Ω) + η(∂M), where η(∂M) is the eta invariant — a spectral invariant measuring the asymmetry of the spectrum of the boundary operator. The adiabatic limit relates the APS index to the b-calculus through stretching the boundary. The Cheeger-Müller theorem equates the analytic torsion (Ray-Singer torsion) with the Reidemeister torsion, a topological invariant. The Witten deformation — deforming the de Rham complex by d_t = e^{-tf} d e^{tf} for a Morse function f — provides analytic proofs of Morse inequalities via supersymmetric quantum mechanics, with instantons connecting critical points. Fukaya's Floer homology has deep connections to microlocal analysis through the study of Lagrangian intersections and the Morse theory of the action functional.
The b-calculus tames boundary singularities by adapting the pseudodifferential machinery to manifolds with boundary, leading to index theorems and connections to topological invariants.
