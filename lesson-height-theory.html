Height Theory — the Faltings Height, the Arakelov Height & the Szpiro Conjecture

Height theory studies the arithmetic complexity of points on algebraic varieties using height functions — a central tool in Diophantine geometry. The Weil height on projective space ℙⁿ(̄ℚ) is defined as h(P) = (1/[K:ℚ]) Σ_{v} log max_i |x_i|_v for a point P = [x₀:…:xₙ] defined over K. For an abelian variety A, the Néron-Tate (canonical) height ̂h(P) = lim_{n→∞} h([n]P)/n² is quadratic and non-degenerate modulo torsion — it provides a positive-definite quadratic form on A(K) ⊗ ℝ. The Faltings height of an abelian variety is a normalized invariant under base change, defined via the Arakelov metric on the Hodge bundle, and plays a central role in Faltings' proof of the Mordell conjecture. The Arakelov height incorporates arithmetic intersection theory at archimedean places. The Szpiro conjecture bounds the minimal discriminant of an elliptic curve by a power of its conductor — equivalent to the ABC conjecture via the Frey curve construction. The Bogomolov conjecture (proved by Ullmo) states that non-torsion points on a curve of genus ≥ 2 have a positive lower bound on the Néron-Tate height. The Vojta's conjecture generalizes these ideas to higher dimensions.

"Heights are the arithmetic shadow of the Arakelov geometry."
— Gerd Faltings
Part 1

📏 The Néron-Tate Height on Abelian Varieties

Canonical Heights
The Weil Height, Néron-Tate Height, and the Néron-Tate Pairing
The Weil height on ℙⁿ(̄ℚ) is defined as h(P) = (1/[K:ℚ]) Σ_{v} log max_i |x_i|_v, where v runs over all places of K and |·|_v are normalized absolute values. For an abelian variety A defined over a number field K, the Néron-Tate (canonical) height is the limit ̂h(P) = lim_{n→∞} h([n]P)/n². This limit exists and satisfies: (1) Quadraticity: ̂h(mP) = m²·̂h(P) for all integers m; (2) The Néron-Tate pairing: ⟨P, Q⟩ = (̂h(P+Q) − ̂h(P) − ̂h(Q))/2 is a symmetric bilinear form on A(K) ⊗ ℝ; (3) Kernel: ̂h(P) = 0 iff P is a torsion point; (4) Positive-definiteness modulo torsion — the pairing induces a positive-definite quadratic form on A(K) ⊗ ℝ / (torsion), a consequence of the Hodge index theorem on the Jacobian. On the Jacobian variety J(C) of a smooth projective curve C, the Néron-Tate height of a divisor class is linked to the canonical height of the curve via the Neron-Tate pairing on the Jacobian. The Schinzel-Zassenhaus theorem gives a lower bound on the minimal height of a non-torsion point: for any non-torsion point P ∈ ℚ̄^×, the maximal absolute value of conjugates of P satisfies an inequality related to the height.
The Néron-Tate height is a canonical quadratic form on abelian varieties; its kernel is precisely the torsion subgroup, making it a powerful tool for studying the Mordell-Weil group.
Part 2

🌍 The Faltings Height and its Applications

Global Invariants
The Faltings Height, the Hodge Bundle, and the Proof of the Mordell Conjecture
The Faltings height h_F(A) of an abelian variety A over a number field K is defined via Arakelov geometry: consider the Hodge bundle ω_{A/K} = det Ω^1_{A/K} (the determinant of the sheaf of invariant differentials). Equip it with the Arakelov metric at archimedean places — the metric induced by the inner product (α, β) = (i/2) ∫_{A(ℂ)} α ∧ β̄ on the space of holomorphic n-forms (n = dim A). The Faltings height is the arithmetic degree of (ω_{A/K}, ‖·‖), normalized by the complex case: h_F(A) = (1/[K:ℚ]) deĝ(ω_{A/K}). Properties: h_F is invariant under isogeny (the Faltings isogeny theorem) and satisfies a lower bound in terms of the discriminant of K. Faltings' proof of the Mordell conjecture proceeds in two steps: (1) The Shafarevich conjecture (proved by Faltings): for a number field K and a finite set S of places, there are only finitely many isomorphism classes of abelian varieties of a fixed dimension g with good reduction outside S. This is proved by showing that the Faltings height is bounded below and that the set of abelian varieties with bounded height is finite. (2) The Parshin trick: given a curve C of genus ≥ 2 over K and a rational point P ∈ C(K), one constructs a finite étale cover C_P → ℙ¹_K that ramifies only over a fixed set S, and associates to each rational point an abelian variety (the Jacobian of C_P) with good reduction outside S. This gives an injection from C(K) into the finite set of such abelian varieties, proving finiteness of C(K). The Tate conjecture for abelian varieties over number fields (also proved by Faltings): the ℓ-adic representation ρ_ℓ: Gal(̄K/K) → GL(V_ℓ(A)) on the Tate module is semisimple, and the natural map Hom_K(A, B) ⊗ ℤ_ℓ → Hom_{Gal}(V_ℓ(A), V_ℓ(B)) is an isomorphism.
The Faltings height is a deep arithmetic invariant of abelian varieties; its boundedness and finiteness properties are the engine behind Faltings' resolution of the Mordell conjecture.
Part 3

🧮 The Szpiro Conjecture and the ABC Conjecture

Conductors and Discriminants
The Szpiro Conjecture, Frey Curves, and the Masser-Oesterlé ABC Conjecture
The Szpiro conjecture (proposed by Szpiro, 1981) concerns elliptic curves over ℚ: for an elliptic curve E/ℚ with minimal discriminant Δ and conductor N, for any ε > 0 there exists a constant C_ε such that |Δ| ≤ C_ε N^{6+ε}. Equivalently, the height h(E) = (1/12) log |Δ| + O(1) is comparable to log N for semistable curves. The exponent 6 is optimal: for elliptic curves with complex multiplication, log|Δ| ∼ 6 log N. The Szpiro conjecture is equivalent to the ABC conjecture (Masser-Oesterlé, 1985): for any ε > 0, there exists C_ε such that for any coprime integers a, b, c with a + b = c, we have rad(abc) ≥ c·C_ε, where rad(n) = Π_{p|n} p. The equivalence uses the Frey curve construction: given a solution a + b = c, the Frey elliptic curve E: y² = x(x − a)(x + b) has minimal discriminant Δ = (abc)² and conductor N ≈ rad(abc). The Szpiro bound |Δ| ≤ C_ε N^{6+ε} becomes (abc)² ≤ C_ε rad(abc)^{6+ε}, which after taking square roots gives the ABC conjecture. Conversely, ABC implies Szpiro. The exponent 6+ε is sharp in the sense that the ABC conjecture with exponent 1+ε is optimal. The Mochizuki's inter-universal Teichmüller theory (IUT) claims to prove the ABC conjecture (and hence Szpiro), but the proof remains controversial and has not been fully accepted by the broader mathematical community. The Mordell-Weil theorem (the group of rational points on an abelian variety is finitely generated) is a fundamental background result: its proof via heights (infinite descent) foreshadows the deep connections between heights and arithmetic.
The Szpiro conjecture bounds the discriminant of an elliptic curve by its conductor; its equivalence to the ABC conjecture shows a deep unity between elliptic curves and additive number theory.
Part 4

🔥 The Bogomolov Conjecture and Vojta's Conjecture

Equidistribution
The Bogomolov Conjecture, Vojta's Conjecture, and the Mordell-Lang Conjecture
The Bogomolov conjecture (proved by Ullmo, 1998): for a smooth projective curve C of genus g ≥ 2 over a number field K, there exists ε > 0 such that the set {P ∈ C(̄K): ̂h(P) < ε} is finite, where ̂h is the Néron-Tate height on the Jacobian J(C) restricted to C via the Abel-Jacobi embedding. This refines the Mordell conjecture by showing that non-torsion points on curves of genus ≥ 2 are not merely finite but have a positive lower bound on their height. The proof uses: (1) Equidistribution of small points (Szpiro-Ullmo-Zhang): if a sequence of points P_n ∈ C(̄K) satisfies ̂h(P_n) → 0, then the Galois orbits of P_n become equidistributed on C(ℂ) with respect to the Arakelov metric — the canonical probability measure obtained from the Arakelov intersection pairing on C. (2) Arakelov theory on the Jacobian: the height of a point on C is the arithmetic degree of the line bundle L = O_C(1) under the Abel-Jacobi map. The Vojta's conjecture (1987) is a far-reaching generalization of the ABC conjecture, the Mordell conjecture, and the Schmidt's subspace theorem in Diophantine approximation. For a proper smooth variety X over a number field K with an ample divisor D and a normal crossings divisor A, Vojta's conjecture gives an inequality relating the height h_D(P) to the ramification of finite covers and the canonical divisor K_X. In the simplest case, for X = ℙ¹ with D = (0) + (∞), Vojta's conjecture reduces to the ABC conjecture. For a curve C of genus ≥ 2, Vojta's conjecture implies the Mordell conjecture (finiteness of rational points). The Lang-Vojta conjecture: on a variety of general type (i.e., with ample canonical bundle), the set of rational points is not Zariski dense — this is a sweeping generalization of the Mordell conjecture. The Mordell-Lang conjecture (proved by McQuillan, Hrushovski, Buium) classifies the intersection of a subvariety of an abelian variety with a finitely generated subgroup: it states that if X ⊂ A is a subvariety of an abelian variety over a field of characteristic 0, and Γ ⊂ A(K) is a finitely generated subgroup, then X(K) ∩ Γ is a finite union of cosets of subgroups of Γ. Heights are central to the proof via the method of infinite descent and the Vojta inequality.
The Bogomolov conjecture gives a positive height gap for non-torsion points on curves; Vojta's conjecture unifies Diophantine approximation, the ABC conjecture, and the Mordell conjecture in a single framework.
