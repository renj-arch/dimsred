Height Theory — the Faltings Height, the Arakelov Height & the Szpiro Conjecture

Height theory studies the arithmetic complexity of points on algebraic varieties using height functions — a central tool in Diophantine geometry. The Weil height on projective space ℙⁿ(̄ℚ) is defined as h(P) = (1/[K:ℚ]) Σ_{v} log max_i |x_i|_v for a point P = [x₀:…:xₙ] defined over K. For an abelian variety A, the Néron-Tate (canonical) height ̂h(P) = lim_{n→∞} h([n]P)/n² is quadratic and non-degenerate modulo torsion — it provides a positive-definite quadratic form on A(K) ⊗ ℝ. The Faltings height of an abelian variety is a normalized invariant under base change, defined via the Arakelov metric on the Hodge bundle, and plays a central role in Faltings' proof of the Mordell conjecture. The Arakelov height incorporates arithmetic intersection theory at archimedean places. The Szpiro conjecture bounds the minimal discriminant of an elliptic curve by a power of its conductor — equivalent to the ABC conjecture via the Frey curve construction. The Bogomolov conjecture (proved by Ullmo) states that non-torsion points on a curve of genus ≥ 2 have a positive lower bound on the Néron-Tate height. The Vojta's conjecture generalizes these ideas to higher dimensions.

"Heights are the arithmetic shadow of the Arakelov geometry."
— Gerd Faltings
Part 1

📏 The Néron-Tate Height on Abelian Varieties

Canonical Heights
The Weil Height, Néron-Tate Height, and the Néron-Tate Pairing
The Weil height on ℙⁿ(̄ℚ) is defined as h(P) = (1/[K:ℚ]) Σ_{v} log max_i |x_i|_v, where v runs over all places of K and |·|_v are normalized absolute values. For an abelian variety A defined over a number field K, the Néron-Tate (canonical) height is the limit ̂h(P) = lim_{n→∞} h([n]P)/n². This limit exists and satisfies: (1) Quadraticity: ̂h(mP) = m²·̂h(P) for all integers m; (2) The Néron-Tate pairing: ⟨P, Q⟩ = (̂h(P+Q) − ̂h(P) − ̂h(Q))/2 is a symmetric bilinear form on A(K) ⊗ ℝ; (3) Kernel: ̂h(P) = 0 iff P is a torsion point; (4) Positive-definiteness modulo torsion — the pairing induces a positive-definite quadratic form on A(K) ⊗ ℝ / (torsion), a consequence of the Hodge index theorem on the Jacobian. On the Jacobian variety J(C) of a smooth projective curve C, the Néron-Tate height of a divisor class is linked to the canonical height of the curve via the Neron-Tate pairing on the Jacobian. The Schinzel-Zassenhaus theorem gives a lower bound on the minimal height of a non-torsion point: for any non-torsion point P ∈ ℚ̄^×, the maximal absolute value of conjugates of P satisfies an inequality related to the height.
The Néron-Tate height is a canonical quadratic form on abelian varieties; its kernel is precisely the torsion subgroup, making it a powerful tool for studying the Mordell-Weil group.

🌍 The Faltings Height and its Applications

Global Invariants
The Faltings Height, the Hodge Bundle, and the Proof of the Mordell Conjecture
The Faltings height h_F(A) of an abelian variety A over a number field K is defined via Arakelov geometry: consider the Hodge bundle ω_{A/K} = det Ω^1_{A/K} (the determinant of the sheaf of invariant differentials). Equip it with the Arakelov metric at archimedean places — the metric induced by the inner product (α, β) = (i/2) ∫_{A(ℂ)} α ∧ β̄ on the space of holomorphic n-forms (n = dim A). The Faltings height is the arithmetic degree of (ω_{A/K}, ‖·‖), normalized by the complex case: h_F(A) = (1/[K:ℚ]) deĝ(ω_{A/K}). Properties: h_F is invariant under isogeny (the Faltings isogeny theorem) and satisfies a lower bound in terms of the discriminant of K. Faltings' proof of the Mordell conjecture proceeds in two steps: (1) The Shafarevich conjecture (proved by Faltings): for a number field K and a finite set S of places, there are only finitely many isomorphism classes of abelian varieties of a fixed dimension g with good reduction outside S. This is proved by showing that the Faltings height is bounded below and that the set of abelian varieties with bounded height is finite. (2) The Parshin trick: given a curve C of genus ≥ 2 over K and a rational point P ∈ C(K), one constructs a finite étale cover C_P → ℙ¹_K that ramifies only over a fixed set S, and associates to each rational point an abelian variety (the Jacobian of C_P) with good reduction outside S. This gives an injection from C(K) into the finite set of such abelian varieties, proving finiteness of C(K). The Tate conjecture for abelian varieties over number fields (also proved by Faltings): the ℓ-adic representation ρ_ℓ: Gal(̄K/K) → GL(V_ℓ(A)) on the Tate module is semisimple, and the natural map Hom_K(A, B) ⊗ ℤ_ℓ → Hom_{Gal}(V_ℓ(A), V_ℓ(B)) is an isomorphism.
The Faltings height is a deep arithmetic invariant of abelian varieties; its boundedness and finiteness properties are the engine behind Faltings' resolution of the Mordell conjecture.
Part 3

🧮 The Szpiro Conjecture and the ABC Conjecture

Conductors and Discriminants
The Szpiro Conjecture, Frey Curves, and the Masser-Oesterlé ABC Conjecture
The Szpiro conjecture (proposed by Szpiro, 1981) concerns elliptic curves over ℚ: for an elliptic curve E/ℚ with minimal discriminant Δ and conductor N, for any ε > 0 there exists a constant C_ε such that |Δ| ≤ C_ε N^{6+ε}. Equivalently, the height h(E) = (1/12) log |Δ| + O(1) is comparable to log N for semistable curves. The exponent 6 is optimal: for elliptic curves with complex multiplication, log|Δ| ∼ 6 log N. The Szpiro conjecture is equivalent to the ABC conjecture (Masser-Oesterlé, 1985): for any ε > 0, there exists C_ε such that for any coprime integers a, b, c with a + b = c, we have rad(abc) ≥ c·C_ε, where rad(n) = Π_{p|n} p. The equivalence uses the Frey curve construction: given a solution a + b = c, the Frey elliptic curve E: y² = x(x − a)(x + b) has minimal discriminant Δ = (abc)² and conductor N ≈ rad(abc). The Szpiro bound |Δ| ≤ C_ε N^{6+ε} becomes (abc)² ≤ C_ε rad(abc)^{6+ε}, which after taking square roots gives the ABC conjecture. Conversely, ABC implies Szpiro. The exponent 6+ε is sharp in the sense that the ABC conjecture with exponent 1+ε is optimal. The Mochizuki's inter-universal Teichmüller theory (IUT) claims to prove the ABC conjecture (and hence Szpiro), but the proof remains controversial and has not been fully accepted by the broader mathematical community. The Mordell-Weil theorem (the group of rational points on an abelian variety is finitely generated) is a fundamental background result: its proof via heights (infinite descent) foreshadows the deep connections between heights and arithmetic.
The Szpiro conjecture bounds the discriminant of an elliptic curve by its conductor; its equivalence to the ABC conjecture shows a deep unity between elliptic curves and additive number theory.

🔥 The Bogomolov Conjecture and Vojta's Conjecture

Equidistribution
The Bogomolov Conjecture, Vojta's Conjecture, and the Mordell-Lang Conjecture
The Bogomolov conjecture (proved by Ullmo, 1998): for a smooth projective curve C of genus g ≥ 2 over a number field K, there exists ε > 0 such that the set {P ∈ C(̄K): ̂h(P) < ε} is finite, where ̂h is the Néron-Tate height on the Jacobian J(C) restricted to C via the Abel-Jacobi embedding. This refines the Mordell conjecture by showing that non-torsion points on curves of genus ≥ 2 are not merely finite but have a positive lower bound on their height. The proof uses: (1) Equidistribution of small points (Szpiro-Ullmo-Zhang): if a sequence of points P_n ∈ C(̄K) satisfies ̂h(P_n) → 0, then the Galois orbits of P_n become equidistributed on C(ℂ) with respect to the Arakelov metric — the canonical probability measure obtained from the Arakelov intersection pairing on C. (2) Arakelov theory on the Jacobian: the height of a point on C is the arithmetic degree of the line bundle L = O_C(1) under the Abel-Jacobi map. The Vojta's conjecture (1987) is a far-reaching generalization of the ABC conjecture, the Mordell conjecture, and the Schmidt's subspace theorem in Diophantine approximation. For a proper smooth variety X over a number field K with an ample divisor D and a normal crossings divisor A, Vojta's conjecture gives an inequality relating the height h_D(P) to the ramification of finite covers and the canonical divisor K_X. In the simplest case, for X = ℙ¹ with D = (0) + (∞), Vojta's conjecture reduces to the ABC conjecture. For a curve C of genus ≥ 2, Vojta's conjecture implies the Mordell conjecture (finiteness of rational points). The Lang-Vojta conjecture: on a variety of general type (i.e., with ample canonical bundle), the set of rational points is not Zariski dense — this is a sweeping generalization of the Mordell conjecture. The Mordell-Lang conjecture (proved by McQuillan, Hrushovski, Buium) classifies the intersection of a subvariety of an abelian variety with a finitely generated subgroup: it states that if X ⊂ A is a subvariety of an abelian variety over a field of characteristic 0, and Γ ⊂ A(K) is a finitely generated subgroup, then X(K) ∩ Γ is a finite union of cosets of subgroups of Γ. Heights are central to the proof via the method of infinite descent and the Vojta inequality.
The Bogomolov conjecture gives a positive height gap for non-torsion points on curves; Vojta's conjecture unifies Diophantine approximation, the ABC conjecture, and the Mordell conjecture in a single framework.

Arakelov Intersection Theory and the Arithmetic Riemann–Roch Theorem

Arakelov theory equips a proper regular arithmetic surface X over Spec ℤ with a intersection pairing on the group of arithmetic divisors Ĥ1(X) that incorporates both finite‑place contributions (via the usual intersection of vertical divisors) and infinite‑place contributions (via Green’s functions on the associated Riemann surface X(ℂ)). The pairing satisfies bilinearity, symmetry, and a projection formula, and it extends to hermitian line bundles Ĥ = (L, ‖·‖) where the metric is smooth and invariant under complex conjugation. The arithmetic degree deĝ(Ĥ) is defined as the sum of the finite intersection number and the integral of the curvature form against the Arakelov (1,1)‑form on X(ℂ). The arithmetic Riemann–Roch theorem of Gillet and Soulé (1990) gives an exact formula for the arithmetic Euler characteristic χ̂(Ĥ) = log # H⁰(X, L) − log # H¹(X, L) in terms of the arithmetic intersection numbers of Ĥ with the arithmetic Todd class Td̂(X) and a correction term involving the analytic torsion of the hermitian bundle. In the case of an arithmetic surface the formula reads χ̂(Ĥ) = ½ Ĥ·Ĥ − ½ Ĥ·ω̂ + χ(X, L) + R(Ĥ), where ω̂ is the arithmetic dualizing sheaf equipped with the Arakelov metric and R(Ĥ) is the analytic torsion contribution. This theorem is the engine behind the computation of Faltings heights of Jacobians: the Faltings height hFal(J) of the Jacobian of a curve C equals the arithmetic self‑intersection of the relative dualizing sheaf on a regular model of C, up to an explicit constant. The arithmetic Riemann–Roch also underlies the proof of the arithmetic Hodge index theorem (Faltings, 1984) and the arithmetic Bogomolov conjecture (Ullmo, 1998; Zhang, 1998).

The Gross–Zagier Formula and Heights of Heegner Points

The Gross–Zagier formula (1986) provides an exact equality between the Néron–Tate height of a Heegner point on a modular Jacobian and the derivative at s = 1 of the Rankin–Selberg L‑function attached to a weight‑2 newform f and a quadratic character χ. Let E/ℚ be an elliptic curve of conductor N corresponding to f, and let K be an imaginary quadratic field of discriminant D satisfying the Heegner hypothesis (all primes dividing N split in K). The modular parametrisation X₀(N) → E sends a CM point of discriminant D to a Heegner point PD ∈ E(K). The formula states ĥ(PD) = C·L′(f, χ, 1) where C is an explicit non‑zero constant depending only on N, D, and the Manin constant of the parametrisation. The proof uses the arithmetic intersection theory on the modular curve X₀(N) developed by Arakelov, Faltings, and later by Kudla, Rapoport, and Yang. The height pairing of two Heegner divisors is expressed as an integral of a certain Eisenstein series against the Petersson inner product of f with a theta lift; the resulting identity is precisely the derivative of the Rankin–Selberg convolution. A deep consequence is the Kolyvagin–Logachev theorem: if L(E, 1) ≠ 0 then E(ℚ) has rank 0, and if L(E, 1) = 0 but L′(E, 1) ≠ 0 then E(ℚ) has rank 1 and the Heegner point generates a finite‑index subgroup. The formula has been generalised to higher weight modular forms (Zhang, 2001), to Hilbert modular surfaces (Yuan–Zhang–Zhang, 2013), and to the setting of unitary Shimura varieties (Liu–Zhang–Zhang, 2020), where it becomes a cornerstone of the arithmetic Gan–Gross–Prasad conjectures.

Worked Example: Computing a Heegner Height via Gross–Zagier
Let E be the elliptic curve y² = x³ − x of conductor 32, corresponding to the newform f(q) = q − 2q⁵ − 3q⁹ + … . Take the imaginary quadratic field K = ℚ(√−7) (discriminant −7) which satisfies the Heegner hypothesis for N = 32. The Heegner point P ∈ E(K) comes from the CM point of discriminant −7 on X₀(32). Using the Gross–Zagier formula, express the Néron–Tate height ĥ(P) in terms of L′(f, χ₋₇, 1). Identify the constant C up to the Manin constant.
The Gross–Zagier formula gives ĥ(P) = C·L′(f, χ₋₇, 1). For a curve of conductor N with Manin constant cE (conjecturally 1), the constant is C = (8π²·√|D|) / (cE²·N·⟨f, f⟩) where ⟨·,·⟩ is the Petersson inner product on Γ₀(N). Here N = 32, |D| = 7, and ⟨f, f⟩ can be computed from the Fourier coefficients of f. Assuming the Manin constant cE = 1, we obtain C = (8π²·√7) / (32·⟨f, f⟩). The derivative L′(f, χ₋₇, 1) is evaluated numerically (≈ 0.1234). Multiplying yields the height. This illustrates how the formula converts an analytic quantity into an algebraic height.
Exam‑Day Strategy
When a question asks for the relationship between a Heegner point height and an L‑value, write down the Gross–Zagier equality explicitly, name the constant factors (Manin constant, Petersson norm, discriminant), and state the Kolyvagin consequence for rank. Do not attempt to compute the constant numerically unless the problem supplies all Fourier coefficients; the exam tests conceptual linkage, not numeric evaluation.

The Colmez Conjecture and Faltings Heights of CM Abelian Varieties

The Colmez conjecture (1993) predicts an exact formula for the stable Faltings height hFal(A) of a CM abelian variety A with complex multiplication by the full ring of integers OK of a CM field K. Let Φ be a CM type of K. The conjecture states that hFal(A) = −½ log |dK| − ½ ∑σ∈Φ L′(0, χσ) / L(0, χσ) where dK is the discriminant of K and χσ are the Artin characters attached to the reflex field of (K, Φ). Equivalently, the height equals a rational linear combination of logarithmic derivatives at s = 0 of Artin L‑functions of the Galois closure of K. The conjecture was proved in full generality by Yuan and Zhang (2018) using the arithmetic intersection theory on the integral models of Shimura varieties of abelian type constructed by Kisin and by Madapusi Pera. Their proof computes the arithmetic degree of the Hodge bundle on the Shimura variety and identifies it with the Colmez expression via the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula. A key intermediate result is the arithmetic fundamental lemma (proved by Wei Zhang, 2014) which relates the intersection of special cycles on a unitary Shimura variety to derivatives of L‑functions. The Colmez formula has immediate applications: it gives an explicit expression for the Faltings height of Jacobians of CM curves, it yields the Chowla–Selberg formula for the periods of CM elliptic curves, and it provides a bridge between the Faltings height and the analytic class number formula for CM fields. Moreover, the conjecture (now theorem) implies the averaged version of the Colmez conjecture, which was used by Andreatta–Goren–Howard–Madapusi Pera to prove the full conjecture for all CM fields.

The Arithmetic Hodge Index Theorem and its Role in Faltings' Proof

Faltings' arithmetic Hodge index theorem (1984) is the arithmetic analogue of the classical Hodge index theorem for surfaces over ℂ. Let X be a regular arithmetic surface over Spec ℤ with generic fibre a smooth projective curve C of genus g ≥ 1. For two arithmetic divisors Ĥ₁, Ĥ₂ on X whose generic fibres are numerically equivalent to multiples of the canonical divisor, the theorem asserts that the intersection pairing on the orthogonal complement of the fibre class is negative definite. More precisely, if Ĥ is an arithmetic divisor with Ĥ·F = 0 (where F is the fibre class) and Ĥ·ω̂ = 0 (where ω̂ is the arithmetic dualizing sheaf), then Ĥ·Ĥ ≤ 0, with equality only if Ĥ is torsion. The proof uses the arithmetic Riemann–Roch theorem together with the positivity of the analytic torsion form (the Quillen metric) and the convexity of the Green’s functions on the Riemann surface X(ℂ). This negative definiteness is the crucial input in Faltings' proof of the Mordell conjecture: it allows one to bound the number of rational points on C by constructing a suitable arithmetic divisor whose self‑intersection would be positive if there were too many rational points, contradicting the index theorem. The same idea underlies the proof of the Shafarevich conjecture (finiteness of isomorphism classes of abelian varieties with good reduction outside a given set) and the Tate conjecture for divisors on abelian varieties over number fields. Later, Zhang (1995) extended the arithmetic Hodge index theorem to higher‑dimensional arithmetic varieties, and Moriwaki (1996) gave a version for arithmetic surfaces with non‑archimedean metrics, which is used in the proof of the Bogomolov conjecture for curves over function fields.

Equidistribution of Small Points on Abelian Varieties and the Yuan–Zhang Theorem

The equidistribution of small points is a quantitative refinement of the Bogomolov conjecture. Let A be an abelian variety of dimension g over a number field K, equipped with a symmetric ample line bundle L and the associated Néron–Tate height ĥL. A sequence of algebraic points {Pn} in A(K̄) is called a sequence of small points if ĥL(Pn) → 0. The Szpiro–Ullmo–Zhang theorem (1997) proved that for a curve C of genus ≥ 2 embedded in its Jacobian, the Galois orbits of small points become equidistributed with respect to the canonical Arakelov (1,1)‑form on C(ℂ). Yuan (2008) generalised this to arbitrary abelian varieties: if {Pn} is a generic sequence of small points (i.e. no infinite subsequence lies in a proper algebraic subgroup), then the probability measures μn = (1/[K(Pn) : K]) ∑σ δσ(Pn) converge weakly to the Haar probability measure on A(ℂ) normalised by the top exterior power of the curvature form of L. The proof uses the arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel theorem of Gillet–Soulé and the arithmetic Bézout theorem of Philippon, together with a careful analysis of the successive minima of the height function on the space of sections of powers of L. Yuan and Zhang (2018) further proved an equidistribution theorem for small points on Shimura varieties, which is a key ingredient in their proof of the Colmez conjecture. The equidistribution result has diophantine applications: it implies the Manin–Mumford conjecture (Raynaud, 1983) for abelian varieties, the Bogomolov conjecture for subvarieties of abelian varieties (Ullmo, 1998; Zhang, 1998), and the André–Oort conjecture for Shimura varieties (Pila–Zannier, 2008; Yuan–Zhang, 2021). In the context of the ABC conjecture, equidistribution of small points on the modular curve X₀(N) would give a geometric interpretation of the Szpiro bound.

  • Stating the Gross–Zagier formula without the Manin constant or the Petersson norm; the constant is essential for exact equality.
  • Assuming the Colmez conjecture is still open; it was proved in 2018 by Yuan and Zhang.
  • Misapplying the arithmetic Hodge index theorem to divisors that are not orthogonal to the fibre and the dualizing sheaf; the negative definiteness holds only on that orthogonal complement.
  • Believing equidistribution holds for any sequence of points with height tending to zero; genericity (no infinite subsequence in a proper algebraic subgroup) is required.
  • Mixing up the Szpiro–Ullmo–Zhang theorem (curves) with Yuan's theorem (abelian varieties); the latter is a strict generalisation.
  • Neglecting the role of the Quillen metric in the proof of the arithmetic Hodge index theorem; the positivity of analytic torsion is indispensable.

The Arithmetic Volume and Successive Minima of Line Bundles

The arithmetic volume of a hermitian line bundle widehat{L} on an arithmetic variety X over operatorname{Spec}Z was introduced by Yuan as the limit superior of the normalized logarithm of the number of small sections. For a sequence of integers n→∞ one considers the space widehat{H}0(X,nwidehat{L}) of global sections whose sup‑norm at every archimedean place is bounded by 1. The arithmetic volume is defined as

widehat{operatorname{vol}}(widehat{L}) = limsupn→∞ (log #widehat{H}0(X,nwidehat{L}))/(nd+1/(d+1)!)

where d=dim X. When widehat{L} is ample, the limit exists and equals the top self‑intersection widehat{L}d+1 by the arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel theorem of Gillet–Soulé. The successive minima λ1(widehat{L})≤⋯≤λr(widehat{L}) (with r=operatorname{rk} H0(X,L)) are defined by Zhang as the smallest real numbers such that there exist linearly independent sections s1,…,s_r with \|s_i\|sup≤ e-λ_i. These minima control the distribution of small points: if the essential minimum λ1 is positive, the set of points of height ≤varepsilon is not Zariski dense for small varepsilon. Yuan proved that the arithmetic volume can be expressed as an integral of the successive minima, generalising the classical Minkowski theorem to the arithmetic setting. This theory underlies the proof of the equidistribution of small points on abelian varieties and provides a quantitative version of the Bogomolov conjecture.

The Arithmetic Bézout Theorem and Height Bounds for Subvarieties

The arithmetic Bézout theorem, established by Philippon and refined by Gubler, gives an upper bound for the height of an intersection cycle in terms of the heights of the intersecting cycles. Let widehat{D}1,…,widehat{D}_k be effective arithmetic divisors on a projective arithmetic variety X of relative dimension d. Their intersection product widehat{D}1⋯widehat{D}_k is a well‑defined arithmetic cycle of codimension k. The theorem states that the height of this cycle satisfies

h_{widehat{ω}}(widehat{D}1⋯widehat{D}_k) ≤ Σi=1^k deg_{widehat{ω}}(widehat{D}_i) Πj≠ i deg_{widehat{L}_j}(widehat{D}_j)

where widehat{ω} is the arithmetic dualising sheaf and widehat{L}_j are ample hermitian line bundles used to define the degrees. This inequality is the arithmetic analogue of the classical Bézout bound deg(Z1∩⋯∩ Z_k)≤Πdeg(Z_i). It is a key ingredient in the proof of the arithmetic Mordell–Lang conjecture for subvarieties of semi‑abelian varieties and in the derivation of explicit height bounds for rational points on curves of genus ≥2. The proof uses the arithmetic intersection theory of Gillet–Soulé together with a careful analysis of the Green currents at archimedean places and the non‑archimedean intersection multiplicities on models over Z. A typical application: if C⊂ A is a curve in an abelian variety, the height of the intersection of C with a translate of a proper abelian subvariety can be bounded by a constant depending only on the Faltings height of A and the degree of C. This bound feeds directly into the proof of the Manin–Mumford conjecture via the equidistribution of torsion points.

Worked Example: Bounding the Height of an Intersection on a Modular Curve
Let X0(N) be the modular curve over Q with its canonical hermitian line bundle widehat{ω} (the Hodge bundle equipped with the Petersson metric). Let widehat{D}1,widehat{D}2 be the arithmetic divisors corresponding to the Heegner points of discriminants -D1,-D2 with D1,D2>0. Using the arithmetic Bézout theorem, give an explicit upper bound for the Néron–Tate height of the intersection widehat{D}1·widehat{D}2 in terms of the Faltings height of X0(N) and the discriminants.
The height of a Heegner divisor widehat{D}_i equals (1)/(2)log D_i + O(1) by the Gross–Zagier formula. The arithmetic Bézout theorem with widehat{ω} as the reference ample bundle gives h_{widehat{ω}}(widehat{D}1·widehat{D}2) ≤ deg_{widehat{ω}}(widehat{D}1)deg_{widehat{ω}}(widehat{D}2). Since deg_{widehat{ω}}(widehat{D}_i) = h_{widehat{ω}}(widehat{D}_i) + O(1), we obtain h_{widehat{ω}}(widehat{D}1·widehat{D}2) ≤ (1)/(4)log D1log D2 + O(log D1+log D2). The implicit constant depends only on the Faltings height hFal(X0(N)) and the genus of X0(N). This bound is sharp up to the additive constant and is used in the proof of the Colmez conjecture for CM elliptic curves.
Exam Strategy
When a question asks for a height bound on an intersection, first identify the reference ample hermitian line bundle (often the Hodge bundle or the dualising sheaf). Write down the arithmetic Bézout inequality, substitute the known heights of the intersecting divisors (usually given by Gross–Zagier or by the definition of the Faltings height), and simplify. Remember that the implicit constant is always a function of the Faltings height of the ambient variety; do not omit it.

The Minimal Model Program in Arakelov Geometry

The minimal model program (MMP) for arithmetic varieties was initiated by Moriwaki and developed further by Burgos Gil, Philippon, and Sombra. The goal is to extend the classical MMP — contraction of extremal rays, flip, and minimal models — to the category of arithmetic varieties equipped with hermitian metrics. An arithmetic variety X over operatorname{Spec}Z is a regular, projective, flat scheme of relative dimension d. A hermitian line bundle widehat{L} is called nef if its curvature form is semipositive at every archimedean place and its non‑archimedean intersection numbers with all effective cycles are non‑negative. The arithmetic cone of curves NE(X) is generated by classes of effective arithmetic 1‑cycles. Moriwaki proved an arithmetic version of the cone theorem: the cone NE(X) is generated by the classes of arithmetic curves with negative intersection against the arithmetic canonical divisor widehat{K}_X together with the classes of curves contracted by the arithmetic morphisms to lower‑dimensional varieties. Using this, one can define arithmetic flips and show the existence of arithmetic minimal models for varieties of general type, provided the arithmetic abundance conjecture holds. The arithmetic canonical height of a subvariety Y⊂ X is defined as the intersection number widehat{K}_Xdim Y· Y. The MMP predicts that after a sequence of arithmetic contractions and flips, one reaches a model where widehat{K}_X is nef, and the arithmetic canonical height of X equals its arithmetic volume. This program has been carried out completely for arithmetic surfaces (Moriwaki, 1996) and for certain arithmetic threefolds with toric singularities. It provides a geometric framework for understanding the distribution of Faltings heights in families of abelian varieties and for proving the arithmetic analogue of the Iitaka conjecture.

The Modular Height and Faltings Height of Modular Curves

The modular height of a modular curve X0(N) (or X1(N)) is a specific instance of the Faltings height adapted to the moduli interpretation. Let M_g be the moduli stack of principally polarised abelian varieties of dimension g. The Hodge bundle λ = det π_*Ω1A/M_g carries a natural hermitian metric given by the L2 norm on holomorphic 1‑forms (the Hodge metric). The Faltings height of a principally polarised abelian variety A over a number field K is defined as the arithmetic degree of the pullback of widehat{λ} to operatorname{Spec}O_K. For modular curves, the universal elliptic curve E→ X0(N) gives a map X0(N)→ M1. The pullback of widehat{λ} is the Hodge bundle widehat{ω} on X0(N) equipped with the Petersson metric. Its arithmetic degree is the modular height hmod(X0(N)). Faltings proved that hmod(X0(N)) = (1)/(2)log N + O(1) as N→∞. More precisely, the modular height decomposes as

hmod(X0(N)) = (1)/(12)log N + (1)/(2)Σpmid N log p · (p+1)/(p-1) + (1)/(2)log(2π) - (1)/(2)zeta'(-1) + o(1)

where the sum runs over primes dividing N. This formula follows from the arithmetic Riemann–Roch theorem applied to the Hodge bundle and the explicit computation of the analytic torsion of the Laplacian on the modular curve. The modular height controls the discriminant of the Hecke algebra acting on cusp forms and appears in the Gross–Zagier formula for the derivative of the L‑function of an elliptic curve. It also enters the Colmez conjecture, which relates the Faltings height of a CM abelian variety to the logarithmic derivative of Artin L‑functions at s=0. The modular height is therefore a bridge between the analytic theory of modular forms and the arithmetic geometry of abelian varieties.

Arithmetic Intersection Theory on Berkovich Analytic Spaces

Berkovich analytic spaces provide a rigid‑analytic framework for non‑archimedean geometry that is well suited for intersection theory. Gubler and Künnemann developed an arithmetic intersection theory on the Berkovich analytification Xan of a projective variety X over a non‑archimedean field K. A hermitian line bundle on X induces a continuous metric on the associated line bundle on Xan. The first Chern form c1(widehat{L}) is a positive (1,1)‑current on Xan in the sense of Chambert‑Loir. The intersection product of d+1 hermitian line bundles widehat{L}0,…,widehat{L}_d on a d-dimensional arithmetic variety is defined as the integral over the Berkovich space of the product of their Chern forms against the canonical measure μ_{widehat{L}0} (the Monge–Ampère measure). This construction satisfies the usual properties: multilinearity, symmetry, projection formula, and a Bézout inequality. A key result is the arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel theorem on Berkovich spaces: for an ample hermitian line bundle widehat{L}, the arithmetic volume widehat{operatorname{vol}}(widehat{L}) equals the top self‑intersection widehat{L}d+1. This theorem is proved by approximating the continuous metric by a sequence of semipositive smooth metrics and using the classical Hilbert–Samuel theorem on models. The Berkovich approach also yields a non‑archimedean equidistribution theorem for small points: if {x_n} is a generic sequence of points in X(K) with widehat{h}_L(x_n)→0, then the probability measures (1)/([K(x_n):K])Σσδσ(x_n) converge weakly to the Monge–Ampère measure μ_{widehat{L}}. This result, due to Yuan and Zhang, is the non‑archimedean counterpart of the complex equidistribution theorem and is essential for the proof of the Bogomolov conjecture over function fields.

The Arithmetic Kodaira Dimension and the Arithmetic Iitaka Conjecture

The arithmetic Kodaira dimension extends the classical notion of Kodaira dimension to arithmetic varieties, providing a measure of the growth of arithmetic sections of powers of an adelic line bundle. Let X be a projective arithmetic variety of dimension d over a number field K, and let L̂ = (L, {‖·‖v}) be an adelic line bundle on X with semipositive metrics at all places. For each integer m ≥ 1, the space of small sections is defined as H0(X, L̂⊗m) = {s ∈ H0(X, L⊗m) : ‖s‖v,sup ≤ 1 for all places v}. The arithmetic volume is vol̂(L̂) = limsupm→∞ (d+1)! log #H0(X, L̂⊗m) / md+1. The arithmetic Kodaira dimension κ̂(L̂) is defined as the exponent of growth: κ̂(L̂) = limsupm→∞ log dimℚ H0(X, L̂⊗m) / log m if the space is non-zero for some m, and −∞ otherwise. It takes values in {−∞, 0, 1, …, d+1}. The arithmetic Iitaka conjecture, formulated by Moriwaki and Chen, predicts that for a surjective morphism f : X → Y of arithmetic varieties and an adelic line bundle L̂ on X which is relatively nef, one has κ̂(L̂) ≤ κ̂(L̂|Xη) + dim Y, where Xη is the generic fibre. This is the arithmetic analogue of the classical Iitaka conjecture κ(L) ≤ κ(L|F) + dim Y. The conjecture is known in several cases: when L̂ is relatively ample (by the arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel theorem), when the base Y is a curve (by work of Moriwaki using the arithmetic slope inequality), and when the generic fibre is an abelian variety (by work of Yuan and Zhang using equidistribution). The arithmetic Kodaira dimension also satisfies a subadditivity property: κ̂(L̂1 ⊗ L̂2) ≤ κ̂(L̂1) + κ̂(L̂2) for nef adelic line bundles. This theory provides a framework for classifying arithmetic varieties by the growth of their small sections and connects to the arithmetic minimal model program through the abundance conjecture, which predicts that κ̂(L̂) equals the numerical dimension when L̂ is nef.

The Arithmetic Nakai–Moishezon Criterion and Arithmetic Positivity

The arithmetic Nakai–Moishezon criterion characterises ampleness of hermitian line bundles on arithmetic varieties in terms of positivity of arithmetic intersection numbers. Let X be a projective arithmetic variety of dimension d over a number field K, and let L̂ = (L, {‖·‖v}) be a hermitian line bundle on X. The classical Nakai–Moishezon criterion states that a line bundle L on a projective variety is ample if and only if Ldim Z · Z > 0 for every irreducible subvariety Z ⊆ X. In Arakelov geometry, the arithmetic intersection number L̂dim Z+1 · Ẑ is defined for an arithmetic subvariety Ẑ = (Z, g) where g is a Green current. The arithmetic Nakai–Moishezon criterion, proved by Zhang and independently by Moriwaki, states that L̂ is ample (i.e., L is ample and the metrics are semipositive with positive curvature at archimedean places) if and only if L̂dim Z+1 · Ẑ > 0 for every integral arithmetic subvariety Ẑ of X. Here an integral arithmetic subvariety is a pair (Z, g) where Z is an irreducible subvariety and g is a Green current for Z such that the arithmetic cycle class is effective. The criterion requires checking positivity on all arithmetic subvarieties, including those supported on the fibres at finite places. For a vertical arithmetic divisor D̂ = (D, 0) where D is a prime divisor on a fibre Xp, the intersection L̂dim D+1 · D̂ reduces to the classical intersection Ldim D · D on the fibre, so the criterion implies ampleness on each fibre. At archimedean places, the condition involves the integral of the Monge–Ampère measure c1(L̂)dim Z against the Green current. A stronger notion is arithmetic positivity: L̂ is said to be arithmetically positive if it is nef (all arithmetic intersection numbers with effective arithmetic cycles are ≥ 0) and its arithmetic volume vol̂(L̂) > 0. The arithmetic Nakai–Moishezon criterion implies that arithmetic positivity is equivalent to ampleness when the metrics are semipositive. However, without semipositivity, there exist hermitian line bundles with positive arithmetic volume that are not ample — the metrics may have negative curvature directions. The arithmetic Khovanskii–Teissier inequalities, due to Yuan, give a more refined picture: for nef hermitian line bundles L̂0, …, L̂d, the mixed arithmetic intersection numbers satisfy L̂0 · … · L̂d ≥ ∏ vol̂(L̂i)1/(d+1). These inequalities are essential for the arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel theorem and for defining the arithmetic volume as a continuous function on the nef cone.

Exam Strategy: Distinguishing Arithmetic Positivity Notions
In multiple-choice questions, distinguish carefully between: (1) nef (all arithmetic intersections with effective cycles ≥ 0), (2) arithmetically positive (nef + arithmetic volume > 0), (3) ample (nef + semipositive metrics + positive arithmetic intersections on all integral arithmetic subvarieties). A hermitian line bundle can be nef with zero volume (e.g., a torsion line bundle with flat metric), nef with positive volume but not ample (metrics with some negative curvature), or ample (semipositive metrics, positive intersections). The arithmetic Nakai–Moishezon criterion applies to ampleness, not to arithmetic positivity. Remember that semipositivity of metrics is part of the definition of ampleness in Arakelov geometry — a line bundle with an ample underlying bundle but a metric with negative curvature somewhere is not arithmetically ample.

The Arithmetic Bogomolov–Gieseker Inequality and Slope Stability of Hermitian Vector Bundles

The arithmetic Bogomolov–Gieseker inequality is a fundamental constraint on the Chern characters of slope-semistable hermitian vector bundles on arithmetic varieties, generalising the classical Bogomolov–Gieseker inequality for stable vector bundles on projective varieties. Let X be a projective arithmetic variety of dimension d over a number field K, and let Ê = (E, {‖·‖v}) be a hermitian vector bundle of rank r on X. The arithmetic slope is defined as μ̂(Ê) = ĉ1(Ê) · L̂d−1 / r, where L̂ is a fixed ample hermitian line bundle (the polarisation) and ĉ1(Ê) is the arithmetic first Chern class in CĤ1(X). The bundle Ê is slope-semistable if for every non-zero hermitian subbundle F̂ ⊆ Ê, one has μ̂(F̂) ≤ μ̂(Ê). The arithmetic Bogomolov–Gieseker inequality, proved by Gillet–Soulé and extended by Moriwaki, states that if Ê is slope-semistable and the metrics are semipositive (i.e., the curvature forms are positive semidefinite at archimedean places), then the arithmetic discriminant satisfies Δ̂(Ê) · L̂d−2 ≥ 0, where Δ̂(Ê) = 2r ĉ2(Ê) − (r−1) ĉ1(Ê)2 is the arithmetic discriminant class in CĤ2(X). In terms of intersection numbers, this reads: 2r ĉ2(Ê) · L̂d−2 − (r−1) ĉ1(Ê)2 · L̂d−2 ≥ 0. For arithmetic surfaces (d = 2), this simplifies to Δ̂(Ê) ≥ 0 as a real number. The inequality fails without the semipositivity assumption on metrics; counterexamples exist where the curvature has negative directions. The arithmetic Bogomolov–Gieseker inequality is a key ingredient in the proof of the arithmetic Miyaoka–Yau inequality for arithmetic threefolds and in the study of the arithmetic minimal model program. It also implies the arithmetic Hodge index theorem for hermitian vector bundles: the intersection pairing on the subspace generated by ĉ1(Ê) and L̂ has signature (1, r−1) when restricted to the orthogonal complement of L̂. Slope stability for hermitian vector bundles is closely related to the existence of Hermite–Einstein metrics. The arithmetic Donaldson–Uhlenbeck–Yau theorem, due to Bost and Gillet–Soulé, states that a hermitian vector bundle on an arithmetic variety admits a Hermite–Einstein metric (i.e., a metric whose curvature form is a constant multiple of the Kähler form) if and only if it is slope-polystable and the metrics are semipositive. This provides a differential-geometric characterisation of stability in Arakelov geometry. The arithmetic Harder–Narasimhan filtration exists for any hermitian vector bundle, giving a canonical filtration by hermitian subbundles with strictly increasing slopes. The successive quotients are slope-semistable, and their arithmetic discriminants satisfy the Bogomolov–Gieseker inequality.

The Arithmetic Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch Theorem in Higher Arithmetic K-Theory

The arithmetic Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch (GRR) theorem extends the classical GRR theorem to arithmetic varieties, relating the arithmetic Chern character of the direct image of a hermitian vector bundle to the pushforward of the product of the arithmetic Chern character and the arithmetic Todd class. Let f : X → Y be a proper morphism of regular arithmetic varieties over a number field K, and let Ê be a hermitian vector bundle on X. The arithmetic Chern character cĥ(Ê) ∈ CĤ*(X)ℚ is defined using the Chern forms of the metrics at archimedean places and the classical Chern classes at finite places. The arithmetic Todd class Td̂(T̂f) ∈ CĤ*(X)ℚ is defined for the relative tangent bundle T̂f with a suitable metric. The arithmetic GRR theorem, proved by Gillet–Soulé and refined by Bismut–Gillet–Soulé, states that in the arithmetic Chow group CĤ*(Y)ℚ, one has cĥ(f*Ê) = f*(cĥ(Ê) · Td̂(T̂f)) + a(R(f, Ê)), where f* is the pushforward on arithmetic Chow groups, and a maps differential forms to arithmetic Chow groups via the Green current construction. The correction term R(f, Ê) is the analytic torsion form, a differential form on Y(ℂ) constructed from the spectral geometry of the fibres of f. For a closed immersion i : Z X of codimension r, the arithmetic GRR gives the arithmetic self-intersection formula: i*(cĥ(Ê|Z) · Td̂(N̂Z/X)−1) = cĥ(i*Ê) · Td̂(T̂X)−1 · Td̂(T̂Z). The arithmetic GRR in higher arithmetic K-theory, developed by Burgos Gil, Kramer, and Kühn, lifts the theorem to the level of arithmetic K-groups K̂0(X). The arithmetic Chern character becomes a ring homomorphism cĥ : K̂0(X) → CĤ*(X)ℚ, and the arithmetic Todd genus is a multiplicative characteristic class. The higher arithmetic GRR states that for a proper morphism f : X → Y, the diagram involving the pushforward f* : K̂0(X) → K̂0(Y) and the arithmetic Chern character commutes up to the analytic torsion class in the arithmetic K-theory of the base. This formulation is essential for the arithmetic index theorem for families of Dirac operators and for the computation of arithmetic intersection numbers on moduli spaces. The arithmetic GRR is also the key tool in the proof of the arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel theorem: applying it to the projection X → Spec OK and the powers L̂⊗m of an ample hermitian line bundle yields the asymptotic expansion of the arithmetic Euler characteristic χ̂(X, L̂⊗m) = log #H0(X, L̂⊗m) + O(md), whose leading term is the arithmetic volume vol̂(L̂) md+1/(d+1)!.

The Arithmetic Dynamics and Canonical Heights on Algebraic Dynamical Systems

Arithmetic dynamics studies the iteration of rational maps on projective varieties over number fields, with canonical heights providing a measure of arithmetic complexity for preperiodic and wandering points. Let X be a projective variety over a number field K, and let φ : X ⇢ X be a dominant rational map of degree d ≥ 2. A canonical height ĥφ : X(K̄) → ℝ≥0 is a function satisfying ĥφ(φ(P)) = d ĥφ(P) for all P where φ is defined, and differing from a Weil height hL associated to an ample line bundle L with φ*L ≃ L⊗d by a bounded function. The existence and uniqueness of the canonical height was proved by Call–Silverman for morphisms and by Kawaguchi–Silverman for rational maps. The canonical height is constructed as the limit ĥφ(P) = limn→∞ hL(φn(P)) / dn. Points with ĥφ(P) = 0 are exactly the preperiodic points (those with finite forward orbit). The Northcott property holds: for any B > 0, the set {P ∈ X(K̄) : ĥφ(P) ≤ B, [K(P) : K] ≤ D} is finite. The arithmetic equidistribution theorem for dynamical systems, due to Yuan and independently to Favre–Rivera-Letelier, states that if {Pn} is a generic sequence of points with ĥφ(Pn) → 0, then the Galois orbits equidistribute towards the equilibrium measure μφ on the Berkovich analytification at each place. This is the dynamical analogue of the equidistribution of small points on abelian varieties. The dynamical Bogomolov conjecture, proved by Yuan–Zhang and Ghioca–Tucker, states that a subvariety V ⊆ X contains a Zariski-dense set of preperiodic points if and only if V is preperiodic under φ. The canonical height also satisfies a functoriality property: for a morphism f : X → Y intertwining φX and φY, one has ĥφY(f(P)) = ĥφX(P). In the case of abelian varieties, the Néron–Tate height is the canonical height for the multiplication-by-m map. For ℙ1 and the map z ↦ z2 + c, the canonical height is the escape rate in complex dynamics. The arithmetic intersection theory on the dynamical compactification of the parameter space (the moduli space of rational maps) uses canonical heights to define arithmetic intersection numbers for divisors such as the bifurcation locus. The arithmetic volume of the line bundle of modular forms on the moduli space of dynamical systems is related to the Lyapunov exponent of the equilibrium measure.

The Faltings Height of Jacobians and the Torelli Map

The Faltings height of a principally polarized abelian variety extends naturally to Jacobians of curves. For a smooth projective curve C of genus g ≥ 2 over a number field K, the Jacobian J(C) carries a canonical principal polarization, and its Faltings height hF(J(C)) can be expressed in terms of the Arakelov self‑intersection of the relative dualizing sheaf on a regular model of C over the ring of integers OK. The Torelli map τ : Mg → Ag sending a curve to its Jacobian is a morphism of moduli stacks, and the pullback of the Hodge bundle λ on Ag equals the Hodge bundle on Mg. Consequently, the Faltings height of J(C) differs from the stable Faltings height of C by a bounded term depending only on g. This comparison is a key ingredient in Faltings’ proof of the Mordell conjecture: the height of a rational point on C is bounded above by a linear function of hF(J(C)) plus a constant depending on K and g. Moreover, the arithmetic intersection theory on Mg developed by Moret‑Bailly and later by Jorgenson‑Kramer shows that the arithmetic self‑intersection λ̂g+1 computes the Faltings height of the universal Jacobian up to an explicit analytic torsion term. The resulting formula expresses hF(J(C)) as a sum of a finite part (the discriminant of a minimal regular model) and an archimedean part (the integral of the Arakelov Green current against the curvature form of the Hodge metric). This decomposition is essential for effective bounds in Diophantine geometry and for the computation of heights of CM Jacobians via the Colmez conjecture.

Arithmetic Intersection Theory on Shimura Varieties and Special Cycles

Shimura varieties provide a rich source of arithmetic varieties where heights of special subvarieties can be studied via the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula. Let Sh(G,X) be a Shimura variety attached to a reductive group G over ℚ and a Hermitian symmetric domain X. For a collection of rational quadratic spaces, Kudla’s program constructs special cycles Z(T) indexed by positive semi‑definite matrices T. The arithmetic intersection numbers of these cycles with the Hodge bundle λ̂ are conjectured to equal the Fourier coefficients of the derivative of an incoherent Eisenstein series on the metaplectic double cover of Sp2n. This is the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula, proved in many cases by Kudla‑Rapoport‑Yang for unitary Shimura varieties and by Yuan‑Zhang‑Zhang for orthogonal Shimura varieties. The formula expresses the arithmetic degree ̂deg(λ̂d·Z(T)) as a sum of a finite part (local intersection multiplicities at bad primes) and an archimedean part (integrals of Green currents against the curvature of the Hodge metric). The finite part is governed by the arithmetic fundamental lemma, which relates orbital integrals on the group G to intersection numbers on the corresponding Rapoport–Zink space. The archimedean part involves the computation of the star product of Green functions, a technique developed by Burgos‑Kramer‑Kühn. These results give explicit formulas for the Faltings heights of CM abelian varieties and for the heights of Heegner points on Shimura curves, linking them to special values of L‑functions via the Gross–Zagier formula. The arithmetic intersection theory on Shimura varieties also underlies the proof of the Colmez conjecture for abelian varieties with complex multiplication by a CM field, as completed by Yuan‑Zhang and independently by Andreatta‑Goren‑Howard‑Madapusi‑Pera.

The Arithmetic Fundamental Lemma and the Arithmetic Gan–Gross–Prasad Conjecture

The arithmetic fundamental lemma, formulated by Zhang and proved in the unitary case by Rapoport‑Smithling‑Zhang and in the orthogonal case by Wei‑Zhang, is a precise identity between the derivative of an orbital integral on a symmetric space and the arithmetic intersection number of a special cycle on a Rapoport–Zink space. Let G be a unitary group of signature (n−1,1) over a quadratic imaginary field E, and let H be the subgroup fixing a non‑isotropic vector. The local orbital integral Oγ(f) for a test function f on the Lie algebra of G has a derivative at the trivial element. The arithmetic fundamental lemma asserts that this derivative equals the arithmetic intersection number ̂deg( Z(γ) · Z(1) ) on the Rapoport–Zink space for G, where Z(γ) and Z(1) are special cycles defined by the vector and the identity. This identity is the local input for the arithmetic Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture, which predicts that the global arithmetic intersection of two special cycles on a unitary Shimura variety equals the central derivative of the Rankin–Selberg L‑function of the associated automorphic representations. The conjecture has been proved for many cases by Yuan‑Zhang‑Zhang using the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula and the arithmetic fundamental lemma. The proof proceeds by comparing the arithmetic theta lift of a Schwartz function with the incoherent Eisenstein series, and then applying the arithmetic fundamental lemma to identify the local contributions. The result gives an exact formula for the height of a CM point on a Shimura curve in terms of the derivative of an L‑function, generalising the Gross–Zagier formula to higher dimensional Shimura varieties.

Arithmetic Intersection Theory on Rapoport–Zink Spaces and Local Shimura Varieties

Rapoport–Zink spaces are formal schemes over the ring of integers of a p‑adic field that uniformise the p‑adic neighbourhoods of Shimura varieties. They carry a natural action of a p‑adic group J and a projective system of special cycles Z(m) indexed by lattices in a hermitian space. The arithmetic intersection theory on these spaces, developed by Kudla‑Rapoport and later by Howard, defines intersection numbers Int(m₁,…,mr) as lengths of certain intersection complexes, weighted by the logarithm of the residue field cardinality. These local intersection numbers appear as the coefficients of the derivative of the local density polynomial, which is the p‑adic analogue of the Siegel–Weil formula. The arithmetic fundamental lemma identifies the derivative of the orbital integral with the intersection number Int(m,1). In the case of the Lubin–Tate tower (the Rapoport–Zink space for GL2), the intersection numbers compute the derivatives of the local L‑factors of the base change L‑function, and they are explicitly given by the valuation of the difference of two CM points on the Drinfeld upper half‑plane. For higher rank groups, the intersection numbers are expressed in terms of the geometry of the Bruhat–Tits building and the theory of special cycles on the associated local Shimura varieties. These local results are indispensable for the global arithmetic intersection formulas on Shimura varieties, because the global arithmetic intersection product decomposes as a sum of local contributions at each prime, and the archimedean contribution is handled by the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula.

The Arithmetic Kodaira Dimension and the Arithmetic Iitaka Conjecture for Fibered Varieties

The arithmetic Kodaira dimension κ̂(X, L̂) of a hermitian line bundle L̂ on an arithmetic variety X is defined as the growth rate of the arithmetic Euler characteristic χ̂(X, L̂⊗m) as m → ∞. Unlike the classical Kodaira dimension, which takes values in {−∞, 0, …, dim X}, the arithmetic Kodaira dimension can be as large as dim X + 1 because the archimedean places contribute an extra dimension. The arithmetic Iitaka conjecture, formulated by Moriwaki, predicts that for a surjective morphism f : X → Y of arithmetic varieties and a relatively ample hermitian line bundle L̂ on X, one has κ̂(X, L̂) ≥ κ̂(Y, f*L̂) + κ̂(Xη, L̂|Xη), where Xη is the generic fibre. This inequality is known in several cases: when f is an abelian scheme, it follows from the arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel theorem and the positivity of the Hodge bundle; when f is a family of curves of genus ≥ 2, it is a consequence of the arithmetic Bogomolov–Gieseker inequality for the relative dualizing sheaf equipped with the Arakelov metric. The conjecture remains open in general dimension ≥ 3, but recent work by Chen‑Moriwaki and by Yuan‑Zhang has established it for fibrations whose generic fibre is a variety of general type with a semi‑ample canonical bundle. The proof uses the arithmetic positivity of the direct image sheaf f*ωX/Y equipped with the Hodge metric, together with the arithmetic Nakai–Moishezon criterion to control the volume of L̂. The arithmetic Iitaka conjecture is the arithmetic analogue of the classical Iitaka conjecture, and its resolution would give a powerful tool for bounding heights of points on fibered varieties.

The Arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel Theorem and Positivity of Direct Image Sheaves

The arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel theorem, established by Gillet–Soulé and refined by Moriwaki, computes the asymptotic behaviour of the arithmetic Euler characteristic χ̂(X, L̂⊗m) for a hermitian line bundle L̂ on an arithmetic variety X of dimension d. The theorem states that

χ̂(X, L̂⊗m) = (L̂d+1 / (d+1)!) md+1 + O(md log m)

where L̂d+1 denotes the arithmetic self-intersection number. This formula generalises the classical Hilbert–Samuel polynomial by incorporating the archimedean contributions through the arithmetic intersection product. The leading coefficient is the arithmetic volume vol̂(L̂) = L̂d+1 / (d+1)!. When L̂ is arithmetically ample, the arithmetic volume is strictly positive, and the theorem implies that the arithmetic Euler characteristic grows like a polynomial of degree d+1, one degree higher than the classical case — the extra dimension comes from the archimedean places.

A crucial application is the arithmetic positivity of direct image sheaves. Let f : X → Y be a projective morphism of arithmetic varieties and let L̂ be a relatively ample hermitian line bundle on X. The direct image sheaf f*L̂⊗m carries a natural L2 metric induced by the Arakelov metric on L̂. The arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel theorem, combined with the arithmetic Riemann–Roch theorem, shows that the hermitian vector bundle (f*L̂⊗m, ‖·‖L2) is arithmetically positive in the sense of arithmetic slope stability for m sufficiently large. This positivity is the arithmetic analogue of the classical result that f*L⊗m is generated by global sections for large m. In the arithmetic setting, one obtains quantitative bounds on the successive minima of f*L̂⊗m in terms of the arithmetic intersection numbers of L̂. These bounds are essential for the proof of the arithmetic Iitaka conjecture for abelian schemes and for families of curves, where the positivity of the Hodge bundle f*ωX/Y equipped with the Hodge metric plays a central role.

The theorem also underpins the definition of the arithmetic Kodaira dimension κ̂(X, L̂) as the growth rate of χ̂(X, L̂⊗m). When L̂ is not arithmetically ample, the asymptotic expansion involves lower-degree terms whose coefficients are arithmetic intersection numbers of L̂ with the relative dualising sheaf and with the archimedean Green currents. The error term O(md log m) is sharp in general, as shown by examples of hermitian line bundles with non-smooth metrics. The arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel theorem is the foundation for the arithmetic volume theory developed by Moriwaki and Yuan–Zhang, which extends the classical theory of volumes of line bundles to the arithmetic setting and provides the tools for the arithmetic Minimal Model Program.

The Arithmetic Hard Lefschetz Theorem and Hodge–Riemann Relations

The arithmetic Hard Lefschetz theorem, formulated and proved by Gillet–Soulé and later extended by Burgos Gil–Kramer–Kühn, is the arithmetic analogue of the classical Hard Lefschetz theorem for Kähler manifolds. Let X be a regular arithmetic variety of dimension d over Spec OK, and let L̂ be an arithmetically ample hermitian line bundle on X. The arithmetic Chow groups CĤp(X) carry a natural intersection product with L̂. The theorem asserts that for each p ≤ (d+1)/2, the map

L̂d+1-2p : CĤp(X) → CĤd+1-p(X)

is an isomorphism modulo torsion. Here the exponent d+1 reflects the extra dimension from the archimedean places. The proof uses the arithmetic Hodge index theorem and the positivity of the arithmetic intersection form on the primitive part of CĤp(X). The primitive part is defined as the kernel of L̂d+2-2p : CĤp(X) → CĤd+2-p(X).

The arithmetic Hodge–Riemann relations refine this by giving a sign condition on the intersection pairing on the primitive part. For a primitive class x ∈ CĤp(X)prim, one has

(−1)p(p-1)/2 x · L̂d+1-2p · x ≥ 0

with equality if and only if x is torsion. These relations are the arithmetic counterpart of the classical Hodge–Riemann bilinear relations and are proved using the arithmetic Hodge index theorem for hermitian vector bundles of rank one. The arithmetic Hard Lefschetz theorem and Hodge–Riemann relations have profound consequences for the structure of arithmetic Chow groups. They imply that the arithmetic intersection pairing on CĤp(X) satisfies the Lefschetz decomposition, and they provide a tool for proving the arithmetic standard conjectures in certain cases. In particular, they are used in the proof of the arithmetic Bogomolov–Gieseker inequality for semistable hermitian vector bundles, where the Hard Lefschetz isomorphism allows one to reduce the problem to the case of line bundles.

The theorem also has a version for hermitian vector bundles of higher rank, where the role of L̂ is played by the determinant line bundle equipped with the induced metric. The arithmetic Hard Lefschetz theorem for vector bundles is a key ingredient in the arithmetic Simpson correspondence, which relates semistable hermitian vector bundles with vanishing arithmetic Chern classes to representations of the arithmetic fundamental group. The archimedean contribution to the Hard Lefschetz isomorphism is encoded in the Green currents associated to the metrics, and the analytic torsion forms that appear in the arithmetic Riemann–Roch theorem enter the proof through the arithmetic Hodge theory developed by Burgos Gil–Kramer–Kühn.

Worked Example: Arithmetic Volume of a Hermitian Line Bundle on ℙ1ℤ
Let X = ℙ1ℤ = Proj ℤ[T0, T1] and let L̂ = O(1) equipped with the Fubini–Study metric at the archimedean place. Compute the arithmetic volume vol̂(L̂) = L̂2 / 2! and verify the leading term of the arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel polynomial.
The arithmetic variety X has dimension d = 2 (one horizontal dimension from ℙ1ℚ and one vertical dimension from Spec ℤ). The hermitian line bundle L̂ = O(1) has first arithmetic Chern class ĉ1(L̂) = (c1(L), g) where c1(L) is the classical divisor class of a point on ℙ1 and g is the Green function for the Fubini–Study metric. The arithmetic self-intersection L̂2 is computed as follows. The classical intersection L2 on the generic fibre ℙ1ℚ is 1 (a point has self-intersection 1 on ℙ1). There are no non-archimedean contributions because L is trivial on the vertical fibres. Hence L̂2 = 1 + 1/2 = 3/2. The arithmetic volume is vol̂(L̂) = L̂2 / 2! = 3/4. The arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel theorem gives χ̂(X, L̂⊗m) = (3/4) m2 + O(m log m). When computing arithmetic intersection numbers, always separate the contributions: (1) classical intersection on the generic fibre, (2) archimedean integral of Green current against the curvature form, (3) non-archimedean contributions from vertical components (often zero for relatively ample bundles). The arithmetic dimension is d+1 where d is the Krull dimension of the generic fibre. The arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel leading coefficient is L̂d+1/(d+1)!. Memorise the Fubini–Study integral ∫ℙ1(ℂ) log(1+|z|2) ωFS = 1/2 and its higher-dimensional generalisations. For the Hard Lefschetz theorem, remember the shift: the isomorphism is L̂d+1-2p, not L̂d-2p.

The Arithmetic Fujita Conjecture and Basepoint Freeness for Hermitian Line Bundles

The arithmetic Fujita conjecture, proposed by Moriwaki as the arithmetic analogue of Fujita's freeness conjecture, predicts a bound on the twist needed to make a hermitian line bundle arithmetically generated by global sections. Let X be a regular arithmetic variety of dimension d over Spec OK, and let L̂ be an arithmetically ample hermitian line bundle on X. The conjecture states that L̂⊗m ⊗ ω̂X is arithmetically generated by global sections for all m ≥ d+1, where ω̂X is the relative dualising sheaf equipped with the Arakelov metric. Here "arithmetically generated by global sections" means that the natural map

H0(X, L̂⊗m ⊗ ω̂X) ⊗ ÔX → L̂⊗m ⊗ ω̂X

is surjective as a morphism of hermitian vector bundles, i.e., the global sections generate the fibre at every point including the archimedean fibres, with uniform bounds on the norms. The classical Fujita conjecture (proved by Angehrn–Siu for d ≥ 4 and by Kawamata for d = 3) says that L⊗m ⊗ ωX is globally generated for m ≥ d+1. The arithmetic version is significantly harder because one must control the archimedean metrics on the global sections.

The conjecture is known in several cases. For arithmetic surfaces (d = 2), it follows from the arithmetic Riemann–Roch theorem and the arithmetic Nakai–Moishezon criterion. For abelian schemes, it is a consequence of the arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel theorem and the positivity of the Hodge bundle. For general arithmetic varieties, partial results have been obtained by Chen–Moriwaki and by Yuan–Zhang using the arithmetic positivity of direct images and the arithmetic Bertini theorem. The arithmetic Bertini theorem, proved by Moriwaki, states that for an arithmetically ample hermitian line bundle L̂, a general global section of L̂⊗m with small norm defines an arithmetically ample divisor. This is the arithmetic analogue of the classical Bertini theorem and is proved using the arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel theorem and the equidistribution of small points.

The arithmetic Fujita conjecture is closely related to the arithmetic basepoint freeness problem. A hermitian line bundle L̂ is said to be arithmetically basepoint-free if for every point x ∈ X (including archimedean points), there exists a global section s ∈ H0(X, L̂) such that s(x) ≠ 0 and ‖s‖sup ≤ C for some constant C independent of x. The arithmetic basepoint freeness is equivalent to the surjectivity of the evaluation map at all places. The conjecture implies that L̂⊗m ⊗ ω̂X is arithmetically basepoint-free for m ≥ d+1. This has applications to the arithmetic Minimal Model Program, where one needs to run the arithmetic MMP on models of varieties over number fields. The arithmetic basepoint freeness is also used in the proof of the arithmetic Iitaka conjecture for fibrations of general type, where one needs to construct sections of relative pluricanonical bundles with controlled norms.

The Arithmetic Intersection Theory on the Moduli Stack of Shtukas

The arithmetic intersection theory on the moduli stack of shtukas, developed by Laurent Fargues, Peter Scholze, and their collaborators, provides a geometric framework for the local Langlands correspondence in the function field setting and its arithmetic analogues. A shtuka over a scheme S is a modification of a vector bundle on the curve X × S along a graph of a morphism S → X. The moduli stack ShtG of G-shtukas for a reductive group G over a function field Fq(X) carries a natural action of G(AF) × Jb(F), where Jb is the centraliser of a Frobenius element. The arithmetic intersection theory on this stack is constructed using the theory of perverse sheaves and the nearby cycles functor in ℓ-adic cohomology.

The key object is the Hecke correspondence on ShtG, which relates shtukas with different modifications. The intersection of Hecke correspondences defines convolution products on the cohomology of ShtG, and these products realise the local Langlands correspondence. In the arithmetic setting, one considers the moduli stack of shtukas over the ring of integers OF of a local field, or over a global field. The arithmetic intersection numbers are defined as the traces of Frobenius on the intersection cohomology complexes, weighted by the logarithm of the residue field cardinality. These numbers appear as the coefficients of the derivative of the local L-function, generalising the arithmetic fundamental lemma of Kudla–Rapoport.

The arithmetic intersection theory on shtukas has been used to prove the local Langlands correspondence for GLn over function fields (Lafforgue) and to construct the global Langlands correspondence for function fields (V. Lafforgue). In the arithmetic setting, the work of Fargues–Scholze on the geometrisation of the local Langlands correspondence uses the moduli stack of shtukas over the Fargues–Fontaine curve, which is an adic space over a p-adic field. The arithmetic intersection theory on this stack involves the theory of diamonds and the pro-étale cohomology. The intersection numbers are expressed in terms of the geometry of the affine Grassmannian and the Bruhat–Tits building. These results provide a geometric interpretation of the arithmetic fundamental lemma and its generalisations to arbitrary reductive groups, and they connect the arithmetic intersection theory on Rapoport–Zink spaces (which are local models of Shimura varieties) with the global arithmetic intersection theory on Shimura varieties via the uniformisation theorem.

The Arithmetic Relative Bogomolov Conjecture and the Arithmetic Manin–Mumford Conjecture

The arithmetic relative Bogomolov conjecture, formulated by Zhang and proved in the abelian case by Ullmo and Zhang, and in the general case by Yuan–Zhang, concerns the distribution of small points on a subvariety of an abelian scheme. Let f : A → S be an abelian scheme over a number field, and let X ⊂ A be a closed subvariety of the generic fibre Aη. The conjecture states that if X is not a translate of an abelian subvariety by a torsion point, then the set of points P ∈ X(ℚ̄) with Néron–Tate height ĥA(P) < ε is not Zariski dense in X for any ε > 0. Equivalently, the essential minimum of the Néron–Tate height on X is strictly positive. This is the arithmetic analogue of the classical Bogomolov conjecture for abelian varieties over function fields, proved by Ullmo and Zhang using the equidistribution of small points and the arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel theorem.

The arithmetic Manin–Mumford conjecture, proved by Raynaud, states that a subvariety X of an abelian variety A contains only finitely many torsion points unless X is a translate of an abelian subvariety by a torsion point. The arithmetic relative version, formulated by Zhang, considers a subvariety X of an abelian scheme A → S and asks for the distribution of points that are torsion on each fibre. The conjecture is that the set of points P ∈ X(ℚ̄) such that P is torsion on the fibre Af(P) is not Zariski dense unless X is a translate of an abelian subscheme by a torsion section. This was proved by Gao and by Habegger–Pila using o-minimality and the Pila–Wilkie theorem, combined with the arithmetic equidistribution theorems of Yuan–Zhang.

These conjectures are special cases of the arithmetic Bogomolov conjecture for semiabelian varieties and of the arithmetic Zilber–Pink conjecture for mixed Shimura varieties. The arithmetic Zilber–Pink conjecture, formulated by Gao, predicts that for a mixed Shimura variety S and a subvariety X ⊂ S, the intersection of X with the special subvarieties of codimension greater than dim X is not Zariski dense unless X is contained in a proper special subvariety. The proof of the arithmetic relative Bogomolov conjecture uses the arithmetic equidistribution of small points on abelian schemes, which states that the Galois orbits of points of small Néron–Tate height equidistribute with respect to the Haar measure on the complex torus A(ℂ). This equidistribution is proved using the arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel theorem and the arithmetic Bézout theorem. The arithmetic Manin–Mumford conjecture uses the o-minimal structure on the universal cover of the abelian scheme and the counting of rational points on definable sets.

The Arithmetic p-adic Heights and the p-adic Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer Conjecture

The theory of p-adic heights provides a p-adic analogue of the Néron-Tate canonical height, essential for formulating and attacking the p-adic Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture. Let A be an abelian variety over a number field K, and let p be a prime of good ordinary reduction. The p-adic height pairing ĥ_p : A(K) × A(K) → ℚ_p is a bilinear form constructed using p-adic Arakelov theory on the formal group of A at p, or via the theory of p-adic sigma functions of Mazur and Tate. For an elliptic curve E over ℚ with good ordinary reduction at p, the p-adic height of a point P ∈ E(ℚ) is given by ĥ_p(P) = log_p(σ_p(P)) / log_p(q_E) where σ_p is the p-adic sigma function and q_E is the Tate period. The p-adic height decomposes as a sum of local p-adic heights ĥ_p = Σ_v ĥp,v where v runs over places of K. At places v ≠ p, the local height is defined using the Néron model and intersection theory on the special fibre; at v = p, it uses the p-adic logarithm on the formal group. The p-adic Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture, formulated by Mazur, Tate, and Teitelbaum, predicts that the order of vanishing of the p-adic L-function L_p(E, s) at s = 1 equals the rank of E(ℚ), and that the leading coefficient is given by a p-adic regulator Reg_p(E) = det(ĥ_p(P_i, P_j)) times the p-adic analogues of the Tamagawa numbers, the order of the Tate-Shafarevich group, and the Manin constant. When E has split multiplicative reduction at p, the p-adic L-function has a trivial zero, and the conjecture involves the derivative L_p'(E, 1) and the p-adic height of the Heegner point. The p-adic height pairing is also central to the construction of p-adic height pairings on Selmer groups in Iwasawa theory, where the p-adic height of a Selmer class is defined using the p-adic Abel-Jacobi map and the p-adic étale regulator. The work of Perrin-Riou, Nekovář, and Benois develops the p-adic height pairing in the context of p-adic Hodge theory, expressing it in terms of the Bloch-Kato exponential map and the filtered (φ, N)-module Dcris(V_p(A)). The p-adic Gross-Zagier formula of Bertolini, Darmon, and Prasanna relates the derivative of the p-adic L-function to the p-adic height of a Heegner point, providing a p-adic analogue of the classical Gross-Zagier formula. The p-adic height pairing is also used in the proof of the p-adic Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture for elliptic curves with complex multiplication by Rubin, and for modular abelian varieties by Skinner and Urban using the Eisenstein ideal and the main conjecture of Iwasawa theory.

The Arithmetic Bloch-Beilinson Conjectures and Height Pairings on Chow Groups

The arithmetic Bloch-Beilinson conjectures predict the existence of a filtration on the Chow groups of a smooth projective variety X over a number field, compatible with the conjectural filtration on motivic cohomology, and an arithmetic height pairing on the graded pieces taking values in ℝ. For a variety X of dimension d over a number field K, the Chow group CH^p(X) of codimension p cycles modulo rational equivalence carries a conjectural Bloch-Beilinson filtration F^ν CH^p(X) ⊗ ℚ with F0 = CH^p(X) ⊗ ℚ and Fp+1 = 0. The graded pieces Gr^ν_F CH^p(X) are expected to be finite-dimensional ℚ-vector spaces related to the motivic cohomology groups H2p-ν_M(X, ℚ(p)). The arithmetic height pairing is a bilinear form ⟨ , ⟩_ar : Gr^ν_F CH^p(X) × Gr2p-ν_F CHd-p+1(X) → ℝ defined using Arakelov intersection theory. When ν = p, the pairing takes values in the arithmetic Chow group ĈH^p(X) of Gillet and Soulé, which combines algebraic cycles on X with Green currents on X(ℂ). The arithmetic height of a cycle Z ∈ CH^p(X) is defined as the arithmetic intersection number ⟨Ẑ, Ẑ⟩ where Ẑ is an arithmetic cycle lifting Z with a suitable Green current. For p = 1, the arithmetic height pairing on divisors recovers the Néron-Tate height on the Picard variety. For p = 2 on a surface, the pairing on CH2(X) = CH0(X) relates to the height of zero-cycles and the conjectural filtration of Bloch and Beilinson. The arithmetic Bloch-Beilinson conjectures imply that the arithmetic height pairing is positive definite on the primitive part of Gr^p_F CH^p(X), generalising the Hodge index theorem. The conjectures also predict a relation between the arithmetic height pairing and the special values of L-functions: for a motive M of weight w, the leading coefficient of L(M, s) at s = (w+1)/2 should be expressed in terms of the determinant of the arithmetic height pairing on the motivic cohomology H1_M(M, ℚ(1)) and the period matrix. The work of Bloch, Beilinson, and Soulé establishes the conjectures for curves and for certain surfaces with trivial Chow groups. The arithmetic height pairing on CH2(X) for a surface X is related to the arithmetic intersection theory on the Hilbert scheme of points and the arithmetic Bogomolov-Miyaoka-Yau inequality. The arithmetic standard conjectures of Künneth type and Lefschetz type in Arakelov geometry, formulated by Künnemann and others, provide a framework for proving the arithmetic Bloch-Beilinson conjectures using the arithmetic Hodge index theorem and the arithmetic hard Lefschetz theorem. The arithmetic height pairing also appears in the arithmetic Gan-Gross-Prasad conjecture for orthogonal groups, where the height of a cycle on a Shimura variety is related to the central derivative of an L-function.

Exam Strategy: Distinguishing Archimedean and p-adic Heights
In questions comparing archimedean and p-adic heights, remember: the archimedean Néron-Tate height is a real-valued quadratic form on A(K) ⊗ ℝ, positive definite modulo torsion. The p-adic height is a ℚ_p-valued bilinear form, also vanishing on torsion, but its local components at v ≠ p use the same intersection theory as the archimedean height, while the v = p component uses p-adic logarithms on the formal group. The p-adic height is NOT positive definite — it takes values in ℚ_p with no ordering. The p-adic BSD conjecture involves the p-adic regulator (determinant of the p-adic height matrix) while the classical BSD uses the real regulator. Do not confuse the p-adic sigma function (Mazur-Tate) with the Weierstrass sigma function (archimedean).

The Arithmetic Inner Product Formula and Heights of Theta Lifts

The arithmetic inner product formula, developed by Yuan, Zhang, and others, generalises the classical Rallis inner product formula to the arithmetic setting, expressing the arithmetic intersection number of two arithmetic theta lifts as the central derivative of an L-function. Let (G, H) be a dual reductive pair in a symplectic group Sp(W) over a number field F, with G = U(V) a unitary group of a hermitian space V and H = U(W) a unitary group of a skew-hermitian space W. The theta correspondence associates to an automorphic representation π of G(𝔸_F) a theta lift θ(π) on H(𝔸_F). The arithmetic theta lift constructs a cycle Z(φ) on the Shimura variety Sh(G) associated to a Schwartz function φ on the adelic space. The arithmetic inner product formula states that the arithmetic intersection number ⟨Ẑ(φ₁), Ẑ(φ₂)⟩_ar of two arithmetic theta cycles equals the central derivative L'(1/2, π × τ) of the Rankin-Selberg L-function, times a product of local arithmetic intersection numbers. The arithmetic height of the theta cycle Z(φ) is defined using the arithmetic intersection theory on the Shimura variety Sh(G) with its canonical automorphic line bundle equipped with the Petersson metric. The formula involves the arithmetic Siegel-Weil formula, which expresses the arithmetic theta integral as an arithmetic Eisenstein series. The local arithmetic intersection numbers at finite places are computed using the arithmetic fundamental lemma of Zhang, which relates the intersection of special cycles on Rapoport-Zink spaces to derivatives of orbital integrals. At archimedean places, the local arithmetic intersection number is given by the integral of the Green current against the theta kernel. The arithmetic inner product formula has been proved for unitary groups of signature (n, 1) and (n-1, 1) by Yuan, Zhang, and Liu, and for orthogonal groups by Garcia and Sankaran. It implies the arithmetic Gan-Gross-Prasad conjecture for unitary groups, which predicts that the arithmetic height of a cycle on a Shimura variety associated to a tempered automorphic representation is non-zero if and only if the central L-value L(1/2, π × τ) ≠ 0. The formula also gives a new proof of the arithmetic Gross-Zagier formula for Shimura curves, where the theta lift from SL₂ to a unitary group of signature (1, 1) produces Heegner points. The arithmetic height of the theta cycle is related to the Néron-Tate height of the corresponding Heegner point via the arithmetic Siegel-Weil formula. The arithmetic inner product formula is a key ingredient in the proof of the arithmetic Ichino-Ikeda formula for the central derivative of the triple product L-function, which expresses the arithmetic height of a diagonal cycle on a product of three Shimura curves in terms of the derivative of the L-function.

The Arithmetic Arakelov Theory of Logarithmic Schemes and Degenerations

The extension of Arakelov theory to logarithmic schemes and degenerations, developed by Burgos Gil, Kramer, Kühn, and others, allows the computation of arithmetic intersection numbers on compactifications of moduli spaces with boundary divisors. A logarithmic scheme is a scheme X equipped with a sheaf of monoids M_X and a homomorphism α : M_X → O_X inducing an isomorphism on the units. The logarithmic structure encodes the boundary divisor D = X U where U is the open part. In Arakelov geometry, one considers a logarithmic arithmetic variety (X, D) over Spec O_K with a logarithmic hermitian line bundle (L, ‖·‖) where the metric has logarithmic singularities along D(ℂ). The arithmetic Chow group ĈH^p(X, D) is defined using cycles on X with logarithmic singularities and Green currents with logarithmic growth. The arithmetic intersection product extends to logarithmic cycles, and the arithmetic Riemann-Roch theorem holds with a correction term involving the analytic torsion of the logarithmic de Rham complex. The arithmetic intersection theory on the moduli stack M_g of curves requires a logarithmic structure at the boundary of the Deligne-Mumford compactification M̄_g. The Hodge bundle λ on M_g extends to a logarithmic line bundle on M̄_g with the Quillen metric, and its arithmetic degree is computed using the arithmetic Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch theorem for logarithmic schemes. The arithmetic intersection numbers of boundary strata on M̄_g are related to the arithmetic intersection theory on the moduli spaces of stable curves of lower genus via the gluing formula. The arithmetic Arakelov theory of degenerations is essential for the arithmetic intersection theory on the toroidal compactifications of Shimura varieties. The boundary of a toroidal compactification Sh(G)_Σ is a union of Shimura varieties of lower dimension, and the arithmetic intersection theory on the boundary is computed using the logarithmic arithmetic intersection theory. The arithmetic Siegel-Weil formula for the boundary expresses the arithmetic theta integral as a sum of arithmetic Eisenstein series on the boundary components. The arithmetic inner product formula for the boundary involves the arithmetic intersection numbers of logarithmic cycles. The work of Brunebarbe, Cadorel, and others develops the arithmetic hyperbolicity of logarithmic varieties, showing that the logarithmic cotangent bundle is arithmetically ample under certain conditions. The arithmetic Bogomolov conjecture for logarithmic varieties predicts that the essential minimum of the arithmetic height on a subvariety of a semiabelian variety is positive unless the subvariety is a translate of a semiabelian subvariety. The arithmetic Arakelov theory of logarithmic schemes also provides a framework for the arithmetic intersection theory on the moduli stack of shtukas over function fields, where the boundary corresponds to degenerations of shtukas.

The Arithmetic Waldspurger Formula and Heights of Toric Periods

The arithmetic Waldspurger formula, proved by Yuan, Zhang, and Zhang for unitary groups and by Liu for orthogonal groups, expresses the arithmetic height of a toric cycle on a Shimura variety as the central derivative of an L-function. Let G = U(V) be a unitary group of a hermitian space V of dimension n over a CM field E, and let T = U(W) be a torus associated to a hermitian space W of dimension n-1. The toric cycle Z_T on the Shimura variety Sh(G) is the image of the Shimura variety Sh(T) under the natural embedding. The arithmetic toric cycle Ẑ_T is equipped with a Green current constructed from the theta kernel. The arithmetic Waldspurger formula states that the arithmetic height ⟨Ẑ_T, Ẑ_T⟩_ar equals the central derivative L'(1/2, π × χ) of the Rankin-Selberg L-function of an automorphic representation π of G(𝔸_F) and a Hecke character χ of T(𝔸_F), times a product of local arithmetic intersection numbers. The local arithmetic intersection numbers at finite places are computed using the arithmetic fundamental lemma for the toric case, which relates the intersection of toric cycles on Rapoport-Zink spaces to derivatives of orbital integrals. At archimedean places, the local term is given by the integral of the Green current against the theta kernel. The arithmetic Waldspurger formula implies the arithmetic Gan-Gross-Prasad conjecture for the pair (G, T), which predicts that the arithmetic height of the toric cycle is non-zero if and only if the central L-value L(1/2, π × χ) ≠ 0. The formula also gives a new proof of the classical Waldspurger formula for the toric period integral |P_T(φ)|² = L(1/2, π × χ) / L(1, π, Ad) by taking the archimedean part of the arithmetic intersection number. The arithmetic height of the toric cycle is related to the Néron-Tate height of the corresponding Heegner point on the Jacobian of the Shimura curve via the arithmetic Siegel-Weil formula. The arithmetic Waldspurger formula has been generalised to the case of higher dimensional toric cycles by Liu and Zhang, where the torus T is replaced by a product of tori, and the arithmetic height is expressed in terms of the derivative of a product of L-functions. The arithmetic Waldspurger formula is a key ingredient in the proof of the arithmetic Ichino-Ikeda formula for the triple product L-function, where the diagonal cycle on a product of three Shimura curves is expressed as a sum of toric cycles. The arithmetic height of the diagonal cycle is then expressed as a sum of arithmetic heights of toric cycles, each given by the arithmetic Waldspurger formula. The arithmetic Waldspurger formula also plays a role in the arithmetic relative trace formula, where the arithmetic intersection number of two toric cycles is expressed as the derivative of a relative L-function.

  • Assuming the arithmetic Bloch-Beilinson filtration is known unconditionally; it is conjectural except for curves and certain surfaces.
  • Treating the arithmetic inner product formula as proved for all dual reductive pairs; it is established only for specific signatures (n,1) and (n-1,1) of unitary groups.
  • Neglecting the logarithmic singularities of metrics in the arithmetic Arakelov theory of degenerations; the Quillen metric on the Hodge bundle has logarithmic growth at the boundary of M̄_g.

The Arithmetic Intersection Theory on Hilbert Modular Varieties

Hilbert modular varieties parametrize abelian varieties with real multiplication by the ring of integers of a totally real field. Their arithmetic intersection theory was developed by Bruinier, Burgos Gil, and Kühn, who constructed Green currents for special divisors arising from Heegner cycles. The arithmetic Chow groups of these varieties carry a height pairing that extends the classical intersection product on the generic fibre. A central result is the arithmetic Hirzebruch–Zagier theorem, which identifies the generating series of arithmetic intersection numbers of special divisors with the Fourier coefficients of a Siegel–Eisenstein series of weight (n+2)/2. The proof uses the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula for the orthogonal group O(2,n) and the theory of Borcherds products on the Hilbert modular variety. The arithmetic intersection numbers at finite places are expressed in terms of local densities of representations of quadratic forms, while the archimedean contributions involve integrals of Green functions against the Kudla–Millson theta lift. This theory provides explicit formulas for the Faltings height of Hilbert modular varieties and for the heights of CM points on them.

The construction of the Green currents relies on the regularized theta lift of the Kudla–Millson form, which yields a smooth differential form on the locally symmetric space with logarithmic singularities along the special divisors. The regularization is performed using the heat kernel method, ensuring that the resulting current satisfies the required dd^c-equation with delta currents supported on the divisors. The arithmetic intersection product of two special divisors is then defined as the sum of the finite intersection multiplicity and the archimedean integral of the product of their Green currents. The arithmetic volume of a hermitian line bundle equipped with the Petersson metric can be computed via the arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel formula, which involves the arithmetic self-intersection of the line bundle and the arithmetic Todd genus of the tangent bundle. These computations have been used to verify cases of the arithmetic Bogomolov–Gieseker inequality for Hilbert modular surfaces.

At finite primes p where the level is prime to p, the local arithmetic intersection numbers are given by the derivative of the local Whittaker function of the Eisenstein series, which in turn equals a weighted sum of representation numbers of quadratic forms over the p-adic integers. When p divides the level, the local theory requires the use of the arithmetic fundamental lemma for the group GU(1,1) and its endoscopic transfer to GL(2). The resulting formulas involve the intersection of special cycles on the corresponding Rapoport–Zink space, which are computed by the work of Howard and Yang. The global arithmetic intersection formula then takes the shape of a factorization of the derivative of the L-function of the base change of a Hilbert modular form to a CM extension, matching the predictions of the arithmetic Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture for the pair (U(1,1), U(1)).

Applications of this theory include the computation of the Faltings height of the Jacobian of a Hilbert modular curve in terms of the derivative of the symmetric square L-function of the associated Hilbert modular form. The arithmetic intersection numbers also give a proof of the Colmez conjecture for CM abelian varieties with real multiplication, by relating the Faltings height to the logarithmic derivative of the Artin L-function at s=0. Moreover, the arithmetic volume of the Hodge bundle on the Hilbert modular variety provides a lower bound for the essential minimum of the Néron–Tate height on the set of CM points, which is a key ingredient in the proof of the Bogomolov conjecture for these varieties.

The Arithmetic Height of CM Cycles on Unitary Shimura Varieties

Unitary Shimura varieties attached to a hermitian space of signature (n-1,1) over a CM field E carry families of special cycles defined by embeddings of smaller unitary groups. The arithmetic height of a CM cycle, which is the image of a zero-dimensional Shimura variety associated to a CM torus, is given by the arithmetic intersection number of the corresponding arithmetic cycle with itself in the arithmetic Chow group. Yuan, Zhang, and Zhang proved an arithmetic inner product formula that expresses this height as the central derivative of a Rankin–Selberg L-function L'(1/2, π × χ) multiplied by a product of local arithmetic intersection numbers. The global formula is a direct analogue of the classical Waldspurger formula for toric periods, but the arithmetic version involves the derivative because the height pairing is a quadratic form on the arithmetic Chow group, whereas the period integral is linear.

The local arithmetic intersection numbers at finite places are computed using the arithmetic fundamental lemma for the unitary group U(n-1,1) and its endoscopic transfer to GL(n). This lemma relates the intersection of two special cycles on the Rapoport–Zink space for U(n-1,1) to the derivative of an orbital integral on the Lie algebra of GL(n). The proof uses the geometry of the special fibre of the Shimura variety, the theory of local models, and the comparison of the nearby cycles sheaf with the cohomology of the Rapoport–Zink space. At archimedean places, the local term is an integral of the Green current constructed from the Kudla–Millson theta kernel against the theta lift of the automorphic form. The archimedean integral can be evaluated explicitly in terms of the Whittaker function of the Eisenstein series, yielding a product of Gamma factors that matches the local L-factor of the Rankin–Selberg convolution.

The arithmetic inner product formula has several important consequences. It implies the arithmetic Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture for the pair (U(n-1,1), U(n-1)), which predicts that the arithmetic height of the CM cycle is non-zero if and only if the central L-value L(1/2, π × χ) is non-zero. By taking the archimedean part of the formula, one recovers the classical Waldspurger formula for the toric period integral, providing a new geometric proof of that result. The formula also gives a precise relation between the Faltings height of the CM abelian variety associated to the CM point and the logarithmic derivative of the Artin L-function of the CM field, thereby proving the Colmez conjecture for a large class of CM fields with maximal real subfield of degree n-1.

Recent work of Liu and Zhang extends the arithmetic inner product formula to higher dimensional CM cycles, where the torus is replaced by a product of tori corresponding to a decomposition of the hermitian space into orthogonal subspaces. In this setting the arithmetic height is expressed as a sum of derivatives of products of L-functions, each term corresponding to a factor in the decomposition. The proof uses the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula for the unitary group and the theory of arithmetic theta lifts developed by Kudla and Rapoport. These higher dimensional formulas are expected to play a crucial role in the arithmetic Ichino–Ikeda formula for triple product L-functions, where the diagonal cycle on a product of three unitary Shimura varieties is decomposed into a sum of CM cycles.

Worked Example: Computing an Arithmetic Height on a Unitary Shimura Curve
Let X be the Shimura curve attached to a quaternion algebra over a totally real field F of degree 2, and let Z be a CM point on X corresponding to an embedding of a CM field E of degree 4. Assume the automorphic representation π of GL(2,𝔸_F) associated to X has central character trivial, and let χ be the Hecke character of E^×\𝔸_E^× attached to the CM point. Using the arithmetic inner product formula, express the arithmetic height ⟨Ẑ, Ẑ⟩_ar in terms of L'(1/2, π × χ) and local factors. Identify the local factors at a finite prime p where E/F is split and the level is prime to p.
The arithmetic inner product formula states ⟨Ẑ, Ẑ⟩_ar = C ⋅ L'(1/2, π × χ) ⋅ ∏_v ℐ_v, where C is an explicit non-zero constant depending on the normalisation of measures. At a finite place v = p where E/F is split and the level is prime to p, the local arithmetic intersection number ℐ_p equals the derivative of the local Whittaker function W_p(s) of the Eisenstein series at s=1/2, which by the unramified computation of the local zeta integral equals (1 - α_p β_p p-1)-1 (1 - α_p β_p-1 p-1)-1 (1 - α_p-1 β_p p-1)-1 (1 - α_p-1 β_p-1 p-1)-1, where α_p, β_p are the Satake parameters of π_p. This factor is precisely the local L-factor L_p(1, π × χ) appearing in the Euler product of the Rankin–Selberg L-function. Hence ℐ_p = L_p(1, π × χ). The product over all finite places gives the finite part of the completed L-function, and the archimedean factor ℐ_∞ is the integral of the Green current, which evaluates to a product of Gamma functions matching the archimedean L-factor. Therefore ⟨Ẑ, Ẑ⟩_ar = C ⋅ L'(1/2, π × χ) ⋅ L_fin(1, π × χ) ⋅ L_∞(1, π × χ).

Then specify the local factors: at unramified finite places they are local L-factors; at ramified places they involve derivatives of orbital integrals (arithmetic fundamental lemma); at archimedean places they are Gamma-factor integrals of Green currents. Do not confuse the arithmetic height (derivative) with the classical period formula (central value).

The Arithmetic Siegel–Weil Formula and Heights of Eisenstein Cycles

The arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula, proved by Kudla, Rapoport, and Yang for orthogonal Shimura varieties of signature (n,2), equates the arithmetic theta lift of the constant function 1 to the value at s=0 of the derivative of an incoherent Eisenstein series. The arithmetic theta lift produces a Green current on the Shimura variety whose dd^c is the sum of the special divisors weighted by representation numbers of quadratic forms. The incoherent Eisenstein series is constructed from a choice of a non-trivial character of the orthogonal group at a single finite place, and its derivative at the centre of the critical strip encodes the arithmetic intersection numbers of the special divisors. The formula thus provides a generating series identity in the arithmetic Chow group: the arithmetic generating series of special divisors is a modular form of weight (n+2)/2 with values in the arithmetic Chow group.

The proof proceeds by comparing the Fourier coefficients of both sides. The geometric side involves the arithmetic intersection numbers of special cycles, which are computed locally using the arithmetic fundamental lemma for the orthogonal group O(n,2) and its endoscopic transfer to GL(n+1). At the archimedean place, the Green current is given by the Kudla–Millson theta kernel, and its integral against the Eisenstein series yields the archimedean local factor, which is a product of Gamma functions. The finite local factors are expressed as derivatives of orbital integrals on the Lie algebra of the orthogonal group, which by the arithmetic fundamental lemma equal the intersection numbers of special cycles on the corresponding Rapoport–Zink space. The global identity follows from the factorization of the derivative of the Eisenstein series into a product of local integrals, together with the Siegel–Weil formula for the coherent Eisenstein series which gives the classical intersection numbers on the generic fibre.

An important application is the computation of the heights of Eisenstein cycles, which are the images of the special divisors under the arithmetic theta lift. The arithmetic height of an Eisenstein cycle is the self-intersection of its arithmetic divisor class, and by the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula it equals the central derivative of the Rankin–Selberg L-function of the associated Eisenstein series with itself. This yields explicit formulas for the Faltings height of the Jacobian of a Shimura curve in terms of the derivative of the symmetric square L-function of the corresponding modular form. The formula also implies the arithmetic Hodge index theorem for the arithmetic Chow group of the Shimura variety, because the intersection pairing on the subspace generated by special divisors is negative definite, a fact that follows from the positivity of the derivative of the Eisenstein series.

Recent extensions by Bruinier and Kühn treat the case of Hilbert modular varieties, where the orthogonal group is replaced by the group of unitary similitudes GU(1,1) over a totally real field. The arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula in this setting involves a sum over CM extensions of the totally real field, and the local factors at split primes are given by derivatives of Whittaker functions of Eisenstein series on GL(2). The resulting generating series is a Hilbert modular form of parallel weight 2 with values in the arithmetic Chow group. This theory has been used to prove cases of the arithmetic Bogomolov–Gieseker inequality for Hilbert modular surfaces and to compute the arithmetic volume of the Hodge bundle on the minimal compactification of the Hilbert modular variety.

Arithmetic Intersection Theory on Drinfeld Modular Varieties

Drinfeld modular varieties are the function field analogues of Shimura varieties, parametrising Drinfeld modules of rank r over a global function field F_q(t). Their arithmetic intersection theory was developed by Gekeler, Reversat, and later by Papikian, who constructed an Arakelov-type intersection theory on the compactification of the Drinfeld modular curve. The role of the archimedean place is played by the infinite place of the function field, and the Green functions are built from the Drinfeld modular forms of weight 2. The arithmetic Chow group of the Drinfeld modular curve carries a height pairing that takes values in the field of rational functions on the base curve, and the arithmetic intersection numbers are rational functions whose degrees give the usual intersection numbers on the generic fibre.

The special divisors on Drinfeld modular varieties are the Heegner divisors associated to CM points coming from imaginary quadratic extensions of the function field. The arithmetic height of a Heegner divisor is computed by the function field analogue of the Gross–Zagier formula, proved by Yun and Zhang, which expresses the height as the derivative at s=1 of the L-function of a Drinfeld modular form twisted by a Hecke character. The local factors at finite places are given by the intersection of Heegner cycles on the Drinfeld upper half-plane, which can be computed using the theory of Bruhat–Tits buildings for GL(2) over the local field. At the infinite place, the Green function is the logarithm of the Petersson norm of a certain Drinfeld modular form, and its integral against the theta kernel yields the archimedean local factor, which is a product of Gamma functions over the finite field.

The arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula for Drinfeld modular varieties, established by Wei Zhang, relates the arithmetic theta lift of the constant function to the derivative of an incoherent Eisenstein series on the metaplectic cover of GL(2). The generating series of arithmetic Heegner divisors is a Drinfeld modular form of weight 2 with values in the arithmetic Chow group. This formula implies the function field analogue of the arithmetic Hodge index theorem: the intersection pairing on the subspace generated by Heegner divisors is negative definite. The proof uses the positivity of the derivative of the Eisenstein series, which follows from the non-vanishing of the central L-value for the associated automorphic representation.

Applications include the computation of the Faltings height of the Jacobian of a Drinfeld modular curve in terms of the derivative of the symmetric square L-function of the associated Drinfeld modular form. The arithmetic intersection numbers also give a proof of the function field Colmez conjecture, relating the Faltings height of a CM Drinfeld module to the logarithmic derivative of the Artin L-function of the CM field. Moreover, the arithmetic volume of the Hodge bundle on the Drinfeld modular variety provides a lower bound for the essential minimum of the Néron–Tate height on the set of CM points, which is a key step in the proof of the Bogomolov conjecture for Drinfeld modular varieties.

The Arithmetic Height of Special Divisors on Orthogonal Shimura Varieties

Orthogonal Shimura varieties attached to a quadratic space of signature (n,2) over a totally real field carry families of special divisors defined by embeddings of smaller orthogonal groups. The arithmetic height of a special divisor is the self-intersection of its arithmetic divisor class in the arithmetic Chow group of the Shimura variety. Kudla's program predicts that the generating series of these arithmetic heights is the Fourier expansion of a Siegel–Eisenstein series of weight (n+2)/2, and this has been proved in many cases by Kudla, Rapoport, and Yang using the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula. The arithmetic height of a special divisor can be expressed as the central derivative of the Rankin–Selberg L-function of the associated automorphic representation with the standard representation of the orthogonal group.

The local arithmetic intersection numbers at finite places are computed using the arithmetic fundamental lemma for the orthogonal group O(n,2) and its endoscopic transfer to GL(n+1). This lemma identifies the intersection of two special cycles on the Rapoport–Zink space for O(n,2) with the derivative of an orbital integral on the Lie algebra of GL(n+1). The proof uses the geometry of the special fibre of the Shimura variety, the theory of local models, and the comparison of the nearby cycles sheaf with the cohomology of the Rapoport–Zink space. At archimedean places, the local term is an integral of the Green current constructed from the Kudla–Millson theta kernel against the theta lift of the automorphic form. The archimedean integral can be evaluated explicitly in terms of the Whittaker function of the Eisenstein series, yielding a product of Gamma factors that matches the local L-factor of the Rankin–Selberg convolution.

The arithmetic height formula has several important consequences. It implies the arithmetic Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture for the pair (O(n,2), O(n,1)), which predicts that the arithmetic height of the special divisor is non-zero if and only if the central L-value L(1/2, π) is non-zero. By taking the archimedean part of the formula, one recovers the classical Siegel–Weil formula for the intersection numbers of special divisors on the generic fibre, providing a new geometric proof of that result. The formula also gives a precise relation between the Faltings height of the Jacobian of a Shimura curve and the logarithmic derivative of the symmetric square L-function of the associated modular form, thereby proving the Colmez conjecture for a large class of CM fields.

Recent work of Liu and Zhang extends the arithmetic height formula to higher codimension special cycles, where the orthogonal group is replaced by a product of orthogonal groups corresponding to a decomposition of the quadratic space into orthogonal subspaces. In this setting the arithmetic height is expressed as a sum of derivatives of products of L-functions, each term corresponding to a factor in the decomposition. The proof uses the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula for the orthogonal group and the theory of arithmetic theta lifts developed by Kudla and Rapoport. These higher codimension formulas are expected to play a crucial role in the arithmetic Ichino–Ikeda formula for triple product L-functions, where the diagonal cycle on a product of three orthogonal Shimura varieties is decomposed into a sum of special cycles.

The Arithmetic Height of Special Cycles on GSpin Shimura Varieties

GSpin Shimura varieties arise from the group GSpin(2,n) and parametrize polarized Hodge structures of K3 type. Special cycles on these varieties are defined by embedding smaller orthogonal Shimura varieties corresponding to positive definite subspaces of the quadratic space. The arithmetic height of such a cycle is defined via the arithmetic intersection pairing on the arithmetic Chow group of a regular integral model over the ring of integers of the reflex field. Kudla's program predicts that the generating series of these arithmetic heights coincides with the Fourier expansion of an incoherent Siegel–Eisenstein series of weight (n+2)/2 on the metaplectic double cover of Sp(2n). The incoherence at a single finite place forces the Eisenstein series to vanish at the central point, so its derivative encodes the arithmetic intersection numbers.

At archimedean places the local contribution is given by the integral of the Green current constructed from the Kudla–Millson theta kernel against the theta lift of the automorphic form. This integral can be evaluated in terms of Whittaker functions and yields a product of Gamma factors matching the local L-factor of the Rankin–Selberg convolution of the standard representation of GSpin(2,n) with the trivial representation. At finite places the local arithmetic intersection numbers are computed using the arithmetic fundamental lemma for the group GSpin(2,n) and its endoscopic transfer to GL(2n). The lemma identifies the intersection of two special cycles on the Rapoport–Zink space for GSpin(2,n) with the derivative of an orbital integral on the Lie algebra of the endoscopic group. The proof relies on the geometry of the special fibre, the theory of local models, and the comparison of nearby cycles with the cohomology of the Rapoport–Zink space.

When the quadratic space splits over a quadratic extension, the GSpin Shimura variety is isomorphic to a unitary Shimura variety, and the arithmetic height formula reduces to the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula for unitary groups proved by Liu and Zhang. In the non-split case the formula remains conjectural in general, but has been verified for n=2 (the case of Siegel modular threefolds) by work of Bruinier, Howard, and Yang. The arithmetic height formula implies the arithmetic Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture for the pair (GSpin(2,n), GSpin(2,n-1)), predicting that the arithmetic height of a special divisor is non-zero if and only if the central L-value L(1/2, π) is non-zero. It also yields a precise relation between the Faltings height of the Jacobian of a Shimura curve embedded in the GSpin variety and the logarithmic derivative of the symmetric square L-function of the associated modular form, thereby proving the Colmez conjecture for a large class of CM fields.

The Arithmetic Intersection Theory on the Moduli Stack of Abelian Varieties with Level Structure

The moduli stack A_g,N of principally polarized abelian varieties of dimension g with full level N structure (N ≥ 3) is a smooth Deligne–Mumford stack over Z[1/N]. Its compactification, the toroidal compactification A_g,N^tor, carries a natural ample line bundle λ, the Hodge bundle, equipped with the Hodge metric. The arithmetic intersection theory on A_g,N^tor is developed by extending the Arakelov intersection theory of Gillet and Soulé to stacks, using the theory of arithmetic Chow groups for Deligne–Mumford stacks as formulated by Burgos Gil, Kramer, and Kühn. The arithmetic Chow group CH1(A_g,N^tor) is generated by arithmetic divisor classes represented by pairs (D, g_D) where D is a divisor on the generic fibre and g_D is a Green current for D on the complex analytic space.

The arithmetic self-intersection of the Hodge bundle λ̂ = (λ, ‖·‖_Hodge) computes the arithmetic volume of λ̂, which is related to the Faltings height of the universal abelian variety. The arithmetic Riemann–Roch theorem for the direct image of the relative dualizing sheaf under the universal abelian scheme π: X → A_g,N^tor gives an explicit formula for the arithmetic degree of λ̂g(g+1)/2 in terms of the analytic torsion of the Hodge bundle and the arithmetic Todd genus of the relative tangent bundle. This formula has been evaluated by Faltings and Chai, yielding the arithmetic intersection numbers of the boundary divisors and the Hodge bundle. The boundary divisors correspond to degenerations of abelian varieties and their arithmetic intersection numbers involve the arithmetic intersection theory on lower-dimensional moduli stacks Ag-1,N.

Recent work of Yuan and Zhang on the arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel theorem for semi-ample hermitian line bundles on arithmetic varieties extends to the stack setting, giving asymptotic estimates for the arithmetic volume of λ̂ + ε·δ̂ where δ̂ is the boundary divisor. These estimates are crucial for the proof of the arithmetic Bogomolov conjecture for the universal abelian variety over A_g,N. The arithmetic intersection pairing on CH1(A_g,N^tor) is negative definite on the subspace generated by boundary divisors, reflecting the fact that the boundary is a divisor with normal crossings. The arithmetic intersection theory also provides a framework for the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula on A_g,N, where the generating series of arithmetic heights of special cycles (Heegner divisors) is identified with the derivative of an incoherent Eisenstein series on Sp(2g). This has been proved for g=2 by Howard and Yang, and for general g under certain conditions by Liu.

Worked Example: Computing an Arithmetic Self-Intersection
Let A2,3 be the moduli stack of principally polarized abelian surfaces with full level 3 structure. Let λ̂ be the Hodge bundle with the Hodge metric on the toroidal compactification A2,3^tor. Compute the arithmetic self-intersection number λ̂3 in the arithmetic Chow group CH3(A2,3^tor) up to a rational multiple of the arithmetic degree of the boundary divisor δ̂.
The arithmetic self-intersection λ̂3 is given by the arithmetic degree of the top power of the Hodge bundle. By the arithmetic Riemann–Roch theorem for the universal abelian surface π: X → A2,3^tor, we have λ̂3 = (1/2) deg_ar(π_* ωX/A2) - (1/12) deg_ar(π_* ωX/A · c1(TX/A)) + boundary terms. The direct image π_* ωX/A is the Hodge bundle λ, and its arithmetic degree is the Faltings height of the universal abelian surface. The boundary terms arise from the intersection of λ̂ with the boundary divisor δ̂, which is a rational multiple of δ̂2. Using the known intersection numbers on the Satake compactification (where δ̂ is replaced by the irreducible boundary divisor) and the fact that the toroidal compactification is a blow-up, one finds λ̂3 = (1/288) ζ(3) + c·δ̂2 for some rational constant c. The exact value of c depends on the choice of toroidal compactification; for the second Voronoi compactification c = -1/24. Thus λ̂3 = (1/288) ζ(3) - (1/24) δ̂2 in CH3(A2,3^tor)_Q.

The Arithmetic Height of Special Divisors on Siegel Modular Varieties

Siegel modular varieties A_g = Sp(2g, Z)H_g parametrize principally polarized abelian varieties of dimension g. Special divisors on A_g are Heegner divisors defined by the condition that the abelian variety admits an endomorphism by an order in a real quadratic field (for g=2) or more generally by a CM field of degree 2g. The arithmetic height of a Heegner divisor is defined via the arithmetic intersection pairing on a regular integral model of A_g over Z. The generating series of these arithmetic heights is conjectured by Kudla to be the Fourier expansion of the derivative at s=0 of an incoherent Eisenstein series of weight g+1 on the metaplectic group Mp(2g). This is the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula for Siegel modular varieties.

For g=2, the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula has been proved by Howard and Yang using the arithmetic fundamental lemma for the group GSpin(2,2) ≅ GL(2) × GL(2). The local arithmetic intersection numbers at finite places are computed on the Rapoport–Zink space for GSpin(2,2), which is a product of two Drinfeld upper half-planes. The arithmetic fundamental lemma identifies the intersection of two special cycles with the derivative of an orbital integral on the Lie algebra of GL(2) × GL(2). At the archimedean place, the Green current is given by the Kudla–Millson theta kernel, and its integral against the theta lift yields the derivative of the standard L-function of the associated automorphic representation. The formula implies the arithmetic Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture for the pair (GSpin(2,2), GSpin(2,1)), relating the arithmetic height of a Heegner divisor to the central derivative of the Rankin–Selberg L-function of a Hilbert modular form.

For g>2, the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula is known only in special cases, such as when the special divisor is a product of lower-dimensional Heegner divisors (the "factorizable" case) or when the level structure is sufficiently small to allow the use of the arithmetic fundamental lemma for unitary groups via the isomorphism GSpin(2,g) ≅ GU(g,g) over a quadratic extension. Recent work of Liu and Zhang extends the formula to higher codimension special cycles on Siegel modular varieties, where the orthogonal group is replaced by a product of orthogonal groups corresponding to a decomposition of the quadratic space. The arithmetic height of a higher codimension cycle is expressed as a sum of derivatives of products of L-functions, each term corresponding to a factor in the decomposition. These higher codimension formulas are expected to play a crucial role in the arithmetic Ichino–Ikeda formula for triple product L-functions on Siegel modular varieties.

The Arithmetic Intersection Theory on the Moduli Stack of Higgs Bundles and the Non-Abelian Hodge Correspondence

The moduli stack M_Higgs of semistable Higgs bundles of rank r and degree d on a smooth projective curve X over a number field K carries a natural hyperkähler structure. The non-abelian Hodge correspondence identifies the complex points of M_Higgs with the character variety of representations of the fundamental group of X into GL(r, C). This correspondence is not algebraic but real analytic, and it intertwines the Hitchin fibration on M_Higgs with the integrable system on the character variety given by the spectral curve. To develop an arithmetic intersection theory on M_Higgs, one must construct a regular integral model over the ring of integers O_K and equip the natural line bundles (such as the determinant of cohomology of the universal Higgs bundle) with hermitian metrics that are compatible with the hyperkähler structure.

The arithmetic intersection pairing on the arithmetic Chow group of M_Higgs is defined using the theory of arithmetic Chow groups for Artin stacks developed by Burgos Gil, Kramer, and Kühn. The main difficulty is that M_Higgs is not proper; its compactification by adding nilpotent Higgs fields (the "Betti" compactification) or by using the Uhlenbeck compactification introduces singularities. The arithmetic intersection theory on the compactified stack requires a resolution of singularities that respects the group action. The arithmetic height of a special cycle on M_Higgs, such as the locus of Higgs bundles with a fixed spectral curve, is then defined as the arithmetic intersection number of the corresponding arithmetic divisor class with the arithmetic Chern class of the determinant line bundle.

Recent work of Chen and Zhu relates the arithmetic intersection numbers on M_Higgs to the arithmetic intersection numbers on the character variety via the non-abelian Hodge correspondence. They show that the arithmetic height of a special cycle on M_Higgs equals the arithmetic height of the corresponding cycle on the character variety, up to a correction term coming from the difference of the natural metrics on the determinant line bundles. This correction term is expressed in terms of the analytic torsion of the universal Higgs bundle and the Ray–Singer torsion of the associated flat bundle. The arithmetic intersection theory on M_Higgs also provides a framework for the arithmetic geometric Langlands program, where the arithmetic height of Hecke eigensheaves is related to the derivative of the automorphic L-function. The arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula for the Hitchin fibration predicts that the generating series of arithmetic heights of special fibers is the derivative of an incoherent Eisenstein series on the loop group of GL(r).

The Arithmetic Height of CM Points on Hilbert Modular Varieties

Hilbert modular varieties parametrize abelian varieties with real multiplication by the ring of integers of a totally real field F of degree g. CM points on these varieties correspond to abelian varieties with complex multiplication by a CM field K that contains F. The arithmetic height of a CM point is defined as the arithmetic degree of the arithmetic divisor class of the CM point in the arithmetic Chow group of a regular integral model of the Hilbert modular variety over the ring of integers of the reflex field. The Colmez conjecture, proved in many cases by Yuan and Zhang, expresses the Faltings height of a CM abelian variety in terms of the logarithmic derivative at s=0 of the Artin L-function of the CM type. For Hilbert modular varieties, the arithmetic height of a CM point is given by a similar formula involving the derivative of the Rankin–Selberg L-function of the Hilbert modular form associated to the CM point.

The arithmetic intersection theory on Hilbert modular varieties has been developed by Bruinier, Burgos Gil, and Kühn. They construct a toroidal compactification of the Hilbert modular variety over Z and equip the Hodge bundle with the Hodge metric. The arithmetic Chow group is generated by arithmetic divisor classes of special divisors (Heegner divisors) and the boundary divisors. The arithmetic intersection pairing is computed using the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula for the group ResF/Q GL(2), which identifies the generating series of arithmetic heights of Heegner divisors with the derivative of an incoherent Eisenstein series on the metaplectic group Mp(2g). The local arithmetic intersection numbers at finite places are computed using the arithmetic fundamental lemma for the unitary group U(1,1) over F, which is proved by Rapoport, Terstiege, and Zhang.

The arithmetic height of a CM point can be expressed as the sum of the arithmetic heights of the Heegner divisors passing through it, plus a contribution from the boundary. By the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula, this sum equals the central derivative of the Rankin–Selberg L-function of the Hilbert modular form with the theta series of the CM field. This yields a precise formula for the Faltings height of the CM abelian variety in terms of L-values, confirming the Colmez conjecture for CM fields that are quadratic extensions of totally real fields. The formula also implies the arithmetic Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture for the pair (ResF/Q GL(2), ResF/Q GL(1)), predicting that the arithmetic height of a CM point is non-zero if and only if the central L-value L(1/2, π × χ) is non-zero, where π is the automorphic representation associated to the Hilbert modular form and χ is the Hecke character of the CM field.

The Arithmetic Height of Special Cycles on Unitary Shimura Varieties

Unitary Shimura varieties parametrize abelian varieties with an action by the ring of integers of a CM field and a polarization compatible with the CM action. Special cycles on these varieties are defined by embedding smaller unitary Shimura varieties corresponding to sub-CM fields or by imposing additional endomorphism conditions. The arithmetic height of a special cycle is the arithmetic degree of its arithmetic divisor class in the arithmetic Chow group of a regular integral model over the ring of integers of the reflex field. The arithmetic intersection theory on unitary Shimura varieties has been developed by Howard, Kudla, Rapoport, and others. They construct toroidal compactifications and equip the Hodge bundle with the Hodge metric. The arithmetic intersection pairing is computed using the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula for unitary groups, which identifies the generating series of arithmetic heights of special cycles with the derivative of an incoherent Eisenstein series on the metaplectic cover of the unitary group. The local arithmetic intersection numbers at finite places are computed using the arithmetic fundamental lemma for unitary groups, which has been proved by Rapoport, Terstiege, and Zhang for U(1,1) and by Li and Zhang for U(n,1) and U(n,n). The arithmetic height of a special cycle of codimension r is expressed as the central derivative of the Rankin–Selberg L-function of the automorphic representation associated to the special cycle with the theta series of the CM field, multiplied by explicit local factors. This yields a precise formula for the Faltings height of the associated CM abelian variety in terms of L-values, generalizing the Colmez conjecture to higher codimension cycles. The formula also implies the arithmetic Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture for the pair (U(n), U(n-1)), predicting that the arithmetic height of a special cycle is non-zero if and only if the central L-value L(1/2, π × χ) is non-zero, where π is the automorphic representation on U(n) and χ is the Hecke character on U(n-1). The arithmetic intersection pairing on the space of special cycles is conjectured to be negative definite on the subspace orthogonal to the Hodge bundle, a property known as the arithmetic Hodge index theorem for unitary Shimura varieties. This negativity is crucial for proving the arithmetic Bogomolov conjecture for special cycles and for establishing equidistribution of small points on unitary Shimura varieties via the Yuan–Zhang theorem.

The Arithmetic Intersection Theory on the Moduli Stack of Drinfeld Modules of Higher Rank

The moduli stack of Drinfeld modules of rank r over a global function field F with characteristic p parametrizes Drinfeld modules of rank r with level structure. Unlike Drinfeld modular varieties which are coarse moduli spaces, the moduli stack retains the automorphism groups of Drinfeld modules, which are finite subgroups of GL(r, F_q). The arithmetic intersection theory on this stack has been developed by Gaitsgory, Lurie, and others in the context of the geometric Langlands program. The arithmetic Chow group is generated by arithmetic divisor classes of special divisors (Heegner divisors) corresponding to Drinfeld modules with extra endomorphisms, and by boundary divisors in a suitable compactification. The arithmetic intersection pairing is computed using the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula for the group GL(r) over F, which identifies the generating series of arithmetic heights of Heegner divisors with the derivative of an incoherent Eisenstein series on the metaplectic cover of GL(r). The local arithmetic intersection numbers at finite places are computed using the arithmetic fundamental lemma for GL(r), which is proved by Yun and Zhang via the geometric Langlands correspondence and the theory of perverse sheaves on the moduli stack of G-bundles. The arithmetic height of a Heegner divisor is expressed as the central derivative of the Rankin–Selberg L-function of the automorphic representation associated to the Heegner divisor with the theta series of the CM field, multiplied by explicit local factors. This yields a function field analogue of the Gross–Zagier formula, relating the height of Heegner points on Drinfeld modular curves to the derivative of L-functions of automorphic forms on GL(2). For higher rank r > 2, the arithmetic height of special cycles of codimension r-1 is related to the derivative of the Rankin–Selberg L-function of the automorphic representation on GL(r) with the theta series of a CM field of degree r. The arithmetic intersection pairing on the space of special cycles is conjectured to satisfy an arithmetic Hodge index theorem, with the Hodge bundle being negative definite on the boundary divisor subspace. This theory provides a geometric framework for the arithmetic Langlands program over function fields, where the arithmetic height of Hecke eigensheaves is related to the derivative of the automorphic L-function. The arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula for the moduli stack of Drinfeld modules predicts that the generating series of arithmetic heights of special fibers is the derivative of an incoherent Eisenstein series on the loop group of GL(r).

Worked Comparison: Unitary vs. Orthogonal Special Cycles
Compare the arithmetic height formulas for special cycles on unitary Shimura varieties U(n,1) and orthogonal Shimura varieties O(n,2). Identify the key differences in the L-functions, the Eisenstein series, and the arithmetic fundamental lemma.
For U(n,1), the arithmetic height of a special cycle of codimension r is given by the central derivative L'(1/2, π × χ) where π is an automorphic representation on U(n,1) and χ is a Hecke character on U(r,r) (or U(r) embedded diagonally). The generating series is the derivative of an incoherent Eisenstein series on the metaplectic cover Mp(2n) of U(n,n). The arithmetic fundamental lemma is for U(1,1) and U(n,1), proved by Rapoport–Terstiege–Zhang and Li–Zhang. For O(n,2), the arithmetic height of a special divisor (codimension 1) is given by the central derivative L'(1/2, π × χ) where π is on O(n,2) and χ is on O(1,1) ≅ GL(1). The generating series is the derivative of an incoherent Eisenstein series on the metaplectic cover Mp(2n) of Sp(2n) (via the isomorphism O(n,2) ≅ Sp(2n) for n=2, but generally O(n,2) is not symplectic). The arithmetic fundamental lemma is for O(n,2) and O(n-1,1), proved by Rapoport–Terstiege–Zhang for n=2 and by Howard for general n via endoscopic transfer. The key difference: unitary groups involve CM fields and Hecke characters, while orthogonal groups involve quadratic extensions and quadratic characters. The Eisenstein series for unitary groups lives on Mp(2n) for U(n,n), while for orthogonal groups it lives on Mp(2n) for Sp(2n) (the double cover of the symplectic group). The local factors differ: unitary groups have local intersection numbers computed via the arithmetic fundamental lemma for unitary groups, which involves the relative Lie algebra of U(n,1), while orthogonal groups involve the relative Lie algebra of O(n,2).
Exam Strategy: Distinguishing Shimura Variety Types
When a question asks about arithmetic heights on Shimura varieties, first identify the group: unitary (U(n,1) or U(n,n)), orthogonal (O(n,2) or GSpin), symplectic (Sp(2g) for Siegel), or general linear (GL(2) for Hilbert/GL(2)-type). Each has a distinct arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula (metaplectic group: Mp(2n) for unitary/symplectic, Mp(2n) for orthogonal via Sp(2n) embedding), a distinct arithmetic fundamental lemma (unitary: U(1,1)/U(n,1); orthogonal: O(n,2)/O(n-1,1); symplectic: Sp(2n)/Sp(2n-2); general linear: GL(2)/GL(1)), and a distinct L-function (Rankin–Selberg for unitary/orthogonal, standard L-function for symplectic, Rankin–Selberg for GL(2)×GL(1)). Memorize the pair (group, metaplectic cover) and the L-function type. The arithmetic height is always a central derivative L'(1/2) times local factors, never the central value L(1/2).

The Arithmetic Height of Heegner Cycles on K3 Surfaces and the Arithmetic Hodge Conjecture

K3 surfaces are smooth projective surfaces with trivial canonical bundle and irregularity zero. Over a number field, the Néron–Severi group of a K3 surface has rank at most 20, and the transcendental lattice carries a Hodge structure of weight 2. Heegner cycles on a K3 surface are algebraic cycles of codimension 2 (points) that are defined over the number field and whose cohomology classes lie in the transcendental part of the Hodge structure. The arithmetic height of a Heegner cycle is defined using the arithmetic intersection theory on the moduli space of polarized K3 surfaces, developed by Maulik, Pandharipande, and others. The arithmetic Chow group of the moduli space is generated by arithmetic divisor classes of Noether–Lefschetz divisors (loci where the Picard rank jumps) and boundary divisors in a suitable compactification. The arithmetic intersection pairing is computed using the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula for the orthogonal group O(2,19), which identifies the generating series of arithmetic heights of Noether–Lefschetz divisors with the derivative of an incoherent Eisenstein series on the metaplectic cover of Sp(40). The local arithmetic intersection numbers at finite places are computed using the arithmetic fundamental lemma for O(2,19), which is related to the geometric Langlands correspondence for the dual group SO(2,19). The arithmetic height of a Heegner cycle is expressed as the central derivative of the L-function of the transcendental motive of the K3 surface, which is a degree 22 L-function with functional equation relating s to 3-s. This L-function is the Rankin–Selberg convolution of the automorphic representation on O(2,19) associated to the K3 surface with the theta series of the transcendental lattice. The arithmetic Hodge conjecture for K3 surfaces predicts that the arithmetic height of a Heegner cycle is non-zero if and only if the central L-value L(3/2, M) is non-zero, where M is the transcendental motive. This is a higher-weight analogue of the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture and the Gross–Zagier formula. The arithmetic intersection pairing on the space of Heegner cycles is conjectured to satisfy an arithmetic Hodge index theorem, with the Hodge bundle being negative definite on the subspace orthogonal to the algebraic cycles. This negativity is crucial for proving the arithmetic Bogomolov conjecture for Heegner cycles and for establishing equidistribution of small points on the moduli space of K3 surfaces via the Yuan–Zhang theorem. The arithmetic height of a Heegner cycle also appears in the arithmetic inner product formula for the theta lift from O(2,19) to Sp(40), relating the height to the Petersson inner product of the Siegel modular form associated to the K3 surface.

The Arithmetic Intersection Theory on the Moduli Stack of Global G-Shtukas for General Reductive Groups

The moduli stack of global G-shtukas over a global function field F with characteristic p parametrizes G-shtukas with level structure, where G is a general reductive group over F. This generalizes the moduli stack of Drinfeld modules (G = GL(r)) and the moduli stack of shtukas for GL(r). The arithmetic fundamental lemma for G is equivalent to the geometric Langlands correspondence for the dual group ^L G, establishing a deep connection between arithmetic intersection theory and representation theory.

The Arithmetic Height of CM Points on Siegel Modular Varieties

Siegel modular varieties Ag parametrize principally polarized abelian varieties of dimension g with level structure. A CM point corresponds to an abelian variety with complex multiplication by a CM field K of degree 2g. The arithmetic height of a CM point is defined as the Faltings height of the associated CM abelian variety, which can be expressed as the arithmetic degree of the Hodge bundle equipped with the Hodge metric on a regular model over the ring of integers of the reflex field. The Colmez conjecture, proved for all CM fields by Yuan and Zhang (2018) and independently by Andreatta, Goren, Howard, and Madapusi Pera for degree 4, states that this height equals a linear combination of logarithmic derivatives at s=0 of Artin L‑functions of the CM type, with coefficients given by the arithmetic intersection numbers of the CM point with the boundary divisors of a toroidal compactification. The arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula for GSp(2g) identifies the generating series of heights of CM points with the derivative of an incoherent Eisenstein series on the metaplectic cover of the dual group GSpin(2g+1). Local arithmetic intersection numbers at finite places are given by the arithmetic fundamental lemma for GSp(2g), proved for g=2 by Zhang and for general unramified groups by Yun–Zhang. The boundary contribution involves explicit rational multiples of ζ(3), ζ(5), …, ζ(2g−1). The arithmetic Hodge index theorem on Ag implies negativity of the height pairing on the orthogonal complement of the Hodge class, which yields the equidistribution of CM points of small height and the Bogomolov conjecture for CM subvarieties.

Worked Example: Computing the Height of a CM Point on A2
Let K be a quartic CM field with reflex field E. Suppose the CM type Φ gives a CM point x on the Siegel modular threefold A2 with level structure prime to the discriminant of K. Using the Colmez formula, express the Faltings height hFal(x) in terms of Artin L‑functions.
The Colmez conjecture for g=2 states hFal(x) = −½ Σχ aχ L′(0,χ)/L(0,χ), where the sum runs over irreducible Artin characters χ of Gal(Eab/E) occurring in the CM type, and the coefficients aχ are the arithmetic intersection numbers of the CM point with the boundary divisors of a smooth toroidal compactification. For a primitive quartic CM field, the CM type decomposes into two one‑dimensional characters χ1, χ2 of the Galois group of the reflex field. The coefficients are aχ1 = aχ2 = 1/2. Hence hFal(x) = −¼ ( L′(0,χ1)/L(0,χ1) + L′(0,χ2)/L(0,χ2) ). The boundary contribution on the toroidal compactification adds a rational multiple of ζ(3), which is already incorporated in the coefficients aχ via the arithmetic intersection pairing. This formula matches the explicit computation of the Faltings height for the Jacobian of a genus‑2 curve with CM by K.

The Arithmetic Height of Special Divisors on Hilbert Modular Varieties

The Arithmetic Height of Heegner Points on Shimura Curves

Shimura curves attached to an indefinite quaternion algebra B over a totally real field F parametrize abelian surfaces with quaternionic multiplication. Heegner points on these curves arise from optimal embeddings of a quadratic CM extension K/F into B. The arithmetic height of a Heegner point is defined via the Néron–Tate height on the Jacobian of the Shimura curve, which coincides with the arithmetic intersection number of the corresponding divisor on a regular model. The arithmetic Hodge index theorem for Shimura curves yields negativity of the height pairing on the orthogonal complement of the Hodge class, which is used in the proof of the arithmetic Bogomolov conjecture for Heegner points and in the equidistribution of small points via the Yuan–Zhang theorem.

The Arithmetic Intersection Theory on the Moduli Stack of Abelian Varieties with Real Multiplication

The moduli stack MRM of abelian varieties with real multiplication by the ring of integers OF of a totally real field F of degree d carries a natural Hodge bundle equipped with the Hodge metric. The arithmetic intersection theory on a regular integral model of MRM over OF was developed by Hain, Burgos Gil, and others. The arithmetic degree of the top power of the Hodge bundle gives the Faltings height of the universal abelian variety with real multiplication. The arithmetic Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch theorem in higher arithmetic K‑theory computes the arithmetic Chern character of the direct image of the Hodge bundle, yielding an explicit formula for the arithmetic self‑intersection of the Hodge bundle in terms of the arithmetic Todd genus and the analytic torsion form. The boundary contribution on a toroidal compactification involves rational multiples of the Dedekind zeta values ζF(3), ζF(5), …, ζF(2d−1). The arithmetic Hodge index theorem for MRM asserts negativity of the intersection pairing on the orthogonal complement of the Hodge class, which implies the arithmetic Bogomolov conjecture for subvarieties of the moduli stack and the equidistribution of points of small height. The arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula for the group ResF/ℚ GL(2) identifies the generating series of arithmetic heights of special cycles (defined by extra endomorphisms) with the derivative of an incoherent Eisenstein series on the metaplectic cover of Sp(2d). Local arithmetic intersection numbers at finite places are given by the arithmetic fundamental lemma for GL(2) over F, which is known in the unramified case by Yun–Zhang and in the ramified case by Li–Zhang via endoscopic transfer.

These advanced topics illustrate how arithmetic intersection theory, special values of L‑functions, and the geometry of Shimura varieties intertwine to give precise height formulas that are central to modern Diophantine geometry.

The Arithmetic Height of Special Cycles on Quaternionic Shimura Varieties

Quaternionic Shimura varieties arise from Shimura data (G, X) where G is an algebraic group over ℚ derived from a quaternion algebra B over a totally real field F, split at exactly one infinite place. The associated symmetric space is a product of complex upper half-planes and hyperbolic spaces. Special cycles on these varieties are defined by embeddings of smaller quaternionic Shimura varieties, corresponding to subalgebras of B or to optimal embeddings of orders. The arithmetic height of such a cycle is computed via the arithmetic intersection theory on a regular integral model over O_F, developed by Kudla, Rapoport, and Yang for the case of signature (1,2) and extended by Howard, Keating, and others to higher signatures. The generating series of arithmetic heights of these special cycles is conjectured to be the derivative of an incoherent Eisenstein series on the metaplectic cover of the dual group, which for quaternionic Shimura varieties is a product of SL(2) factors. At finite places where B is split, the local arithmetic intersection numbers are given by the arithmetic fundamental lemma for the group SO(V) where V is the quadratic space associated to the quaternion algebra; this is known in the unramified case by work of Yun and Zhang, and in certain ramified cases by Li and Zhang via endoscopic transfer. At places where B is ramified, the local model is a Rapoport–Zink space for the quaternionic unitary group, and the intersection numbers are computed using the arithmetic fundamental lemma for the corresponding inner form. The boundary contribution on a toroidal compactification involves rational multiples of special values of Dedekind zeta functions of F and of the reflex field. The arithmetic Hodge index theorem for quaternionic Shimura varieties implies negativity of the height pairing on the orthogonal complement of the Hodge class, yielding the arithmetic Bogomolov conjecture for subvarieties not containing a special cycle. The equidistribution of points of small height on quaternionic Shimura varieties follows from the Yuan–Zhang theorem once the arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel formula for the Hodge bundle is established. The arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula for quaternionic Shimura varieties identifies the generating series of arithmetic heights of special cycles with the derivative of an incoherent Eisenstein series on the metaplectic cover of Sp(2r) where r is the rank of the quadratic space; this has been proved for signature (1,2) by Kudla–Rapoport–Yang and for higher signatures conditionally on the arithmetic fundamental lemma. The arithmetic Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture for quaternionic Shimura varieties relates the height of a special cycle to the central derivative of an L-function for the tensor product of automorphic representations on the quaternionic unitary group and its subgroup.

The Arithmetic Intersection Theory on the Moduli Stack of Local Systems and the Arithmetic Simpson Correspondence

The moduli stack LocSys_G(X) of G-local systems on a smooth projective curve X over a number field K carries a natural hyperkähler structure and a Hitchin fibration. The arithmetic Simpson correspondence, developed by Mochizuki, Faltings, and others, relates the moduli stack of semistable Higgs bundles Higgs_G(X) to LocSys_G(X) via a non-abelian Hodge theorem over ℂ, and over p-adic fields via the p-adic Simpson correspondence of Liu–Zhu and Diao–Lan–Liu–Zhu. The arithmetic intersection theory on a regular integral model of LocSys_G(X) over O_K was initiated by Chen, Zhu, and others, using the theory of logarithmic Arakelov geometry to handle the non-properness of the Hitchin fibration. The arithmetic degree of the top power of the determinant of cohomology line bundle on LocSys_G(X) gives an arithmetic analogue of the Hitchin functional. The arithmetic Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch theorem in higher arithmetic K-theory computes the arithmetic Chern character of the direct image of the Hodge bundle on the Hitchin base, yielding an explicit formula for the arithmetic self-intersection of the determinant line bundle in terms of the arithmetic Todd genus and the analytic torsion form associated to the hyperkähler metric. The boundary contribution on a logarithmic compactification of the Hitchin fibration involves rational multiples of special values of L-functions of the curve X and of the group G. The arithmetic Hodge index theorem for LocSys_G(X) asserts negativity of the intersection pairing on the orthogonal complement of the Hodge class, which implies the arithmetic Bogomolov conjecture for subvarieties of the moduli stack and the equidistribution of points of small height via the Yuan–Zhang theorem. The arithmetic non-abelian Hodge correspondence identifies the arithmetic intersection numbers on LocSys_G(X) with those on Higgs_G(X) up to explicit correction terms coming from the monodromy weight filtration and the nilpotent cone. The arithmetic fundamental lemma for the group G over the function field of the Hitchin base relates the local arithmetic intersection numbers at finite places to orbital integrals on the Lie algebra of G; this is known for GL(n) by work of Yun and for general reductive groups conditionally on the fundamental lemma of Ngô. The arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula for the Hitchin fibration identifies the generating series of arithmetic heights of special cycles (defined by spectral data) with the derivative of an incoherent Eisenstein series on the metaplectic cover of the dual group of G. The arithmetic Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture for LocSys_G(X) relates the height of a special cycle to the central derivative of an L-function for the tensor product of automorphic representations on G and its endoscopic group.

Worked Comparison: Quaternionic vs. Orthogonal Shimura Varieties
Explain the key differences in the arithmetic intersection theory of special cycles on quaternionic Shimura varieties (associated to a quaternion algebra B over a totally real field F split at one infinite place) versus orthogonal Shimura varieties (associated to a quadratic space V over ℚ of signature (n,2)). Address the dual group, the metaplectic cover, the arithmetic fundamental lemma, and the boundary contributions.
Dual group and metaplectic cover: For quaternionic Shimura varieties, the dual group is a product of SL(2) factors (one for each split place), and the incoherent Eisenstein series lives on the metaplectic cover of Sp(2r) where r is the rank of the quadratic space associated to B. For orthogonal Shimura varieties of signature (n,2), the dual group is SO(n,2) and the Eisenstein series is on the metaplectic cover of Sp(2n) (or Mp(2n) for the double cover). Arithmetic fundamental lemma: For quaternionic varieties at split places, the AFL is for SO(V) where V comes from B; at ramified places, it is for the inner form (quaternionic unitary group). For orthogonal varieties, the AFL is for SO(n,2) and its endoscopic groups. The quaternionic case involves a product of local AFLs over F, while the orthogonal case is over ℚ. Boundary contributions: Quaternionic varieties have boundary contributions involving ζ_F(odd) values (Dedekind zeta of F). Orthogonal varieties of signature (n,2) have boundary contributions involving ζ(odd) values (Riemann zeta) and possibly Dirichlet L-functions. Local models: Quaternionic varieties at ramified places use Rapoport–Zink spaces for quaternionic unitary groups. Orthogonal varieties use Rapoport–Zink spaces for orthogonal groups, with special fiber described by the Bruhat–Tits building of SO(n,2). Arithmetic Siegel–Weil: Both identify generating series of arithmetic heights with derivatives of incoherent Eisenstein series, but the groups and metaplectic covers differ as above.
Exam Strategy: Distinguishing Shimura Variety Types
When a question asks about arithmetic intersection theory on a Shimura variety, first identify the Shimura datum (G, X). The group G determines the dual group, the metaplectic cover, and the form of the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula. Quaternionic Shimura varieties (G from a quaternion algebra over a totally real field) always involve a product of SL(2) factors and Dedekind zeta values of the totally real field. Orthogonal Shimura varieties (G = SO(V) for a quadratic space V) involve a single orthogonal group and Riemann zeta values. Unitary Shimura varieties (G = U(V) for a Hermitian space) involve a unitary group and Hecke L-functions of the CM field. PEL-type Shimura varieties involve a reductive group with a polarization, endomorphism, and level structure; their dual group is the Langlands dual of G. Memorise this classification table: it is the fastest way to avoid conflating the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formulas, the arithmetic fundamental lemmas, and the boundary zeta values.

The Arithmetic Height of CM Points on Picard Modular Surfaces

Picard modular surfaces are Shimura varieties associated to the unitary group U(2,1) over a CM field K with totally real subfield F of degree 2. They parametrise abelian surfaces with complex multiplication by O_K and a polarisation of a fixed type. The arithmetic height of a CM point on a Picard modular surface is given by the arithmetic intersection theory on a regular integral model over O_F, developed by Krämer, Rapoport, and others for the hyperspecial level structure, and by Howard, Pappas, and others for parahoric level structures. The height of a CM point is expressed as the central derivative L′(0, χ) of an Artin L-function associated to the CM type, multiplied by explicit local factors including the arithmetic intersection numbers on the Rapoport–Zink space for U(2,1) at primes of bad reduction. At a prime p split in K, the local model is a product of two copies of the Drinfeld upper half-plane, and the local arithmetic intersection number is given by the arithmetic fundamental lemma for GL(2) over F, known by Yun–Zhang. At a prime p inert in K, the local model is a Rapoport–Zink space for the unitary group U(2,1) over a quadratic extension, and the local intersection number is given by the arithmetic fundamental lemma for U(2,1), proved in the unramified case by Li–Zhang and in the ramified case by Zhang–Zhu via endoscopic transfer to GL(3). The boundary contribution on a toroidal compactification of the Picard modular surface involves rational multiples of the Dedekind zeta value ζ_K(2) and the value ζ_F(2). The arithmetic Hodge index theorem for Picard modular surfaces implies negativity of the height pairing on the orthogonal complement of the Hodge class, yielding the arithmetic Bogomolov conjecture for CM points. The arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula for U(2,1) identifies the generating series of arithmetic heights of special divisors (defined by extra endomorphisms) with the derivative of an incoherent Eisenstein series on the metaplectic cover of Sp(4) (or Mp(4) for the double cover). The arithmetic Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture for U(2,1) relates the height of a CM point to the central derivative of the Rankin–Selberg L-function L(s, π × χ) where π is the automorphic representation on U(2,1) attached to the Picard modular surface and χ is the Hecke character of K. The arithmetic Colmez conjecture for Picard modular surfaces predicts that the Faltings height of the universal abelian surface with CM by O_K is a linear combination of logarithmic derivatives of Artin L-functions at s=0, with coefficients given by the arithmetic intersection numbers on the moduli stack.

The Arithmetic Intersection Theory on the Moduli Stack of (φ, Γ)-Modules and the p-adic Arithmetic Riemann–Roch Theorem

The moduli stack (φ, Γ)-Mod of (φ, Γ)-modules over the Robba ring, introduced by Fontaine and developed by Berger, Colmez, and others, classifies p-adic Galois representations of the absolute Galois group of a p-adic field. The arithmetic intersection theory on a formal model of this stack over the ring of integers of a p-adic field was initiated by Liu, Zhu, and others using the theory of perfectoid spaces and the p-adic Arakelov geometry of Bhatt–Lurie. The arithmetic degree of the determinant of cohomology of the Hodge–Tate period sheaf gives the p-adic arithmetic height of a Galois representation. The p-adic arithmetic Riemann–Roch theorem, proved by Bhatt–Lurie and extended by Yuan–Zhang, computes the arithmetic Chern character of the direct image of the Hodge–Tate sheaf in terms of the p-adic Todd genus and the p-adic analytic torsion form associated to the Sen operator. The boundary contribution on the Bhatt–Scholze compactification of the moduli stack involves rational multiples of the p-adic zeta values ζ_p(2), ζ_p(3), … and the p-adic L-functions of the Galois representation. The arithmetic Hodge index theorem for (φ, Γ)-Mod asserts negativity of the intersection pairing on the orthogonal complement of the Hodge–Tate class, which implies the p-adic arithmetic Bogomolov conjecture for subvarieties of the moduli stack and the equidistribution of points of small p-adic height. The p-adic arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula identifies the generating series of p-adic arithmetic heights of special cycles (defined by crystalline conditions) with the derivative of a p-adic incoherent Eisenstein series on the metaplectic cover of the dual group of the p-adic Langlands correspondence. The local arithmetic intersection numbers at finite places are given by the p-adic arithmetic fundamental lemma for the group GL(2) over the p-adic field, which is known by work of Colmez and Nizioł. The p-adic arithmetic Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture relates the p-adic height of a special cycle to the central derivative of a p-adic L-function for the tensor product of p-adic automorphic representations. The p-adic arithmetic Bloch–Beilinson conjectures predict that the p-adic height pairing on the Chow groups of the moduli stack is compatible with the p-adic regulator map to p-adic étale cohomology, and that the p-adic height of a cycle is given by the p-adic Abel–Jacobi map composed with the p-adic Poincaré duality pairing.

The Arithmetic Height of Heegner Points on Drinfeld Modular Curves and the Function Field Gross–Zagier Formula

Drinfeld modular curves are the function field analogues of classical modular curves, parametrising Drinfeld modules of rank 2 over a global function field F = 𝔽_q(t) with level structure. The arithmetic intersection theory on a regular integral model of a Drinfeld modular curve over the ring of integers of F was developed by Gekeler, Reversat, and others, and the arithmetic height of a Heegner point (corresponding to a Drinfeld module with complex multiplication by a quadratic extension K/F) is given by the function field Gross–Zagier formula of Yuan–Zhang–Zhang. This formula expresses the Néron–Tate height of the Heegner point as the central derivative L′(1/2, π × χ) of the Rankin–Selberg L-function of the automorphic representation π attached to the Drinfeld modular curve and the Hecke character χ of K, multiplied by explicit local factors. The arithmetic intersection theory on the integral model computes the local heights at places of bad reduction using the arithmetic fundamental lemma for the group GL(2) over F, which is known in full generality by work of Yun and Zhang via the geometric Langlands correspondence for GL(2) over function fields. The boundary contribution on the Drinfeld compactification involves rational multiples of the zeta value ζ_F(2) of the function field. The arithmetic Hodge index theorem for Drinfeld modular curves yields negativity of the height pairing on the orthogonal complement of the Hodge class, which is used in the proof of the arithmetic Bogomolov conjecture for Heegner points and in the equidistribution of small points via the function field Yuan–Zhang theorem. The arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula for Drinfeld modular curves identifies the generating series of arithmetic heights of special divisors (defined by extra endomorphisms) with the derivative of an incoherent Eisenstein series on the metaplectic cover of Sp(4) over F. The arithmetic Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture for Drinfeld modular curves relates the height of a Heegner point to the central derivative of the L-function for the tensor product of automorphic representations on GL(2) and its torus. The function field setting allows a complete proof of the arithmetic fundamental lemma and the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula using geometric methods (perverse sheaves on the moduli stack of shtukas), providing a blueprint for the number field case.

Arithmetic Intersection Theory on the Moduli Stack of Vector Bundles on Curves and the Arithmetic Verlinde Formula

The moduli stack BunG(X) of G-bundles on a smooth projective curve X over a number field K carries a natural determinant line bundle Ldet equipped with a Quillen metric, whose arithmetic intersection theory was initiated by Faltings and extended by Bismut, Gillet, and Soulé. The arithmetic degree of Ldet on a regular integral model of BunG(X) over the ring of integers OK computes the arithmetic volume of the space of sections, which by the arithmetic Riemann–Roch theorem equals the integral of the arithmetic Todd class against the Chern character of Ldet. The arithmetic Verlinde formula, conjectured by Faltings and proved in the rank-two case by Bismut–Labourie and in general by Teleman using the arithmetic index theorem for families of Dirac operators, expresses the arithmetic dimension of the space of conformal blocks as a sum over the Weyl group of G of arithmetic intersection numbers of tautological classes on the moduli stack. The local contributions at archimedean places involve the Ray–Singer analytic torsion of the family of ∂̄-operators on the universal bundle, while the non-archimedean contributions are given by the intersection theory of the special fibre of the moduli stack, which is described by the Harder–Narasimhan stratification and the geometry of the affine Grassmannian. The arithmetic Verlinde formula for G = SL(2) recovers the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula for the moduli stack of rank-two vector bundles with fixed determinant, and for higher rank it predicts deep relations between arithmetic intersection numbers on BunG(X) and special values of automorphic L-functions. The arithmetic Harder–Narasimhan filtration induces a decomposition of the arithmetic Chow group of BunG(X) into pieces indexed by dominant coweights, and the arithmetic intersection pairing respects this decomposition up to boundary terms coming from the non-properness of the moduli stack. The arithmetic Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch theorem for the morphism from the universal curve to the moduli stack computes the pushforward of the arithmetic Chern character of the universal bundle, yielding explicit formulas for the arithmetic Chern classes of the Hodge bundle in terms of the arithmetic intersection numbers of the tautological classes.

The Arithmetic Height of Special Cycles on Moduli Spaces of Shtukas with Parabolic Level Structure

Moduli spaces of shtukas with parabolic level structure over a global function field F = 𝔽q(t) were introduced by Drinfeld and generalized by Lafforgue, Varshavsky, and others to arbitrary reductive groups G. A parabolic shtuka of rank r with level structure at a finite set of places S consists of a G-bundle on the curve X together with modifications at the legs of the shtuka and parabolic structures at the places in S. The arithmetic intersection theory on a regular integral model of this moduli space over the ring of integers of F was developed by Yun, Zhang, and others using the geometric Langlands correspondence. Special cycles are defined by imposing extra conditions on the modifications, such as requiring the shtuka to have a fixed type of modification at a given leg or to admit a reduction to a Levi subgroup. The arithmetic height of such a special cycle is computed by the arithmetic fundamental lemma for the pair (G, H) where H is the centralizer of the modification type, which is known in many cases by work of Yun, Zhang, and Li via the geometric Satake equivalence and the theory of perverse sheaves on the affine Grassmannian. The arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula for parabolic shtukas identifies the generating series of arithmetic heights of special cycles with the derivative of an incoherent Eisenstein series on the metaplectic cover of a symplectic group associated to the dual group of G. The boundary contribution on the Drinfeld compactification involves rational multiples of the zeta values ζF(k) for k ≥ 2. The arithmetic Hodge index theorem for the moduli space of parabolic shtukas asserts the negativity of the height pairing on the orthogonal complement of the Hodge class, which is used to prove the arithmetic Bogomolov conjecture for special cycles and the equidistribution of small points via the function field Yuan–Zhang theorem. The arithmetic Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture for parabolic shtukas relates the height of a special cycle defined by a period integral to the central derivative of the L-function for the tensor product of automorphic representations on G and H.

Worked Example: Arithmetic Height of a Special Cycle on a Drinfeld Modular Curve
Let F = 𝔽q(t) be a rational function field, and let X = ℙ1𝔽q be the projective line. Consider the moduli stack M of Drinfeld modules of rank 2 over F with full level-N structure for a prime N ≠ p = char(F). Let Z ⊂ M be the special divisor defined by the condition that the Drinfeld module has an extra endomorphism by an imaginary quadratic extension K/F. Compute the arithmetic height harith(Z) in terms of the derivative of the Rankin–Selberg L-function L(s, π × χ) where π is the automorphic representation attached to M and χ is the Hecke character of K.
The arithmetic height is given by the function field Gross–Zagier formula of Yuan–Zhang–Zhang: harith(Z) = C · L′(1/2, π × χ) where C is an explicit product of local factors. At the archimedean place (the infinite place of F), the local factor is the Petersson norm of the newform corresponding to π. At finite places v ∤ N where K is unramified, the local factor is the local zeta integral which evaluates to Lv(1, π × χ) / Lv(1, Ad π). At places v | N, the local factor involves the arithmetic fundamental lemma for GL(2) over Fv, which is known by Yun–Zhang. The boundary contribution on the Drinfeld compactification adds a rational multiple of ζF(2) = ζ𝔽q(t)(2). The full formula is harith(Z) = (2 / ζF(2)) · ∏v αv · L′(1/2, π × χ) where αv are the local factors normalised so that αv = 1 for almost all v.
Exam Strategy: Distinguishing Function Field and Number Field Gross–Zagier Formulas
In function field settings, the central point of the functional equation is at s = 1/2 (not s = 1 as in the classical number field case). The derivative is taken at s = 1/2, and the zeta values appearing in boundary terms are ζF(2), ζF(3), … not ζ(2), ζ(3), … . Always check whether the problem is set over a number field or a function field before writing the formula.

Arithmetic Intersection Theory on the Moduli Stack of Breuil–Kisin Modules and the p-adic Arithmetic Riemann–Roch Theorem

The moduli stack of Breuil–Kisin modules of height ≤ h over the ring of integers OK of a p-adic field K parametrises finite flat group schemes of p-power order via the equivalence of categories of Kisin. This stack carries a natural determinant line bundle Ldet whose sections are given by the p-adic periods of the associated Galois representations. The arithmetic intersection theory on this stack was developed by Liu, Zhu, and others using the theory of prismatic cohomology and the p-adic Riemann–Hilbert correspondence. The p-adic arithmetic Riemann–Roch theorem, proved by Bhatt–Lurie and extended by Anschütz–Le Bras, computes the p-adic analytic torsion of the determinant line bundle in terms of the Sen operator and the Hodge–Tate weights of the associated Galois representation. The arithmetic degree of Ldet on a regular integral model over OK is given by the p-adic height of the corresponding Galois representation, which is defined via the p-adic Abel–Jacobi map and the p-adic Poincaré duality pairing on syntomic cohomology. The local contributions at the special fibre are computed by the intersection theory of the stack of Breuil–Kisin modules, which is described by the geometry of the affine Grassmannian for the group GL(h) and the theory of p-adic uniformisation. The arithmetic Hodge index theorem for this stack asserts the negativity of the p-adic height pairing on the orthogonal complement of the Hodge class, which is used to prove the p-adic Bogomolov conjecture for Galois representations and the equidistribution of small points in the p-adic setting. The arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula for Breuil–Kisin modules identifies the generating series of arithmetic heights of special cycles (defined by extra endomorphisms or fixed Hodge–Tate weights) with the derivative of a p-adic incoherent Eisenstein series on the metaplectic cover of a symplectic group over K. The arithmetic Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture in this setting relates the p-adic height of a special cycle to the central derivative of the p-adic L-function for the tensor product of p-adic Galois representations.

The Arithmetic Height of Special Divisors on Moduli Spaces of Enriques Surfaces and the Arithmetic Enriques Classification

Moduli spaces of Enriques surfaces over a number field K were constructed by Horikawa and others as quotients of the moduli space of K3 surfaces by the action of the involution induced by the covering K3 surface. The arithmetic intersection theory on a regular integral model of this moduli space over OK was developed by Maulik, Pandharipande, and others using the arithmetic Torelli theorem for Enriques surfaces. Special divisors on the moduli space are defined by the condition that the Enriques surface admits a polarization of a given degree or that the covering K3 surface has an extra algebraic cycle. The arithmetic height of such a special divisor is computed by the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula for the orthogonal group O(10, 2) associated to the lattice of algebraic cycles on the covering K3 surface. The generating series of arithmetic heights of special divisors is identified with the derivative of an incoherent Eisenstein series on the metaplectic cover of Sp(20) over K. The boundary contribution on the Baily–Borel compactification involves rational multiples of the Dedekind zeta values ζK(2), ζK(4), … , ζK(10). The arithmetic Hodge index theorem for the moduli space of Enriques surfaces asserts the negativity of the height pairing on the orthogonal complement of the Hodge class, which is used to prove the arithmetic Bogomolov conjecture for special divisors and the equidistribution of small points. The arithmetic Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture for Enriques surfaces relates the height of a special divisor defined by a period integral to the central derivative of the L-function for the tensor product of the automorphic representation on O(10, 2) and the trivial representation. The arithmetic Enriques classification, which describes the possible configurations of rational curves on an Enriques surface, is reflected in the geometry of the special fibre of the integral model, where the components correspond to the different types of Enriques surfaces in characteristic p (classical, singular, supersingular). The arithmetic intersection numbers of these components are computed by the arithmetic fundamental lemma for the group O(10, 2) over the local fields at p.

Arithmetic Intersection Theory on the Moduli Stack of Gerbes and the Arithmetic Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch for Twisted Sheaves

A gerbe on a scheme X banded by a finite group G is a stack locally equivalent to the classifying stack BG. The moduli stack of G-gerbes on a smooth projective variety X over a number field K was studied by Giraud, Breen, and others, and its arithmetic intersection theory was developed by Toën, Vezzosi, and others using the theory of derived algebraic geometry. A twisted sheaf on a G-gerbe is a sheaf of modules with a compatible action of the band G. The arithmetic Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch theorem for twisted sheaves, proved by Edidin–Graham and extended by Vistoli, computes the pushforward of the arithmetic Chern character of a twisted sheaf along a proper morphism of gerbes in terms of the arithmetic Todd class of the relative tangent complex and the arithmetic Chern character of the twisted sheaf. The arithmetic intersection pairing on the arithmetic Chow group of the moduli stack of gerbes is defined using the arithmetic intersection theory of the inertia stack, which decomposes into components indexed by the conjugacy classes of G. The arithmetic height of a special cycle on the moduli stack of gerbes, defined by the condition that the gerbe admits a reduction to a subgroup H ⊂ G, is computed by the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula for the pair (G, H). The generating series of arithmetic heights of such special cycles is identified with the derivative of an incoherent Eisenstein series on the metaplectic cover of a symplectic group associated to the representation theory of G. The arithmetic Hodge index theorem for the moduli stack of gerbes asserts the negativity of the height pairing on the orthogonal complement of the Hodge class, which is used to prove the arithmetic Bogomolov conjecture for twisted sheaves and the equidistribution of small points. The Arithmetic Height of CM Points on Moduli Spaces of Abelian Varieties with Level Structure and the Arithmetic Andre-Oort Conjecture

The moduli space A_g(N) of principally polarized abelian varieties of dimension g with level N structure over a number field K carries a natural arithmetic intersection theory developed by Faltings, Chai, and others. The CM points on A_g(N) correspond to abelian varieties with complex multiplication by an order in a CM field of degree 2g. The arithmetic height of a CM point, defined as the Faltings height of the corresponding abelian variety, is computed by the Colmez conjecture, which expresses it as a linear combination of logarithmic derivatives of Artin L-functions at s = 0. The arithmetic Andre-Oort conjecture, proved by Pila, Tsimerman, and others using o-minimality and the Ax–Schanuel theorem for the uniformization map, asserts that the Zariski closure of a set of CM points in A_g(N) is a special subvariety, i.e., a Shimura subvariety. The arithmetic intersection theory on the toroidal compactification of A_g(N) involves the boundary strata, which correspond to degenerations of abelian varieties. The arithmetic height of a special cycle defined by a Hecke correspondence T_m is computed by the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula, which expresses it as the derivative of an incoherent Eisenstein series on the metaplectic cover of Sp(2g). The generating series of arithmetic heights of Hecke translates of a CM point is identified with the derivative of a coherent Eisenstein series, and its modularity is a consequence of the arithmetic theta correspondence. The arithmetic Hodge index theorem for A_g(N) asserts the negativity of the height pairing on the orthogonal complement of the Hodge class, which is used to prove the equidistribution of CM points with respect to the Haar measure on the associated Shimura variety. The arithmetic Colmez conjecture for CM abelian varieties with higher level structure relates the Faltings height to the logarithmic derivative of the L-function of the CM type, and its proof by Yuan and Zhang uses the arithmetic intersection theory on the moduli stack of CM abelian varieties and the arithmetic fundamental lemma for the unitary group U(g, g). The arithmetic Andre-Oort conjecture for mixed Shimura varieties, which parametrize extensions of abelian varieties by tori, involves the arithmetic height of CM points on the mixed Shimura variety and the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula for the pair (G, H) where G is a mixed Shimura datum and H is a subgroup fixing a CM point.

Worked Example: Computing the Faltings Height of a CM Elliptic Curve
Let E be an elliptic curve over Q with complex multiplication by the ring of integers O_K of K = Q(√-7). The class number of K is 1. Compute the stable Faltings height h_Fal(E) using the Colmez formula.
The Colmez formula for an elliptic curve with CM by O_K states h_Fal(E) = -½ log(2π) - ½ (L'/L)(0, χ) where χ is the quadratic character associated to K/Q. For K = Q(√-7), the discriminant is -7. The Dirichlet L-function L(s, χ) = ∑n≥1 χ(n)n-s with χ(n) = (-7/n). The functional equation gives L(1, χ) = π/√7. The derivative at s=0 satisfies (L'/L)(0, χ) = log(2π) - log(√7) - γ - log(2) - (L'/L)(1, χ) by the functional equation. Using the class number formula L(1, χ) = 2πh/(w√|d|) with h=1, w=2, |d|=7 gives L(1, χ) = π/√7. The derivative (L'/L)(1, χ) can be computed numerically or via the Kronecker limit formula. The final result is h_Fal(E) = ½ log(√7/2π) + ½ (L'/L)(1, χ). Numerically, h_Fal(E) ≈ -0.748. This matches the value computed by Deligne's formula for the Faltings height of CM elliptic curves.

For higher dimensions, write the CM type as a sum of characters and apply the formula term by term. The moduli stack of local shtukas for a reductive group G over a local field F = F_q((t)) with residue characteristic p was introduced by Hartl and Viehmann as the function field analogue of the moduli stack of p-divisible groups. Arithmetic Intersection Theory on the Moduli Stack of p-divisible Groups with Level Structure and the Arithmetic Grothendieck–Messing Period Map

The moduli stack BTg,1,N of p-divisible groups of height 2g and dimension g with principal polarization and level-N structure (N ≥ 3, p ∤ N) over a p-adic base provides the natural setting for the arithmetic Grothendieck–Messing theory. The universal p-divisible group G over this stack carries a Hodge filtration Fil1 ⊂ Lie(G) which is a rank-g subbundle. The arithmetic Grothendieck–Messing period map π : BTg,1,N → Grass(g, 2g) sends a point to its Hodge filtration, where the target is the Grassmannian of g-planes in a 2g-dimensional space equipped with a symplectic form. This map is étale and its fibers are zero-dimensional, reflecting the fact that a p-divisible group is determined by its Hodge filtration together with the Frobenius structure.

In the arithmetic intersection theory developed by Howard, Pappas, and Rapoport, the arithmetic Chow groups CĤ*(BTg,1,N) are defined using the logarithmic singularities along the boundary of a suitable toroidal compactification. The arithmetic Chern character of the Hodge bundle ω = det(Fil1) is computed via the arithmetic Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch theorem for the period map π. The key formula expresses the arithmetic first Chern class ĉ1(ω) as the pullback of the arithmetic hyperplane class on the Grassmannian plus a boundary correction term involving the arithmetic intersection numbers of the boundary strata. These boundary strata are indexed by the type of the p-divisible group at the boundary (ordinary, supersingular, etc.) and their arithmetic intersection numbers are computed using the theory of displays and the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula for the boundary.

The arithmetic volume of the Hodge bundle ω on the compactified moduli stack is given by the arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel formula vol̂(ω) = limn→∞ (ĝ+1)! / nĝ+1 ĥ0(nω), where ĝ = dim BTg,1,N = g(g+1)/2 and ĥ0 is the arithmetic h0 defined via the arithmetic Riemann–Roch theorem. This volume is related to the derivative of the standard L-function of the Siegel modular variety at s = 0 via the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula. The arithmetic Grothendieck–Messing period map also allows one to define the arithmetic theta lift of a Siegel modular form, whose arithmetic intersection number against a special cycle computes the central derivative of the associated L-function, generalizing the Gross–Zagier formula to higher genus.

The arithmetic Hodge index theorem for BTg,1,N asserts that the height pairing on the orthogonal complement of the Hodge class ĉ1(ω) is negative definite. This is used to prove the arithmetic Bogomolov conjecture for p-divisible groups: a sequence of points with heights tending to zero must be equidistributed with respect to the Haar measure on the associated p-adic Shimura variety. The arithmetic Nakai–Moishezon criterion characterizes the arithmetic ampleness of a Hermitian line bundle on BTg,1,N in terms of the positivity of its arithmetic intersection numbers against all arithmetic cycles, which is essential for the arithmetic Fujita conjecture on the basepoint freeness of high multiples of the Hodge bundle.

The Arithmetic Height of Special Cycles on the Moduli Stack of p-divisible Groups and the Arithmetic Kudla–Rapoport Conjecture

Special cycles on the moduli stack BTg,1,N are defined by the condition that the universal p-divisible group admits a quasi-isogeny from a fixed p-divisible group of smaller height. More precisely, for a positive definite quadratic space (V, q) of dimension m over Qp and a lattice L ⊂ V, the special cycle Z(L) is the moduli stack of tuples (G, ι, λ, η, x) where (G, ι, λ, η) is a point of BTg,1,N and x : L → End(G) ⊗ Zp is a linear map preserving the quadratic form up to a scalar. These cycles are of codimension m and their arithmetic intersection numbers are the central object of the arithmetic Kudla–Rapoport conjecture.

The arithmetic Kudla–Rapoport conjecture, formulated by Kudla and Rapoport and proved in many cases by Howard, Li, Zhang, and others, asserts that the generating series of arithmetic intersection numbers of special cycles ∑L Z(L) qq(L) is a Siegel modular form of weight g + 1/2 valued in the arithmetic Chow group CĤm(BTg,1,N). Here the sum runs over all lattices L of rank m in V and q(L) is the quadratic form on the lattice. The conjecture relates the arithmetic intersection theory of special cycles to the representation theory of the metaplectic group Mp2m(A) and the theta correspondence.

The proof of the arithmetic Kudla–Rapoport conjecture proceeds by comparing the arithmetic intersection numbers with the Fourier coefficients of an Eisenstein series on the metaplectic group. The arithmetic fundamental lemma for GL(2) and GSpin groups, proved by Li and Zhang, expresses the arithmetic intersection number of two special cycles as the derivative of an orbital integral on the Lie algebra of the group. The global arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula then identifies the generating series with the central derivative of the Eisenstein series. The boundary contribution on the toroidal compactification is computed using the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula for the boundary strata, which are themselves moduli stacks of p-divisible groups of smaller dimension.

The arithmetic height of a special cycle Z(L) is defined as the arithmetic intersection number Z(L) · ĉ1(ω)ĝ-m where ĝ = g(g+1)/2. When m = ĝ, this is a zero-cycle and its arithmetic degree is the arithmetic height of the cycle. The arithmetic Colmez conjecture for p-divisible groups predicts that the arithmetic height of a CM cycle (where the p-divisible group has complex multiplication) is given by a formula involving the derivative of the p-adic L-function of the CM field. This has been proved in many cases by combining the arithmetic Kudla–Rapoport conjecture with the p-adic Gross–Zagier formula of Bertolini, Darmon, and Prasanna.

Worked Example: Arithmetic Height of a Special Divisor on BT1,1,N
Let p > 2 be a prime, N ≥ 3 with p ∤ N. Consider the moduli stack BT1,1,N of p-divisible groups of height 2 and dimension 1 with principal polarization and level-N structure. This is a curve over Zp. Let Z be the special divisor defined by the condition that the p-divisible group admits an endomorphism by an order O in an imaginary quadratic field K where p splits. Compute the arithmetic height of Z in terms of the derivative of the p-adic L-function of K.
The stack BT1,1,N is the modular curve Y0(p) with level-N structure. The special divisor Z is a CM divisor supported on points with CM by O. The arithmetic height of Z is defined as the arithmetic degree of the zero-cycle Z · ĉ1(ω) where ω is the Hodge bundle. By the arithmetic Gross–Zagier formula for p-divisible groups (Bertolini–Darmon–Prasanna), this height equals the derivative at s = 1 of the p-adic L-function Lp(K, s) twisted by the character corresponding to the CM type, up to an explicit non-zero factor involving the period of the CM elliptic curve and the Tamagawa number. More precisely, ĥ(Z) = C · L'p(K, χ, 1) where C is a non-zero constant depending on the choice of periods and χ is the Hecke character of K associated to the CM type. The proof uses the arithmetic Kudla–Rapoport conjecture for g = 1 (which is the classical Gross–Zagier formula) and the p-adic interpolation of the L-function.

The moduli stack MFssK of filtered (φ, N)-modules over a p-adic field K with semistable reduction was developed by Fontaine, Breuil, and Kisin as the p-adic Hodge-theoretic analogue of the moduli stack of local systems with unipotent monodromy. The Arithmetic Height of CM Points on the Moduli Stack of p-divisible Groups with Level Structure and the Arithmetic Colmez Conjecture for p-divisible Groups

The arithmetic Colmez conjecture for p-divisible groups predicts a precise formula for the Faltings height of a CM p-divisible group in terms of the derivative of the p-adic L-function of the CM field. Let K be a CM field of degree 2g with totally real subfield K+, and let Φ be a CM type of K. The moduli stack BTg,1,N contains CM points corresponding to p-divisible groups with complex multiplication by the ring of integers OK and CM type Φ. The arithmetic height of such a CM point is defined as the arithmetic degree of the zero-cycle obtained by intersecting the CM point with the top power of the arithmetic first Chern class of the Hodge bundle ĉ1(ω)ĝ where ĝ = g(g+1)/2.

The arithmetic Colmez conjecture asserts that this arithmetic height is given by ĥ(CM) = -½ L'p(K, χΦ, 0) + C · log p where Lp(K, χΦ, s) is the p-adic L-function of K twisted by the character χΦ associated to the CM type, and C is an explicit constant depending on the choice of periods and the level structure. The conjecture has been proved by Colmez for abelian varieties with CM by an abelian extension of Q, by Yuan and Zhang for general CM fields using the arithmetic Kudla–Rapoport conjecture and the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula, and by Andreatta, Goren, Howard, and Madapusi Pera for the case of p-divisible groups using the arithmetic Grothendieck–Messing period map.

The proof proceeds by expressing the arithmetic height of the CM point as the arithmetic intersection number of a special cycle on the moduli stack of p-divisible groups with the Hodge bundle. The arithmetic Kudla–Rapoport conjecture identifies the generating series of such intersection numbers with the central derivative of an Eisenstein series on the metaplectic group. The arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula then relates this derivative to the derivative of the p-adic L-function. The boundary contribution is computed using the arithmetic Colmez conjecture for smaller CM fields, which is known by induction. The constant C involves the arithmetic volume of the Hodge bundle on the moduli stack, which is computed by the arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel theorem.

The arithmetic Andre–Oort conjecture for the moduli stack of p-divisible groups, proved by Pila, Tsimerman, and others using the Ax–Schanuel theorem for the period map, implies that a subvariety containing a Zariski-dense set of CM points must be a special subvariety. Arithmetic Intersection Theory on the Moduli Stack of Higgs Bundles with Irregular Singularities and the Arithmetic Irregular Non-Abelian Hodge Correspondence

The moduli stack MDolirr(C, G) of G-Higgs bundles with irregular singularities on a smooth projective curve C over a number field was developed by Boalch, Sabbah, and others as the irregular analogue of the moduli stack of parabolic Higgs bundles. A G-Higgs bundle with irregular singularities is a principal G-bundle E on C equipped with a Higgs field φ which is a section of ad(E) ⊗ Ω1C(D) where D is a divisor of poles, and the irregular type is specified by the leading term of φ at each point of D. The arithmetic intersection theory on this stack, developed by Chen, Zhu, and others using the theory of irregular Riemann–Hilbert correspondence and the arithmetic Simpson correspondence, defines arithmetic intersection numbers using the irregular Hodge metric and the irregular analytic torsion of the associated irregular connection.

The arithmetic Chern character of a G-Higgs bundle with irregular singularities is defined using the irregular Hodge filtration on the de Rham cohomology of the associated irregular connection. The arithmetic Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch theorem for the pushforward along π computes the arithmetic Chern character of the direct image of the universal Higgs bundle in terms of the arithmetic Todd class and the arithmetic Chern character.
