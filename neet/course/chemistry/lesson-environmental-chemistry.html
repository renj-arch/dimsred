Key Concepts

What is Environmental Chemistry?

Environmental chemistry studies the chemical processes occurring in the environment and the impact of human activities on natural systems. For NEET, key topics include air pollution, water pollution, soil pollution, ozone layer depletion, greenhouse effect, and waste management. Understanding the causes, effects, and control measures of environmental issues is essential.

Key Concepts

Air Pollution: Major pollutants include CO, SO2, NOx, particulate matter (PM2.5, PM10), and volatile organic compounds (VOCs). Greenhouse Effect: Gases like CO2, CH4, N2O, CFCs trap heat in the atmosphere, causing global warming. Ozone Depletion: CFCs release chlorine atoms that catalytically destroy ozone: Cl + O3 → ClO + O2, ClO + O → Cl + O2. Water Pollution: BOD (Biochemical Oxygen Demand) measures organic pollution. Higher BOD indicates more pollution. Photochemical Smog: Formed by reaction of NOx and VOCs in sunlight, producing ozone and PAN (peroxyacyl nitrate).

Solved Examples

Example 1
Which of the following is primarily responsible for the depletion of the ozone layer?

a) CO2 b) SO2 c) CFCs d) CH4
Solution: Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) are the primary cause of ozone depletion. When CFCs reach the stratosphere, UV radiation breaks them down, releasing chlorine atoms that catalytically destroy ozone molecules. Hence option (c) is correct.
Example 2
What is the permissible level of lead in drinking water as per WHO standards?

a) 0.05 ppm b) 0.5 ppm c) 1.0 ppm d) 5.0 ppm
Solution: The WHO permissible limit for lead in drinking water is 0.05 ppm (parts per million). Lead is toxic and can cause neurological damage, especially in children. Hence option (a) is correct.
Example 3
Which gas is mainly responsible for the greenhouse effect?

a) O2 b) N2 c) CO2 d) Ar
Solution: Carbon dioxide (CO2) is the primary greenhouse gas responsible for global warming. It traps infrared radiation emitted by the Earth's surface, preventing heat from escaping into space. Although methane and CFCs are more potent per molecule, CO2 is the most abundant. Hence option (c) is correct.

Shortcut Techniques

  • For identifying acid rain, remember that pH of normal rain is about 5.6 (due to dissolved CO2). Acid rain has pH < 5.6 due to HNO3 and H2SO4.
  • Catalytic converters in vehicles convert CO to CO2, NOx to N2, and unburnt hydrocarbons to CO2 and H2O.
  • Eutrophication occurs when excess nutrients (phosphates, nitrates) enter water bodies, causing algal blooms.
  • The ozone layer is located in the stratosphere, between 15-35 km above Earth's surface.
Pro Tip
For NEET, remember key environmental acts and years: Water Act (1974), Air Act (1981), Environment Protection Act (1986). Also know the major air quality index (AQI) parameters and their health effects for application-based questions.
