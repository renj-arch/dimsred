A. 0.01 mol L⁻¹
B. 0.02 mol L⁻¹
C. 0.03 mol L⁻¹
D. 0.04 mol L⁻¹
Correct Answer: D

B. 98
C. 0.02
D. 50
Correct Answer: A

A parallel plate capacitor is charged to a potential difference V and then disconnected from the battery. A dielectric slab of dielectric constant K is inserted between the plates, completely filling the space. The new energy stored in the capacitor becomes:
A. Initial energy / K^2
B. K times initial energy
C. Initial energy / K
D. K^2 times initial energy
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut: With battery disconnected, charge Q is constant; energy U = Q²/2C. Capacitance increases K times (C' = KC), so energy decreases K times (U' = U/K). Full Solution: Initial energy U = Q²/(2C₀). After inserting dielectric, C = KC₀. Since Q is constant, final energy U' = Q²/(2KC₀) = U/K.

In a photoelectric effect experiment, the frequency of incident light is kept constant while the intensity is doubled. What is the effect on the stopping potential (V_s) and the saturation photocurrent (I_s)?
A. V_s doubles, I_s doubles
B. V_s remains same, I_s doubles
C. V_s halves, I_s remains same
D. V_s remains same, I_s remains same
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: Stopping potential depends only on frequency (Einstein's equation: eV_s = hν - φ), so it is unchanged; saturation current is directly proportional to intensity (photon flux), so it doubles. Full Solution: Since frequency ν is constant, the maximum kinetic energy of photoelectrons (eV_s) remains constant, leaving V_s unchanged. Doubling intensity doubles the number of incident photons per second, doubling the photoelectron emission rate and hence the saturation current I_s.

What is the pH of a 0.01 M aqueous solution of acetic acid (Ka = 1.8 × 10⁻⁵) at 298 K?
A. 2.87
B. 3.37
C. 3.87
D. 4.37
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: For a weak acid HA with Ka << C, [H⁺] ≈ √(Ka·C); pH = ½(pKa – log C). Here pKa = 4.74, log C = –2 → pH = ½(4.74 + 2) = 3.37. Full method: Write Ka = x²/(C–x) ≈ x²/C, solve x = √(1.8×10⁻⁵ × 0.01) = 4.24×10⁻⁴ M, pH = –log(4.24×10⁻⁴) = 3.37.
