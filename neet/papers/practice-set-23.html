Q1. Zoology
Which chamber of the human heart receives oxygenated blood from the pulmonary veins?
A. Right atrium
B. Left atrium
C. Right ventricle
D. Left ventricle
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: Eliminate right-sided chambers (right atrium, right ventricle) as they handle deoxygenated blood; the left ventricle pumps blood systemically but does not receive pulmonary venous return directly. Full solution: The pulmonary veins carry oxygenated blood from the lungs into the left atrium, which then passes it to the left ventricle.
Q2. Zoology
Which of the following organisms possesses a copper-containing respiratory pigment (Hemocyanin) dissolved in its plasma, giving its oxygenated blood a characteristic blue color?
A. Earthworm (Pheretima)
B. Cockroach (Periplaneta)
C. Snail (Pila)
D. Frog (Rana)
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut: **Phylum-based Pigment Rule**. **Mollusca** (Snail, Octopus) & **Crustacea** (Prawn, Crab) → **Hemocyanin (Cu, Blue)**. **Annelida** (Earthworm) & **Chordata** (Frog) → **Hemoglobin (Fe, Red)**. **Insecta** (Cockroach) → **No pigment** (Tracheal system delivers O2 directly). Full Solution: Hemocyanin is a copper-based respiratory pigment found in the phyla Mollusca and Arthropoda (specifically subphylum Crustacea). Snail (Pila) belongs to Mollusca and uses hemocyanin. Earthworm (Annelida) uses hemoglobin dissolved in plasma. Cockroach (Insecta) lacks respiratory pigments entirely, relying on a network of tracheae for gas exchange. Frog (Chordata/Amphibia) uses hemoglobin packed inside Red Blood Cells (RBCs).
Q3. Botany
In plant tissue culture, the morphogenetic fate of an undifferentiated callus is manipulated by the ratio of auxin to cytokinin. A high auxin-to-cytokinin ratio in the culture medium predominantly induces the formation of which structures?
A. Axillary shoot buds
B. Adventitious roots
C. Somatic embryos
D. Undifferentiated callus mass
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: Use the mnemonic 'Auxin Roots (Down), Cytokinin Shoots (Up)'. High Auxin/Cytokinin ratio → Root induction. High Cytokinin/Auxin ratio → Shoot induction. Balanced ratio → Callus proliferation. Worked: The classic Skoog and Miller (1957) experiment on tobacco pith callus established this polarity. High auxin concentration promotes rhizogenesis (root formation) by stimulating cell division and differentiation in the vascular parenchyma. High cytokinin promotes caulogenesis (shoot formation).
Q4. Botany
In the photorespiratory pathway (C2 cycle), the oxidative decarboxylation of glycine to serine, releasing CO₂ and NH₃, is catalyzed by the glycine decarboxylase complex. This specific reaction occurs in which subcellular compartment?
A. Chloroplast Stroma
B. Peroxisome Matrix
C. Mitochondrial Matrix
D. Cytosol
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut: Memorize the C2 cycle shuttle: Glycolate (Peroxisome) → Glycine (Mitochondria) → Serine (Peroxisome). Glycine decarboxylase releases CO₂/NH₃ exclusively in mitochondria. Worked: RuBisCO oxygenase activity produces phosphoglycolate in chloroplasts. It is converted to glycolate, moves to peroxisomes, transaminated to glycine. Glycine enters mitochondria where Glycine Decarboxylase Complex (GDC) cleaves it to serine, CO₂, and NH₃. Serine returns to peroxisomes for further metabolism.
Q5. Chemistry
For a galvanic cell, the standard cell potential E°cell is 0.59 V at 298 K. If the reaction involves the transfer of 2 electrons, the equilibrium constant K for the cell reaction is approximately: (Given 2.303 RT/F = 0.059 V)
A. 10^10
B. 10^20
C. 10^30
D. 10^40
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: Use log K = nE°/0.059 at 298 K. Here n=2, E°=0.59 V → log K = 2×0.59/0.059 = 20 → K = 10^20. Full solution: The relationship between standard cell potential and equilibrium constant is ΔG° = -nFE° = -RT ln K. Converting to base-10 log gives log K = nE°/(2.303RT/F) = nE°/0.059. Substituting values yields log K = 20, so K = 10^20.
Q6. Physics
A Carnot engine operates between a source at 500 K and a sink at 300 K. If the engine absorbs 2000 J of heat from the source in each cycle, the work done by the engine per cycle is:
A. 400 J
B. 800 J
C. 1200 J
D. 1600 J
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: Carnot efficiency $\eta = 1 - \frac{T_C}{T_H} = 1 - \frac{300}{500} = 0.4$ (40%). Work done $W = \eta \times Q_H = 0.4 \times 2000 = 800 \text{ J}$. Full Solution: $\eta = 1 - \frac{T_2}{T_1} = 1 - \frac{3}{5} = \frac{2}{5}$. Work output $W = \eta Q_1 = \frac{2}{5} \times 2000 = 800 \text{ J}$.
Q7. Chemistry
The packing efficiency of a body-centered cubic (BCC) lattice is approximately:
A. 52%
B. 74%
C. 68%
D. 80%
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut: Memorize BCC packing efficiency = 68% (π√3/8). Full solution: In BCC, atoms touch along body diagonal: √3a = 4r → a = 4r/√3. Volume of unit cell = a^3 = 64r^3/(3√3). Number of atoms per cell = 2. Volume occupied = 2×(4/3)πr^3 = 8πr^3/3. Packing efficiency = (8πr^3/3) / (64r^3/(3√3)) = π√3/8 ≈ 0.68 = 68%.
Q8. Physics
The work function of a metal is 2.0 eV. Light of wavelength 400 nm is incident on the metal surface. The maximum kinetic energy of emitted photoelectrons is approximately:
A. 0.5 eV
B. 1.0 eV
C. 1.1 eV
D. 1.5 eV
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut: Use hc = 1240 eV·nm; photon energy = 1240/400 = 3.1 eV. Maximum KE = 3.1 eV - 2.0 eV = 1.1 eV. Full solution: Einstein's photoelectric equation KEmax = hc/λ - φ. Substituting hc = 1240 eV·nm, λ = 400 nm gives photon energy = 3.1 eV. Subtracting work function φ = 2.0 eV yields KEmax = 1.1 eV.
Q9. Botany
As per the table below showing the standing crop biomass (g/m²) at successive trophic levels in a temperate freshwater pond ecosystem, which type of ecological pyramid is represented by the biomass data?
Trophic LevelBiomass (g/m²)
Producers (Phytoplankton)15
Primary Consumers (Zooplankton)45
Secondary Consumers (Small Fish)30
Tertiary Consumers (Large Fish)20
A. Upright Pyramid of Biomass
B. Inverted Pyramid of Biomass
C. Upright Pyramid of Numbers
D. Inverted Pyramid of Energy
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: Aquatic ecosystems (ponds/lakes) typically show Inverted Pyramid of Biomass. Producers (phytoplankton) have high turnover (productivity) but low standing biomass at any moment. Consumers (zooplankton/fish) live longer, accumulating higher standing biomass. Check table: Producer biomass (15) < Primary Consumer biomass (45). Base is narrower than next level → Inverted. Pyramid of Energy is ALWAYS upright. Worked: The data shows biomass at producer level (15 g/m²) is significantly lower than at primary consumer level (45 g/m²). This inversion occurs because phytoplankton reproduce and are consumed rapidly (high P/B ratio), maintaining a small standing crop, whereas zooplankton and fish have slower turnover and larger standing stocks. This structure defines an Inverted Pyramid of Biomass.
Q10. Zoology
Which enzyme in *E. coli* possesses 5'→3' exonuclease activity specifically required for the removal of RNA primers during DNA replication?
A. DNA Polymerase III
B. DNA Polymerase I
C. DNA Ligase
D. Primase
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: Pol I = 'I' for Incision/Removal of primers (5'→3' exonuclease activity for nick translation). Pol III = Main synthesis (high processivity, 3'→5' proofreading). Ligase = Joins fragments. Primase = Synthesizes primers. Full Solution: DNA Polymerase I removes RNA primers via its 5'→3' exonuclease activity while simultaneously synthesizing DNA in the 5'→3' direction (nick translation) to fill the gaps. DNA Polymerase III is the primary replicative polymerase with high processivity but lacks 5'→3' exonuclease activity. DNA Ligase then seals the remaining nicks by forming phosphodiester bonds between adjacent nucleotides.
Q11. Physics
A projectile is thrown with an initial speed $u$ at an angle $\theta$ with the horizontal. Its horizontal range is $R$. For the same initial speed $u$, there are two possible angles of projection to achieve a range $R$ (where $R < R_{\text{max}}$). What is the sum of these two angles?
A. 30°
B. 45°
C. 90°
D. 180°
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut: For a given initial velocity, two complementary angles of projection ($\theta$ and $90^\circ-\theta$) yield the same horizontal range (except at $45^\circ$). Their sum is always $90^\circ$. Full Solution: Range formula $R = \frac{u^2 \sin 2\theta}{g}$. For same $R$, $\sin 2\theta_1 = \sin 2\theta_2 \implies 2\theta_2 = 180^\circ - 2\theta_1 \implies \theta_1 + \theta_2 = 90^\circ$.
Q12. Chemistry
For a certain reaction, ΔH° = −50 kJ mol⁻¹ and ΔS° = −100 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. The temperature (in K) above which the reaction becomes non-spontaneous is:
A. 500 K
B. 400 K
C. 600 K
D. 300 K
Correct Answer: A
Shortcut: At the threshold of spontaneity, ΔG = 0 = ΔH − TΔS → T = ΔH/ΔS. Convert ΔH to J: −50 kJ = −50,000 J. T = (−50,000) / (−100) = 500 K. For T > 500 K, the −TΔS term becomes positive and larger than |ΔH|, making ΔG > 0. Worked: ΔG = ΔH − TΔS. Given ΔH = −50,000 J mol⁻¹, ΔS = −100 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. For spontaneity, ΔG < 0. Since both ΔH and ΔS are negative, the reaction is spontaneous only at low temperatures. Set ΔG = 0: 0 = −50,000 − T(−100) → 100T = 50,000 → T = 500 K. Above 500 K, ΔG > 0 (non-spontaneous).
Q13. Chemistry
For a zero-order reaction, the half-life (t1/2) is directly proportional to the initial concentration [A]0. If the half-life is 20 minutes when [A]0 = 0.1 M, what will be the half-life when [A]0 = 0.4 M?
A. 10 min
B. 20 min
C. 40 min
D. 80 min
Correct Answer: D
Shortcut: For zero-order reactions, t1/2 ∝ [A]0. Since [A]0 increases 4 times (0.1→0.4 M), t1/2 becomes 4×20 = 80 min. Full solution: Zero-order rate law: -d[A]/dt = k → [A] = [A]0 - kt. At t1/2, [A] = [A]0/2 → [A]0/2 = [A]0 - k t1/2 → t1/2 = [A]0/(2k). Thus t1/2 is directly proportional to [A]0. With [A]0 quadrupled, t1/2 quadruples to 80 min.
Q14. Botany
A crop plant exhibits necrotic spots, hooking, and eventual death of the terminal buds and young leaves, while the older, lower leaves remain dark green and healthy. Deficiency of which nutrient is most consistent with these symptoms, considering its immobility in the phloem?
A. Nitrogen (N)
B. Potassium (K)
C. Calcium (Ca)
D. Magnesium (Mg)
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut: Classify nutrients by phloem mobility. Mobile (N, P, K, Mg, Cl) → Deficiency shows in OLD leaves first (nutrients translocated to new growth). Immobile (Ca, B, Fe, S, Cu, Zn) → Deficiency shows in YOUNG leaves first (cannot be remobilized). Symptom on terminal buds/young leaves → Immobile nutrient. Eliminate N, K, Mg (Mobile). Select Ca. Worked: Calcium is a structural component of cell walls (calcium pectate) and membranes. It is not retranslocated via phloem. Therefore, during deficiency, Ca cannot move from older leaves to support meristematic activity in new growth, causing tip burn, necrosis, and meristem death in young tissues while older leaves retain Ca and stay green.
Q15. Physics
Two capacitors of capacitance $2 \mu\text{F}$ and $4 \mu\text{F}$ are connected in series across a 12 V battery. The charge stored on the $2 \mu\text{F}$ capacitor is:
A. 16 µC
B. 8 µC
C. 24 µC
D. 32 µC
Correct Answer: A
Shortcut: In series combination, charge $Q$ is same on all capacitors. Equivalent capacitance $C_{\text{eq}} = \frac{C_1 C_2}{C_1+C_2} = \frac{8}{6} = \frac{4}{3} \mu\text{F}$. Total charge $Q = C_{\text{eq}} V = \frac{4}{3} \times 12 = 16 \mu\text{C}$. This is the charge on the $2 \mu\text{F}$ capacitor. Full Solution: $\frac{1}{C_{\text{eq}}} = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{4} \implies C_{\text{eq}} = \frac{4}{3} \mu\text{F}$. $Q = C_{\text{eq}} V = \frac{4}{3} \times 12 = 16 \mu\text{C}$. Since series, $Q_{2\mu\text{F}} = Q = 16 \mu\text{C}$.
