Two projectiles are launched with the same initial speed at angles of 30° and 60° with the horizontal. What is the ratio of their horizontal ranges (R₃₀ : R₆₀)?
A. 1:2
B. 1:1
C. √3:1
D. 1:√3
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: For complementary angles of projection (θ and 90°−θ) with same initial speed, the horizontal ranges are identical. Since 30° + 60° = 90°, the ratio is 1:1. Full Solution: Range formula R = u²sin(2θ)/g. For θ=30°, R₃₀ = u²sin(60°)/g = u²(√3/2)/g. For θ=60°, R₆₀ = u²sin(120°)/g = u²(√3/2)/g. Thus R₃₀/R₆₀ = 1.

Q6. Botanymodel-generated · 2026-11-17
A crop plant shows distinct chlorosis and necrosis in the young, developing leaves while the older, mature leaves remain green and healthy. Based on the mobility of nutrients in the phloem, which nutrient deficiency is most likely the cause?
A. Nitrogen (N)
B. Potassium (K)
C. Calcium (Ca)
D. Magnesium (Mg)
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut: 'Young leaves affected = Immobile nutrient'; 'Old leaves affected = Mobile nutrient'. Calcium (Ca) and Boron (B) are classic phloem-immobile nutrients. N, K, Mg are highly mobile. Worked Solution: Mobile nutrients (N, P, K, Mg, Mo) are translocated from older senescing leaves to younger growing leaves via phloem; thus deficiency symptoms appear first on older leaves. Immobile nutrients (Ca, B, S, Fe, Zn, Cu) cannot be remobilized; deficiency symptoms appear first on young leaves/meristems. Since symptoms are on young leaves, the deficient nutrient must be immobile. Among options, only Calcium is immobile.

In a potentiometer experiment, the null point for a test cell of emf E is found at 75 cm. If the emf of the driver cell is reduced to half its original value while the potentiometer wire resistance remains unchanged, the new null point length will be:
A. 37.5 cm
B. 75 cm
C. 112.5 cm
D. 150 cm
Correct Answer: D
Shortcut: Potential gradient k ∝ V_driver. At null point, E = k·l ⇒ l ∝ 1/k ∝ 1/V_driver. Halving V_driver doubles the null length. 75 cm × 2 = 150 cm. Full Solution: Initially E = k₁l₁ where k₁ = V₁/R_wire (assuming series resistance negligible or constant). New gradient k₂ = (V₁/2)/R_wire = k₁/2. New length l₂ = E/k₂ = E/(k₁/2) = 2(E/k₁) = 2l₁ = 150 cm.

One mole each of a monoatomic gas (γ = 5/3) and a diatomic gas (γ = 7/5) undergo adiabatic expansion from the same initial state (P₁, V₁) to the same final volume V₂. The ratio of work done by the monoatomic gas to the diatomic gas (Wₘ : W_d) is:
A. 5:3
B. 1:1
C. 3:5
D. 2:3
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut: Adiabatic work W = (P₁V₁ - P₂V₂)/(γ-1) = nR(T₁-T₂)/(γ-1). For same initial state and final volume, W ∝ 1/(γ-1). Mono: γ-1 = 2/3 ⇒ factor 3/2. Di: γ-1 = 2/5 ⇒ factor 5/2. Ratio = (3/2):(5/2) = 3:5. Full Solution: Using W = (nRΔT)/(γ-1) and adiabatic relation TV^(γ-1)=const, the temperature drop ΔT depends on γ. However, for fixed initial (P,V) and final V, the work ratio simplifies directly to the inverse ratio of (γ-1). Mono (γ=5/3) gives 1/(2/3)=1.5; Di (γ=7/5) gives 1/(2/5)=2.5. Ratio 1.5:2.5 = 3:5.
