Schubert Calculus — Grassmannians, Schubert Varieties & Intersection Theory

Schubert calculus is the branch of enumerative algebraic geometry that studies the cohomology rings of Grassmannians and flag varieties. Named after Hermann Schubert, it provides an algebraic framework for solving intersection problems: given a family of geometric conditions (e.g., lines intersecting given subspaces), Schubert calculus computes the number of solutions. The central objects are Schubert varieties — closures of Bruhat cells in the Grassmannian Gr(k,n) — whose cohomology classes (Schubert classes) form a basis of the integral cohomology ring. The multiplicative structure is governed by Pieri's rule and the Littlewood-Richardson rule, which encode the combinatorics of Young diagrams and tableaux. Giambelli's formula expresses any Schubert class as a determinant of elementary ones. Modern developments include quantum Schubert calculus (quantum cohomology with Gromov-Witten invariants counting rational curves), Schubert polynomials for flag varieties, and applications to the geometric Satake correspondence and K-theory. Classical enumerative problems — the number of lines meeting four lines in space (2), the 27 lines on a cubic surface, the 2875 lines on a quintic threefold — are all elegantly solved via Schubert calculus.

"Schubert calculus is the algebra of enumerative geometry."
— Hermann Schubert
Part 1

📏 Grassmannians and Their Cellular Decomposition

Cellular
The Grassmann Variety, Plücker Embedding, and Schubert Cells
The Grassmannian Gr(k,n) is the set of k-dimensional linear subspaces of ℂⁿ (or ℝⁿ). It is a projective variety via the Plücker embedding φ: Gr(k,n) → ℙ(Λ𝑝 ℂⁿ) sending span{v₁,...,vₖ}
to v₁∧...∧vₖ. The coordinates of this embedding are the Plücker coordinates pi₁...iₖ (the k×k minors of a k×n matrix representing the subspace), satisfying the Plücker relations (quadratic equations cutting out Gr(k,n) in projective space). The Grassmannian admits a natural cell decomposition: for each strictly increasing sequence 1 ≤ i₁ < ... < iₖ ≤ n, the Schubert cell Ω(i₁,...,iₖ) = { V ∈ Gr(k,n) : dim(V ∩ ℂij) ≥ j } is isomorphic to affine space 𝔼∑(ij − j). These cells form the Bruhat decomposition: Gr(k,n) = ∏w ∈ Sn/Sk×Sn−k Ωw, indexed by cosets of the symmetric group (or partitions fitting in a k×(n−k) box). The closure of a Schubert cell is a Schubert variety Xλ = Ω̄λ ≳ Ωλ, whose fundamental class [Xλ] in homology is the Schubert class σλ. The codimension of Xλ is |λ| = Σ λi, where λ is a partition with at most k parts and λ1 ≤ n−k.
The Grassmannian Gr(k,n) is a compactification of affine cells indexed by Young diagrams fitting in a k×(n−k) rectangle; Schubert varieties are the closures of these cells and their classes generate the cohomology ring.
Part 2

🌍 The Cohomology Ring of the Grassmannian

Cohomology
Schubert Classes, Pieri's Rule, and Giambelli's Formula
The integral cohomology ring of the Grassmannian H*(Gr(k,n), ℤ) is a free abelian group generated by the Schubert classes σλ where λ runs over partitions with at most k parts and λ1 ≤ n−k. In terms of the tautological bundle S (the universal subbundle of rank k) and the quotient bundle Q (of rank n−k), we have an exact sequence 0 → S → ℂⁿ → Q → 0. The cohomology ring admits a presentation: H*(Gr(k,n)) ≅ ℤ[c1,...,ck,c'1,...,c'n−k] / ((1+c1+...+ck)(1+c'1+...+c'n−k) = 1), where ci = ci(S) are Chern classes of the tautological bundle. The special Schubert classes are σp = [X(p)] (codimension p). Pieri's rule describes the product of a special class with an arbitrary one: σp · σλ = Σ σμ summed over all partitions μ obtained from λ by adding p boxes, at most one per column. Giambelli's formula (also called the determinantal formula) expresses any Schubert class as a determinant of special classes: σλ = det(σλi + j − i)1≤i,j≤k, where we set σ0 = 1 and σp = 0 for p < 0 or p > n−k. This is the geometric analogue of the Jacobi-Trudi identity for Schur polynomials.
The cohomology ring of the Grassmannian is isomorphic to the ring of symmetric polynomials truncated by the rectangle condition; Pieri and Giambelli give the full multiplicative structure.
Part 3

🧮 The Littlewood-Richardson Rule and Enumerative Applications

Enumerative
Littlewood-Richardson Coefficients and Classical Counting Problems
The full ring structure is given by the Littlewood-Richardson rule: σλ · σμ = Σν cλμν σν where the Littlewood-Richardson coefficients cλμν count the number of Littlewood-Richardson tableaux of shape ν\λ with weight μ (a semistandard tableau whose reverse-reading word is a lattice word). These coefficients are symmetric in λ and μ and satisfy the Pieri rule as a special case. Classical enumerative problems solved by Schubert calculus include: the number of lines in ℙ³ meeting 4 given lines in general position = 2; the number of conics tangent to 5 given conics (Schubert's 15 conics problem) = 3264; the number of conics through 5 points = 1; the number of twisted cubics meeting 12 lines = 144. The famous 27 lines on a cubic surface is computed via Schubert calculus on the Grassmannian Gr(2,4) of lines in ℙ³. For the quintic threefold (a Calabi-Yau hypersurface in ℙ⁴), the number of rational curves of degree d is predicted by mirror symmetry: at d=1, there are 2875 lines (verified by Schubert calculus and Gromov-Witten theory). The Kleiman-Bertini theorem ensures that for general translates in the homogeneous space, intersections are transverse, making the Schubert cycle counts enumeratively meaningful.
Littlewood-Richardson coefficients encode the multiplication of Schubert classes and solve centuries-old enumerative geometry problems — counting lines, conics, and rational curves in projective space.
Part 4

🔥 Quantum Schubert Calculus and Flag Varieties

Quantum
Quantum Cohomology, Flag Varieties, and Schubert Polynomials
Quantum Schubert calculus studies the quantum cohomology ring QH*(Gr(k,n)) = H*(Gr(k,n)) ⊗ ℤ[q] with product ∗ incorporating Gromov-Witten invariants. The quantum Pieri rule (Bertram-Ciocan-Fontanine-Fulton): σ1 ∗ σλ = Σ σλ+ei + q · Σ σλ−ej, where the first sum adds a box to the i-th row and the second removes a box from the j-th row (if the result remains a valid partition). The quantum Giambelli formula expresses σλ as a determinant in QH* with q terms. The 3-point Gromov-Witten invariants cλμν,d count degree-d rational curves in Gr(k,n) meeting three Schubert varieties Xλ, Xμ, Xν∨. The ring QH*(Gr(k,n)) is isomorphic to the quotient of ℤ[q][x1,...,xk] by the quantum deformation of the symmetric relations. For the complete flag variety Fl(n) = {0 ⊂ V1 ⊂ ... ⊂ Vn = ℂⁿ}, Schubert varieties Xw are indexed by permutations w ∈ Sn and their classes σw form a basis of H*(Fl(n)). The cohomology ring H*(Fl(n)) ≅ ℤ[x1,...,xn]/(e1,...,en), where ek are elementary symmetric polynomials. Schubert polynomials ᗒw(x) (defined by Lascoux-Schützenberger) represent these classes and satisfy the Monk formula and its generalizations. Applications extend to the K-theory of Grassmannians (where structure constants are given by the Knuth relations and the geometric Satake correspondence), and to equivariant Schubert calculus for torus actions.
Quantum Schubert calculus extends classical intersection theory by counting rational curves; flag varieties bring Schubert polynomials and the rich combinatorics of permutations into the picture.
