Chromatic Homotopy — the Morava E-theories, the Nilpotence Theorem & the Periodic Table

Chromatic homotopy theory is the study of the stable homotopy category through a filtration by "heights" — a chromatic filtration that organizes phenomena by periodicity families. The central idea, due to Jack Morava, is that the stable homotopy category can be decomposed into layers governed by the moduli space of formal groups of height n. The Nilpotence theorem (Devinatz-Hopkins-Smith, 1988) and the periodicity theorem provide the foundational structural results: the former says that the MU-Hurewicz map detects nilpotence in the stable homotopy ring of a finite spectrum, while the latter guarantees the existence of v_n self-maps. The Morava K-theories K(n) and the Morava E-theories E_n serve as the local "probes" at each height. The chromatic spectral sequence assembles these layers into the homotopy groups of a general spectrum. The telescope conjecture — now known to be false — asked whether the Bousfield localization at K(n) could be realized more concretely. The interplay of these ideas leads to deep computations of the homotopy groups of spheres, the classification of thick subcategories of finite spectra, and connections to topological modular forms and the Langlands program.

"Chromatic homotopy is the systematic study of v_n-periodicity."
— Ethan Devinatz
Part 1

📏 The Nilpotence Theorem and its Consequences

Foundations
The Nilpotence Theorem, the Periodicity Theorem, and the Thick Subcategory Theorem
The Nilpotence theorem (Devinatz-Hopkins-Smith, 1988) is one of the deepest results in algebraic topology. It states: for any finite spectrum X, an element α ∈ π_*(X) maps to 0 under the MU-Hurewicz map π_*(X) → MU_*(X) iff α is nilpotent — i.e., some smash power α^{∧k} = 0. Equivalently, the kernel of the MU-Hurewicz map consists entirely of nilpotent elements. The proof uses the X(n)-construction and the chromatic filtration of the moduli space of formal groups. The Periodicity theorem (Hopkins-Smith): for any finite spectrum X and any n ≥ 1, there exists a v_n self-map f: Σ^k X → X such that K(n)_*(f) is an isomorphism and K(m)_*(f) = 0 for m ≠ n. The thick subcategory theorem (Hopkins-Smith) classifies the thick subcategories of the category of finite spectra: they are precisely the subcategories C_n of spectra of type ≥ n (those for which v_0, …, v_{n−1} vanish). The chromatic filtration is the tower of Bousfield localizations L_n with respect to K(0) ∨ … ∨ K(n). The chromatic convergence theorem states that for a finite spectrum X, the natural map X → holim_n L_n X is an equivalence.
The nilpotence theorem is the fundamental detection result: the MU-Hurewicz map sees all nilpotence in the stable homotopy of finite spectra, giving a powerful algebraic grip on a geometric phenomenon.
Part 2

🌍 Morava K-theories and E-theories

Heights
Morava K-theories K(n), Morava E-theories E_n, and Lubin-Tate Theory
For each prime p and integer 0 ≤ n ≤ ∞, the Morava K-theory K(n) is a complex oriented cohomology theory with coefficient ring K(n)_* = 𝔽_p[v_n, v_n^{-1}] where |v_n| = 2(p^n − 1). K(0) = Hℚ and K(∞) = H𝔽_p. The theory K(n) detects v_n-periodicity: a map f: X → Y is a K(n)-equivalence iff it induces an isomorphism in v_n^{-1}E(n)_*(-). The Morava E-theory E_n (also called complete Lubin-Tate theory) is the spectrum whose coefficient ring is E_n^* = W(𝔽_{p^n})[[u_1, …, u_{n-1}]][u, u^{-1}] where |u| = 2. Its formal group is the universal deformation of the Honda formal group of height n. The Goerss-Hopkins-Miller theorem endows E_n with an E_∞-ring structure and an action by the extended Morava stabilizer group G_n = O_n^× ⋊ Gal(𝔽_{p^n}/𝔽_p). The homotopy fixed points E_n^{hG_n} recover topological modular forms: E_n^{hG_n} ≃ TMF(p). The Smith-Toda complexes V(n) are finite spectra realizing the Morava K-theory of height n.
Morava K-theories and E-theories provide the local "coordinate charts" on the moduli space of formal groups, organizing stable homotopy by the height parameter.
Part 3

🧮 The Chromatic Spectral Sequence

Spectral
The Chromatic Fracture Square, the Chromatic Spectral Sequence, and Gross-Hopkins Duality
The chromatic fracture square expresses the K(n)-localization L_{K(n)}X in terms of the E(n)-localization: there is a homotopy pullback square L_{K(n)}X → L_{E(n)}X → L_{E(n-1)}X. The chromatic spectral sequence (CSS) has E_1^{p,q} = π_{p+q}(M_p ⊗ X) ⇒ π_{p+q}(L_{K(p)}X) where M_p are the monochromatic layers (the fibers of L_{E(p)}X → L_{E(p-1)}X). The Adams-Novikov spectral sequence (ANSS) can be organized by chromatic height: its E_2-term is the continuous cohomology of the moduli stack of formal groups, naturally filtered by the chromatic filtration. The Gross-Hopkins dual I_n = Σ^{n^2-n}E_n^∨ (the Spanier-Whitehead dual of E_n) satisfies a self-duality property. The Gross-Hopkins duality formula: the Brown-Comenetz dual of K(n) is Σ^{2(p^n - 1)n}K(n). The Morava module of a spectrum X at height n is the continuous G_n-module K(n)_*(X) = E_n^*(X) ⊗_{E_n^*} K(n)_*. The chromatic decomposition of the homotopy groups of spheres is one of the great triumphs of chromatic homotopy.
The chromatic spectral sequence systematically assembles the homotopy information at each height into a computable algebraic framework, with the Morava stabilizer group governing the symmetries.
Part 4

🔥 The Telescope Conjecture and Applications

Periodicity
The Telescope Conjecture, Its Disproof, and Modern Applications
The telescope conjecture (Smith, Ravenel, 1980s) asserted that the Bousfield localization L_n^f at the wedge K(0) ∨ … ∨ K(n) on finite spectra is equivalent to smashing with the telescope T(n) of a v_n self-map on a finite type-n spectrum: L_n X ≃ X ∧ T(n). This conjecture, if true, would have dramatically simplified the chromatic picture. However, it is now known to be false in general: counterexamples were constructed by Mahowald-Ravenel-Shick and, more recently, by Barthel-David-Salch for n = 2 and p ≥ 5. The thick subcategory theorem has powerful applications to the classifying spaces of finite groups: the classification of thick subcategories of D^b(Coh(BG)). Applications to the Cohomology of the Steenrod algebra and the Adams spectral sequence: the first 61 stems of the homotopy groups of spheres have been computed (up to 2015) using chromatic methods. A modern application: the Behrens-Lawson theorem on the exotic periodicity in TMF reveals a duality between 2-adic and ∞-adic geometry in the theory of topological modular forms, linking chromatic height 2 to the moduli of elliptic curves.
The failure of the telescope conjecture shows that chromatic localization is subtler than originally hoped; yet the interplay of these ideas continues to drive progress in stable homotopy theory and its connections to number theory.
