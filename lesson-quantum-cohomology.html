Quantum Cohomology — Gromov–Witten Invariants, the WDVV Equation & Mirror Symmetry

Deep dive into Quantum Cohomology — Gromov–Witten invariants (virtual counts of pseudoholomorphic curves in a symplectic manifold X, defining intersection numbers on the moduli space of stable maps M̅_{g,n}(X, β) with Kontsevich’s virtual fundamental class), the quantum cohomology ring QH^*(X) = H^*(X) ⊗ ℂ[[q]] as a deformation of the ordinary cohomology ring with the quantum cup product α ⋆ β = Σ_{β∈H₂(X)} q^β Σ_{a,b} GW_{0,3}^{X,β}(α, β, γ_a) g^{ab} γ_b, the WDVV (Witten–Dijkgraaf–Verlinde–Verlinde) equations (the associativity of the quantum product expressed as differential equations for the prepotential), Frobenius manifolds (Dubrovin, 1992: a flat metric with a compatible associative commutative product satisfying the WDVV equations), mirror symmetry (equality of A-model GW potential and B-model period integrals), and the Mirror Theorem of Givental and Lian–Liu–Yau for counting rational curves on Calabi–Yau threefolds.

“Quantum cohomology is the algebraic structure underlying the counting of holomorphic curves.”
— Maxim Kontsevich
Part 1

⚡ Gromov–Witten Invariants

GW Theory
Moduli of Stable Maps, Virtual Fundamental Class, and Gromov–Witten Invariants
Let (X, ω) be a symplectic manifold (or a smooth projective variety). A stable map (Kontsevich, 1994) is a pair (C, f) where C is a nodal curve (a connected union of smooth rational curves meeting at nodes) and f: C → X is a holomorphic map, with the stability condition: the automorphism group of (C, f) is finite. The moduli space M̅_{g,n}(X, β) parameterizes stable maps of genus g with n marked points, representing β ∈ H₂(X). This is a Deligne–Mumford stack with a perfect obstruction theory (Behrend–Fantechi, Li–Tian) giving a virtual fundamental class [M̅_{g,n}(X,β)]^{vir} ∈ A_{vd}(M̅_{g,n}(X,β)) where vd = (dim X − 3)(1−g) + c₁(X)·β + n. The Gromov–Witten invariant is GW_{g,n}^{X,β}(α₁,…,α_n) = ∫_{[M̅_{g,n}(X,β)]^{vir}} ∏ ev_i^*(α_i) where ev_i: M̅_{g,n}(X,β) → X is evaluation at the i-th marked point. For genus 0, these count rational curves of class β through constraints Poincaré dual to α_i. The g=0, n=3 case gives the structure constants of quantum cohomology. The string equation, dilaton equation, and divisor equation are universal relations. The Kontsevich–Manin axioms include: effective (GW_{g,n}^{X,β}=0 if β=0 and (g,n)≠(0,1),(0,2),(0,3),(1,0)); symmetry under permutation of marked points; fundamental class relation; and the splitting (degeneration) formula.
Gromov–Witten invariants are virtual counts of holomorphic curves, defined via the virtual fundamental class of the moduli space of stable maps.
Part 2

🌀 Quantum Cohomology Rings

Quantum
Deformation of the Cup Product, the Quantum Ring, and the Divisor Equation
The quantum cohomology ring QH^*(X) = (H^*(X; ℂ) ⊗ ℂ[[q₁,…,q_r]], ⋆) deforms the ordinary cup product, parametrized by quantum parameters q_β = exp(−∫_β ω) (in the Novikov ring). Choose basis {γ_a} of H^*(X) with intersection matrix g_{ab} = ∫_X γ_a ∪ γ_b, and inverse g^{ab}. The quantum cup product: γ_a ⋆ γ_b = Σ_{c,d} (∂_a ∂_b ∂_c F₀) g^{cd} γ_d, where F₀ is the genus-zero GW potential: F₀(t) = Σ_{n≥0} (1/n!) Σ_{β,a₁,…,a_n} GW_{0,n}^{X,β}(γ_{a₁},…,γ_{a_n}) t^{a₁}…t^{a_n}. The structure constants: ⟨γ_a ⋆ γ_b, γ_c⟩ = Σ_{β∈H₂(X)} q^β GW_{0,3}^{X,β}(γ_a, γ_b, γ_c). The product is associative (WDVV), graded (deg(q_β) = 2c₁(β)), and commutative. The divisor equation: for D ∈ H²(X), GW_{0,n}^{X,β}(D, α₁,…,) = (∫_β D) GW_{0,n−1}^{X,β}(α₁,…) + Σ GW_{0,n−1}^{X,β}(α₁,…,α_i ∪ D,…). For ℗^N, QH^*(℗^N) ≃ ℂ[H, q]/(H^{N+1} = q). The Batyrev ring for toric varieties.
Quantum cohomology deforms the cup product by contributions from holomorphic spheres, governed by the GW potential.
Part 3

🌊 WDVV Equations and Frobenius Manifolds

Frobenius
The WDVV Equations, Frobenius Manifolds, and Dubrovin’s Theory
The WDVV equations (Witten, Dijkgraaf–Verlinde–Verlinde, 1990) are the associativity of the quantum product as third-order PDEs for F(t): ∂_a ∂_b ∂_e F · g^{ef} · ∂_f ∂_c ∂_d F = ∂_a ∂_c ∂_e F · g^{ef} · ∂_f ∂_b ∂_d F for all a,b,c,d. A Frobenius manifold (Dubrovin, 1992) is a smooth manifold M with a flat metric g, a commutative associative product ◦ on TM with unit e, such that (i) g(u◦v,w) = g(u,v◦w); (ii) the prepotential F satisfies ∂_a ∂_b ∂_c F = g(u_a◦u_b,u_c); (iii) flatness of the connection ∇ + z◦ for all z (the Dubrovin connection). The quantum D-module is the ℂ[[z]]-module H^*(X)⊗ℂ[[z]] with the quantum connection ∇_{∂_a} = ∂_a + (1/z) (γ_a ⋆ ·). Its flat sections give the J-function (Givental’s formalism). The Givental’s Lagrangian cone encodes all genus-zero GW invariants via the formal loop space. The reconstruction theorem (Kontsevich–Manin): genus-zero GW invariants are determined by the WDVV equations and the divisor axiom from the initial data of three-point invariants. The Bershadsky–Cecotti–Ooguri–Vafa (BCOV) theory for higher-genus invariants via the holomorphic anomaly equation. Mirror symmetry for Calabi–Yau threefolds: the A-model GW potential F₀ of X equals the B-model Yukawa coupling on the mirror Y.
Frobenius manifolds formalize the algebraic structure of quantum cohomology; the WDVV equations encode associativity as a system of PDEs for the prepotential.
Part 4

✨ Mirror Symmetry and the Mirror Theorem

Mirror
The Mirror Theorem, Givental’s Formalism, and Enumerative Applications
The Mirror Theorem (Givental, 1996; Lian–Liu–Yau, 1997) gives an explicit formula for genus-zero GW invariants of Calabi–Yau hypersurfaces in toric varieties in terms of the hypergeometric series (the I-function or Yukawa coupling) of the mirror manifold. For the quintic threefold X₅ = {Σ x_i⁵ = 0} ⊂ ℗⁴, the mirror Y is the resolution of the orbifold (℗⁴/Ξ⁵)_ψ (the Dwork family). The mirror map q = e⁻^t identifies the Kähler parameter t of X with the complex modulus ψ of Y. The genus-zero GW potential of X is F₀(t) = ⅔ t³ + 5 ∑_{d≥1} N_d e^{dt} where N_d are the instanton numbers (virtual counts of degree-d rational curves). The mirror theorem: N_d = ∑_{k|d} k⁻^³ n_k where n_k are integers (the BPS invariants). For d=1: N₁ = 2875 (the number of lines on the quintic). d=2: 609250 (conics). d=3: 317206375 (twisted cubics). The BCOV holomorphic anomaly equation (Bershadsky–Cecotti–Ooguri–Vafa, 1993) extends mirror symmetry to higher-genus GW invariants. The Givental’s quantization formalism (the Frobenius manifold of genus-zero invariants determines all higher-genus invariants via the TQFT formalism). The Kontsevich’s homological mirror symmetry connects quantum cohomology to the Fukaya category and the derived category. Applications to enumerative geometry: counting curves on del Pezzo surfaces, the Gopakumar–Vafa invariants (integer invariants derived from GW invariants via the Gopakumar–Vafa formula), and the Donaldson–Thomas/GW correspondence.
The Mirror Theorem allows exact computation of Gromov–Witten invariants via hypergeometric series on the mirror, revolutionizing enumerative geometry.
