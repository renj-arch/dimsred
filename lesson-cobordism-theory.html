Cobordism Theory — Cobordism Groups, Thom Spaces & the Pontryagin-Thom Construction

Cobordism theory studies smooth manifolds up to the equivalence relation of cobordism: two closed n-manifolds M and N are cobordant if their disjoint union M⊔N bounds a compact (n+1)-manifold W. This notion, introduced by Pontryagin and systematized by Thom, turns the set of equivalence classes into a graded ring Ω_* under disjoint union (addition) and Cartesian product (multiplication). Thom's theorem (1954) identifies the unoriented cobordism ring Ω_*^O with a polynomial ring over ℤ/2, a landmark result that initiated the modern theory of cobordism. The Pontryagin-Thom construction reduces the computation of cobordism groups to stable homotopy theory via the Thom spectrum MG. Complex cobordism (MU) connects to formal group laws through Quillen's theorem: π_*(MU) is the Lazard ring. The Adams-Novikov spectral sequence uses MU to compute stable homotopy groups of spheres. Cobordism also underpins the Hirzebruch signature theorem, genera (L-genus, Â-genus, Todd genus, Witten genus), and the theory of topological modular forms (TMF).

"Cobordism is the most computable of all generalized cohomology theories."
— John Milnor
Part 1

📏 Cobordism Groups

Foundations
Cobordism Groups, the Cobordism Ring, and Thom's Theorem
A closed smooth n-manifold M is null-cobordant (or bounds) if M = ∂W for some compact (n+1)-manifold W. Two closed n-manifolds M, N are cobordant (written M ~ N) if M⊔N is null-cobordant — i.e., there is a compact (n+1)-manifold W with ∂W = M⊔N. The unoriented cobordism group Ω_n^O is the set of equivalence classes; the operation is disjoint union, which makes each group a vector space over ℤ/2 because every manifold is its own inverse (M⊔M = ∂(M×[0,1])). The oriented cobordism group Ω_n^SO requires all manifolds and bordisms to be oriented. Examples: Ω_0^O ≅ ℤ/2 (a point is not a boundary since the boundary of a 1-manifold has an even number of points), Ω_1^O = 0 (the circle S^1 bounds the disk D^2), Ω_2^O ≅ ℤ/2 (the real projective plane ℝℙ² is not orientable and does not bound a 3-manifold; but 2ℝℙ² ≅ ℝℙ²#ℝℙ² bounds). The graded abelian group Ω_*^O = ⊕_{n≥0} Ω_n^O becomes a cobordism ring with multiplication given by Cartesian product. Thom's theorem (1954): Ω_*^O ≅ ℤ/2[x_2, x_4, x_5, x_6, x_7, x_8, ...] where there is one generator in each degree n not of the form 2ᵏ−1. The generator in degree 2 is represented by ℝℙ²; in degree 4 by ℝℙ⁴; and so on. For oriented cobordism, Ω_*^SO ⊗ ℚ ≅ ℚ[ℂℙ², ℂℙ⁴, ...] — a polynomial ring over ℚ on generators in even degrees.
Cobordism groups classify manifolds up to the relation of bounding a common manifold; the unoriented cobordism ring is a polynomial algebra over ℤ/2 with one generator in most degrees.
Part 2

🌍 The Pontryagin-Thom Construction

Thom Spaces
The Pontryagin-Thom Map, Thom Spectra, and MG
The Pontryagin-Thom construction is a bridge between manifold geometry and stable homotopy theory. Given a closed smooth n-manifold Mⁿ, embed it in ℝ^{n+k} for large k. The normal bundle ν has a tubular neighborhood T ≅ D(ν) (the disk bundle of ν). Collapse the complement of T to a point: this yields the Pontryagin-Thom map τ: S^{n+k} → Th(ν), where Th(ν) = D(ν)/S(ν) is the Thom space of ν (disk bundle modulo boundary sphere). For a G-structure on M (e.g., orientation, spin, complex structure), the normal bundle is classified by a map ν: M → BG(k). The resulting map S^{n+k} → Th(γ_k) (where γ_k is the universal bundle over BG(k)) represents an element of π_{n+k}(Th(γ_k)). Taking k → ∞ gives the Thom spectrum MG: the sequence of Thom spaces {Th(γ_k)} with suspension maps Σ Th(γ_k) → Th(γ_{k+1}). The Pontryagin-Thom theorem states: π_n(MG) ≅ Ω_n^G — the nth stable homotopy group of the Thom spectrum MG is precisely the cobordism group of n-dimensional G-manifolds. For unoriented cobordism, G = O and π_*(MO) ≅ Ω_*^O. For oriented cobordism, G = SO. For complex cobordism, G = U. The spectrum MO has π_*(MO) ≅ ℤ/2[x_2, x_4, x_5, ...] as a graded ring. The Thom isomorphism theorem: H^*(Th(γ_k)) ≅ H^*(BG(k)) (a shift), which is used to compute the cohomology of Thom spectra via the Serre spectral sequence.
The Pontryagin-Thom construction translates the geometric problem of classifying manifolds up to cobordism into the homotopy-theoretic problem of computing stable homotopy groups of Thom spectra.
Part 3

🧮 Complex Cobordism and Formal Group Laws

MU
Complex Cobordism, the Lazard Ring, and Quillen's Theorem
Complex cobordism MU is the Thom spectrum for the unitary group U — that is, the spectrum whose spaces are Th(γ_k) for the universal complex k-plane bundles over BU(k). The coefficient ring MU_* = π_*(MU) is a graded ring concentrated in even degrees: MU_* ≅ ℤ[x_1, x_2, ...] with |x_i| = 2i. Quillen's theorem (1969): the coefficient ring π_*(MU) is canonically isomorphic to the Lazard ring L — the universal ring for 1-dimensional formal group laws. A 1-dimensional formal group law over a ring R is a power series F(x,y) = x + y + Σ_{i,j≥1} a_{ij} x^i y^j ∈ R[[x,y]] satisfying associativity, commutativity, and unit axioms. The Lazard ring L is the polynomial ring ℤ[a_{ij}]/(relations from formal group law axioms), graded such that |a_{ij}| = 2(i+j−1). Quillen proved that the formal group law on MU comes from the universal formal group law via the canonical map L → MU_*. The formal group law of a complex-oriented cohomology theory E is encoded by its formal group law F_E(x,y) = sum_{i,j} a_{ij}^E x^i y^j, where the coefficients are images of the Lazard generators. The Conner-Floyd theorem: MU^*(X) ⊗_{MU_*} ℤ ≅ H^*(X, ℤ). More generally, any complex-oriented cohomology theory E is a module over MU via the universal property of the Lazard ring. The Adams-Novikov spectral sequence (ANSS) is an important computational tool: E₂^{s,t} = Ext_{MU_*MU}^s(MU_*, MU_*(X)) ⇒ π_{t−s}(X). For X = S⁰, the ANSS computes the stable homotopy groups of spheres. The MU_*MU-comodule structure encodes deep number-theoretic information — the chromatic filtration organizes stable homotopy theory by heights of formal group laws (the Morava stabilizer group at each prime and height n).
Complex cobordism MU carries the universal formal group law; Quillen's identification with the Lazard ring makes MU the universal complex-oriented cohomology theory and the foundation for chromatic stable homotopy theory.
Part 4

🔥 Cobordism in Algebraic Topology and Applications

Genera
The Hirzebruch Signature Theorem, Genera, and Topological Modular Forms
A genus is a ring homomorphism φ: Ω_*^SO → R from the oriented cobordism ring to a commutative ring R. Each genus is determined by its values on ℂℙ^{2k} (the even-dimensional complex projective spaces). The Hirzebruch signature theorem: for a closed oriented 4k-manifold M, the signature sign(M) satisfies sign(M) = ⟨L(p), [M]⟩, where L(p) is the Hirzebruch L-genus — a certain multiplicative sequence in the Pontryagin classes of M. The Â-genus (A-hat genus) is the genus corresponding to the Dirac operator index; it vanishes on manifolds that admit a Riemannian metric of positive scalar curvature (Lichnerowicz theorem). The Todd genus Td(M) is the genus associated to complex cobordism; for a complex manifold, Td(M) = χ(M, O_M) by the Hirzebruch-Riemann-Roch theorem. The Witten genus is a genus for string manifolds (those with w₁ = 0, w₂ = 0, and p₁/2 = 0) taking values in modular forms; it is the ∫-genus in elliptic cohomology. The Ochanine elliptic genus is a multiplicative genus with values in the ring of modular forms. The Stolz-Teichner program proposes that supersymmetric quantum field theories provide cocycles for cohomology theories: 0-dimensional SUSY field theories → K-theory; 1-dimensional SUSY field theories → TMF (topological modular forms). TMF is the spectrum of topological modular forms constructed by Hopkins-Miller and Goerss-Hopkins-Miller: it is the universal elliptic cohomology theory and its coefficient ring π_*(TMF) is the ring of integral modular forms. The Ando-Hopkins-Rezk orientation MString → TMF lifts the Witten genus to a map of ring spectra, showing that every string manifold has a TMF-orientation. The Hopkins-Mahowald theorem relates the Hurewicz image of the sphere spectrum in TMF to modular forms. These ideas interconnect cobordism, number theory, and mathematical physics at the deepest level.
Genera are cobordism invariants that connect manifold topology to number theory and modular forms; topological modular forms (TMF) represent the universal elliptic cohomology theory and receive an orientation from string cobordism.
