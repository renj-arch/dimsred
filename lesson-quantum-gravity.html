Quantum Gravity — LQG, Black Hole Information Paradox & Holographic Principle

Part 1

⚛ The Problem of Quantum Gravity

UV Completion
Non-Renormalizability, Effective Field Theory, and the Weinberg-Witten Theorem
The Einstein-Hilbert action S_EH = (1/16πG) ∫ d⁴x √−g R is perturbatively non-renormalizable: the coupling constant G has mass dimension −2. Power counting shows that at ℓ loops, divergences involve R^{ℓ+1} terms. Pure gravity is finite at one loop (the 't Hooft-Veltman result), but divergences appear at two loops (Goroff-Sagnotti 1985). The effective field theory (EFT) approach treats GR as the leading term in a derivative expansion: S_EFT = ∫ √−g (Λ + M_Pl² R + c₁ R² + c₂ R_{μν}R^{μν} + …), valid below the Planck scale M_Pl = √(ħc/G) ≈ 1.22 × 10^19 GeV. The Weinberg-Witten theorem (1980) forbids a massless spin-2 particle (the graviton) from carrying a conserved Lorentz-covariant current, constraining the possible constructions of quantum gravity. Causality and unitarity conflict with naive approaches: the graviton's spin-2 nature leads to issues with causality at the quantum level. String theory and loop quantum gravity are the two main approaches to UV-completing gravity. String theory introduces extended objects (strings) to soften UV divergences, while LQG maintains 4D spacetime but quantizes geometry itself. The asymptotic safety scenario (Weinberg) posits a non-Gaussian fixed point for the gravitational coupling at high energies, making gravity non-perturbatively renormalizable. The causal dynamical triangulations (CDT) approach uses lattice methods and suggests a smooth macroscopic de Sitter phase emerges from the quantum theory. The gravitational path integral Z = ∫ Dg e^{iS_EH[g]} is the formal definition of quantum gravity, but its precise meaning — including the sum over topologies and the measure — remains an open problem.
Quantum gravity is the search for a consistent UV completion of general relativity — a theory that must reconcile the principles of quantum mechanics with the geometric nature of spacetime.
Part 2

🌀 Loop Quantum Gravity and Spinfoams

LQG
Ashtekar Variables, Holonomies, Spin Networks, and the Discrete Spectrum of Area
Loop quantum gravity (Rovelli, Smolin, Ashtekar 1988) quantizes GR in 4D using the Ashtekar-Barbero variables: an SU(2) connection A_a^i (the Ashtekar connection) and a densitized triad E^a_i (the electric field), where a is a spatial index and i is an internal SU(2) index. In these variables, the constraints of GR become polynomial: the Gauss constraint G_i = D_a E^a_i = 0 (SU(2) gauge invariance), the diffeomorphism constraint C_a = F_{ab}^i E^b_i = 0 (spatial diffeomorphisms), and the Hamiltonian constraint H = ε_{ijk} E^a_i E^b_j F_{ab}^k / √det(E) = 0 (time reparametrizations). The holonomy h_e(A) = P exp(∫_e A) along an edge e is the fundamental variable for the connection. The flux E(S,f) = ∫_S E^a_i n_a f^i d²σ across a surface S is the conjugate variable. The holonomy-flux algebra [h_e, E(S,f)] = i ℓ_Pl² (something) defines the kinematics. The spin network states |Γ, j_e, i_v⟩ are eigenstates of area and volume: a graph Γ with edges labeled by SU(2) representations j_e and vertices labeled by intertwiners i_v. The area operator Â(S) acting on a surface S pierced by edges has discrete eigenvalues A = 8π γ ℓ_Pl² Σ_p √(j_p(j_p+1)), where γ ≈ 0.2375 is the Barbero-Immirzi parameter. The volume operator also has a discrete spectrum. The spin foam (a 2-complex of faces, edges, and vertices) is a path integral over spin network histories, defining a sum-over-histories formulation of LQG. The EPRL (Engle-Pereira-Rovelli-Livine) vertex amplitude gives the dynamics. Loop quantum cosmology (LQC) applies LQG to symmetry-reduced cosmological models, replacing the Big Bang by a Big Bounce at a critical density ρ_c ≈ 0.41 ρ_Pl. LQC predicts a scale factor that contracts, bounces, and expands, resolving the initial singularity.
LQG quantizes geometry itself: area and volume are discrete at the Planck scale, with spin networks providing a background-independent description of quantum spacetime.
Part 3

🔥 Black Hole Information Paradox

Information
Hawking Radiation, the Page Curve, the Island Formula, and Replica Wormholes
Hawking radiation (Hawking 1974, 1975): quantum effects near a black hole horizon create particle pairs, with one member falling in (negative energy) and the other escaping to infinity. The escaping radiation has a thermal (Planck) spectrum with temperature T_H = ħc³/(8πG M k_B). For a Schwarzschild black hole, the Hawking temperature is T_H ∼ 10^{−7} K (M_⊙/M). The black hole evaporates over the Hawking evaporation time t_ev ≈ 5120 π G² M³ / (ħ c⁴) ∼ 10^67 (M/M_⊙)³ years. The information paradox: Hawking's original calculation shows the radiation is exactly thermal, independent of the initial state that formed the black hole. If the black hole evaporates completely, an initially pure quantum state evolves into a mixed state — violating unitarity (the conservation of quantum information). The Page curve (Page 1993): for an evaporating black hole entangled with its radiation, the fine-grained (von Neumann) entropy S(R) of the radiation should first rise (as the black hole emits), peak at the Page time t_Page ≈ t_ev/2 when half the radiation has been emitted, and then decrease back to zero, following the Page curve (the entropy of a random pure state in a bipartite system). The island formula (Almheiri-Engelhardt-Maldacena 2019): S(R) = min_ext_I [ Area(∂I)/4G + S_semi-classical(R ∪ I) ], where I is an "island" region behind the horizon. Replica wormholes (Penington-Shenker-Stanford-Yang 2019) and the entanglement wedge reconstruction show how the island formula emerges from the gravitational path integral. The black hole interior is encoded in the Hawking radiation via a non-isometric map. The smoothness of the horizon (no drama) requires the radiation to be entangled with the interior, leading to the firewall paradox (Almheiri-Marolf-Polchinski-Sully 2012) which questions whether the equivalence principle can be maintained.
The information paradox reveals a deep tension between general relativity and quantum mechanics; its resolution via the island formula and replica wormholes suggests that spacetime geometry itself is emergent from quantum entanglement.
Part 4

🪞 Holographic Principle and the AdS/CFT Correspondence

Holography
The Bekenstein Bound, the Holographic Entropy Bound, AdS/CFT, and ER=EPR
The Bekenstein-Hawking entropy S_BH = A/(4Għ) of a black hole scales with the horizon area A, not the volume. This motivated the holographic principle (ʼt Hooft 1993, Susskind 1995): the maximum entropy in any region of space is bounded by the area of its boundary: S ≤ A/4G. The Bekenstein bound S ≤ 2πER/ħc limits the information content of a system of energy E and size R. The AdS/CFT correspondence (Maldacena 1997) provides a concrete realization of holography: Type IIB string theory in AdS₅×S⁵ is equivalent to N=4 super-Yang-Mills theory on the 4D boundary. The dictionary: a bulk scalar field φ of mass m corresponds to a boundary operator O of scaling dimension Δ where Δ(Δ−4) = m²L². The extrapolate dictionary: lim_{z→0} φ(z, x) / z^{4−Δ} = ⟨O(x)⟩. The holographic entanglement entropy (Ryu-Takayanagi 2006): the entanglement entropy of a boundary region A equals the area of the minimal bulk surface γ_A homologous to A divided by 4G: S(A) = Area(γ_A) / 4G. The entanglement wedge is the bulk region bounded by A ∪ γ_A. The quantum extremal surface (Engelhardt-Wall 2014) generalizes to include bulk quantum corrections. The ER=EPR conjecture (Maldacena-Susskind 2013) posits that Einstein-Rosen bridges (wormholes) are equivalent to Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen entangled pairs — connecting quantum entanglement and spacetime connectivity. The complexity equals action or complexity equals volume conjectures relate the computational complexity of the boundary state to the action or volume of a bulk Wheeler-DeWitt patch. The SYK model (Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev) is a solvable 1D model of fermions with random all-to-all interactions that is holographically dual to 2D Jackiw-Teitelboim gravity.
The holographic principle asserts that the universe is a projection of data stored on its boundary — AdS/CFT is its precise mathematical realization, showing that quantum gravity in the bulk is equivalent to a gauge theory on the boundary.
