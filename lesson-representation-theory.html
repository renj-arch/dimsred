Representation Theory — Linearizing Group Actions

Representation theory studies groups by their actions on vector spaces — turning abstract group elements into matrices. This linearizes group theory, making it amenable to linear algebra. It's one of the most powerful and unifying subjects in mathematics, with applications from number theory (automorphic forms) to quantum physics (particle classification).

"Representation theory is the study of symmetry through linear algebra — it's the language of quantum mechanics and the key to the Fourier transform."
— T.A. Springer (paraphrased)
Part 1

🏛️ Foundations — Representations and Characters

Linearizing Groups
Group Representations, G-Modules, and Characters
A representation of a group G on a vector space V is a homomorphism ρ: G → GL(V). Equivalently, V is a G-module where g·v = ρ(g)v. A representation is irreducible if it has no nontrivial G-invariant subspaces. Schur's lemma: any G-equivariant map between irreducible representations is either zero or an isomorphism; over ℂ, it's a scalar multiple of the identity. The character χ_V(g) = Tr(ρ(g)) is a class function (constant on conjugacy classes). For finite groups, the character table captures all irreducible representations. The orthogonality of characters: (1/|G|) Σ χ_i(g) χ_j(g) = δ_{ij}. Every representation decomposes as a direct sum of irreducibles: V = ⊕ n_i V_i where n_i = ⟨χ_V, χ_i⟩. Maschke's theorem: for finite groups with char(F) ∤ |G|, every representation is completely reducible.
Characters are the harmonic analysis of finite groups — they diagonalize the group algebra and reveal the internal structure.
Part 2

🔄 Induction, Restriction, and Frobenius Reciprocity

Building Representations
Induced Representations — From Subgroups to the Whole Group
If H ≤ G and W is an H-representation, the induced representation Ind_H^G W = ℂ[G] ⊗_{ℂ[H]} W is a G-representation. It's the universal way to extend an H-rep to G. Frobenius reciprocity: Hom_G(Ind_H^G W, V) ≅ Hom_H(W, Res_H^G V). Induction and restriction are adjoint functors. The Mackey formula describes how induction and restriction compose: Res_K^G Ind_H^G ≅ ⊕_{KgH} Ind_{K∩gHg^{-1}}^K Ad_g Res_{g^{-1}Kg∩H}^H. Mackey's irreducibility criterion tells when an induced representation is irreducible. Clifford's theorem describes the structure of representations of a group restricted to a normal subgroup — they break into orbits under conjugation.
Induction builds global representations from local data — the representation theory of a group is determined by its subgroups.
Part 3

🔬 Lie Groups and Their Representations

Continuous Symmetry
Representations of Compact Lie Groups — The Highest Weight Classification
For a compact Lie group G, every continuous representation is unitarizable and completely reducible (Weyl). The Peter-Weyl theorem: L²(G) ≅ ⊕_π (V_π ⊗ V_π^*) where π runs over irreducible representations — a non-commutative Fourier transform. Irreducible representations of compact Lie groups are classified by highest weights (Cartan-Weyl theory). Pick a maximal torus T ≅ U(1)^r; the root system tells how the Lie algebra decomposes under T. The Weyl character formula gives characters of irreps as alternating sums over the Weyl group. For SU(2), irreps are V_n (spin n/2) of dimension n+1. The Borel-Weil-Bott theorem realizes irreps as cohomology groups of line bundles on the flag variety G/B — a geometric construction. Tensor products decompose via the Clebsch-Gordan (SU(2)) or Littlewood-Richardson (GL(n)) rules.
Highest weight theory classifies all symmetries of the continuous world — from SU(2) spin to the exceptional groups E₈.
Part 4

🔗 Quivers, Category O, and Geometric Representation Theory

Modern Directions
Quiver Representations, BGG Category O, and Springer Theory
A quiver is a directed graph. A quiver representation assigns a vector space to each vertex and a linear map to each arrow. Gabriel's theorem: a quiver has finite representation type iff its underlying graph is a Dynkin diagram (A_n, D_n, E₆, E₇, E₈). Representations of the preprojective algebra of a Dynkin quiver correspond to the nilpotent cone. The BGG category O (Bernstein-Gelfand-Gelfand) is the category of representations of a semisimple Lie algebra with certain finiteness conditions — it has a highest weight theory, Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials, and Koszul duality. Geometric representation theory studies representations via varieties: the Springer resolution T*(G/B) → N gives a geometric construction of Weyl group representations on the cohomology of Springer fibers. The Lusztig conjecture (now theorem) expresses characters of simple modules in positive characteristic via Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials, connecting to the Kac-Moody and affine Lie algebra representations.
Modern representation theory is a vast web connecting algebra, geometry, and combinatorics — from quivers to Springer theory to the geometric Langlands program.
