Sentence Improvement

Updated 7 Nov 2026
Choose the best replacement for a underlined part without changing the meaning.
Sentence Improvement · Replacement · Usage

Learning Objectives

  • Understand the fundamental concepts of Sentence Improvement
  • Apply key formulas and techniques to solve problems
  • Practice with exam-level questions to build speed and accuracy

Key Concepts

What the Question Asks

A sentence-improvement question gives a sentence with one underlined part and four replacements. You must choose the replacement that makes the sentence grammatically correct and idiomatic without changing its meaning. The original is sometimes already correct; an option that repeats it is then the answer. Read the whole sentence before looking at the options, because the error often depends on a word outside the underlined part.

What to Check, in Order

  • Subject-verb agreement — find the real subject and match the verb.
  • Tense and sequence — check time markers and the tense of the other clauses.
  • Pronoun reference — a pronoun must have one clear antecedent and match it in number.
  • Parallelism — items joined by and, or, not only ... but also must have the same grammatical form.
  • Modifiers — a modifier should sit next to the word it describes; avoid dangling participles.
  • Prepositions and articles — check fixed collocations.
  • Redundancy — remove repeated ideas such as return back, discuss about, more better.

Common Replacements

One of my friend becomes one of my friends. He is senior than me becomes he is senior to me. She prefers coffee than tea becomes she prefers coffee to tea. I am used to get up early becomes I am used to getting up early. Hardly had I reached when it started raining is correct; hardly ... than is wrong. Neither of the boys were present becomes neither of the boys was present in a strict exam. The scenery are beautiful becomes the scenery is beautiful.

Example 1
He is one of the best player in the team.

a) one of the best players b) one of best player c) one of the better player d) one best player
Solution: a) One of the best players. One of takes a plural noun.
Example 2
She prefers reading than watching television.

a) than to watch b) to watching c) than watch d) to watch
Solution: b) To watching. The pair is prefer X to Y, and both X and Y should be gerunds here.
Example 3
The teacher asked the students to discuss about the topic.

a) discuss about b) discuss on c) discuss d) discuss over
Solution: c) Discuss. Discuss is transitive and takes no preposition.
Exam tip
If two options are grammatically possible, choose the shorter one unless the sentence needs the extra word for meaning. Exam setters often include a wordy but correct option and a concise correct option; the concise one is usually the key.

Modifiers and Parallelism

A modifier must sit next to the word it describes. Walking down the road, I saw a tower is correct because I was walking; Walking down the road, the tower came into view is wrong because the tower was not walking. Fix a dangling participle by naming the doer in the main clause or by turning the participle into a full clause.

Parallelism means items joined by and, or, but, not only ... but also or either ... or must have the same grammatical form. She likes reading, writing and to paint should become She likes reading, writing and painting. When a comparison uses than or as, compare like with like: The population of Mumbai is larger than that of Pune, not larger than Pune.

Redundancy and idiom are the last checks. Cut return back, discuss about, more better, most unique, the reason is because, and each and every when one word is enough. Then confirm the fixed collocation: comply with, differ from, capable of, responsible for. If the underlined part is already correct, the option that repeats it is the answer.
