Key Concepts

Para Jumbles & Sentence Rearrangement

Para jumbles present 4–6 sentences in random order; you must rebuild the original paragraph. The logic is in the connectors, pronouns, chronology, and the flow of ideas.

  • Opening sentence: Usually a general statement, introduction of a topic/name, or a definition — not a pronoun reference, not a continuation (hence, therefore, but, so), not an example without a preceding idea.
  • Pronoun chain: He, she, it, they, this, that, these, those refer to a noun introduced earlier — the sentence with the noun comes before the sentence with the pronoun.
  • Chronology: Time markers (before, after, then, later, finally, first, second, yesterday, 2010, now) establish sequence.
  • Connectors/transitions: however, therefore, moreover, consequently, hence, otherwise, although, while, whereas signal logical links (contrast, cause-effect, addition).
  • Definite article 'the': Usually refers to something already mentioned — 'a/an' introduces, 'the' continues.
  • Abbreviations/acronyms: Full form first (World Health Organization), abbreviated form later (WHO).
  • Examples/illustrations: For example, for instance, such as, like — follow the general statement they illustrate.
  • Contrast pairs: On one hand…on the other hand; although…yet; not only…but also — keep paired sentences together.
  • Cause-effect: because, since, as, therefore, hence, thus, so, consequently — cause sentence precedes effect sentence.
  • Question-answer / statement-elaboration: A general claim is followed by explanation, detail, or example.

Step-by-Step Method

  1. Read all sentences and identify the topic.
  2. Find the opening sentence (general introduction, no references backward).
  3. Link sentences using pronouns, articles, connectors, chronology.
  4. Form pairs/triplets (cause-effect, contrast, example-elaboration).
  5. Arrange pairs into a sequence and check flow.
  6. Verify the last sentence sounds like a conclusion (finally, in conclusion, thus, ultimately).
Technique: Pronoun chain
Sentences: (P) He was very happy. (Q) Rahul bought a new bicycle. (R) He rode it to school. (S) It was a gift from his uncle.
Answer: Q introduces Rahul and bicycle → P 'He' = Rahul → S 'It' = bicycle → R 'He/it' continue. Order: Q P S R (or Q S P R depending on intended flow; pronoun chain Q→P is strongest).
Technique: Cause-effect
Sentences: (A) Therefore, he missed the train. (B) He reached the station late. (C) His car broke down.
Answer: C (cause) → B (intermediate) → A (effect/conclusion). Order: C B A.

Common Traps

  • Starting with a sentence beginning with 'However', 'Therefore', 'Moreover', 'But', 'So' — these are rarely openers.
  • Placing a pronoun sentence before its antecedent noun.
  • Placing an example before the general statement it illustrates.
  • Ignoring time markers and jumbling chronology.
  • Splitting obvious pairs (on one hand…on the other hand).

Memory Tricks

  • OPEN: Opening sentence = general, no backward references, no pronouns, no transitions.
  • PANT: Pronoun → Antecedent; Article 'a' → 'the'; Example after statement; Time order.
  • FADE: First introduce, then elaborate, then example, then conclude.
Para jumble exam strategy
Find one certain pair first (pronoun-noun or cause-effect), build around it, then use answer options to verify — you often don't need the full sequence, just enough to pick the right option.
