Learning Objectives

Key Concepts

Articles (a, an, the)

  • Indefinite article 'a/an': Used before singular countable nouns when mentioning something for the first time or when the noun is not specific. 'a' before consonant sound (a boy, a university — 'u' sounds like 'yu'), 'an' before vowel sound (an apple, an hour — 'h' silent, vowel sound). Choice depends on sound, not spelling letter.
  • Definite article 'the': Used before a particular person/thing already mentioned or known (the book I bought), unique things (the sun, the moon, the earth), superlatives (the best, the tallest), musical instruments (the piano), rivers/seas/ranges (the Ganga, the Indian Ocean, the Himalayas), newspapers (the Hindu), holy books (the Gita), and nationalities (the Indians).
  • Omission of articles: No article before proper nouns (Delhi, John), abstract nouns used generally (Honesty is best policy), material nouns (Gold is precious), languages (Hindi is easy), meals (breakfast, lunch, dinner), bed/church/college/hospital/prison/school when used for primary purpose (go to school = study; go to the school = visit building), plural countable nouns used generally (Cars are expensive).
  • 'A/an' vs 'The': 'A man and a woman were walking. The man was tall.' (first mention = a/an, second = the).

Prepositions

  • Time: at (exact point — at 5 o'clock, at noon, at night), in (period — in May, in 2024, in the morning), on (day/date — on Monday, on 15th August, on Christmas day). 'In' for longer periods, 'on' for specific days, 'at' for exact points.
  • Place: at (point — at the door, at the station), in (enclosed space — in the room, in Delhi, in the box), on (surface — on the table, on the wall, on the floor).
  • Movement/direction: to (towards — go to school), into (enter — go into the room), onto (on top — jump onto the bed), towards (direction — walk towards the market), through (pass from one side to other — walk through the tunnel), across (from one side to other — swim across the river), along (follow length — walk along the road), against (touching and opposing — lean against the wall), among (more than two — divide among three), between (two — divide between Ram and Shyam).
  • Instrument/agent: by (agent — killed by a thief; mode — travel by bus), with (instrument — cut with a knife; accompaniment — go with a friend), in (manner — write in ink), on (basis — depend on).
  • Other common prepositions: about (concerning — talk about politics), above (higher than — above the roof), below (lower than — below the poverty line), over (covering — a roof over the head), under (below — under the table), before (earlier than — before 5 pm), after (later than — after lunch), since (point in past — since Monday), for (duration — for 3 hours), from (starting point — from Monday to Friday), till/until (up to — wait till 6), during (within a period — during the holidays), within (before end — within a week), without (lacking — without water), unlike (different from — unlike his brother), like (similar to — like his father), beside (next to — sit beside me), besides (in addition to — besides English, she knows French), except (excluding — all except John), including (including — including tax).

Important Preposition Pairs/Rules

  • In vs On vs At (place): at (point) < in (enclosed) — I live at No. 5, in Delhi, in India.
  • In vs On vs At (time): at (exact) < on (day) < in (month/year) — at 5 pm, on Monday, in January.
  • Between vs Among: between = two (between you and me), among = more than two (distribute among the poor).
  • Since vs For: since = point (since 2010), for = duration (for 10 years). Both used with perfect tenses.
  • By vs With: by = agent/doer (killed by a thief), with = instrument (cut with a knife).
  • Beside vs Besides: beside = next to (sit beside me), besides = in addition to (besides English, she knows Hindi).
  • From vs Since: from = starting point (works from 9 to 5), since = point in past continued to present (works here since 2010 — with perfect tense).
  • Into vs In: into = movement entering (jump into the well), in = position (swim in the pool).
  • On vs Upon: upon is more formal/literary (once upon a time; upon reflection).
  • Above vs Over: above = higher than, no contact (flags above the building); over = covering/moving across (a roof over us, flew over the city).
  • Below vs Under: below = lower than, no contact (below the poverty line); under = directly below/contact (under the table).
  • Between vs From...to: between two points (between Delhi and Mumbai), from...to (from Monday to Friday).

Conjunctions

  • Coordinating conjunctions: join equal elements — and, but, or, nor, for, so, yet (FANBOYS).
  • Subordinating conjunctions: join dependent clause — because, although, though, since, if, unless, until, while, when, before, after, as, as if, as though, lest, so that.
  • Correlative conjunctions (pairs): either...or, neither...nor, both...and, not only...but also, whether...or, scarcely...when, no sooner...than, rather...than.
  • Important rules:
    • No sooner...than (No sooner did he arrive than it started raining — inversion).
    • Scarcely/Hardly...when (Scarcely had he reached when it rained — inversion, past perfect).
    • Lest = that...not, followed by should (Walk carefully lest you should fall).
    • Unless = if not (Unless you work hard, you will fail — no 'not' with unless).
    • Both...and takes plural verb (Both Ram and Shyam are present).
    • Not only...but also — verb agrees with nearer subject (Not only the teacher but also the students were present).
Technique: Article choice
Question: "___ honest man is respected by all." Choose article.
Answer: 'honest' starts with silent 'h' but vowel sound /ɒn/ → use 'an'. "An honest man is respected by all."
Technique: Since vs For
Question: "She has been living here ___ 2015." Choose preposition.
Answer: 2015 is a point in time → 'since'. "She has been living here since 2015."

Common Traps

  • Using 'a/an' before uncountable nouns (a milk, a furniture — wrong; milk is uncountable).
  • Using 'the' before proper nouns (the Delhi — wrong; Delhi is correct).
  • Confusing 'in' and 'at' for time/place.
  • Using 'between' for more than two people/things.
  • Using 'unless' with 'not' (unless you don't work — wrong; unless you work — correct).
  • Using 'the' before names of meals used generally (I take the breakfast — wrong; I take breakfast — correct).

Memory Tricks

  • Articles: a/an = first mention, general; the = specific, unique, second mention.
  • Sound rule: 'a university' (consonant sound), 'an hour' (vowel sound) — depends on sound, not letter.
  • Preposition place/time: at → on → in (smallest → largest unit).
  • Between vs Among: Between = 2; Among = 3+.
Articles/prepositions exam strategy
In error spotting, check articles before countable nouns, preposition choice (since/for, in/on/at), and 'unless' without 'not'. Read the sentence for meaning — wrong prepositions often change meaning entirely.
