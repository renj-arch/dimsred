Key Concepts

The Twelve Tenses

English has three main times (present, past, future) × four aspects (simple, continuous, perfect, perfect continuous) = twelve tenses. Each expresses when an action happens and whether it is complete or ongoing.

TenseFormulaUseExample
Present SimpleV1 (+s/es)Habit, fact, universal truthShe works here. The sun rises in the east.
Present Continuousam/is/are + V1+ingAction happening now, around nowShe is working now.
Present Perfecthas/have + V3Past action with present result, unfinished timeShe has worked here since 2020.
Present Perfect Cont.has/have been + V1+ingAction started in past, still continuingShe has been working here for 3 years.
Past SimpleV2Completed action in pastShe worked here last year.
Past Continuouswas/were + V1+ingOngoing action at a past momentShe was working when I called.
Past Perfecthad + V3Earlier of two past actionsShe had left before I arrived.
Past Perfect Cont.had been + V1+ingOngoing action before another past actionShe had been working for 2 hours when I called.
Future Simplewill/shall + V1Future fact, prediction, decisionShe will join tomorrow.
Future Continuouswill be + V1+ingAction ongoing at a future timeShe will be working then.
Future Perfectwill have + V3Action complete before future timeShe will have finished by 5 pm.
Future Perfect Cont.will have been + V1+ingDuration of action up to future pointShe will have been working for 5 years by 2026.

Key Verbs: V1, V2, V3

  • V1 (base): go, write, eat, see, take, give, know, come, do, make.
  • V2 (past): went, wrote, ate, saw, took, gave, knew, came, did, made.
  • V3 (past participle): gone, written, eaten, seen, taken, given, known, come, done, made.
  • Regular verbs: add -ed (walk → walked, play → played).
  • Irregular verbs: must be memorised (go-went-gone, see-saw-seen).

Signal Words

  • Present Simple: always, usually, often, sometimes, never, every day/week, generally.
  • Present Continuous: now, at present, at the moment, look!, listen!, today (with now).
  • Present Perfect: already, yet, just, ever, never, since, for, today, this week (unfinished).
  • Past Simple: yesterday, last night/week/month/year, ago, in 1999, once, then.
  • Past Perfect: already, before, by the time, when (earlier action), after (earlier action).
  • Future Simple: tomorrow, next week/month/year, soon, in 2026, probably.

Important Rules

  • Since vs For: since = point of time (since 2020, since Monday); for = period of time (for 3 years, for two hours). Used with perfect tenses.
  • Past Perfect for earlier action: When two past actions are mentioned, the earlier one takes Past Perfect (had + V3), the later one Past Simple. "The train had left before I reached the station."
  • No future tense in time/condition clauses: "If it rains, I will stay" (not "If it will rain"). "When he comes, we will start."
  • Present Perfect not used with definite past time: "I have seen him yesterday" (wrong) → "I saw him yesterday" (right).
  • Stative verbs usually not continuous: know, believe, love, hate, want, need, seem, understand — use simple, not continuous ("I know her," not "I am knowing her").
Technique: Choosing the tense
Question: "She ___ (work) here since 2020." Choose correct form.
Answer: "since 2020" = unfinished time starting in past → Present Perfect or Present Perfect Continuous. If focus on result: "has worked." If focus on duration: "has been working." Both acceptable depending on emphasis.
Technique: Earlier of two past actions
Question: "I ___ (reach) the station after the train ___ (leave)."
Answer: Two past actions — train left earlier (Past Perfect: had left), I reached later (Past Simple: reached). "I reached the station after the train had left."

Common Traps

  • Using Present Perfect with "yesterday/last year/ago" — wrong.
  • Using "will" in "if/when" clauses — wrong.
  • Confusing Past Simple and Past Perfect when only one past action is mentioned — use Simple, not Perfect.
  • Using continuous with stative verbs (know, believe, want) — generally wrong.
  • Subject-verb agreement with Present Simple third person singular (he/she/it + s/es).

Memory Tricks

  • 12 tenses: 3 times × 4 aspects. Perfect = completed effect; Continuous = ongoing.
  • Since = point; For = period.
  • V2 = finished past; had + V3 = earlier past.
  • will + V1 = future simple (decision/prediction).
Tense exam strategy
In error spotting, check signal words first (yesterday → past tense; since/for → perfect; every day → present simple). In sentence improvement, choose the tense that matches the time word. Don't overthink — most tense errors are basic form mistakes.
