Key Concepts

Subject-Verb Agreement (Concord)

Subject-verb agreement means the verb must match its subject in number (singular/plural) and person (first, second, third). This is one of the most-tested grammar rules in competitive exams.

  • Basic rule: Singular subject takes singular verb; plural subject takes plural verb. He goes (singular), They go (plural).
  • Person: First person (I/we), second person (you), third person (he/she/it/they). Third person singular takes -s/-es in present simple (He works, She has, It goes).
  • Two or more subjects joined by 'and': Usually plural verb (Ram and Shyam are friends). Exception: if the two subjects refer to the same person/thing or a single idea, use singular (Bread and butter is my breakfast; The horse and carriage is waiting).
  • Subjects joined by 'or', 'either...or', 'neither...nor', 'not only...but also': Verb agrees with the subject nearer to it (Proximity rule). Neither the teacher nor the students were present (students → were). Neither the students nor the teacher was present (teacher → was).
  • Collective nouns: Singular when the group acts as one unit (The committee has decided); plural when members act individually (The committee are divided in their opinions).
  • Indefinite pronouns: Everyone, everybody, anyone, anybody, someone, somebody, no one, nobody, each, either, neither — take singular verb (Everyone is present; Neither of the boys is guilty).
  • 'Each' and 'every': Before plural subject → singular verb (Each of the students has a book; Every boy and girl was rewarded).
  • Subjects separated from verb by phrases: Verb agrees with the real subject, not the noun in the phrase. The box of chocolates is expensive (box → is). The quality of the mangoes is good (quality → is).
  • Plural-form nouns with singular meaning: News, physics, mathematics, economics, politics, measles, civics, ethics — usually singular (The news is true; Mathematics is difficult). Some exceptions: economics/politics can be plural when referring to separate aspects (The economics of the project are uncertain).
  • Plural nouns always plural: Scissors, trousers, pants, spectacles, jeans, goods, clothes, alms, premises, thanks, regards — take plural verb (The scissors are sharp; His clothes are dirty).
  • 'A number of' vs 'The number of': A number of = several → plural verb (A number of students are absent). The number of = the total → singular verb (The number of students is 50).
  • Most, some, all, any, none, half, part, rest: Verb depends on the noun after 'of'. Most of the work is done; Most of the students are present. None can be singular or plural depending on meaning (None of the students was/were present — both acceptable).
  • There + be: Verb agrees with the real subject after 'be'. There is a book on the table; There are two books on the table.
  • More than one: Followed by singular noun and verb (More than one student was absent).
  • Many a: Followed by singular noun and verb (Many a student has failed).
  • Either/Neither as subject (alone): Singular (Either is acceptable; Neither of them knows the answer — strictly singular, though 'know' is common in informal use).
  • Relative pronoun 'who/which/that': Verb agrees with its antecedent. This is one of the best books that have been written (books → have). This is the only book that has been written (book → has).
  • Inverted sentences (starting with here/there): Verb agrees with the real subject. Here comes the bus; Here come the buses.
Technique: Proximity rule
Question: "Neither the principal nor the teachers ___ present." Choose correct verb.
Answer: Subjects joined by 'nor' — verb agrees with nearer subject (teachers, plural) → "were". "Neither the principal nor the teachers were present."
Technique: Real subject vs intervening phrase
Question: "The quality of the mangoes ___ good." Choose correct verb.
Answer: Real subject is 'quality' (singular), not 'mangoes'. The phrase 'of the mangoes' does not affect verb → "is". "The quality of the mangoes is good."

Common Traps

  • The verb agreeing with the nearest noun instead of the real subject.
  • Treating collective nouns as always plural — context matters (unit vs individual members).
  • Treating 'none' as always plural — it can be singular or plural.
  • Using plural verb with 'Each of', 'Either of', 'Neither of' — these take singular verb.
  • Confusing plural-form singular-meaning nouns (news, physics, measles) with plural nouns.

Memory Tricks

  • SVA checklist: Find real subject → check number → match verb → ignore intervening phrases.
  • Indefinite pronouns = singular: Everyone, anybody, someone, nobody, each, either, neither.
  • Always plural nouns: Scissors, trousers, spectacles, jeans, goods, clothes, premises, thanks.
  • Number of: A number of → plural; The number of → singular.
SVA exam strategy
First strip away phrases between subject and verb to find the real subject. Then check if the subject is singular or plural. Watch for collective nouns, indefinite pronouns, and 'number of' phrases — these are the most common exam traps.
