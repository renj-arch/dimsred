One-Word Substitutions

Learning Objectives

  • Understand the fundamental concepts of One-Word Substitutions
  • Apply key formulas and techniques to solve problems
  • Practice with exam-level questions to build speed and accuracy

Key Concepts

What the Question Looks Like

A one-word substitution replaces a whole phrase with a single word. The stem gives a definition and the options are four words; the trap is a word that shares a root but has a different meaning. Learn the word with its exact definition, and group the words by category so that recall is faster in the exam hall.

More Exam Favourites

Extempore — a speech without preparation. Obituary — a notice of someone's death. Illegible — impossible to read. Invisible — impossible to see. Invincible — impossible to defeat. Inaudible — impossible to hear. Incorrigible — impossible to correct. Indispensable — impossible to do without. Inevitable — impossible to avoid. Omnipotent — all-powerful. Omnipresent — present everywhere. Omniscient — all-knowing. Anarchy — absence of government. Autocracy — government by one person. Bureaucracy — government by officials. Democracy — government by the people. Plutocracy — government by the wealthy. Theocracy — government by religious authority.

Example 1
One who is unable to pay his debts is called:

a) bankrupt b) miser c) creditor d) debtor
Solution: a) Bankrupt. A debtor owes money; a bankrupt is legally unable to pay debts. A creditor is owed money.
Example 2
A place where bees are kept is an:

a) aviary b) apiary c) aquarium d) arsenal
Solution: b) Apiary. An aviary keeps birds, an aquarium keeps fish and an arsenal stores weapons.
Example 3
A person who speaks without preparation gives an:

a) oration b) extempore c) elegy d) epilogue
Solution: b) Extempore. An oration is a formal speech, an elegy is a mournful poem and an epilogue is the closing part of a work.
Exam tip
Learn roots: bene good, male bad, omni all, phil love, phob fear, logy study. A root turns one memorised word into a family of words and makes elimination much faster.

Government, Law and Money

One-word questions in banking and civil-services papers draw on administration and law: amnesty (general pardon), armistice (agreement to stop fighting), referendum (direct vote on one issue), impeachment (charge of misconduct against a public official), quorum (minimum members needed for a meeting), mandate (authority given by voters), tariff (tax on imports), subsidy (government payment to lower a price), deficit (shortfall between income and spending), audit (official inspection of accounts).

Words about words and writing are also frequent: autobiography (life written by oneself), biography (life written by another), epitaph (words on a tomb), epilogue (closing section of a book), prologue (opening section), monologue (a long speech by one person), soliloquy (a speech to oneself on stage), manuscript (handwritten document), anecdote (a short amusing story), plagiarism (using another's work as one's own).

Build the answer by eliminating on the root. If the stem says "a person who studies insects", -ology words are the candidates and entomology is the answer; if it says "a place where bees are kept", the -ary place words are the candidates and apiary is the answer. Root families reduce four options to one much faster than memorising a list.
