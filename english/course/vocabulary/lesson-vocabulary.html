Vocabulary Building — Roots, Prefixes, Suffixes

Building vocabulary through word parts (roots, prefixes, suffixes) lets you decode unfamiliar words instead of memorizing them one by one. This is the most efficient way to handle synonyms, antonyms, and one-word substitutions in exams.

  • Suffixes (added to back — change part of speech/meaning):
    • -able/-ible = can be (readable, credible)
    • -al = relating to (national, seasonal)
    • -ance/-ence = state/quality (assistance, patience)
    • -ant/-ent = one who/that (assistant, student)
    • -ary = relating to (library, ordinary)
    • -ate = to make (activate, create)
    • -dom = state/realm (freedom, kingdom)
    • -ee = one who (employee, trainee)
    • -en = to make (weaken, shorten)
    • -er/-or = one who (teacher, actor)
    • -ful = full of (beautiful, careful)
    • -ible/-able = can be (flexible, reasonable)
    • -ic = relating to (scenic, historic)
    • -ing = action/process (reading, building)
    • -ion/-tion/-sion = state/result (education, expression)
    • -ish = like (childish, foolish)
    • -ism = doctrine/practice (capitalism, optimism)
    • -ist = one who (artist, scientist)
    • -ity/-ty = state/quality (society, reality)
    • -ive = having nature of (active, creative)
    • -less = without (careless, hopeless)
    • -like = like (childlike, lifelike)
    • -ly = in manner of (quickly, friendly)
    • -ment = state/result (development, agreement)
    • -ness = state (kindness, darkness)
    • -ology = study of (biology, psychology)
    • -ous/-ious = full of (famous, curious)
    • -ship = state (friendship, leadership)
    • -ty = state (unity, purity)
    • -ure = act/process (closure, failure)
    • -ward = direction (forward, backward)
    • -y = full of (rainy, sunny)
  • Word-building method: root + prefix + suffix = new word (re + struct + ion = reconstruction; de + struct + ion = destruction; un + kind + ly = unkindly).
  • Exam use: Break unfamiliar synonym/antonym/one-word-substitution words into parts; eliminate options whose root meaning contradicts the stem.
Technique: Root-based elimination
Question: Synonym of 'benevolent'.
Answer: bene = good, vol = will → 'benevolent' = wishing well, kind. Choose 'kind/charitable', not 'cruel'.
Technique: Suffix part-of-speech
Question: '-tion' words are usually what part of speech?
Answer: '-tion' forms nouns (education, action, creation). If options include verbs/adjectives, eliminate them.

Common Traps

  • Assuming similar-looking words share a root (e.g., 'dessert' vs 'desert' — different roots).
  • Over-reliance on roots without checking actual meaning (some roots shift over time).
  • Confusing '-able' (can be done) with '-ible' (able to be) — usage is conventional, not rule-based.
  • Thinking all '-ly' words are adverbs (friendly, lonely, costly are adjectives).

Memory Tricks

  • Root sentence: "The spectator inspected the prospectus and respected the inspector." (spect = see)
  • Prefix contrast: in = not/in; inter = between; intra = within; trans = across.
  • Suffix function: -ment/-tion/-ness = nouns; -ful/-ous/-ive = adjectives; -ly = adverb (usually); -ize/-ify = verbs.
Vocabulary exam strategy
Learn 10–15 roots/prefixes/suffixes per week; they unlock hundreds of words. For synonyms/antonyms, use root meaning to eliminate 2–3 options even if you don't know the exact word. Maintain a personal word-list with sentences, not just definitions.
