Key Concepts

Phrasal Verbs

A phrasal verb = verb + particle (adverb/preposition), with a meaning often different from the verb alone (give up = quit, not literally 'give'). Exams test meaning, usage, separability, and particles.

  • Structure: verb + adverb particle (look up, give up) OR verb + preposition (look after, depend on). Some have both: look into, look up to.
  • Separable vs inseparable:
    • Separable with noun object: turn off the TV / turn the TV off (both OK).
    • Separable with pronoun object ALWAYS between: turn it off (not turn off it).
    • Inseparable: look after the children / look after them (not look them after).
  • Three-part phrasal verbs: verb + adverb + preposition (get on with, look up to, put up with, catch up with, come up with) — inseparable.
  • Meaning often idiomatic: give up = quit; put off = postpone; put up with = tolerate; run out of = exhaust supply; come across = find by chance.

Common Traps

  • Pronoun object goes BETWEEN verb and particle (turn IT off, not turn off IT).
  • Three-part phrasals are inseparable (put up WITH it, not put it up with).
  • Same particles change meaning completely (give up vs give in vs give out vs give away).
  • Literal meaning traps ('look up' = search info, not physically look upward).

Memory Tricks

  • Particle meanings: up = complete/increase; off = stop/separation; out = exhaustion/disappearance; over = review/completion; back = return; down = reduction/defeat.
  • Personal sentences: Write one sentence per phrasal verb.
  • Theme clusters: Learn 10–15 phrasal verbs per theme per week.
Phrasal verb exam strategy
Identify the verb + particle in the stem, then match meaning to context. For pronoun-object questions, check separability first. Eliminate literal-meaning options.
