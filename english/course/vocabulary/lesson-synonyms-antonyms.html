Key Concepts

Synonyms & Antonyms

Synonym-antonym questions test direct word knowledge and fine distinctions between near-synonyms. Exams repeat high-frequency word pairs and use near-miss options to trap careless readers.

  • Synonym: Word with same or nearly same meaning (happy → joyful, glad, pleased). Context matters — 'big' vs 'large' vs 'huge' vs 'enormous' differ in shade and collocation.
  • Antonym: Word with opposite meaning (happy → sad, unhappy, miserable, gloomy). Antonyms may be gradable (hot → cold) or complementary (dead → alive, no middle).
  • Near-miss options: Words related in topic but not exact synonyms (e.g., for 'cautious', options might include 'brave', 'fearful', 'careful' — 'careful' is closest but 'cautious' implies wariness of risk).
  • Context clue: The sentence around the target word shows the intended sense ('bright student' = intelligent; 'bright light' = shining; 'bright colors' = vivid).
  • Elimination: Remove options with opposite or unrelated meaning, then pick the closest shade among remaining.
  • Root/prefix help: Use word parts to guess (e.g., 'benevolent' = well-wishing → kind; 'malevolent' = ill-wishing → cruel).

Common Traps

  • Choosing a related-word option instead of exact synonym (e.g., 'brave' for 'cautious').
  • Ignoring collocation ('big house' OK, 'large house' OK, 'great house' = important, not size).
  • Missing negative prefixes (un-, in-, dis-) as quick antonym generators.
  • Confusing gradable antonyms (good → bad) with complementary (alive → dead).
  • Picking an option that's a hyponym (specific type) rather than true synonym.

Memory Tricks

  • Prefix antonyms: happy/unhappy, able/disable, appear/disappear, agree/disagree, honest/dishonest.
  • Root pairs: bene (good) → male (bad): benevolent/malevolent; construct/destruct; include/exclude.
  • Personal word bank: Keep 5–10 new pairs per week with example sentences.
Synonym-antonym exam strategy
Read the stem word with its context. Eliminate clearly unrelated/opposite options first. Among close options, pick the one matching the stem's shade and register (formal vs informal). Use root meaning to guess unfamiliar options.
