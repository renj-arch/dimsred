Hodge Theory — Hodge Decomposition, Mixed Hodge Structures & the Hard Lefschetz Theorem

Hodge theory is the study of harmonic forms on Riemannian and Kähler manifolds, providing a deep link between analysis, topology, and algebraic geometry. On a compact Riemannian manifold, the Hodge theorem states that every de Rham cohomology class has a unique harmonic representative, giving an isomorphism Hᵏ_dR(M, ℝ) ≅ ℋᵏ(M). On a Kähler manifold, the complex structure interacts with the metric to produce the Hodge decomposition Hᵏ(M, ℂ) = ⊕_{p+q=k} H^{p,q}(M), refining the cohomology by type. The Hard Lefschetz theorem gives an isomorphism between cohomology groups via cup product with powers of the Kähler form. Deligne's Mixed Hodge Structures extend these ideas to singular and non-compact varieties, providing a powerful tool for understanding the topology of algebraic varieties. The Hodge conjecture — one of the Clay Millennium Problems — asks whether every Hodge class is algebraic.

"Hodge theory is the bridge between topology and analysis on manifolds."

Part 1

📏 The Hodge Theorem on Riemannian Manifolds

Harmonic Theory
The Hodge Star, the Laplacian, and the Hodge Theorem
On an oriented Riemannian n-manifold (M, g), the Hodge star operator ⋆: Ωᵏ(M) → Ω^{n−k}(M) is defined by α ∧ ⋆β = g(α, β) dvol. The L² inner product on k-forms is ⟨α,β⟩ = ∫_M α ∧ ⋆β. The codifferential δ: Ωᵏ → Ω^{k−1} is the formal adjoint of d: δ = (−1)^{n(k+1)+1} ⋆ d ⋆. The Hodge Laplacian Δ = dδ + δd. A form α is harmonic if Δα = 0; denote the space of harmonic k-forms by ℋᵏ(M). The Hodge theorem: dim ℋᵏ(M) < ∞, and every de Rham cohomology class has a unique harmonic representative. This yields the orthogonal decomposition Ωᵏ(M) = ℋᵏ(M) ⊕ dΩ^{k−1}(M) ⊕ δΩ^{k+1}(M) and the isomorphism Hᵏ_{dR}(M, ℝ) ≅ ℋᵏ(M). The theory extends to elliptic complexes via general elliptic operator theory on compact manifolds, culminating in the Atiyah-Singer index theorem: the analytic index of an elliptic operator equals its topological index.
The Hodge theorem bridges analysis and topology: harmonic forms are the unique minimizers of the Dirichlet energy in each cohomology class, giving a canonical representative.
Part 2

🌍 Hodge Theory on Kähler Manifolds

Kähler
Kähler Identities, Hodge Decomposition, and Hard Lefschetz
On a Kähler manifold (M, g, J, ω), the complex structure J and the Riemannian metric g are compatible, and the Kähler form ω(·,·) = g(·, J·) is closed (dω = 0). The Dolbeault operators ∂: Ω^{p,q} → Ω^{p+1,q} and ∂̄: Ω^{p,q} → Ω^{p,q+1} satisfy d = ∂ + ∂̄. The Kähler identities relate the Lefschetz operator L(α) = ω∧α and its adjoint Λ: [Λ, ∂] = i∂̄^* and [Λ, ∂̄] = −i∂^*. These imply the fundamental equality of Laplacians: Δ_d = 2Δ_∂ = 2Δ_∂̄. The Hodge decomposition for compact Kähler manifolds: Hᵏ(M, ℂ) = ⊕_{p+q=k} H^{p,q}(M), where H^{p,q} ≅ H^q(M, Ω^p_M) is the Dolbeault cohomology. The Hodge numbers h^{p,q} = dim H^{p,q} satisfy: h^{p,q} = h^{q,p} (conjugation symmetry) and h^{p,q} = h^{n−p,n−q} (Serre duality). The Hard Lefschetz theorem: the map L^{n−k}: Hᵏ(M, ℂ) → H^{2n−k}(M, ℂ) given by cup product with ω^{n−k} is an isomorphism for all k. The primitive decomposition: every harmonic form decomposes uniquely into primitive forms, giving the Lefschetz decomposition of the cohomology.
On Kähler manifolds, Riemannian, symplectic, and complex structures harmonize, producing the refined Hodge decomposition and powerful constraints on topology.
Part 3

🧮 Mixed Hodge Structures

Deligne
Pure and Mixed Hodge Structures, Deligne's Theorem
A pure Hodge structure of weight k consists of a ℚ-vector space V with a decomposition V⊗ℂ = ⊕_{p+q=k} V^{p,q} such that V^{p,q} = \bar{V}^{q,p} (complex conjugation exchanges the summands). A mixed Hodge structure (MHS) is a ℚ-vector space V equipped with an increasing weight filtration W_•: 0 ⊂ W_0 ⊂ W_1 ⊂ … ⊂ W_{2k} = V and a decreasing Hodge filtration F^•: V⊗ℂ ⊃ … ⊃ F^p ⊃ F^{p+1} ⊃ … such that on each graded piece Gr^W_k V = W_k/W_{k−1}, the filtration F^• induces a pure Hodge structure of weight k. Deligne's theorem (1971-74): the cohomology Hᵏ(X, ℚ) of any algebraic variety X over ℂ carries a canonical mixed Hodge structure, functorial for algebraic maps. For a smooth projective variety, the MHS is pure (the weight filtration is trivial: W_k = 0 for k < m and W_k = Hᵏ(X, ℚ) for k ≥ m). For a singular variety, the weight filtration encodes the cohomology of a desingularization. For a complement X\D of a divisor with normal crossings, the MHS captures the logarithmic forms. The MHS also exists on the fundamental group (the Malcev completion) and on nearby cycle cohomology.
Mixed Hodge structures are a powerful generalization of classical Hodge theory to all algebraic varieties, providing a systematic framework for understanding their cohomology.
Part 4

🔥 Hodge Polynomials and the Hodge Conjecture

Conjecture
Hodge Polynomials, the Hodge Conjecture, and Related Problems
The Hodge polynomial of a smooth projective variety M is χ_u(M) = Σ_{p,q} h^{p,q} u^{p+q} ∈ ℤ[u]. The Hodge-Tate polynomial refines this. The topological Euler characteristic recovers as χ(M) = Σ (−1)^{p+q} h^{p,q} = χ_u(M)|_{u=−1}. The Hodge conjecture (one of the seven Clay Millennium Problems): for a smooth projective variety X over ℂ, every Hodge class — a cohomology class in H^{p,p}(X, ℚ) ⊂ H^{2p}(X, ℚ) — is the cohomology class of an algebraic cycle (a ℚ-linear combination of subvarieties of codimension p). The conjecture is known in codimension 1 (the Lefschetz (1,1)-theorem: every (1,1)-class is the Chern class of a line bundle) and in codimension n−1 (by Hard Lefschetz and Poincaré duality). It is open in general. Cattani-Deligne-Kaplan proved the Hodge conjecture for "typical" Hodge structures (those with largest possible Hodge numbers), showing it holds for very general varieties. The conjecture relates to the Tate conjecture in étale cohomology and to the Bloch-Beilinson filtration on algebraic K-theory, which proposes a K-theoretic refinement of the Hodge conjecture.
The Hodge conjecture — seeking to characterize algebraic cycles via cohomological conditions — remains one of the deepest open problems in algebraic geometry.

L² Cohomology and the Zucker Conjecture on Non‑Compact Kähler Manifolds

On a complete Kähler manifold that is not compact, the ordinary de Rham cohomology no longer captures the analytic information carried by harmonic forms. The L² cohomology groups H⁽²⁾ᵏ(M) are defined as the cohomology of the complex of square‑integrable differential forms with respect to the Kähler metric. A fundamental result of Zucker (1979) and, independently, of Looijenga and Saper‑Stern identifies these groups with the intersection cohomology IH⁽ᵏ⁾(X) of a suitable projective compactification X of M, provided the compactification has normal‑crossing boundary divisors and the metric has Poincaré‑type growth near the boundary. The isomorphism H⁽²⁾ᵏ(M) ≅ IH⁽ᵏ⁾(X) is canonical and respects the mixed Hodge structures on both sides. Consequently, the Hodge decomposition extends to L² harmonic forms: each L² cohomology class contains a unique harmonic representative, and the Hodge numbers hp,q(2) = dim Hp,q(2)(M) satisfy the same symmetry and duality relations as in the compact case. This theory underpins the study of automorphic forms on locally symmetric varieties, where the L² cohomology computes the contribution of discrete series representations to the cohomology of arithmetic quotients.

Variation of Hodge Structure, Period Maps, and Griffiths Transversality

A variation of Hodge structure (VHS) over a smooth base S is a locally constant ℚ‑local system V together with a decreasing Hodge filtration F^• on the holomorphic vector bundle V⊗𝒪_S such that each fiber carries a pure Hodge structure of fixed weight and the filtration varies holomorphically. The period map Φ: S → ΓD sends a point s to the Hodge filtration on the fiber V_s, where D is the classifying period domain and Γ is the monodromy group. Griffiths transversality is the differential constraint dΦ(T_s S) ⊂ Fp-1/F^p ⊗ Ω¹_S, which follows from the fact that the Gauss‑Manin connection ∇ satisfies ∇(F^p) ⊂ Fp-1⊗Ω¹_S. This infinitesimal property forces the period map to be horizontal with respect to the natural homogeneous bundle structure on D. As a consequence, the image of Φ is an integral submanifold of the horizontal distribution, and its dimension is bounded by the rank of the Kodaira‑Spencer map. The theory of VHS provides the bridge between Hodge theory and moduli: the Torelli map for curves, the period map for K3 surfaces, and the construction of Shimura varieties all arise as period maps of specific variations of Hodge structure.

Worked Example: Griffiths Transversality for a Family of Elliptic Curves
Consider the Legendre family of elliptic curves E_λ: y² = x(x-1)(x-λ) over S = ℙ¹ {0,1,∞}. The first cohomology H¹(E_λ, ℂ) carries a weight‑1 Hodge structure with Hodge numbers h1,0=h0,1=1. Show that the period map Φ: S → ℍ/ SL₂(ℤ) satisfies Griffiths transversality.
The holomorphic 1‑form ω_λ = dx/y spans H1,0(E_λ). The Gauss‑Manin connection ∇ acts on ω_λ by ∇(ω_λ) = η ⊗ dλ where η is a (0,1)-form representing the Kodaira‑Spencer class. Since ∇(F¹) ⊂ F⁰⊗Ω¹_S and F¹ = ⟨ω_λ⟩, the derivative of the period map lands in Hom(F¹, F⁰/F¹) ≅ ℂ, which is precisely the horizontal tangent space of the period domain ℍ. Thus dΦ(T_λ S) lies in the horizontal subspace, verifying Griffiths transversality.
Exam‑Day Strategy
When a question asks for the differential of a period map, first identify the Hodge filtration on the relevant cohomology, then write the Gauss‑Manin connection on a local frame of F^p. The transversality condition is exactly the statement that the connection does not increase the filtration degree. Remember that the period domain is a homogeneous space G_ℝ/V, and the horizontal distribution is the G_ℝ‑invariant subbundle associated to the Lie algebra grading.

Mixed Hodge Modules and Saito’s Decomposition Theorem

Mixed Hodge modules, introduced by Morihiko Saito in the late 1980s, provide a sheaf‑theoretic framework that simultaneously generalizes variations of Hodge structure, perverse sheaves, and the classical mixed Hodge structures on cohomology of algebraic varieties. A mixed Hodge module on a complex algebraic variety X is a triple (M, F^•, W_•) where M is a regular holonomic D‑module, F^• is the Hodge filtration, and W_• is the weight filtration, satisfying a list of axioms that include strictness of the filtered de Rham complex and compatibility with the Riemann‑Hilbert correspondence. Saito proved that the category of mixed Hodge modules is abelian and stable under the six Grothendieck operations (pushforward, pullback, tensor product, duality, etc.). The decomposition theorem for a proper morphism f: X → Y of algebraic varieties states that the direct image f_* ℚ_X[dim X] splits in the derived category of mixed Hodge modules as a direct sum of shifted intersection cohomology mixed Hodge modules of subvarieties of Y, each equipped with a pure Hodge structure of the appropriate weight. This result refines the topological decomposition theorem of Beĭlinson‑Bernstein‑Deligne‑Gabber by adding Hodge‑theoretic data, and it implies the hard Lefschetz theorem and the Hodge‑Riemann bilinear relations for intersection cohomology of projective varieties.

Hard Lefschetz, Standard Conjectures, and the Bloch–Beilinson Filtration

The hard Lefschetz theorem for a smooth projective variety X of dimension n asserts that cup product with the Kähler class ωn-k gives an isomorphism Ln-k: H^k(X, ℚ) → H2n-k(X, ℚ). Grothendieck’s standard conjectures predict that this isomorphism is induced by an algebraic cycle, i.e. that the Lefschetz operator L is algebraic. Conjecture B (the Lefschetz type standard conjecture) states that the inverse Λ of L is also given by an algebraic correspondence. If true, the projectors onto the primitive cohomology P^k = ker(Ln-k+1) would be algebraic, yielding a canonical decomposition of the motive of X into primitive pieces. The Bloch–Beilinson filtration on Chow groups CH^p(X) is a conjectural decreasing filtration F^ν CH^p(X) whose graded pieces are expected to be isomorphic to the extensions groups Ext2ν-1MHS(ℚ(0), H2p-ν(X, ℚ(p))) in the category of mixed Hodge structures. The first non‑trivial step F¹ CH^p(X) is the subgroup of homologically trivial cycles, and the Abel‑Jacobi map identifies its quotient by algebraic equivalence with the intermediate Jacobian J2p-1(X). The hard Lefschetz isomorphism, together with the Hodge‑Riemann bilinear relations, provides the necessary positivity to define the filtration and to prove its compatibility with the conjectural motivic t‑structure. Progress on these conjectures includes the work of Künnemann on the standard conjectures for abelian varieties and the recent advances of Voisin and others on the Bloch–Beilinson filtration for certain complete intersections.

  • Confusing L² cohomology with ordinary de Rham cohomology on non‑compact manifolds; the former requires square‑integrability and a complete metric.
  • Assuming the period map is holomorphic without checking Griffiths transversality; the horizontal condition is essential for the image to lie in the period domain.
  • Treating mixed Hodge modules as ordinary mixed Hodge structures; they carry a D‑module structure and are subject to the six functor formalism.
  • Believing the hard Lefschetz isomorphism is always induced by an algebraic cycle; this is precisely the content of the standard conjecture B, which remains open in general.
  • Neglecting the weight filtration when applying the decomposition theorem; the theorem splits the direct image in the category of mixed Hodge modules, not merely in the derived category of ℚ‑sheaves.
  • Overlooking the role of the Hodge‑Riemann bilinear relations in defining the Bloch–Beilinson filtration; positivity of the intersection form on primitive classes is the analytic input.
  • Misidentifying the intermediate Jacobian with the full Hodge structure; it captures only the extension data of weight −1 Hodge structures.

These advanced topics illustrate how Hodge theory extends beyond compact Kähler manifolds into non‑compact geometry, moduli, sheaf‑theoretic categories, and the deep conjectural landscape linking cycles, motives, and cohomology.
