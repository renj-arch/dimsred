Hodge Theory — Hodge Decomposition, Mixed Hodge Structures & the Hard Lefschetz Theorem

Hodge theory is the study of harmonic forms on Riemannian and Kähler manifolds, providing a deep link between analysis, topology, and algebraic geometry. On a compact Riemannian manifold, the Hodge theorem states that every de Rham cohomology class has a unique harmonic representative, giving an isomorphism Hᵏ_dR(M, ℝ) ≅ ℋᵏ(M). On a Kähler manifold, the complex structure interacts with the metric to produce the Hodge decomposition Hᵏ(M, ℂ) = ⊕_{p+q=k} H^{p,q}(M), refining the cohomology by type. The Hard Lefschetz theorem gives an isomorphism between cohomology groups via cup product with powers of the Kähler form. Deligne's Mixed Hodge Structures extend these ideas to singular and non-compact varieties, providing a powerful tool for understanding the topology of algebraic varieties. The Hodge conjecture — one of the Clay Millennium Problems — asks whether every Hodge class is algebraic.

"Hodge theory is the bridge between topology and analysis on manifolds."
— William Hodge
Part 1

📏 The Hodge Theorem on Riemannian Manifolds

Harmonic Theory
The Hodge Star, the Laplacian, and the Hodge Theorem
On an oriented Riemannian n-manifold (M, g), the Hodge star operator ⋆: Ωᵏ(M) → Ω^{n−k}(M) is defined by α ∧ ⋆β = g(α, β) dvol. The L² inner product on k-forms is ⟨α,β⟩ = ∫_M α ∧ ⋆β. The codifferential δ: Ωᵏ → Ω^{k−1} is the formal adjoint of d: δ = (−1)^{n(k+1)+1} ⋆ d ⋆. The Hodge Laplacian Δ = dδ + δd. A form α is harmonic if Δα = 0; denote the space of harmonic k-forms by ℋᵏ(M). The Hodge theorem: dim ℋᵏ(M) < ∞, and every de Rham cohomology class has a unique harmonic representative. This yields the orthogonal decomposition Ωᵏ(M) = ℋᵏ(M) ⊕ dΩ^{k−1}(M) ⊕ δΩ^{k+1}(M) and the isomorphism Hᵏ_{dR}(M, ℝ) ≅ ℋᵏ(M). The theory extends to elliptic complexes via general elliptic operator theory on compact manifolds, culminating in the Atiyah-Singer index theorem: the analytic index of an elliptic operator equals its topological index.
The Hodge theorem bridges analysis and topology: harmonic forms are the unique minimizers of the Dirichlet energy in each cohomology class, giving a canonical representative.
Part 2

🌍 Hodge Theory on Kähler Manifolds

Kähler
Kähler Identities, Hodge Decomposition, and Hard Lefschetz
On a Kähler manifold (M, g, J, ω), the complex structure J and the Riemannian metric g are compatible, and the Kähler form ω(·,·) = g(·, J·) is closed (dω = 0). The Dolbeault operators ∂: Ω^{p,q} → Ω^{p+1,q} and ∂̄: Ω^{p,q} → Ω^{p,q+1} satisfy d = ∂ + ∂̄. The Kähler identities relate the Lefschetz operator L(α) = ω∧α and its adjoint Λ: [Λ, ∂] = i∂̄^* and [Λ, ∂̄] = −i∂^*. These imply the fundamental equality of Laplacians: Δ_d = 2Δ_∂ = 2Δ_∂̄. The Hodge decomposition for compact Kähler manifolds: Hᵏ(M, ℂ) = ⊕_{p+q=k} H^{p,q}(M), where H^{p,q} ≅ H^q(M, Ω^p_M) is the Dolbeault cohomology. The Hodge numbers h^{p,q} = dim H^{p,q} satisfy: h^{p,q} = h^{q,p} (conjugation symmetry) and h^{p,q} = h^{n−p,n−q} (Serre duality). The Hard Lefschetz theorem: the map L^{n−k}: Hᵏ(M, ℂ) → H^{2n−k}(M, ℂ) given by cup product with ω^{n−k} is an isomorphism for all k. The primitive decomposition: every harmonic form decomposes uniquely into primitive forms, giving the Lefschetz decomposition of the cohomology.
On Kähler manifolds, Riemannian, symplectic, and complex structures harmonize, producing the refined Hodge decomposition and powerful constraints on topology.
Part 3

🧮 Mixed Hodge Structures

Deligne
Pure and Mixed Hodge Structures, Deligne's Theorem
A pure Hodge structure of weight k consists of a ℚ-vector space V with a decomposition V⊗ℂ = ⊕_{p+q=k} V^{p,q} such that V^{p,q} = \bar{V}^{q,p} (complex conjugation exchanges the summands). A mixed Hodge structure (MHS) is a ℚ-vector space V equipped with an increasing weight filtration W_•: 0 ⊂ W_0 ⊂ W_1 ⊂ … ⊂ W_{2k} = V and a decreasing Hodge filtration F^•: V⊗ℂ ⊃ … ⊃ F^p ⊃ F^{p+1} ⊃ … such that on each graded piece Gr^W_k V = W_k/W_{k−1}, the filtration F^• induces a pure Hodge structure of weight k. Deligne's theorem (1971-74): the cohomology Hᵏ(X, ℚ) of any algebraic variety X over ℂ carries a canonical mixed Hodge structure, functorial for algebraic maps. For a smooth projective variety, the MHS is pure (the weight filtration is trivial: W_k = 0 for k < m and W_k = Hᵏ(X, ℚ) for k ≥ m). For a singular variety, the weight filtration encodes the cohomology of a desingularization. For a complement X\D of a divisor with normal crossings, the MHS captures the logarithmic forms. The MHS also exists on the fundamental group (the Malcev completion) and on nearby cycle cohomology.
Mixed Hodge structures are a powerful generalization of classical Hodge theory to all algebraic varieties, providing a systematic framework for understanding their cohomology.
Part 4

🔥 Hodge Polynomials and the Hodge Conjecture

Conjecture
Hodge Polynomials, the Hodge Conjecture, and Related Problems
The Hodge polynomial of a smooth projective variety M is χ_u(M) = Σ_{p,q} h^{p,q} u^{p+q} ∈ ℤ[u]. The Hodge-Tate polynomial refines this. The topological Euler characteristic recovers as χ(M) = Σ (−1)^{p+q} h^{p,q} = χ_u(M)|_{u=−1}. The Hodge conjecture (one of the seven Clay Millennium Problems): for a smooth projective variety X over ℂ, every Hodge class — a cohomology class in H^{p,p}(X, ℚ) ⊂ H^{2p}(X, ℚ) — is the cohomology class of an algebraic cycle (a ℚ-linear combination of subvarieties of codimension p). The conjecture is known in codimension 1 (the Lefschetz (1,1)-theorem: every (1,1)-class is the Chern class of a line bundle) and in codimension n−1 (by Hard Lefschetz and Poincaré duality). It is open in general. Cattani-Deligne-Kaplan proved the Hodge conjecture for "typical" Hodge structures (those with largest possible Hodge numbers), showing it holds for very general varieties. The conjecture relates to the Tate conjecture in étale cohomology and to the Bloch-Beilinson filtration on algebraic K-theory, which proposes a K-theoretic refinement of the Hodge conjecture.
The Hodge conjecture — seeking to characterize algebraic cycles via cohomological conditions — remains one of the deepest open problems in algebraic geometry.
