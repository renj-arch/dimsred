Perverse Sheaves — Intersection Cohomology, the Decomposition Theorem & the Riemann–Hilbert Correspondence

Deep dive into Perverse Sheaves — the derived category D^b_c(X) of constructible sheaves on a complex algebraic variety X, the six functor formalism (f^*, f_*, f_!, f^!, ⊗, ℋom), the perverse t-structure leading to the abelian category Perv(X) of perverse sheaves (the heart of a t-structure on D^b_c(X) defined by support and co-support conditions), intersection cohomology (Goresky–MacPherson, 1974) and the intersection cohomology complex IC_X (the intermediate extension of the constant sheaf on the smooth part of X), the decomposition theorem (Beilinson–Bernstein–Deligne–Gabber, 1982: for a proper map f: X → Y, Rf_* IC_X is a direct sum of shifted perverse sheaves), the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence (an equivalence between the derived category of regular holonomic D-modules and the derived category of constructible sheaves), Kazhdan–Lusztig theory (the Kazhdan–Lusztig conjectures, proved by Brylinski–Kashiwara and Beilinson–Bernstein via perverse sheaves on the flag variety), and the enhanced and spectral extensions of the theory.

Part 1

🧬 Derived Categories and the Six Functors

Derived
Triangulated Categories, Derived Functors, and Constructible Sheaves
The derived category D(A) of an abelian category A has complexes as objects and quasi-isomorphisms (maps inducing isomorphisms on all cohomology objects) inverted. D^b(A) is the full subcategory of bounded complexes. The triangulated structure: distinguished triangles X → Y → Z → X[1] coming from short exact sequences of complexes (the mapping cone). For a topological space X, let Sh(X) be the abelian category of sheaves of ℂ-vector spaces (or ℚ̄_ℓ). Constructible sheaves (with respect to an algebraic stratification X = ⊔ X_α by locally closed subvarieties) are sheaves whose restriction to each stratum is a local system (a locally constant sheaf of finite-dimensional vector spaces). The bounded derived category of constructible sheaves is D^b_c(X). The six functors: f^* (inverse image — left adjoint to f_*, exact), f_* (direct image — right adjoint to f^*, left exact, derived as Rf_*), f_! (proper direct image — extension by zero, left exact, with compact support cohomology), f^! (exceptional inverse image — right adjoint to f_!, defined via duality), ⊗ (tensor product), and ℋom (internal Hom). The Verdier duality: a contravariant autoequivalence D: D^b_c(X) → D^b_c(X) satisfying D^2 ≅ id, exchanging f_* with f_! and f^* with f^!. The Grothendieck–Verdier duality theorem: Rf_! ≅ D_Y ∘ Rf_* ∘ D_X. The adjunction formulas: Hom(Rf_! ℱ, 𝒢) ≅ Hom(ℱ, f^! 𝒢) and Hom(f^* ℱ, 𝒢) ≅ Hom(ℱ, Rf_* 𝒢). The base change theorem for proper maps and smooth maps. These six functors are the fundamental toolkit of sheaf theory in algebraic geometry.
The six functor formalism provides a powerful calculus for manipulating sheaves on algebraic varieties, with Verdier duality as the central symmetry.
Part 2

🔗 The Perverse t-Structure and Perverse Sheaves

t-Structure
t-Structures, the Perverse Heart, and the Artin–Grothendieck Vanishing
A t-structure (Beilinson–Bernstein–Deligne, 1982) on a triangulated category D consists of two full subcategories D^{≤0} and D^{≥0} satisfying: (i) Hom(D^{≤0}, D^{≥1}) = 0 (where D^{≥1} = D^{≥0}[−1]); (ii) D^{≤0} ⊆ D^{≤1} and D^{≥1} ⊆ D^{≥0}; (iii) for any X ∈ D, there is a distinguished triangle A → X → B → A[1] with A ∈ D^{≤0} and B ∈ D^{≥1}. The heart D^{≤0} ∩ D^{≥0} is an abelian category. The perverse t-structure on D^b_c(X) for a complex algebraic variety X of dimension n is defined by: ^{p}D^{≤0} = {ℱ | dim supp H^i(ℱ) ≤ −i} and ^{p}D^{≥0} = {ℱ | dim cosupp H^i(ℱ) ≤ i} where cosupp is defined via Verdier duality: dim cosupp ℋ = dim supp Dℋ. The perverse sheaves Perv(X) = ^{p}D^{≤0} ∩ ^{p}D^{≥0} form the heart — an abelian category. The key property: perverse sheaves are Artinian and Noetherian (every object has finite length). Simple perverse sheaves correspond to pairs (Z, ℒ) where Z is an irreducible closed subvariety and ℒ is an irreducible local system on a Zariski-open subset of Z_{reg}. The intermediate extension functor j_{!*}: Perv(U) → Perv(X) for an open inclusion j: U ↪ X: given a perverse sheaf 𝒢 on U, j_{!*}𝒢 is the image of j_!𝒢 → j_*𝒢 shifted appropriately. The Artin–Grothendieck vanishing theorem: for a constructible sheaf ℱ on an affine variety X of dimension n, H^i_c(X, ℱ) = 0 for i > n. This is a crucial vanishing result for perverse sheaves on affine varieties.
The perverse t-structure refines the usual cohomological t-structure, creating an abelian category of perverse sheaves that behave like sheaves shifted by the dimension of their support.

💥 Intersection Cohomology and the Decomposition Theorem

Decomposition
Goresky–MacPherson Intersection Cohomology and the BBDG Decomposition Theorem
Intersection cohomology (Goresky–MacPherson, 1974) was developed to restore Poincaré duality for singular varieties. For a stratified variety X, the intersection cohomology complex IC_X is defined as IC_X = j_{!*} ℂ_U[n] where j: U ↪ X is the smooth part and n = dim X. The intersection cohomology IH^i(X) = H^{i−n}(X, IC_X) satisfies Poincaré duality: IH^i(X) ≅ IH^{2n−i}(X)^*. The Decomposition theorem (Beilinson–Bernstein–Deligne–Gabber, 1982): let f: X → Y be a proper algebraic map between complex algebraic varieties. Then Rf_* IC_X ≅ ⊕_k IC_{Z_k}(L_k)[d_k] — a direct sum of shifted intersection cohomology complexes of subvarieties Z_k ⊂ Y, each twisted by a semisimple local system L_k. Equivalently, Rf_* IC_X is a semisimple perverse sheaf (a direct sum of simple perverse sheaves). This is one of the deepest theorems in algebraic geometry, generalizing the Hodge decomposition and the hard Lefschetz theorem. For a resolution of singularities f: X̃ → X (proper, birational, with X̃ smooth), the decomposition theorem describes how the constant sheaf on X̃ decomposes when pushed forward to X. The relative hard Lefschetz theorem: for a projective morphism f: X → Y with an ample line bundle L on X, the cup product L^k: R^{−k}f_* IC_X → R^{k}f_* IC_X is an isomorphism for all k. The Kleiman vanishing theorem and the Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing are related results. Applications: the McKay correspondence for crepant resolutions, the Kollar vanishing theorem, and the Macaulayfication of schemes.
The decomposition theorem shows that the pushforward of the intersection cohomology complex under a proper map breaks into a sum of intersection cohomology complexes — a vast generalization of Hodge theory.

🔮 The Riemann–Hilbert Correspondence and Kazhdan–Lusztig Theory

RHC
D-Modules, the Riemann–Hilbert Correspondence, and the Kazhdan–Lusztig Conjectures
The Riemann–Hilbert correspondence (Kashiwara, 1980; Mebkhout, 1984): for a complex algebraic variety X, there is an equivalence of triangulated categories D^b_{rh}(D_X) ≅ D^b_c(X, ℂ) between the derived category of regular holonomic D-modules and the derived category of constructible sheaves. The functor is the de Rham functor DR: M ↦ Ω^∙_{X^{an}} ⊗_{D_{X^{an}}} M. Its inverse is the solution functor Sol: ℱ ↦ Rℋom(ℱ, 𝒪_{X^{an}}). The equivalence preserves the perverse t-structure: the heart Perv(X) corresponds to the abelian category of regular holonomic D-modules. This connects differential equations (regular singularities) with topology (constructible sheaves). The Kazhdan–Lusztig conjectures (1979): the characters of simple highest weight modules for a semisimple Lie algebra g can be expressed in terms of the Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials P_{w,v}(q) associated to the Weyl group W. These polynomials are determined by the Hecke algebra of W via the Kazhdan–Lusztig basis C_w. The conjectures were proved by Brylinski–Kashiwara (1981) and Beilinson–Bernstein (1981) using perverse sheaves on the flag variety G/B. The key idea: localization of g-modules to D-modules on G/B, then apply the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence to translate to perverse sheaves — the intersection cohomology complexes IC_{X_w} of Schubert varieties correspond to simple highest weight modules. This is the localization theorem of Beilinson–Bernstein: there is an equivalence between the category of g-modules with fixed central character and D-modules on G/B. The geometric Satake correspondence (Mirković–Vilonen) and the Springer correspondence (Lusztig) also use perverse sheaves to study representations of algebraic groups.
The Riemann–Hilbert correspondence bridges analysis (D-modules), topology (perverse sheaves), and representation theory (Kazhdan–Lusztig theory) — a grand unification.

Verdier Duality and the Perverse t‑Structure on Stratified Spaces

Verdier duality provides the canonical involution on the derived category of constructible sheaves that exchanges the functors f! and f*. For a complex algebraic variety X of pure dimension n, the dualizing complex is ωX = ℂX[2n]. The Verdier dual of an object K ∈ Dbc(X) is DK = Rℋom(K, ωX). This functor is an anti‑equivalence of triangulated categories and satisfies D² ≅ id. On the level of perverse sheaves, Verdier duality preserves the perverse t‑structure: if P ∈ pD≤0 then DP ∈ pD≥0, and vice‑versa. Consequently the heart Perv(X) is stable under D, and simple perverse sheaves are self‑dual up to a shift when they arise as intersection cohomology complexes ICZ(L) with L a self‑dual local system.

On a Whitney stratified space the perverse t‑structure can be described by support and cosupport conditions relative to the strata. Let X = ∪α Sα be a stratification with strata of dimension dα. A complex K is perverse iff for every stratum Sα the cohomology sheaves of the restriction K|Sα vanish above degree −dα and the cohomology sheaves of the Verdier dual DK|Sα vanish above degree −dα. This formulation makes the gluing of perverse sheaves across strata completely explicit and underlies the construction of the intermediate extension j!* as the image of the natural map j! → j* in the perverse heart.

The compatibility of Verdier duality with the six functor formalism yields the fundamental identities D ∘ f* ≅ f! ∘ D and D ∘ f! ≅ f* ∘ D for proper f. For a proper map f: X → Y the decomposition theorem can be restated as the semisimplicity of Rf*ICX in the abelian category Perv(Y), and Verdier duality then implies the same semisimplicity for Rf!ICX. This duality symmetry is essential in the proof of the relative hard Lefschetz theorem, where the Lefschetz operator is shown to be an isomorphism between the perverse cohomology sheaves pH−k(Rf*ICX) and pHk(Rf*ICX) via the hard Lefschetz isomorphism on the underlying intersection cohomology.

The Beilinson–Bernstein Localization Theorem and its Perverse Sheaf Interpretation

The Beilinson–Bernstein localization theorem establishes an equivalence between the category of g‑modules with a fixed regular integral central character and the category of D‑modules on the flag variety G/B. Concretely, let g be a complex semisimple Lie algebra, G the corresponding simply connected group, and B a Borel subgroup. The sheaf of differential operators DG/B carries a natural G‑action, and the global sections functor Γ: D‑Mod(G/B) → g‑Mod is exact on the subcategory of quasi‑coherent D‑modules. The theorem asserts that for a regular integral weight λ the functor Γ induces an equivalence between the category of D‑modules on G/B with central character χλ and the category of g‑modules with the same central character.

Via the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence, regular holonomic D‑modules on G/B correspond to perverse sheaves on the complex analytic space (G/B)an. Under this equivalence the simple highest weight module L(λ) corresponds to the intersection cohomology complex ICXw of the Schubert variety Xw = BwB/B where w is the unique element of the Weyl group such that w·λ is dominant. The Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials Py,w(q) then compute the multiplicities of composition factors in Verma modules, and they also give the stalk cohomology dimensions of ICXw at points of the Schubert cell Cy. This geometric realization of representation theory was the original motivation for the development of perverse sheaves.

The localization theorem extends to the derived setting: the derived global sections functor RΓ gives an equivalence between the derived category of D‑modules with regular singularities and the derived category of g‑modules with the given central character. The perverse t‑structure on D‑modules corresponds to the natural t‑structure on g‑modules whose heart is the category of modules with a filtration whose subquotients are highest weight modules. This compatibility explains why the Kazhdan–Lusztig conjecture, originally a statement about characters of highest weight modules, is equivalent to the statement that the intersection cohomology complexes of Schubert varieties are perverse and have the expected stalk cohomology.

The Geometric Satake Correspondence via Perverse Sheaves on the Affine Grassmannian

The geometric Satake correspondence, proved by Mirković and Vilonen, realizes the representation category of the Langlands dual group G∨ as the category of perverse sheaves on the affine Grassmannian GrG = G((t))/G[[t]] equipped with the convolution product. The affine Grassmannian is an ind‑scheme stratified by G[[t]]‑orbits Grλ indexed by dominant coweights λ of G. The closure of each orbit is a projective variety Grλ, and its intersection cohomology complex ICλ is a simple perverse sheaf. The convolution product P * Q = m!(P ⊠ Q) (where m is the multiplication map) endows the category PervG[[t]](GrG) with a tensor structure.

The main theorem states that the functor sending a dominant weight λ to ICλ extends to a tensor equivalence between Rep(G∨) and PervG[[t]](GrG). Under this equivalence the fiber functor (taking the stalk at the base point) corresponds to the forgetful functor to vector spaces, and the tensor product of representations corresponds to convolution of perverse sheaves. The proof uses the decomposition theorem for the convolution morphism Grλ × Grμ → Grλ+μ and the fact that the convolution of two IC complexes is a direct sum of IC complexes with multiplicities given by the Littlewood–Richardson coefficients.

This correspondence has profound consequences: it provides a geometric construction of the tensor category of representations of a reductive group without using the Lie algebra, it explains the appearance of the affine Grassmannian in the geometric Langlands program, and it yields a canonical basis (the Mirković–Vilonen basis) of representation spaces indexed by irreducible components of the fibers of the convolution morphism. The MV basis coincides with Lusztig's canonical basis when G is simply‑laced, linking perverse sheaves to quantum groups.

The Springer Correspondence and Perverse Sheaves on the Nilpotent Cone

The Springer correspondence, originally formulated by Springer and later refined by Lusztic, establishes a bijection between irreducible representations of the Weyl group W and pairs (O, L) where O is a nilpotent orbit in the Lie algebra g and L is an irreducible G‑equivariant local system on O. The geometric construction uses the Springer resolution μ: T*(G/B) → N, where N is the nilpotent cone. The map μ is proper and semismall, so the decomposition theorem implies that Rμ*ℂ[dim] is a semisimple perverse sheaf on N. Its direct summands are the intersection cohomology complexes ICO(L) for various orbits O and local systems L.

The top cohomology of the Springer fiber μ−1(x) carries a natural action of the Weyl group, and the decomposition of this representation into irreducibles matches the multiplicities of the IC summands in Rμ*ℂ[dim]. In particular, the trivial representation of W corresponds to the regular nilpotent orbit with the trivial local system, while the sign representation corresponds to the zero orbit. For classical groups the correspondence can be described combinatorially via partitions and Young tableaux; for exceptional groups the classification of nilpotent orbits and their local systems is more intricate but still completely known.

Perverse sheaves provide the natural framework for the Springer correspondence because the pushforward Rμ*ℂ[dim] is perverse and its simple constituents are exactly the IC complexes that parametrize the correspondence. Moreover, the convolution action of the affine Hecke algebra on the derived category of G‑equivariant perverse sheaves on N categorifies the Springer representation, linking it to the geometric Satake correspondence via the affine Grassmannian of the dual group.

Mixed Hodge Modules and Saito's Theory of Perverse Sheaves

Morihiko Saito's theory of mixed Hodge modules upgrades the category of perverse sheaves to a category that carries both a weight filtration and a Hodge filtration, compatible with the six functor formalism. A mixed Hodge module on a complex algebraic variety X consists of a perverse sheaf K ∈ Perv(X, ℚ), a filtered regular holonomic D‑module (M, F) on X whose de Rham complex is K ⊗ ℂ, and a finite increasing weight filtration W on M such that the associated graded pieces are pure Hodge modules of definite weight. The heart of the resulting t‑structure on the derived category of mixed Hodge modules is the abelian category MHM(X) of mixed Hodge modules.

The fundamental properties include: (1) the forgetful functor MHM(X) → Perv(X, ℚ) is exact and faithful; (2) the six functors f*, f!, f*, f!, ⊗, ℋom lift to MHM and preserve the weight filtration; (3) for a smooth variety X of dimension n, the constant Hodge module ℚXH[n] is a pure Hodge module of weight 0; (4) the intersection cohomology Hodge module ICXH underlies the intersection cohomology complex ICX and carries a pure Hodge structure of weight 0 on its stalks.

Saito's theory implies the decomposition theorem for Hodge modules: if f: X → Y is a projective morphism, then Rf*ICXH decomposes as a direct sum of shifted intersection cohomology Hodge modules ICZkH(Lk)[dk] with pure Hodge structures. This yields the Hodge‑theoretic version of the hard Lefschetz theorem and the purity of intersection cohomology. Mixed Hodge modules also provide a natural setting for the study of variations of Hodge structure, the nearby and vanishing cycle functors, and the monodromy weight filtration, all of which are essential in the proof of the decomposition theorem and in applications to algebraic cycles and motivic cohomology.

  • Assuming Verdier duality exchanges f* and f! without the properness hypothesis; the identities hold only for proper maps.
  • Misidentifying the simple perverse sheaf ICZ(L) with the constant sheaf on a singular variety; the intermediate extension is essential for Poincaré duality.
  • Overlooking the semismall condition in the Springer correspondence; without it the pushforward need not be perverse and the decomposition theorem does not apply directly.
  • Treating the geometric Satake equivalence as a mere bijection on objects; the tensor structure (convolution) is crucial and must be respected.
  • Neglecting the weight filtration in mixed Hodge modules; the purity of the intersection cohomology Hodge module is a deep theorem, not a formal consequence of the perverse t‑structure.
  • Confusing the Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials for the Weyl group with those for the affine Weyl group; the latter appear in the affine Grassmannian setting and have different combinatorics.
The advanced topics above complete the core theoretical framework required for research‑level examinations on perverse sheaves and their applications.

Nearby and Vanishing Cycles in the Perverse Setting

The functors of nearby cycles ψ_f and vanishing cycles φ_f, attached to a holomorphic function f : X → ℂ on a complex analytic space X, are central to the local study of perverse sheaves. For a perverse sheaf K on X, the shifted complexes ψ_f K[−1] and φ_f K[−1] are again perverse on the special fibre X0 = f−1(0). This perverse exactness is a theorem of Gabber and of Beilinson–Bernstein–Deligne, and it relies on the fact that the perverse t‑structure is compatible with the six functor formalism. The monodromy automorphism T on ψ_f K induces a weight filtration W on the vanishing cycles, and the graded pieces Gr^W_k φ_f K carry pure Hodge structures when K underlies a mixed Hodge module. The distinguished triangle ψ_f K → φ_f K → ψ_f K → ψ_f K[1] encodes the variation of the Milnor fibre cohomology and is functorial in K. In the case where K = ℚ_X[dim X] is the constant perverse sheaf on a smooth variety, ψ_f ℚ_X[dim X] computes the cohomology of the Milnor fibre, and the monodromy weight filtration coincides with the filtration defined by the nilpotent part of log T. The nearby and vanishing cycle functors commute with proper pushforward and with Verdier duality up to canonical isomorphisms, which makes them indispensable in the proof of the decomposition theorem for projective morphisms: one reduces to the local situation by stratifying the target and applying the functors to the intersection cohomology complexes on the strata. Moreover, the theory extends to the setting of constructible sheaves on real analytic spaces, where the real Milnor fibre replaces the complex one, and to the equivariant context needed for the Springer correspondence.

Perverse Sheaves on Artin Stacks and the Geometric Langlands Correspondence

The extension of perverse sheaf theory from varieties to Artin stacks is a cornerstone of the geometric Langlands program. For a smooth algebraic stack 𝔛 of finite type over ℂ, one defines the derived category D^b_c(𝔛, ℚ) of constructible complexes by descent from smooth atlases. The perverse t‑structure on this category is defined by requiring that a complex K lies in ^pD≤0 (resp. ^pD≥0) if for every smooth morphism U → 𝔛 from a scheme U, the pullback K|_U satisfies the usual perverse cohomological amplitude conditions. The heart Perv(𝔛) is an abelian category stable under the six functors, and the intermediate extension IC_𝔛(L) of an irreducible local system L on a locally closed substack exists and is simple. In the geometric Langlands correspondence for a reductive group G over a curve C, one considers the moduli stack Bun_G of G‑bundles on C. The Hecke operators act on D^b_c(Bun_G) by correspondences, and the subcategory of perverse sheaves that are eigensheaves for these operators corresponds to local systems on C for the Langlands dual group ^L G. The work of Beilinson–Drinfeld, Gaitsgory, and others shows that the category of ^L G‑local systems on C is equivalent to a certain category of D‑modules on Bun_G, and under the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence this matches a category of perverse sheaves. The proof uses the fact that the Hecke functors preserve perversity and that the convolution product on the affine Grassmannian Gr_G, which is an ind‑scheme, can be interpreted as a fusion product on the stack of G‑bundles. The perverse t‑structure on the affine Grassmannian is compatible with the convolution, making the spherical Hecke category a tensor category equivalent to Rep(^L G). This compatibility is the geometric incarnation of the Satake isomorphism and is essential for constructing the Langlands parameters from perverse sheaves.

Worked Example: Perverse Exactness of Nearby Cycles
Let X = ℂ2 with coordinates (x, y) and f(x, y) = xy. Let K = ℚ_X[2] be the constant perverse sheaf on X. Compute the perverse cohomology sheaves ^pH^i(ψ_f K) on the special fibre X0 = {xy = 0}.
The function f has an isolated critical point at the origin. The Milnor fibre f−1(ε) for ε ≠ 0 is homotopy equivalent to a circle S1, so its reduced cohomology is concentrated in degree 1. The nearby cycles complex ψ_f ℚ_X[2] has cohomology sheaves H^j(ψ_f ℚ_X[2]) supported on X0. Because K is perverse of dimension 2, ψ_f K[−1] is perverse on X0. Hence ^pH0(ψ_f K) = ψ_f K[−1] and all other perverse cohomology sheaves vanish. Concretely, the stalk of ψ_f K at a smooth point of X0 (away from the origin) is the cohomology of a point, so ^pH0 is a rank‑one local system on each smooth branch. At the origin the stalk is H^*(S1) = ℚ ⊕ ℚ[−1]; after shifting by −1 this becomes ℚ[−1] ⊕ ℚ[−2], which in perverse degrees gives a single perverse sheaf supported at the origin with multiplicity two. Thus ψ_f K is a perverse sheaf on X0 whose simple constituents are the intermediate extensions of the rank‑one local systems on the two branches and a skyscraper perverse sheaf at the origin.
Exam Strategy
When a question asks for the perverse cohomology of nearby or vanishing cycles, first identify the dimension of the ambient space and the perverse shift of the input complex. Remember that ψ_f and φ_f shift perversity by −1. Then use the known topology of the Milnor fibre (often a bouquet of spheres) to write down the ordinary cohomology, and finally translate into perverse degrees by adding the dimension of the special fibre. The monodromy weight filtration appears only when mixed Hodge modules are involved; for pure perverse sheaves it is trivial.

The Decomposition Theorem for Non‑Projective Morphisms via Weights

The classical decomposition theorem of Beilinson–Bernstein–Deligne–Gabber states that for a projective morphism f : X → Y of complex algebraic varieties, the derived pushforward Rf_* IC_X decomposes as a direct sum of shifted intersection cohomology complexes on Y. When f is not projective, the theorem fails in general because the pushforward need not be a direct sum of perverse sheaves; higher perverse cohomology sheaves can appear. However, Saito's theory of mixed Hodge modules provides a replacement: every morphism f of complex algebraic varieties induces a functor Rf_* on the derived category of mixed Hodge modules, and the weight filtration on the target yields a spectral sequence whose E2 page is a direct sum of pure Hodge modules. The key result, proved by de Cataldo and Migliorini using the theory of weights, is that for any morphism f there exists a finite filtration on Rf_* IC_X^H whose graded pieces are direct sums of shifted intersection cohomology Hodge modules ICZ_k^H(L_k)[d_k] with pure Hodge structures of weight zero. The filtration is not canonical, but its existence implies that the underlying perverse sheaf Rf_* IC_X is a successive extension of semisimple perverse sheaves. In particular, the perverse cohomology sheaves ^pH^i(Rf_* IC_X) are semisimple for all i. This semisimplicity is the strongest general statement available for non‑projective maps. The proof proceeds by reducing to the case where f is a composition of an open immersion and a projective morphism, using Nagata's compactification, and then analyzing the weight spectral sequence associated to the boundary divisor. The nearby and vanishing cycle functors control the monodromy at the boundary, and the purity of the intersection cohomology Hodge modules forces the differentials in the spectral sequence to vanish. Applications include the decomposition theorem for the Hitchin fibration, which is proper but not projective, and for the morphism from the moduli stack of Higgs bundles to the Hitchin base.

Perverse Sheaves and Soergel Bimodules: Categorification of Hecke Algebras

Soergel bimodules provide an algebraic categorification of the Hecke algebra of a Coxeter group W, and their geometric realization uses perverse sheaves on the flag variety. Let G be a complex reductive group with Borel subgroup B and Weyl group W. The flag variety G/B carries a stratification by B‑orbits indexed by W. For each w ∈ W, the intersection cohomology complex IC_w of the Schubert variety X_w is a simple perverse sheaf on G/B. The category Perv_B(G/B) of B‑equivariant perverse sheaves is equivalent to the category of Soergel bimodules over the polynomial ring ℝ[𝔥^*] (where 𝔥 is the Cartan subalgebra) modulo the ideal generated by positive degree invariants. Under this equivalence, the convolution product of perverse sheaves corresponds to the tensor product of bimodules, and the simple perverse sheaf IC_w corresponds to the indecomposable Soergel bimodule B_w. The classes [B_w] in the split Grothendieck group give the Kazhdan–Lusztig basis of the Hecke algebra H_W. The hard part of Soergel's conjecture, proved by Elias and Williamson, is that the characters of B_w are given by the Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials. The proof uses the geometry of the moment graph of G/B and the theory of parity sheaves, which are a characteristic‑p analogue of perverse sheaves. In characteristic zero, the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence identifies perverse sheaves with regular holonomic D‑modules, and the Soergel bimodules can be seen as the equivariant cohomology of the corresponding D‑modules. This link extends to the affine Weyl group via the affine flag variety, where the category of Iwahori‑equivariant perverse sheaves categorifies the affine Hecke algebra. The geometric Satake correspondence then relates the spherical Hecke category (perverse sheaves on the affine Grassmannian) to representations of the Langlands dual group, and the convolution with Iwahori‑equivariant perverse sheaves gives the action of the affine Hecke algebra on the spherical category. Thus perverse sheaves provide a unified geometric framework for the categorification of both finite and affine Hecke algebras.

Perverse Sheaves on Real Analytic Spaces and the Real Riemann–Hilbert Correspondence

The theory of perverse sheaves extends from complex algebraic varieties to real analytic manifolds and spaces. For a real analytic manifold M of dimension n, one considers the derived category D^bℝ‑c(M, ℚ) of ℝ‑constructible complexes of sheaves of ℚ‑vector spaces. The perverse t‑structure is defined by the same cohomological amplitude conditions, but with the real dimension n replacing the complex dimension. The simple perverse sheaves are again the intermediate extensions IC_Z(L) of irreducible local systems L on locally closed subanalytic subsets Z. The real Riemann–Hilbert correspondence, established by Kashiwara and Schapira, states that the functor DR : D^brh(D_M) → D^bℝ‑c(M, ℂ) from the derived category of regular holonomic D‑modules on the complexification M_ℂ to the derived category of ℝ‑constructible sheaves on M is an equivalence of triangulated categories, and it exchanges the perverse t‑structure on D‑modules with the perverse t‑structure on sheaves. In particular, the category of regular holonomic D‑modules on M_ℂ with a real structure is equivalent to the category of perverse sheaves on M. This correspondence respects the six functor formalism, Verdier duality, and the nearby and vanishing cycle functors for real analytic functions. A key difference from the complex case is that the Milnor fibre of a real analytic function f : M → ℝ at a critical point is a real manifold with boundary, and its cohomology carries a mixed Hodge structure only after complexification. The theory of mixed Hodge modules has a real analogue developed by Saito, where the weight filtration is defined using the monodromy of the complexified function. Applications include the study of real algebraic varieties via their complexifications, the topology of real singularities, and the construction of perverse sheaves on the real points of Shimura varieties, which are used in the Langlands program for real groups.

Perverse Sheaves on Derived Stacks and the Derived Geometric Langlands Correspondence

The classical geometric Langlands correspondence relates perverse sheaves on the moduli stack of G‑bundles on a smooth projective curve to D‑modules on the moduli stack of Langlands dual group bundles. In the derived setting, the moduli stacks are replaced by derived Artin stacks that remember higher automorphisms and obstruction theories. The category of perverse sheaves is then defined on the underlying classical truncation, but the six‑functor formalism extends to the derived context via the theory of ind‑coherent sheaves and the !‑pullback on derived stacks. Arinkin and Gaitsgory proved that the derived category of ind‑coherent sheaves on the derived stack of G‑local systems is equivalent, as a monoidal category, to the derived category of quasi‑coherent sheaves on the derived stack of Langlands dual local systems. The perverse t‑structure on the classical stack induces a t‑structure on the derived category whose heart is equivalent to the category of perverse sheaves on the classical stack. This compatibility allows one to transport the Hecke eigensheaf property to the derived level, where the Hecke functors act by convolution with kernels that are themselves perverse sheaves on the derived Hecke correspondence. The derived correspondence also incorporates the categorical trace of the loop group, leading to a categorification of the affine Hecke algebra that is compatible with the geometric Satake equivalence. A key technical input is the existence of a well‑behaved !‑pullback for ind‑coherent sheaves on derived stacks, which relies on the theory of quasi‑smooth morphisms and the cotangent complex. The result provides a framework where the spectral side of Langlands is naturally a derived stack, and the automorphic side is a category of perverse sheaves enriched by higher categorical structures.

The p‑adic Riemann–Hilbert Correspondence and Perverse Sheaves on Rigid Analytic Spaces

Over a p‑adic field K, the analogue of the complex Riemann–Hilbert correspondence relates regular holonomic D‑modules on a smooth rigid analytic variety to overconvergent isocrystals or, equivalently, to constructible sheaves of Qℓ‑vector spaces on the associated Berkovich space. The theory was developed by Kedlaya, Liu, and others, building on the work of Berthelot on arithmetic D‑modules. A regular holonomic arithmetic D‑module on a smooth formal scheme over the ring of integers of K gives rise, after generic fibre, to an overconvergent F‑isocrystal. The de Rham functor DR sends such a D‑module to a complex of sheaves on the Berkovich analytic space that is constructible with respect to a suitable semistable stratification. The perverse t‑structure on the derived category of constructible ℓ‑adic sheaves is defined by the same cohomological amplitude conditions as in the complex case, using the real dimension of the Berkovich space. The p‑adic Riemann–Hilbert theorem asserts that DR induces an equivalence between the derived category of regular holonomic arithmetic D‑modules and the derived category of constructible ℓ‑adic sheaves, and that this equivalence exchanges the natural t‑structure on D‑modules (defined by the order of poles) with the perverse t‑structure on sheaves. In particular, simple perverse sheaves correspond to irreducible regular holonomic D‑modules, and intermediate extension of a local system on a stratum matches the minimal extension of the corresponding D‑module. The correspondence respects the six functor formalism, Verdier duality, and the nearby and vanishing cycle functors for a rigid analytic function. A subtle point is that the weight filtration on the nearby cycles is governed by the monodromy operator coming from the action of the inertia group, and the purity of graded pieces follows from the p‑adic analogue of the decomposition theorem proved by Abe and Caro using the theory of weights for overconvergent isocrystals.

Let X be a derived Artin stack locally of finite presentation over ℂ, and let X^cl be its classical truncation. Suppose F ∈ D^b_c(X^cl, ℚ) is a constructible complex. Explain why the condition “F is perverse on X^cl” is equivalent to “the !‑pullback of F to any derived atlas U → X lies in the perverse heart of D^b_c(U, ℚ)”.
The perverse t‑structure on a derived stack is defined by descent: a complex F on X^cl is perverse iff for every smooth (or étale) atlas U → X, the pullback F|_U is perverse on the smooth variety U. Since the atlas is smooth, the pullback functor is t‑exact for the perverse t‑structure. Conversely, if F|_U is perverse for a covering atlas, the descent data for the perverse heart (which is a stack of categories) guarantees that F itself belongs to the heart on X^cl. The derived structure does not affect the underlying topological space, so the cohomological amplitude conditions are checked on the classical truncation. Hence the two conditions are equivalent.

Perverse Sheaves on Moduli Spaces of Higgs Bundles and the Non‑Abelian Hodge Correspondence

The moduli space M_Higgs(G) of semistable Higgs bundles of rank n and fixed determinant on a smooth projective curve carries a natural hyperkähler structure. The non‑abelian Hodge correspondence, due to Simpson, identifies the underlying topological space of M_Higgs(G) with the character variety M_B(G) of representations of the fundamental group into the complex reductive group G. Under this homeomorphism, the perverse sheaf IC(M_Higgs) of intersection cohomology corresponds to the perverse sheaf IC(M_B) on the character variety. The decomposition theorem for the Hitchin fibration h: M_Higgs → A (where A is an affine space of Hitchin base) yields a direct sum decomposition of Rh_*ℚ[dim M_Higgs] into shifted simple perverse sheaves on A. The support of each summand is a Lagrangian subvariety of A, and the generic stalk is an irreducible representation of the relative Weyl group. This geometric picture underlies the proof of the P = W conjecture (de Cataldo–Hausel–Migliorini) which equates the perverse Leray filtration on the cohomology of M_Higgs with the weight filtration on the cohomology of M_B. The perverse sheaves appearing in the decomposition are precisely the intermediate extensions of local systems on the regular locus of the Hitchin base, and their endomorphism algebras are isomorphic to the cohomology rings of the corresponding Hitchin fibres. Moreover, the Hecke correspondences on M_Higgs act by convolution with perverse sheaves on the Hecke stack, providing a geometric realization of the Langlands dual group action on the cohomology of M_Higgs. The theory extends to parabolic Higgs bundles and to wild character varieties, where the perverse sheaves acquire irregular singularities and the corresponding D‑modules are meromorphic connections with Stokes data.

Perverse Sheaves and the Theory of Constructible Functions: Euler Characteristics and the MacPherson Transformation

Constructible functions on a complex algebraic variety X form an abelian group CF(X) generated by indicator functions of constructible subsets. The Euler characteristic with compact support gives a homomorphism χ_c: CF(X) → ℤ. MacPherson defined a natural transformation c_*: CF(X) → H_*BM(X, ℤ) (Borel–Moore homology) that sends the constant function 1_X to the total Chern class of the tangent bundle evaluated on the fundamental class, and satisfies c_*(1_Z) = c_*(IC_Z) for any locally closed subvariety Z. The theory of perverse sheaves provides a sheaf‑theoretic lift of this transformation: the functor that sends a constructible complex K to its Euler characteristic constructible function χ(K) (stalkwise Euler characteristic) factors through the perverse heart. In fact, the group of constructible functions is isomorphic to the Grothendieck group of the category of perverse sheaves, and the MacPherson transformation corresponds to the Chern class map from the Grothendieck group of perverse sheaves to Borel–Moore homology. This identification is compatible with the six functor formalism: pushforward of constructible functions matches the Euler characteristic of the derived pushforward, and the nearby cycle functor on constructible functions corresponds to the perverse nearby cycles. The theory has been extended to the equivariant setting by Ohmoto, where the target is equivariant Borel–Moore homology, and to the motivic setting where constructible functions are replaced by classes in the Grothendieck ring of varieties. A practical consequence is that the Euler characteristic of a perverse sheaf can be computed by integrating its constructible function against the MacPherson class, which reduces many intersection cohomology calculations to combinatorial data on a stratification.

Perverse Sheaves on Orbifolds and Global Quotient Stacks: Equivariant Intersection Cohomology

Let X be a complex algebraic variety with an action of a finite group G. The quotient stack [X/G] is a smooth Deligne–Mumford stack if the action has finite stabilizers. The derived category of constructible sheaves on [X/G] is equivalent to the G‑equivariant derived category D^bG,c(X, ℚ). The perverse t‑structure on the stack is defined by the same cohomological amplitude conditions, using the real dimension of the coarse moduli space. The equivariant intersection cohomology IH^*_G(Z) is the hypercohomology of the equivariant IC complex. For non‑free actions, the decomposition theorem for the quotient map π: X → [X/G] yields a direct sum decomposition of Rπ_*ℚ_X[dim X] into shifted simple perverse sheaves on the stack, reflecting the representation theory of the stabilizer groups. The equivariant perverse sheaves also appear in the geometric Satake correspondence for disconnected groups, where the affine Grassmannian is replaced by a global quotient stack of the form [Gr_G / Γ] for a finite group Γ acting by diagram automorphisms. The category of Γ‑equivariant perverse sheaves on Gr_G categorifies the representation category of the Langlands dual group extended by Γ.

Perverse Sheaves on Toric Varieties and the Combinatorial Intersection Cohomology

Let X be a complex projective toric variety defined by a rational fan Σ in a lattice N ≅ ℤⁿ. The torus orbits O(σ) for cones σ ∈ Σ give a natural stratification of X by affine spaces. The intersection cohomology complex IC_X(ℚ) is a perverse sheaf whose stalk cohomology at a point of the orbit O(σ) is concentrated in degrees determined by the local h‑vector of the star of σ. Barthel, Brasselet, and Fieseler proved that the Poincaré polynomial of the stalk IC_X(ℚ)_x for x ∈ O(σ) equals the h‑polynomial of the link of σ in the order complex of Σ. This combinatorial description makes the decomposition theorem for a toric morphism f : X → Y induced by a fan refinement completely explicit: Rf_* IC_X(ℚ) splits as a direct sum of shifted IC complexes of orbit closures in Y, and the multiplicities are given by the local h‑vectors of the corresponding cones. De Cataldo and Migliorini used this to give a purely combinatorial proof of the hard Lefschetz theorem for toric varieties, showing that the Lefschetz operator acts on the intersection cohomology as multiplication by the class of an ample torus‑invariant divisor, and the primitive decomposition corresponds to the Lefschetz decomposition of the Stanley–Reisner ring of Σ. The perverse t‑structure on the derived category of constructible sheaves on X coincides with the t‑structure whose heart consists of complexes whose cohomology sheaves are constructible with respect to the torus orbits and satisfy the usual support and cosupport conditions; this heart is equivalent to the category of perverse sheaves on the associated toric stack. A practical consequence is that the intersection cohomology Betti numbers of any projective toric variety can be read off from the f‑vector of its fan, and the decomposition theorem for toric resolutions of singularities reduces to a statement about the positivity of certain local h‑vectors, which is a theorem of Stanley.

Perverse Sheaves on Moduli Spaces of Parabolic Bundles and the Parabolic Hitchin Fibration

Let C be a smooth projective curve of genus g ≥ 2 and fix a divisor D = p₁ + … + p_m of distinct points. A parabolic bundle of rank r and weights α_i ∈ (0,1) at each p_i is a vector bundle E on C together with a flag of subspaces in the fibre Ep_i and a weight for each step. The moduli space M_par of stable parabolic bundles with fixed determinant is a smooth quasi‑projective variety of dimension r²(g−1)+1 + Σ_i (r−1). The parabolic Hitchin map h_par : M_par → A_par sends a parabolic Higgs bundle (E, Φ) to the characteristic polynomial of Φ, where Φ is required to have residues compatible with the parabolic structure. The base A_par is an affine space of dimension equal to half the dimension of M_par. The decomposition theorem of Beĭlinson–Bernstein–Deligne–Gabber applies to the proper morphism h_par after a suitable compactification (e.g. the parabolic Higgs moduli space of semistable objects). The direct image Rh_par* ℚM_par[dim M_par] splits into a direct sum of shifted simple perverse sheaves on A_par, each supported on a locally closed stratum corresponding to a spectral curve with prescribed singularities at the points over D. The perverse Leray filtration P_k H^*(M_par) induced by the truncation functors τ≤k on the perverse t‑structure coincides with the weight filtration coming from the ℂ*‑action scaling the Higgs field. Hausel and Thaddeus computed the E‑polynomials of the fibres and showed that the perverse filtration is multiplicative with respect to the cup product. The nearby and vanishing cycle functors for the parabolic Hitchin fibration are perverse and their stalks encode the monodromy of the spectral curves, giving a geometric realization of the affine Springer fibres for the loop group of GL_r with parabolic level structure.

Perverse Sheaves and the Categorical Action of Braid Groups on Derived Categories

The braid group B_n acts on the bounded derived category of constructible sheaves on the flag variety Fl(ℂⁿ) via spherical twists associated to the simple roots. Seidel and Thomas showed that for each simple reflection s_i there is a spherical object S_i in D^b_c(Fl) whose twist functor TS_i satisfies the braid relations up to natural isomorphism. In the perverse setting, the spherical objects are the IC complexes of the Schubert varieties corresponding to the simple reflections; their convolution product realises the Hecke algebra, and the braid group action is obtained by passing to the graded version of the Hecke category. This compatibility implies that the braid group acts on the intersection cohomology of Schubert varieties, giving a geometric interpretation of the Kazhdan–Lusztig basis. Moreover, the categorical action extends to the derived category of mixed Hodge modules, where the weight filtration provides a grading that matches the q‑parameter of the quantum group.

Perverse Sheaves on Character Varieties and the Non‑Abelian Hodge Correspondence for Higher Rank

Let Y be a projective Calabi–Yau variety with at worst Gorenstein terminal singularities, and let π : X → Y be a crepant resolution. Perverse Sheaves on Shimura Varieties and the Cohomology of Automorphic Forms

Shimura varieties provide a central testing ground for the interplay between perverse sheaves, the Langlands program, and the cohomology of automorphic forms. Let ShK(G, X) be a Shimura variety of Hodge type associated to a reductive group G over Q and a compact open subgroup K ⊂ G(Af). The Baily–Borel compactification ShK* is a projective variety with a natural stratification by locally symmetric spaces attached to boundary components. The intersection cohomology complex IC(ShK*, Vλ) for an irreducible algebraic representation Vλ of G carries a pure Hodge structure and an action of the Hecke algebra H(G(Af)//K). The decomposition theorem for the proper map ShK → ShK* expresses Rj*Vλ as a direct sum of shifted simple perverse sheaves, each of which is an intersection cohomology complex of a local system on a stratum. The ℓ-adic realization of these perverse sheaves yields Galois representations whose Frobenius traces are given by the Arthur–Selberg trace formula. The work of Kottwitz, Harris–Taylor, and Scholze establishes that the cohomology of the Shimura variety, computed via the perverse Leray filtration of the Hitchin-type fibration on the moduli space of G-bundles, realizes the local Langlands correspondence for G(Qp). In particular, the nearby cycles complex ψf(IC) for the Hasse invariant f on the special fibre of a Shimura variety at a prime of bad reduction is a perverse sheaf whose stalks are the cohomology of affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties. The perverse filtration on H*(ShK, Vλ) induced by the map to the minimal compactification coincides with the weight filtration on the corresponding automorphic representations, a geometric incarnation of the Arthur conjectures. Perverse Sheaves and the Microlocal Theory of Sheaves: The Kashiwara–Schapira Approach

The microlocal theory of sheaves, developed by Kashiwara and Schapira in Sheaves on Manifolds, provides a symplectic geometric framework for understanding perverse sheaves on real and complex analytic spaces. Let X be a real analytic manifold and T*X its cotangent bundle. The microsupport SS(F) of a constructible complex F ∈ Dbc(X) is a closed conic subset of T*X that measures the directions in which F fails to be locally constant. For a perverse sheaf P on a complex algebraic variety X stratified by a Whitney stratification S, the microsupport is contained in the union of conormal bundles TS*X. The fundamental result of Kashiwara–Schapira states that the category of perverse sheaves on X with respect to a middle perversity is equivalent to the category of constructible sheaves whose microsupport is Lagrangian and satisfies a non-degeneracy condition along the zero section. This equivalence is realized by the Fourier–Sato transform, which exchanges the perverse t-structure with a t-structure defined by support conditions in the cotangent bundle. The microlocal Euler characteristic χμ(F) defines a constructible function on T*X that is invariant under contact transformations. For a holonomic D-module M, the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence identifies its solution complex Sol(M) as a perverse sheaf whose microsupport is the characteristic variety Char(M). The microlocal Morse lemma describes the stalk of the nearby cycles functor ψf(F) in terms of the microlocal monodromy along the conormal direction df. This formalism underlies the proof of the index theorem for constructible sheaves and the computation of the Euler characteristic of perverse sheaves via the microlocal intersection index. In the context of the geometric Langlands program, the microlocal theory provides a symplectic interpretation of the Hecke eigensheaf condition: a perverse sheaf F on BunG is a Hecke eigensheaf with eigenvalue a local system E if and only if its microsupport is contained in the global nilpotent cone and its singular support matches the spectral data of E. Worked Example: Microsupport of the Intersection Cohomology Complex on a Stratified Space

Perverse Sheaves on the Hilbert Scheme of Points and the McKay Correspondence

The Hilbert scheme of n points on a smooth surface X, denoted X[n], parametrizes zero-dimensional subschemes of length n. When X = C2, the Hilbert scheme C2[n] is a crepant resolution of the symmetric product Symn(C2) ≅ C2/Sn. The McKay correspondence relates the representation theory of the symmetric group Sn to the geometry of C2[n]. The intersection cohomology of the symmetric product is isomorphic to the ordinary cohomology of the Hilbert scheme: IH*(Symn(C2)) ≅ H*(C2[n]). This isomorphism is realized by the decomposition theorem for the Hilbert–Chow morphism π : C2[n] → Symn(C2). The pushforward Rπ*CC2[n][2n] splits as a direct sum of shifted simple perverse sheaves on Symn(C2). The simple perverse sheaves are the intersection cohomology complexes IC(Zλ) of the strata Zλ indexed by partitions λ of n, each corresponding to an irreducible representation of Sn. The stalk cohomology of IC(Zλ) at a point of type μ is given by the Kostka numbers Kλμ, which are the transition coefficients between Schur functions and monomial symmetric functions. The perverse filtration on H*(C2[n]) induced by π coincides with the weight filtration on the cohomology of the Hilbert scheme, and the associated graded pieces are the irreducible representations of Sn. The work of Nakajima and Grojnowski identifies the direct sum ⊕n≥0 H*(C2[n]) with the Fock space representation of the Heisenberg algebra, where the creation operators are given by the correspondences Zn,n+1 ⊂ C2[n] × C2[n+1]. The perverse sheaf interpretation of this action uses the nearby cycles functor for the map C2[n] → C2 given by the support of the subscheme. For a finite subgroup Γ ⊂ SL2(C), the McKay correspondence extends to the minimal resolution Y → C2/Γ. The derived category Dbcoh(Y) is equivalent to the derived category of Γ-equivariant coherent sheaves on C2, and under this equivalence the perverse coherent t-structure on Y corresponds to a perverse t-structure on the equivariant derived category. The simple perverse coherent sheaves are the structure sheaves of the exceptional curves, and their intersection cohomology complexes realize the irreducible representations of Γ.

Perverse Sheaves and the Geometric Eisenstein Series in the Langlands Program

The geometric Eisenstein series is a functor Eis : Db(BunT) → Db(BunG) that plays a central role in the geometric Langlands program, where G is a reductive group, T a maximal torus, and BunG the moduli stack of G-bundles on a curve X. The functor is defined as the composition Eis = p!q* where q : BunB → BunT and p : BunB → BunG are the natural maps from the moduli stack of B-bundles. The geometric Eisenstein series does not preserve the perverse t-structure in general; its perverse cohomology sheaves pHi(Eis(F)) are computed by the geometric constant term functor CT = q!p*. The key result of Braverman–Gaitsgory and Drinfeld–Gaitsgory states that the perverse cohomology of the Eisenstein series is controlled by the Weyl group action on the category of perverse sheaves on BunT. For a character sheaf F on BunT corresponding to a local system E on X, the Eisenstein series Eis(F) is a perverse sheaf if and only if E is in general position with respect to the roots of G. When E is not in general position, the perverse cohomology sheaves are supported on the strata of BunG corresponding to Levi subgroups containing the centralizer of E. The constant term functor CT admits a filtration whose associated graded pieces are indexed by the Weyl group, and the perverse cohomology of Eis(F) is computed by a spectral sequence whose E1-page involves the perverse sheaves on BunM for Levi subgroups M. This spectral sequence degenerates at E2 and yields the geometric analogue of the Langlands–Shahidi method for L-functions. The geometric Eisenstein series also provides a construction of the Whittaker sheaf: for a non-degenerate character ψ of the unipotent radical U, the Whittaker functor Whitψ = p!(q* ⊗ ψ) maps perverse sheaves on BunG to perverse sheaves on BunT. The composition Whitψ ∘ Eis is isomorphic to a direct sum of shifts of the identity functor, reflecting the functional equation of the Eisenstein series. The perverse sheaf interpretation of the geometric Eisenstein series is essential for the proof of the geometric Langlands correspondence for GLn by Lafforgue and for the categorical geometric Langlands program of Gaitsgory–Rozenblyum.

Perverse Sheaves and the Theory of Weights: Purity, Mixedness, and the Decomposition Theorem

The theory of weights, developed by Deligne in Weil II, provides the arithmetic foundation for the decomposition theorem over finite fields and its extension to complex varieties via the comparison theorems. Let X be a variety over a finite field Fq. A constructible ℓ-adic sheaf F on X is pure of weight w if for every closed point x ∈ X, the eigenvalues of the geometric Frobenius Frobx on the stalk Fx are algebraic numbers all of whose complex absolute values equal qw/2. A complex K ∈ Dbc(X, Qℓ) is mixed of weight ≤ w if its cohomology sheaves Hi(K) are mixed of weight ≤ w + i. The perverse t-structure is compatible with weights: if K is mixed of weight ≤ w, then pHi(K) is mixed of weight ≤ w. For a proper morphism f : X → Y with X smooth of dimension d, the complex Rf*Qℓ[d] is mixed of weight ≤ 0. The decomposition theorem states that Rf*Qℓ[d] ≅ ⊕i pHi(Rf*Qℓ[d])[−i], where each perverse cohomology sheaf pHi is a direct sum of simple perverse sheaves IC(Z, L) that are pure of weight i. The purity of the intersection cohomology complexes is the geometric incarnation of the Riemann hypothesis for varieties over finite fields. Over C, the theory of mixed Hodge modules of Saito provides an analogous weight filtration W• on the cohomology of perverse sheaves. The decomposition theorem for a projective morphism f : X → Y of complex algebraic varieties states that Rf*ICX ≅ ⊕i pHi(Rf*ICX)[−i], and the perverse filtration P• on H*(X) induced by the truncation functors pτ≤k coincides with the weight filtration W• on the mixed Hodge structure of H*(X). The relative hard Lefschetz theorem for perverse sheaves states that for an ample line bundle L on X, the cup product with c1(L) induces isomorphisms pH−k(Rf*ICX) ≅ pHk(Rf*ICX) for all k ≥ 0. This isomorphism is a morphism of mixed Hodge modules and preserves the weight filtration. The theory of weights is also essential for the proof of the decomposition theorem for non-projective morphisms via the method of weights developed by de Cataldo and Migliorini, where one reduces to the projective case by compactifying the morphism and using the purity of the nearby cycles complex.

Perverse Sheaves on the Affine Flag Variety and the Bezrukavnikov Equivalence

The affine flag variety FlG of a reductive group G over an algebraically closed field carries a natural stratification by Iwahori orbits indexed by the affine Weyl group Waff. The category of constructible complexes on FlG with respect to this stratification admits a perverse t‑structure whose heart consists of perverse sheaves that are equivariant for the Iwahori subgroup. Bezrukavnikov proved that the derived category of Iwahori‑equivariant perverse sheaves on FlG is equivalent, as a triangulated category, to the derived category of modules over the affine Hecke algebra Haff at a generic parameter. The equivalence is realized by a convolution functor with the Steinberg variety StG = FlG ×GrG FlG, where GrG is the affine Grassmannian. Under this equivalence, the standard perverse sheaves IC(w) corresponding to Schubert cells map to the standard modules of Haff, and the simple perverse sheaves map to the simple modules. The convolution product on perverse sheaves corresponds to the multiplication in the affine Hecke algebra, and the Verdier duality on FlG translates to the involution on Haff induced by the anti‑automorphism of the affine Weyl group. The Bezrukavnikov equivalence also extends to the setting of mixed Hodge modules, where the weight filtration on perverse sheaves matches the Hodge filtration on the corresponding Haff‑modules, giving a Hodge‑theoretic interpretation of the affine Hecke algebra representation theory.

Perverse Sheaves and the Wild Geometric Langlands Correspondence

The wild geometric Langlands program studies the relationship between perverse sheaves on the moduli stack LocSysG(X, D) of G‑local systems on a smooth projective curve X with prescribed irregular singularities along a divisor D, and coherent sheaves on the moduli stack of Higgs bundles with wild ramification. The irregular singularities are encoded by a formal type at each point of D, leading to a stratification of LocSysG(X, D) by the Jordan normal form of the leading term of the connection. The perverse t‑structure on this stratified stack is defined using the middle perversity, and the simple perverse sheaves are the intersection cohomology complexes of the closures of the strata, twisted by local systems arising from the monodromy of the irregular connection. The wild geometric Langlands conjecture, formulated by Arinkin and Gaitsgory, predicts an equivalence between the derived category of perverse sheaves on LocSysG(X, D) and the derived category of ind‑coherent sheaves on the corresponding moduli of Higgs bundles with wild ramification. This equivalence is expected to be compatible with the Hecke functors on the automorphic side, which are realized as convolution with perverse sheaves on the affine Grassmannian with level structure determined by the irregular type. The nearby and vanishing cycles functors for the deformation of the irregular connection play a crucial role in constructing the perverse sheaves that correspond to the spectral data. In the case of G = GLn, the conjecture has been proved for certain irregular types using the theory of opers and the geometric Langlands correspondence for the group G with a Borel reduction. The proof relies on the decomposition theorem for the Hitchin fibration with wild ramification, where the perverse Leray filtration coincides with the weight filtration on the mixed Hodge structure of the moduli space. The wild ramification introduces additional Stokes data, which are captured by the microlocal theory of perverse sheaves: the microsupport of the relevant perverse sheaves is a Lagrangian subvariety of the cotangent bundle of LocSysG(X, D) that encodes the Stokes matrices. This microlocal description provides a bridge to the categorical action of the braid group on the derived category of perverse sheaves, as the Stokes matrices satisfy the braid relations.

Worked Example: Perverse Sheaf on an Affine Flag Stratum
Let G = SL2 over C. The affine flag variety FlSL2 has two Iwahori orbits: the open cell C0 (dimension 1) and the closed point C1 (dimension 0). Compute the stalk cohomology of the intersection cohomology complex IC(C0) at the closed point.
The stratum C0 is isomorphic to C* and its closure is the whole affine flag variety, which is a union of C0 and the point C1. The intersection cohomology complex IC(C0) is the intermediate extension of the constant sheaf C[1] on C0. Since the link of C1 in the closure is a circle S1, the stalk cohomology at C1 is the reduced cohomology of the link shifted by the middle perversity. For a 1‑dimensional stratum, the middle perversity gives p = 0, so the stalk cohomology is H0(S1) = C in degree 0 and H1(S1) = C in degree 1. However, the intermediate extension kills the top cohomology of the link, leaving only the degree 0 part. Thus the stalk of IC(C0) at C1 is C concentrated in degree 0. The costalk is zero in all degrees because the link has no reduced cohomology in negative degrees. This matches the known fact that the Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomial for the affine Weyl group of type Ã1 is 1.
Exam‑Day Strategy
When a question asks for the stalk or costalk of an intersection cohomology complex on a stratified space, first identify the link of the stratum in the closure. Remember that the intermediate extension kills the top non‑vanishing cohomology of the link. This algorithm works for any middle perversity on a complex algebraic variety.

Perverse Sheaves and the Categorical Action of the Double Affine Hecke Algebra

The double affine Hecke algebra (DAHA) of type Ãn−1 is generated by the affine Hecke algebra Haff, a polynomial algebra in the variables X1, …, Xn, and a second copy of the affine Hecke algebra acting by the variables Y1, …, Yn, subject to the cross relations TiXi = Xi+1Ti + (q−1)Xi and TiYi = Yi+1Ti + (q−1)Yi. Bezrukavnikov and collaborators constructed a categorical action of the DAHA on the derived category of coherent sheaves on the Hilbert scheme of points on C2, and equivalently on the derived category of Iwahori‑equivariant perverse sheaves on the affine flag variety of GLn. The generators Xi act by tensoring with the line bundles O(Di) on the Hilbert scheme, while the generators Yi act by the Fourier–Mukai transforms associated to the universal family. On the perverse sheaf side, the X‑action corresponds to the convolution with the perverse sheaves supported on the Schubert divisors in the affine flag variety, and the Y‑action corresponds to the nearby cycles functors for the deformation of the affine flag variety to the affine Grassmannian. The braid group relations in the DAHA are realized by the categorical braid group action on the derived category of perverse sheaves, which is induced by the monodromy of the universal family over the space of parameters. The polynomial representation of the DAHA is recovered by taking the Grothendieck group of the category of perverse sheaves and identifying the classes of the standard perverse sheaves with the nonsymmetric Macdonald polynomials. The categorical action also yields a geometric interpretation of the Cherednik–Dunkl operators: they arise as the endomorphisms of the perverse sheaf corresponding to the trivial representation of the finite Hecke algebra. This framework has been extended to other root systems using the theory of perverse sheaves on the affine flag varieties of arbitrary reductive groups, and it provides a geometric proof of the Macdonald positivity conjecture for the nonsymmetric Macdonald polynomials.

Perverse Sheaves and the Decomposition Theorem for Semi‑Small Resolutions

The decomposition theorem of Beilinson–Bernstein–Deligne–Gabber asserts that for a proper morphism f : X → Y of complex algebraic varieties, the derived pushforward Rf*ℚX[dim X] splits in the derived category of constructible sheaves as a direct sum of shifted intersection cohomology complexes of subvarieties of Y. When f is a resolution of singularities that is semi‑small — i.e. the dimension of the fibre over any point y ∈ Y is at most half the codimension of the stratum containing y — the decomposition takes a particularly simple form: only the intersection cohomology complex of Y itself appears, and it occurs with multiplicity one. This purity phenomenon is a cornerstone of the geometric Satake correspondence and of the proof of the Kazhdan–Lusztig conjectures. In the semi‑small case the perverse Leray filtration on the cohomology of the fibres coincides with the weight filtration coming from the mixed Hodge structure on the intersection cohomology of Y. The proof uses the relative hard Lefschetz theorem for the ample line bundle on the fibres and the fact that the perverse cohomology sheaves pHi(Rf*ℚX[dim X]) are semi‑simple perverse sheaves. A standard example is the Springer resolution T*(G/B) → N of the nilpotent cone; it is semi‑small and its decomposition yields the Springer correspondence between irreducible representations of the Weyl group and irreducible components of the Springer fibres.

Key Mechanism

  • The semi‑small condition guarantees that the direct image complex is perverse and concentrated in degree zero.
  • Verdier duality identifies the summands with their duals, forcing the decomposition to be self‑dual.
  • The support of each summand is the closure of a stratum where the map is a locally trivial fibration.

The geometric Langlands correspondence for GLn predicts an equivalence between the derived category of ℓ‑adic perverse sheaves on the moduli stack BunGLn of rank‑n vector bundles on a smooth projective curve X and the derived category of coherent sheaves on the moduli stack LocSysGLn of rank‑n local systems. Laumon proved the correspondence for n=1, Drinfeld for n=2, and Gaitsgory–Lurie–Nadler–Raskin established it for general n using the theory of perverse sheaves on Artin stacks and the categorical geometric Langlands equivalence. The resulting perverse sheaf is automatically a Hecke eigensheaf, and its eigenvalue is the local system obtained by the spectral decomposition of the Hitchin fibration.

Perverse Sheaves and the Non‑Abelian Hodge Correspondence in Higher Dimensions

Simpson’s non‑abelian Hodge correspondence on a smooth projective variety X of dimension > 1 relates the moduli space of semistable Higgs bundles with vanishing Chern classes to the moduli space of semisimple local systems. The correspondence is a homeomorphism of the underlying analytic spaces but not an isomorphism of algebraic varieties. In higher dimensions the decomposition theorem for the Hitchin fibration fails in general because the fibres are not abelian varieties and the map is not proper. However, using the theory of mixed Hodge modules and Saito’s theory of perverse sheaves, one can construct a perverse sheaf on the moduli stack of Higgs bundles whose hypercohomology carries a mixed Hodge structure that matches the weight filtration on the Betti moduli space. The key tool is the perverse filtration on the cohomology of the Higgs moduli space induced by the Hitchin map, which is conjectured to coincide with the weight filtration on the local system side — the P = W conjecture, proved for curves by de Cataldo–Hausel–Migliorini and extended to certain higher‑dimensional cases by Maulik–Shen. The perverse sheaves appearing are the intersection cohomology complexes of the closures of the loci where the Hitchin fibre has a given singularity type, and their supports are the very stable loci. The categorical version of the correspondence, due to Arinkin–Gaitsgory and others, uses the derived category of perverse sheaves on the moduli stack of Higgs bundles and the derived category of coherent sheaves on the moduli stack of local systems, linked by a Fourier–Mukai transform with kernel given by the universal Higgs bundle.

Perverse Sheaves on the Moduli of G‑Local Systems and the Spectral Decomposition

The moduli stack LocSysG(X) of G‑local systems on a smooth projective curve X carries a natural perverse t‑structure whose heart consists of perverse sheaves constructible with respect to the stratification by Harder–Narasimhan type. The spectral decomposition, pioneered by Drinfeld and Laumon in the 1990s, expresses the constant sheaf on the stack of G‑bundles as a direct sum of intersection cohomology complexes of the closures of the very stable loci, each equipped with a Hecke eigensheaf property. The key mechanism is the geometric Satake equivalence for the affine Grassmannian GrG, which identifies the category of spherical perverse sheaves on GrG with the representation category of the Langlands dual group LG. Under this equivalence, the Hecke operators act on perverse sheaves on BunG by convolution with spherical perverse sheaves, and the eigen‑condition translates into a factorisation property of the corresponding local system on X. The decomposition theorem for the Hitchin fibration, proved by Ngô Bao Châu for the fundamental lemma, provides the global geometric input: the pushforward of the constant sheaf along the Hitchin map splits into a direct sum of shifted perverse sheaves, each supported on a Lagrangian subvariety of the Hitchin base. The summands are precisely the intersection cohomology complexes of the closures of the very stable loci, and their endomorphism algebras realise the Hecke algebra of LG. This picture extends to arbitrary reductive groups via the work of Yun and others on the global nilpotent cone, where the perverse sheaves appearing are the Springer sheaves attached to nilpotent orbits in the Lie algebra of LG. The spectral decomposition thus furnishes a geometric realisation of the automorphic Langlands correspondence for function fields, with perverse sheaves playing the role of automorphic forms.

Perverse Sheaves and the Decomposition Theorem for Non‑Proper Morphisms via Compactifications

The classical decomposition theorem of Beilinson–Bernstein–Deligne–Gabber applies to proper morphisms of algebraic varieties. For non‑proper morphisms, one can often obtain a decomposition by choosing a suitable compactification that preserves the perverse t‑structure. Nagata’s compactification theorem guarantees the existence of a proper morphism f̄ : X̄ → Y extending a given separated morphism f : X → Y of finite type. The crucial point is to select a compactification such that the boundary divisor is a strict normal crossings divisor relative to the stratification on X and the perverse sheaf K on X extends to a perverse sheaf K̄ on X̄ with no contributions from the boundary in the perverse cohomology. This is achieved by requiring that the pair (X̄, D) be a good compactification in the sense of Saito’s theory of mixed Hodge modules: the nearby and vanishing cycles functors along the boundary must be t‑exact for the perverse t‑structure. When such a compactification exists, the decomposition theorem for f̄ yields a direct sum decomposition of Rf̄*K̄ into shifted intersection cohomology complexes on Y. Restricting to the open subset Y gives the desired decomposition for Rf*K. The method was used by de Cataldo and Migliorini to prove the decomposition theorem for the Hitchin fibration after a suitable compactification of the total space by adding the nilpotent cone. A common pitfall is to assume that any compactification works; the perverse t‑structure is not preserved under arbitrary blow‑ups, and the boundary contributions can introduce extra summands that do not descend to the original morphism. The correct criterion is that the compactification be perversely smooth, i.e. the inclusion of the boundary is a perverse morphism. This condition is satisfied for semi‑small resolutions and for the Bala–Carter compactification of the nilpotent cone, but fails for many naive projective closures.

Perverse Sheaves and the Riemann–Hilbert Correspondence for Irregular Connections on Higher‑Dimensional Varieties

The classical Riemann–Hilbert correspondence of Deligne and Kashiwara establishes an equivalence between regular holonomic D‑modules on a smooth complex variety X and perverse sheaves constructible with respect to a stratification. For irregular connections, the correspondence must be enriched by Stokes data. In dimension one, the wild Riemann–Hilbert correspondence of Malgrange, Sibuya, and later Kedlaya, describes irregular connections on a punctured disc in terms of perverse sheaves on the real blow‑up equipped with a Stokes filtration. In higher dimensions, the theory was developed by Sabbah and Mochizuki using the notion of good formal structure and the irregular Riemann–Hilbert functor Solirr which lands in the category of enhanced perverse sheaves on the real oriented blow‑up of X along the divisor of irregularity. The enhanced perverse sheaves are pairs (P, E) where P is a perverse sheaf on the real blow‑up and E is a local system of finite‑dimensional vector spaces on the Stokes torus, satisfying a compatibility condition with the Stokes matrices. The decomposition theorem for the pushforward of an irregular holonomic D‑module along a projective morphism was proved by Mochizuki using the theory of wild harmonic bundles and the associated twistor D‑modules. The perverse sheaves appearing in the decomposition are the intersection cohomology complexes of the closures of the strata where the irregular type is constant, twisted by the Stokes local systems. A key difference from the regular case is that the perverse t‑structure on the category of enhanced perverse sheaves is not the standard one; it is shifted by the irregularity complex, and the duality functor involves the Stokes dual local system. The correspondence is functorial with respect to pullback and pushforward for morphisms that are compatible with the irregular divisors, and it respects the tensor product up to a convolution of Stokes data. This framework underlies the geometric Langlands correspondence for wild ramification, where the Hecke eigensheaves are replaced by enhanced perverse sheaves on the moduli stack of irregular connections.

Perverse Sheaves and the Categorical Action of the Affine Hecke Algebra

The affine Hecke algebra Haff of a reductive group G acts categorically on the derived category of constructible sheaves on the affine flag variety FlG. This action was constructed by Bezrukavnikov and Finkelberg using the geometry of the Steinberg variety and the convolution product on the equivariant derived category DG(O)×G(O)(FlG × FlG). The generators of Haff correspond to the perverse sheaves ICw supported on the closures of the Iwahori orbits indexed by the affine Weyl group elements w. The convolution of these perverse sheaves realises the quadratic and braid relations of the affine Hecke algebra at the level of complexes, not merely on Grothendieck groups. The categorical action extends to an action of the double affine Hecke algebra (DAHA) when one incorporates the loop rotation equivariance, as shown by Bezrukavnikov, Finkelberg, and Mirković. The perverse sheaves ICw are the intersection cohomology complexes of the Schubert varieties in FlG, and their stalks compute the Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials for the affine Weyl group. The categorical action is compatible with the geometric Satake equivalence: under the Satake functor, the spherical Hecke category (perverse sheaves on the affine Grassmannian) maps to the category of representations of the Langlands dual group, and the affine Hecke action corresponds to the action of the affine Hecke algebra on the principal series representations. A crucial technical point is that the convolution product is t‑exact for the perverse t‑structure only after a suitable shift; the correct normalisation uses the dimension of the corresponding Schubert cell. The categorical action also appears in the study of the modular representation theory of reductive groups over fields of positive characteristic, where the perverse sheaves are replaced by parity sheaves and the affine Hecke algebra is replaced by its modular version.

Perverse Sheaves on Singular Varieties with Non‑Isolated Singularities and the Intersection Cohomology Complex

Intersection cohomology was originally defined by Goresky and MacPherson for pseudomanifolds with isolated singularities. The extension to varieties with non‑isolated singularities uses the notion of a stratification satisfying Whitney’s conditions (a) and (b). The intersection cohomology complex ICX is the unique (up to isomorphism) perverse sheaf on X whose restriction to the regular part is the shifted constant sheaf and whose stalks and costalks on singular strata satisfy the support and cosupport conditions: for a stratum S of codimension d, the stalk cohomology vanishes above degree −d and the costalk cohomology vanishes below degree d. When the singularities are not isolated, the link of a stratum may itself be singular, and the local intersection cohomology groups are computed by the intersection cohomology of the link. The decomposition theorem for a proper map f : X → Y with X possibly singular states that Rf*ICX splits into a direct sum of shifted IC complexes of the closures of the images of the relevant strata, provided f is semi‑small or more generally that the perverse Leray filtration degenerates. In the presence of non‑isolated singularities, the semi‑small condition must be checked on each stratum: the dimension of the fibre of f over a point in a stratum S must be at most half the codimension of S in X. If this fails, the pushforward may contain perverse sheaves that are not intersection cohomology complexes of subvarieties, but rather more general perverse sheaves with non‑trivial monodromy along the strata. Saito’s theory of mixed Hodge modules provides a robust framework: the intersection cohomology complex underlies a pure Hodge module of weight dim X, and the decomposition theorem holds in the category of mixed Hodge modules for any projective morphism. The weight filtration on the perverse cohomology sheaves gives a refinement of the decomposition, and the graded pieces are again intersection cohomology complexes of strata closures with twisted coefficients. This weighted decomposition is essential for the proof of the P = W conjecture for certain moduli spaces, where the perverse Leray filtration on the Hitchin fibration is identified with the weight filtration on the Betti moduli space.

Examiner's technique: identifying the correct perverse shift in a convolution product
Let G be a reductive group over ℂ, FlG its affine flag variety, and ICw the intersection cohomology complex of the Schubert variety Xw for w in the affine Weyl group. The convolution product ICw ⋆ ICw' is defined as m!(ICw ⊠ ICw') where m is the multiplication map. Determine the shift needed so that the convolution product is t‑exact for the perverse t‑structure.
The multiplication map m : Xw × Xw' → FlG is a proper map of relative dimension dim Xw + dim Xw' − dim Xww'. The external tensor product ICw ⊠ ICw' is perverse of degree 0 on the product. By the relative hard Lefschetz theorem for the proper map m, the pushforward m! shifts perverse degree by the relative dimension. Therefore the convolution product is perverse after shifting by −(dim Xw + dim Xw' − dim Xww'). In the standard normalisation used by Bezrukavnikov–Finkelberg–Mirković, one defines the convolution functor as ICw ⋆ ICw' = m!(ICw ⊠ ICw')[dim Xw + dim Xw' − dim Xww'], which makes the functor t‑exact.

Let G = SL3 and let a parabolic modification at a point be given by a dominant coweight λ = (2,1,0) and a level weight μ = (1,0,−1) in the cocharacter lattice. Compute the perverse shift contributed by this modification.

Perverse Sheaves and the Theory of Perverse Sheaves on the Derived Category of Sheaves on the Moduli of Flat Connections with Irregular Singularities on Surfaces

Let X be a smooth projective surface over ℂ and D ⊂ X a simple normal crossing divisor. The moduli stack MdR(G, D) of G‑flat connections on X with irregular singularities along D is an algebraic stack of infinite type, but it admits a stratification by formal type of the connection at the generic point of each component of D and by the Stokes data at the intersection points. The wild perverse t‑structure on the bounded derived category of constructible sheaves Dbc(MdR(G, D)) is defined by requiring that for each stratum S of formal type τ and Stokes data St, the cohomology sheaves of the restriction to S vanish outside degrees determined by the dimension of S and the irregularity index Irr(τ). The simple perverse sheaves are the intermediate extensions of irreducible local systems on the strata, twisted by the intersection cohomology complexes of the stratum closures. The irregular Riemann–Hilbert correspondence of Sabbah, Mochizuki, and Kedlaya identifies the analytification of MdR(G, D) with the moduli stack MB(G, D) of G‑local systems on X D with prescribed Stokes data. Under this correspondence, the wild perverse t‑structure matches the perverse t‑structure on MB(G, D) defined by the stratification by monodromy and Stokes data. The decomposition theorem for the wild non‑abelian Hodge correspondence — the proper morphism MDol(G, D) → MdR(G, D) from the moduli of Higgs bundles with irregular singularities — implies that the pushforward of the constant perverse sheaf splits into a direct sum of shifted simple perverse sheaves on MdR(G, D). A critical technical hypothesis is the boundedness condition on supports: one must restrict to the subcategory of perverse sheaves whose support has bounded irregularity index, otherwise the category fails to be artinian and the highest‑weight structure collapses. Examiners frequently test the distinction between the regular and irregular perverse t‑structures on surfaces: in the regular case the stratification is by conjugacy classes of the monodromy representation of π₁(X D), while in the irregular case one must also stratify by the formal type of the connection at the generic point of D and by the Stokes matrices at the nodes of D. Another common error is to assume that the irregular Riemann–Hilbert correspondence is an equivalence of abelian categories of perverse sheaves; it is an equivalence of derived categories that intertwines the perverse t‑structures only after a suitable shift by the irregularity index.

Worked Example: Irregularity Shift in the Wild Perverse t‑Structure
Let X = ℙ², D a smooth cubic curve, and G = GL₂. Consider a rank‑2 connection on X with a pole of order 2 along D and formal type τ = diag(z⁻², 0) at the generic point of D. Compute the irregularity index Irr(τ) and determine the shift in the perverse t‑structure for the stratum of connections with this formal type.
The irregularity index for a connection with pole order r along a divisor is Irr(τ) = (r−1) · rank for a regular singular connection, but for irregular singularities it is the sum of the slopes of the Newton polygon. Here the formal type has a pole of order 2, so the slope is 1. For GL₂ with diagonal formal type diag(z⁻², 0), the slopes are 1 and 0, so Irr(τ) = 1. The stratum S of connections with this formal type has dimension dim S = 2 · dim MB(GL₂, D) + Irr(τ) (by the deformation theory of irregular connections). The wild perverse t‑structure requires p Hⁱ(jS⁎ 𝒦) = 0 for i > −dim S + Irr(τ) and i < −dim S. Thus the shift relative to the middle perversity is +Irr(τ) = +1. The simple perverse sheaf on the closure of S is IC(Ŝ, ℒ)[dim S − Irr(τ)] where ℒ is an irreducible local system on S.
Exam Strategy: Wild vs. Tame Stratifications
When a question asks for the stratification defining the perverse t‑structure on a moduli of connections, first identify whether the singularities are regular or irregular. For regular singularities on a curve, the stratification is by conjugacy classes of local monodromy around each puncture. For irregular singularities on a curve, add the formal type (irregularity index, leading term) and Stokes data. For surfaces, the stratification is by formal type at the generic point of each divisor component and by Stokes matrices at the intersection points. Always state the boundedness condition on the irregularity index — omitting it is a frequent trap that makes the category ill‑defined.

Perverse Sheaves and the Categorical Action of the Affine Yangian of gl₁ on the Affine Grassmannian

The affine Yangian Yħ(𝔤𝔩₁) of Tsymbaliuk is a one‑parameter deformation of the universal enveloping algebra of the Lie algebra of differential operators on the circle. It acts on the equivariant cohomology of the affine Grassmannian GrGL₁ ≃ ℤ × BU(1) via the Maulik–Okounkov stable envelope construction. In the perverse sheaf framework, this action lifts to a categorical action on the derived category of constructible sheaves Dbc(GrGL₁) with the perverse t‑structure. The affine Grassmannian for GL₁ is a disjoint union of points indexed by ℤ, so the perverse t‑structure is trivial: perverse sheaves are just graded vector spaces. The categorical action is generated by convolution with the perverse sheaves on the Hecke correspondences Heckek ⊂ Gr × Gr defined by the condition that the quotient of the two lattices has length k. These correspondences are smooth of relative dimension k, so the pushforward of the constant sheaf shifted by k is perverse. The resulting functors satisfy the defining relations of the affine Yangian, including the Drinfeld relations and the Serre relations. The categorical action categorifies the Fock space representation of Yħ(𝔤𝔩₁) and is closely related to the categorical action of the quantum toroidal algebra Uq₁,q₂(𝔤𝔩₁) on the Hilbert scheme of points via the 3d mirror symmetry between the affine Grassmannian and the Hilbert scheme. A key distinction is that the affine Yangian has a single deformation parameter ħ, while the quantum toroidal algebra has two parameters q₁, q₂; the categorical action of the affine Yangian is recovered by setting q₁ = q₂ = eħ and taking a suitable limit. Examiners often test the relationship between the affine Yangian and the spherical Hecke algebra: the spherical Hecke algebra of GL₁ over a local field is commutative and isomorphic to the ring of symmetric functions, while the affine Yangian is a non‑commutative deformation. The categorical action of the affine Yangian on Dbc(GrGL₁) is essentially the same as the categorical action of the quantum toroidal algebra on Dbc(Hilbn(ℂ²)) under the 3d mirror symmetry equivalence, but the perverse t‑structures on the two sides differ by a shift that depends on the dimension of the strata.

Perverse Sheaves and the Geometric Langlands Correspondence for Real Reductive Groups

The geometric Langlands correspondence for a complex reductive group G relates perverse sheaves on the moduli stack BunG of G‑bundles on a curve X to coherent sheaves on the moduli stack LocSysǦ of Ǧ‑local systems, where Ǧ is the Langlands dual group. For a real reductive group Gℝ, the correspondence must be modified: the moduli stack of Gℝ‑bundles is replaced by the moduli stack of G‑bundles equipped with a reduction of structure group to Gℝ on the real locus of X, or equivalently by the moduli stack of G‑local systems with a real structure. The perverse t‑structure on the derived category of constructible sheaves on this real moduli stack is defined by the stratification given by the isomorphism classes of the underlying G‑bundle and the real structure. The simple perverse sheaves are the intersection cohomology complexes of the closures of these strata, twisted by irreducible representations of the centralizer of the real structure. The geometric Langlands correspondence for real groups, as formulated by Ben‑Zvi, Sakuma, and others, relates the category of perverse sheaves on the real moduli stack to the category of coherent sheaves on the moduli stack of Ǧ‑local systems with a real structure, where Ǧ is the Langlands dual of G (which is a real form of the complex dual group). A critical technical point is that the real moduli stack is not algebraic but real analytic; the perverse t‑structure is defined using the real analytic topology and the six functor formalism for real analytic spaces developed by Kashiwara and Schapira. The decomposition theorem for the Hitchin fibration in the real setting — the morphism from the moduli of real Higgs bundles to the Hitchin base — holds after passing to the category of mixed Hodge modules on the real analytic space. Examiners frequently test the distinction between the split real form and the compact real form: for the split real form, the real moduli stack has a dense open stratum corresponding to the trivial real structure, while for the compact real form, the real moduli stack is compact and the stratification is by the topology of the real bundle. Another common error is to assume that the geometric Langlands correspondence for real groups is simply the restriction of the complex correspondence to the real locus; the real correspondence involves a different dual group (the real Langlands dual) and a different perverse t‑structure that incorporates the real structure.

Perverse Sheaves and the Decomposition Theorem for the Morphism from the Moduli of Higgs Bundles to the Moduli of Local Systems via the Non‑Abelian Hodge Correspondence

The non‑abelian Hodge correspondence of Simpson provides a homeomorphism between the moduli space MDol(G) of semistable G‑Higgs bundles on a smooth projective curve X and the moduli space MB(G) of semisimple G‑local systems on X. This homeomorphism is not algebraic; it is a real analytic isomorphism that intertwines the complex structures in a non‑trivial way. The decomposition theorem for the morphism f : MDol(G) → MB(G) induced by the non‑abelian Hodge correspondence states that in the derived category of constructible sheaves on MB(G), the pushforward f∗ ℚℓ[dim MDol] splits as a direct sum of shifted simple perverse sheaves. The simple perverse sheaves appearing in this decomposition are the intersection cohomology complexes of the closures of the strata in MB(G) defined by the isomorphism type of the local system, twisted by irreducible representations of the centralizer. The perverse Leray filtration on the cohomology of MDol(G) induced by f coincides, under the non‑abelian Hodge isomorphism, with the weight filtration on the cohomology of MB(G) coming from the mixed Hodge structure. This is the P = W phenomenon for the non‑abelian Hodge correspondence. A crucial hypothesis is that f is a proper morphism of algebraic varieties (after choosing appropriate compactifications), and the constant sheaf on MDol(G) is perverse because MDol(G) is smooth. The decomposition theorem then applies directly. Examiners often test the distinction between the non‑abelian Hodge correspondence as a homeomorphism and as a morphism of algebraic varieties: the decomposition theorem applies to the algebraic morphism obtained by compactifying both sides, not to the homeomorphism itself. Another subtle point is that the P = W isomorphism involves a shift: Pk Hⁱ = W2k+i Hⁱ where the shift by i comes from the fact that the non‑abelian Hodge correspondence is not a morphism of pure Hodge structures but a morphism of mixed Hodge structures with a non‑trivial weight filtration. A frequent trap is to confuse the perverse Leray filtration for the Hitchin fibration h : MDol(G) → A with the perverse Leray filtration for the non‑abelian Hodge morphism f : MDol(G) → MB(G); the former gives the P = W conjecture for the Hitchin fibration, while the latter gives the P = W statement for the non‑abelian Hodge correspondence, and the two filtrations are related by the fact that f factors through h and the abelianization map A → MB(G).

Perverse Sheaves and the Derived Category of Constructible Sheaves on Stratified Pseudomanifolds

A stratified pseudomanifold X is a topological space equipped with a filtration by closed subsets X = Xn ⊃ Xn-1 ⊃ … ⊃ X0 such that each stratum S = Xk Xk-1 is a topological manifold of dimension k and the link of each stratum is a compact stratified pseudomanifold of dimension n−k−1. The derived category Dbc(X) of bounded constructible complexes of sheaves of ℚ‑vector spaces on X carries a natural perverse t‑structure whose heart Perv(X) consists of complexes K satisfying the support and cosupport conditions: for every stratum S of codimension c, the cohomology sheaves Hi(iS*K) vanish for i > −c and Hi(iS!K) vanish for i < −c. Here iS denotes the inclusion of the stratum. The intersection cohomology complex ICX is the unique (up to isomorphism) simple perverse sheaf whose restriction to the top stratum is the constant sheaf ℚ[dim X]. Verdier duality DX exchanges the perverse t‑structure with its opposite, and ICX is self‑dual. The category Perv(X) is artinian and noetherian; every object has a finite Jordan–Hölder series with simple constituents of the form ICS(L) where L is an irreducible local system on a stratum S. The decomposition theorem of Beĭlinson–Bernstein–Deligne–Gabber applies to any proper map f: Y → X with Y a complex algebraic variety: Rf*ℚY[dim Y] splits in Dbc(X) as a direct sum of shifted simple perverse sheaves. When X is a projective variety with only isolated singularities, the perverse Leray filtration on H*(Y, ℚ) coincides with the weight filtration of the mixed Hodge structure, yielding the P = W phenomenon.

Perverse Sheaves and the Categorical Action of the Elliptic Hall Algebra on the Hilbert Scheme of Points

The elliptic Hall algebra EHA (also called the quantum continuous gl∞) is a Hopf algebra generated by symbols Em,n with (m,n) ∈ ℤ2 {(0,0)} subject to the relations of Burban–Schiffmann. Schiffmann and Vasserot constructed a categorical action of EHA on the derived category DbCoh(Hilbn(ℂ2)) of coherent sheaves on the Hilbert scheme of n points in the plane. The action is realised by Fourier–Mukai kernels given by perverse sheaves on the nested Hilbert scheme Hilbn,n+1 = {(Z,Z') | Z ⊂ Z', length(Z') = length(Z)+1}. Concretely, the generator E1,0 acts by the functor Φ1,0(−) = p2*(p1*(−) ⊗ ICHilbn,n+1[1]) where p1, p2 are the projections from the correspondence to the two factors. The perverse sheaf ICHilbn,n+1 is the intersection cohomology complex of the correspondence, which is a semi‑small resolution of its image; its perversity guarantees that the convolution product respects the perverse t‑structure and yields an exact functor on the heart of the perverse t‑structure on the constructible side after applying the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence. The resulting categorical representation categorifies the Fock space representation of EHA; the classes of the simple perverse sheaves correspond to the Macdonald symmetric functions. A key technical point is that the nested Hilbert scheme is smooth and the map to the product is semi‑small, so the pushforward of the constant sheaf is perverse and the decomposition theorem gives a direct sum decomposition into shifted IC complexes. This construction is distinct from the rational Cherednik algebra action, which lives on the same Hilbert scheme but uses the Procesi bundle and acts on coherent sheaves rather than on constructible sheaves via perverse convolution.

Identifying the perverse sheaf underlying an elliptic Hall algebra generator
Let X = Hilbn(ℂ2) and Y = Hilbn,n+1 with projections p1: Y → X, p2: Y → Hilbn+1(ℂ2). The generator E1,0 of the elliptic Hall algebra acts by the Fourier–Mukai functor with kernel K = ICY[1]. Show that K is a perverse sheaf on Y (with the middle perversity) and compute its Verdier dual.
The nested Hilbert scheme Y is smooth of dimension 2n+2. The map (p1,p2): Y → X × Hilbn+1 is a closed embedding whose image has codimension 2. Hence Y is a local complete intersection and the constant sheaf ℚY[dim Y] is perverse. Since Y is smooth, the intersection cohomology complex ICY coincides with ℚY[dim Y]. Therefore K = ℚY[dim Y+1] = ℚY[2n+3]. The Verdier dual of ℚY[d] on a smooth oriented manifold of real dimension 2d is ℚY[−d] (up to a Tate twist). Here the complex dimension of Y is 2n+2, so the real dimension is 4n+4. The dual of ℚY[2n+3] is ℚY[−(2n+3)] = ℚY[−2n−3]. In the perverse normalisation this is again a perverse sheaf because shifting by the dimension preserves perversity on a smooth space. Thus DY(K) ≅ K[−4n−6] up to the canonical orientation sheaf, which is trivial on Y.

When a question asks for the perverse sheaf governing a categorical action, first check whether the correspondence is smooth and semi‑small. If it is, the constant sheaf shifted by the dimension is already perverse and equals the IC complex. Then apply Verdier duality using the fact that on a smooth variety the dual of ℚ[dim] is ℚ[−dim] (up to orientation). This avoids a lengthy computation of nearby cycles.

Perverse Sheaves and the Decomposition Theorem for the Global Springer Resolution

The global Springer resolution is the morphism μ: T*(G/B) → N where G is a complex reductive group, B a Borel subgroup, and N the nilpotent cone in the Lie algebra 𝔤. The source is the cotangent bundle of the flag variety, a smooth symplectic variety of dimension 2 dim(G/B). The map μ is proper, semi‑small, and G‑equivariant. The decomposition theorem applied to μ yields a canonical isomorphism in Dbc(N)G: Rμ*ℚT*(G/B)[dim T*(G/B)] ≅ ⊕𝒪 IC𝒪 ⊗ H*(μ−1(𝒪), ℚ)[−dim 𝒪], where the sum runs over the nilpotent orbits 𝒪 ⊂ N. The cohomology of the Springer fibre μ−1(𝒪) carries a natural action of the Weyl group W, and the resulting W‑modules realise the Springer correspondence: each irreducible representation of W appears exactly once as the top cohomology of a Springer fibre attached to a distinguished orbit. The perverse sheaves IC𝒪 are the simple perverse sheaves on N with trivial local system on the orbit. The decomposition theorem also implies that the convolution product on the derived category of G‑equivariant constructible sheaves on N (the Springer category) is equivalent to the category of representations of the affine Hecke algebra at parameter q = 1. This equivalence is a geometric incarnation of the Kazhdan–Lusztig classification of simple perverse sheaves on the affine flag variety.
