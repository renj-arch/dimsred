Perverse Sheaves — Intersection Cohomology, the Decomposition Theorem & the Riemann–Hilbert Correspondence

Deep dive into Perverse Sheaves — the derived category D^b_c(X) of constructible sheaves on a complex algebraic variety X, the six functor formalism (f^*, f_*, f_!, f^!, ⊗, ℋom), the perverse t-structure leading to the abelian category Perv(X) of perverse sheaves (the heart of a t-structure on D^b_c(X) defined by support and co-support conditions), intersection cohomology (Goresky–MacPherson, 1974) and the intersection cohomology complex IC_X (the intermediate extension of the constant sheaf on the smooth part of X), the decomposition theorem (Beilinson–Bernstein–Deligne–Gabber, 1982: for a proper map f: X → Y, Rf_* IC_X is a direct sum of shifted perverse sheaves), the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence (an equivalence between the derived category of regular holonomic D-modules and the derived category of constructible sheaves), Kazhdan–Lusztig theory (the Kazhdan–Lusztig conjectures, proved by Brylinski–Kashiwara and Beilinson–Bernstein via perverse sheaves on the flag variety), and the enhanced and spectral extensions of the theory.

"Perverse sheaves are the most beautiful objects in algebraic geometry — they sit at the crossroads of topology, Hodge theory, and representation theory."
— Mark Goresky
Part 1

🧬 Derived Categories and the Six Functors

Derived
Triangulated Categories, Derived Functors, and Constructible Sheaves
The derived category D(A) of an abelian category A has complexes as objects and quasi-isomorphisms (maps inducing isomorphisms on all cohomology objects) inverted. D^b(A) is the full subcategory of bounded complexes. The triangulated structure: distinguished triangles X → Y → Z → X[1] coming from short exact sequences of complexes (the mapping cone). For a topological space X, let Sh(X) be the abelian category of sheaves of ℂ-vector spaces (or ℚ̄_ℓ). Constructible sheaves (with respect to an algebraic stratification X = ⊔ X_α by locally closed subvarieties) are sheaves whose restriction to each stratum is a local system (a locally constant sheaf of finite-dimensional vector spaces). The bounded derived category of constructible sheaves is D^b_c(X). The six functors: f^* (inverse image — left adjoint to f_*, exact), f_* (direct image — right adjoint to f^*, left exact, derived as Rf_*), f_! (proper direct image — extension by zero, left exact, with compact support cohomology), f^! (exceptional inverse image — right adjoint to f_!, defined via duality), ⊗ (tensor product), and ℋom (internal Hom). The Verdier duality: a contravariant autoequivalence D: D^b_c(X) → D^b_c(X) satisfying D^2 ≅ id, exchanging f_* with f_! and f^* with f^!. The Grothendieck–Verdier duality theorem: Rf_! ≅ D_Y ∘ Rf_* ∘ D_X. The adjunction formulas: Hom(Rf_! ℱ, 𝒢) ≅ Hom(ℱ, f^! 𝒢) and Hom(f^* ℱ, 𝒢) ≅ Hom(ℱ, Rf_* 𝒢). The base change theorem for proper maps and smooth maps. These six functors are the fundamental toolkit of sheaf theory in algebraic geometry.
The six functor formalism provides a powerful calculus for manipulating sheaves on algebraic varieties, with Verdier duality as the central symmetry.
Part 2

🔗 The Perverse t-Structure and Perverse Sheaves

t-Structure
t-Structures, the Perverse Heart, and the Artin–Grothendieck Vanishing
A t-structure (Beilinson–Bernstein–Deligne, 1982) on a triangulated category D consists of two full subcategories D^{≤0} and D^{≥0} satisfying: (i) Hom(D^{≤0}, D^{≥1}) = 0 (where D^{≥1} = D^{≥0}[−1]); (ii) D^{≤0} ⊆ D^{≤1} and D^{≥1} ⊆ D^{≥0}; (iii) for any X ∈ D, there is a distinguished triangle A → X → B → A[1] with A ∈ D^{≤0} and B ∈ D^{≥1}. The heart D^{≤0} ∩ D^{≥0} is an abelian category. The perverse t-structure on D^b_c(X) for a complex algebraic variety X of dimension n is defined by: ^{p}D^{≤0} = {ℱ | dim supp H^i(ℱ) ≤ −i} and ^{p}D^{≥0} = {ℱ | dim cosupp H^i(ℱ) ≤ i} where cosupp is defined via Verdier duality: dim cosupp ℋ = dim supp Dℋ. The perverse sheaves Perv(X) = ^{p}D^{≤0} ∩ ^{p}D^{≥0} form the heart — an abelian category. The key property: perverse sheaves are Artinian and Noetherian (every object has finite length). Simple perverse sheaves correspond to pairs (Z, ℒ) where Z is an irreducible closed subvariety and ℒ is an irreducible local system on a Zariski-open subset of Z_{reg}. The intermediate extension functor j_{!*}: Perv(U) → Perv(X) for an open inclusion j: U ↪ X: given a perverse sheaf 𝒢 on U, j_{!*}𝒢 is the image of j_!𝒢 → j_*𝒢 shifted appropriately. The Artin–Grothendieck vanishing theorem: for a constructible sheaf ℱ on an affine variety X of dimension n, H^i_c(X, ℱ) = 0 for i > n. This is a crucial vanishing result for perverse sheaves on affine varieties.
The perverse t-structure refines the usual cohomological t-structure, creating an abelian category of perverse sheaves that behave like sheaves shifted by the dimension of their support.
Part 3

💥 Intersection Cohomology and the Decomposition Theorem

Decomposition
Goresky–MacPherson Intersection Cohomology and the BBDG Decomposition Theorem
Intersection cohomology (Goresky–MacPherson, 1974) was developed to restore Poincaré duality for singular varieties. For a stratified variety X, the intersection cohomology complex IC_X is defined as IC_X = j_{!*} ℂ_U[n] where j: U ↪ X is the smooth part and n = dim X. The intersection cohomology IH^i(X) = H^{i−n}(X, IC_X) satisfies Poincaré duality: IH^i(X) ≅ IH^{2n−i}(X)^*. The Decomposition theorem (Beilinson–Bernstein–Deligne–Gabber, 1982): let f: X → Y be a proper algebraic map between complex algebraic varieties. Then Rf_* IC_X ≅ ⊕_k IC_{Z_k}(L_k)[d_k] — a direct sum of shifted intersection cohomology complexes of subvarieties Z_k ⊂ Y, each twisted by a semisimple local system L_k. Equivalently, Rf_* IC_X is a semisimple perverse sheaf (a direct sum of simple perverse sheaves). This is one of the deepest theorems in algebraic geometry, generalizing the Hodge decomposition and the hard Lefschetz theorem. For a resolution of singularities f: X̃ → X (proper, birational, with X̃ smooth), the decomposition theorem describes how the constant sheaf on X̃ decomposes when pushed forward to X. The relative hard Lefschetz theorem: for a projective morphism f: X → Y with an ample line bundle L on X, the cup product L^k: R^{−k}f_* IC_X → R^{k}f_* IC_X is an isomorphism for all k. The Kleiman vanishing theorem and the Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing are related results. Applications: the McKay correspondence for crepant resolutions, the Kollar vanishing theorem, and the Macaulayfication of schemes.
The decomposition theorem shows that the pushforward of the intersection cohomology complex under a proper map breaks into a sum of intersection cohomology complexes — a vast generalization of Hodge theory.
Part 4

🔮 The Riemann–Hilbert Correspondence and Kazhdan–Lusztig Theory

RHC
D-Modules, the Riemann–Hilbert Correspondence, and the Kazhdan–Lusztig Conjectures
The Riemann–Hilbert correspondence (Kashiwara, 1980; Mebkhout, 1984): for a complex algebraic variety X, there is an equivalence of triangulated categories D^b_{rh}(D_X) ≅ D^b_c(X, ℂ) between the derived category of regular holonomic D-modules and the derived category of constructible sheaves. The functor is the de Rham functor DR: M ↦ Ω^∙_{X^{an}} ⊗_{D_{X^{an}}} M. Its inverse is the solution functor Sol: ℱ ↦ Rℋom(ℱ, 𝒪_{X^{an}}). The equivalence preserves the perverse t-structure: the heart Perv(X) corresponds to the abelian category of regular holonomic D-modules. This connects differential equations (regular singularities) with topology (constructible sheaves). The Kazhdan–Lusztig conjectures (1979): the characters of simple highest weight modules for a semisimple Lie algebra g can be expressed in terms of the Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials P_{w,v}(q) associated to the Weyl group W. These polynomials are determined by the Hecke algebra of W via the Kazhdan–Lusztig basis C_w. The conjectures were proved by Brylinski–Kashiwara (1981) and Beilinson–Bernstein (1981) using perverse sheaves on the flag variety G/B. The key idea: localization of g-modules to D-modules on G/B, then apply the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence to translate to perverse sheaves — the intersection cohomology complexes IC_{X_w} of Schubert varieties correspond to simple highest weight modules. This is the localization theorem of Beilinson–Bernstein: there is an equivalence between the category of g-modules with fixed central character and D-modules on G/B. The geometric Satake correspondence (Mirković–Vilonen) and the Springer correspondence (Lusztig) also use perverse sheaves to study representations of algebraic groups.
The Riemann–Hilbert correspondence bridges analysis (D-modules), topology (perverse sheaves), and representation theory (Kazhdan–Lusztig theory) — a grand unification.
