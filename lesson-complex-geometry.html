Complex Geometry — Holomorphic Bundles, Kähler Manifolds & Hodge Theory

Complex geometry studies manifolds equipped with a complex structure — a way to locally identify neighborhoods with ℂⁿ such that transition maps are holomorphic. A complex manifold M has a complex structure J (an endomorphism with J² = −Id) that is integrable (vanishing Nijenhuis tensor). A Kähler manifold is a complex manifold with a Hermitian metric whose associated 2-form ω is closed (dω = 0) — this gives a rich interaction between Riemannian, symplectic, and complex geometry. The Hodge decomposition Hᵏ(M, ℂ) = ⊕_{p+q=k} H^{p,q}(M) for compact Kähler manifolds is a powerful tool. The Calabi conjecture (proved by Yau) shows the existence of Ricci-flat Kähler metrics on compact Kähler manifolds with c₁ = 0 — these are Calabi-Yau manifolds central to string theory. Holomorphic vector bundles, Chern classes, and the Kodaira vanishing theorem complete the picture.

"Complex geometry is the meeting point of algebra, analysis, and topology."
— Phillip Griffiths
Part 1

📏 Complex Manifolds and Almost Complex Structures

Foundations
Complex Coordinates, Almost Complex Structures, and Integrability
A complex manifold of dimension n is a topological manifold covered by charts U_α → ℂⁿ with holomorphic transition maps. Equivalently, an almost complex structure J: TM → TM with J² = −Id. The Nijenhuis tensor N_J(X,Y) = [X,Y] + J[JX,Y] + J[X,JY] − [JX,JY] vanishes iff J is integrable (Newlander-Nirenberg theorem). The tangent bundle splits T_ℂM = T^{1,0}M ⊕ T^{0,1}M into holomorphic and antiholomorphic parts: vectors of type (1,0) satisfy JX = iX; type (0,1) satisfy JX = −iX. Holomorphic functions are those with ∂̄f = 0. The Dolbeault complex (Ω^{p,q}, ∂̄) computes Dolbeault cohomology H^{p,q}_∂̄(M). The Kodaira vanishing theorem: on a compact Kähler manifold with positive canonical bundle, H^q(M, K_M ⊗ L) = 0 for q > 0 when L is ample.
Complex manifolds are locally modeled on ℂⁿ with holomorphic transition maps; the integrability condition ensures the almost complex structure comes from actual holomorphic coordinates.
Part 2

🌍 Holomorphic Vector Bundles and Chern Classes

Bundles
Holomorphic Vector Bundles, Hermitian Metrics, and Chern Classes
A holomorphic vector bundle E → M is a complex vector bundle whose transition functions are holomorphic. The canonical bundle K_M = Λ^n T^{1,0}M^* is the top exterior power of the holomorphic cotangent bundle. A Hermitian metric h on E gives a unique Chern connection — the unique connection compatible with both the holomorphic structure and h. Its curvature Θ ∈ Ω^{1,1}(M, End(E)) is of type (1,1). The Chern classes c_k(E) ∈ H^{2k}(M, ℤ) are defined via c_k = [i/(2π) Tr(Θ^k)] (up to normalization). The Chern character ch(E) = Σ e^{x_i} (in terms of Chern roots) satisfies ch(E⊕F) = ch(E) + ch(F) and ch(E⊗F) = ch(E)·ch(F). Hirzebruch-Riemann-Roch: χ(M, E) = ∫_M ch(E)·Td(TM). The Kodaira embedding theorem: a compact complex manifold with a positive holomorphic line bundle embeds into projective space.
Holomorphic vector bundles combine complex analysis with topology; Chern classes are topological invariants that measure the curvature of a bundle.
Part 3

🧮 Kähler Manifolds and Hodge Theory

Kähler
Kähler Metrics, Hodge Decomposition, and Lefschetz Theorems
A Kähler manifold (M, g, J, ω) has a Riemannian metric g compatible with J and a closed Kähler form ω(·,·) = g(·, J·) with dω = 0. The Kähler identities relate the operators ∂, ∂̄, and their adjoints. The Hodge decomposition for compact Kähler: Hᵏ(M, ℂ) = ⊕_{p+q=k} H^{p,q}(M) with H^{p,q} ≅ H^{q,p} (conjugation) and H^{p,p}(M) ≠ 0. The Hodge numbers h^{p,q} = dim H^{p,q} satisfy h^{p,q} = h^{q,p} = h^{n-p,n-q} (Serre duality). The Lefschetz hyperplane theorem: if X ⊂ ℙ^N is a smooth projective variety, the inclusion i: Y ↪ X of a hyperplane section induces isomorphisms i_*: π_k(Y) → π_k(X) for k < n−1. The Hard Lefschetz theorem: cup product with ω^{n−k} is an isomorphism Hᵏ(M, ℝ) → H^{2n−k}(M, ℝ). The Hodge-Riemann bilinear relations impose positivity constraints on the intersection form.
Kähler geometry is the ideal setting where Riemannian, symplectic, and complex structures harmonize — leading to powerful Hodge theory constraints on topology.
Part 4

🔥 Calabi-Yau Manifolds and Mirror Symmetry

Calabi-Yau
The Calabi Conjecture, Ricci-Flat Metrics, and Mirror Symmetry
The Calabi conjecture (proved by Yau, 1977): on a compact Kähler manifold with c₁ = 0, there exists a unique Ricci-flat Kähler metric in each Kähler class. These manifolds are Calabi-Yau manifolds — they have SU(n) holonomy. K3 surfaces are the only compact Calabi-Yau 2-folds; in dimension 3, there are thousands of distinct topological types (e.g., the quintic hypersurface in ℙ⁴). Mirror symmetry (proposed by string theorists): for a Calabi-Yau 3-fold M, there exists a mirror M̂ such that h^{1,1}(M) = h^{2,1}(M̂) and h^{2,1}(M) = h^{1,1}(M̂) — the Hodge diamonds are flipped. The Mirror symmetry conjecture relates the complex structure moduli space of M to the Kähler moduli space of M̂ and predicts equality of Gromov-Witten invariants and period integrals. The Batyrev's construction: toric hypersurfaces provide a rich source of mirror pairs. Candelas-de la Ossa-Green-Parkes (1991) predicted the number of rational curves on the quintic using mirror symmetry — a landmark verification.
Calabi-Yau manifolds are Ricci-flat Kähler spaces central to string theory; mirror symmetry reveals deep dualities between a priori distinct geometric worlds.
