Tropical Geometry — Tropical Curves, Amoebas & the Maslov Torus

Tropical geometry is a piecewise-linear shadow of algebraic geometry obtained by replacing the usual arithmetic with the tropical semiring (ℝ ∪ {∞}, ⊕, ⊗) where a⊕b = min(a,b) and a⊗b = a+b (the min-plus algebra). A tropical polynomial P(x₁,…,xₙ) = ⊕ c_α ⊗ x^α = min(c_α + α·x) is a convex piecewise-linear function, and its tropical hypersurface V(P) = {x ∈ ℝⁿ : the minimum is attained at least twice} is a rational polyhedral complex. The tropicalization of a classical algebraic variety V ⊂ (ℂ^*)ⁿ is the set Trop(V) = lim_{t→0} Log_t(V) where Log_t(z) = (log_t|z₁|,…,log_t|zₙ|) — this is the amoeba limit under Maslov dequantization. The Kapranov theorem states that for a hypersurface defined by a Laurent polynomial f, the tropicalization Trop(V(f)) equals the tropical hypersurface V(trop(f)). The subject has exploded since the 1990s: Mikhalkin's correspondence theorem equates enumerative counts of algebraic curves in toric surfaces with counts of tropical curves, the Adiprasito-Huh-Katz Hodge theory for matroids proved Rota's conjecture, and tropical methods now permeate algebraic geometry, combinatorics, mirror symmetry, and even phylogenetics.

"Tropical geometry is algebraic geometry over the tropical semiring."
— Bernd Sturmfels
Part 1

📏 The Tropical Semiring and Tropical Polynomials

Semiring
Min-Plus Algebra, Tropical Polynomials, and Tropical Hypersurfaces
The tropical semiring 𝕋 = (ℝ ∪ {∞}, ⊕, ⊗) is defined by a⊕b = min(a,b) and a⊗b = a+b. The additive identity is ∞ and the multiplicative identity is 0. A tropical monomial is c⊗x₁^{a₁}⊗⋯⊗xₙ^{aₙ} = c + Σ aᵢxᵢ, and a tropical polynomial f(x) = ⊕ c_α ⊗ x^α = min_α(c_α + α·x) is a piecewise-linear convex function on ℝⁿ. The tropical hypersurface V(f) = {x ∈ ℝⁿ : the minimum is attained at least twice} is a rational polyhedral complex of codimension 1. The Fundamental Theorem of Tropical Geometry (Kapranov, Speyer-Sturmfels): for an algebraically closed field K with non-Archimedean valuation val, the tropicalization Trop(V(I)) = ⋂_{f∈I} V(trop(f)) is the support of a rational polyhedral complex. The Newton polytope Newt(f) = conv{α : c_α ≠ ∞} determines the combinatorial type of V(f): the tropical hypersurface is the codimension-1 skeleton of the dual subdivision of Newt(f) given by the regular subdivision induced by the coefficients c_α.
Tropical polynomials are convex piecewise-linear functions; their hypersurfaces are rational polyhedral complexes dual to subdivisions of Newton polytopes — this is the combinatorial heart of tropical geometry.
Part 2

🌍 Tropical Curves and Their Geometry

Curves
Balanced Polyhedral Complexes, Genus, and Mikhalkin's Correspondence Theorem
A tropical curve in ℝ² is a finite graph Γ embedded as a 1-dimensional polyhedral complex whose edges have rational slopes and are weighted by positive integers (multiplicities). The balancing condition must hold at every vertex: for a vertex v with incident edges e₁,…,e_k having weights wᵢ and primitive direction vectors vᵢ pointing away from v, we require Σ wᵢ·vᵢ = 0. This is the tropical analogue of the zero-sum condition for tangent vectors at a node of an algebraic curve. The genus of a tropical curve is the first Betti number g = b₁(Γ) (the number of independent cycles in the graph). Tropical intersection theory (Allermann-Rau, Katz) gives well-defined tropical intersection products on tropical subvarieties. The tropical Bézout theorem: two tropical curves in ℝ² of degrees d₁ and d₂ intersect in exactly d₁·d₂ points (counted with multiplicities). Mikhalkin's correspondence theorem (2005): the number of degree-d tropical curves in ℝ² through (3d+g−1) points in general position equals the number of algebraic curves of genus g and degree d in a toric surface passing through the same number of points — i.e., tropical curve counts equal Gromov-Witten invariants. The Newton polygon of a tropical plane curve determines its genus via Pick's formula: g = #(interior lattice points of Newt).
Tropical curves are balanced weighted graphs whose combinatorial counts match algebraic Gromov-Witten invariants — connecting discrete geometry to enumerative algebraic geometry.
Part 3

🧮 Amoebas and the Maslov Dequantization

Amoebas
Logarithmic Limit Sets, Ronkin Functions, and the Tropicalization Functor
The amoeba of an algebraic variety V ⊂ (ℂ^*)ⁿ is the image 𝒜_V = Log(V) ⊂ ℝⁿ where Log(z₁,…,zₙ) = (log|z₁|,…,log|zₙ|). The amoeba is a closed set whose complement consists of finitely many convex regions, each corresponding to an integer lattice point of the Newton polytope Newt(V) (the Gelfand-Kapranov-Zelevinsky correspondence). The Ronkin function N_V: ℝⁿ → ℝ defined by N_V(x) = (1/(2πi)ⁿ) ∫_{Log⁻¹(x)} log|f(z)|·Π (dzᵢ/zᵢ) is convex and piecewise-linear at infinity; its Legendre transform gives the tropical limit. The spine of the amoeba is the set where the Ronkin function is not strictly convex — it is a tropical variety (the tropical skeleton). Maslov dequantization (Litvinov-Maslov): consider the family of algebras (ℝ, +_h, ×) where a +_h b = h·log(e^{a/h} + e^{b/h}) and × is ordinary addition; as h → 0⁺, this deformation converges to the tropical semiring (min-plus). Applying this deformation to the equations defining a classical variety gives the tropicalization. The Bergman fan of a matroid M is the tropical variety Trop(V(I_M)) associated to the linear ideal over the Puiseux series. The tropicalization of a subvariety V ⊂ (K^*)ⁿ (K a valued field) is the closure of val(V) in ℝⁿ, which is the support of a rational polyhedral complex of pure dimension dim(V).
Amoebas are logarithmic images of complex varieties; their spines are tropical varieties, revealing that tropical geometry emerges as the semiclassical limit of complex geometry via Maslov dequantization.
Part 4

🔥 Applications and Tropical Mirror Symmetry

Applications
Enumerative Geometry, Hodge Theory for Matroids, and Tropical Moduli
Enumerative geometry via tropical curves: Mikhalkin's correspondence theorem allows computing Gromov-Witten invariants of toric surfaces by counting tropical curves, which reduces a transcendental problem to a finite combinatorial enumeration (e.g., the number of rational degree-d curves through 3d−1 points in ℙ²). Tropical compactifications (Tevelev): given a subvariety V ⊂ (K^*)ⁿ, its tropicalization Trop(V) defines a fan whose associated toric variety X(Δ) gives a compactification of V with controlled boundary — the closure of V in X(Δ) is proper and flat. Adiprasito-Huh-Katz (2016): using tropical Hodge theory they proved the Hodge-Riemann relations for matroids, establishing the Rota-Heron-Welsh conjecture (the log-concavity of the characteristic polynomial). Tropical moduli spaces: the moduli space of tropical curves M_g^{trop} is a tropical variety (a generalized cone complex) whose cells correspond to stable graph types; it computes the boundary of the Deligne-Mumford compactification M̅_g. Tropical Grassmannians (the Dressian Dr(k,n)) parameterize tropical linear spaces and are related to the classical Grassmannian via the tropicalization map. Grigoriev-Podolskii proved a tropical analogue of the Sturmfels theorem on sparse elimination. Applications extend to algebraic statistics (tropical PCA), phylogenetics (the space of phylogenetic trees is a tropical variety — the Bergman fan of the graphic matroid), and optimization (tropical convexity provides new algorithms for discrete convex analysis).
Tropical geometry has unlocked enumerative computations, proved deep combinatorial conjectures via Hodge theory, and built bridges to statistics, phylogenetics, and optimization.
