Stochastic Processes — Randomness Through Time

Stochastic processes model random systems evolving in time. From the jitter of a pollen grain (Brownian motion) to the rise and fall of stock prices, stochastic processes are the mathematical language of randomness with memory. The field covers Markov chains (discrete), martingales (fair games), Brownian motion (continuous limit), Itô calculus (stochastic integration), Lévy processes (jumps), and ergodic theory (long-run behavior).

"Stochastic processes are the calculus of uncertainty — they quantify how randomness unfolds."
— Joseph Doob (paraphrased)
Part 1

🎲 Markov Chains — Memoryless Evolution

Memoryless
Discrete-Time Markov Chains — Transition Matrices and Stationary Distributions
A Markov chain is a sequence X₀, X₁, X₂,... where P(X_{n+1}=j | X_n=i, past) = P_{ij} (the Markov property: the future depends only on the present). The transition matrix P = (P_{ij}) is stochastic: row sums = 1. The n-step transition matrix is Pⁿ. A distribution π is stationary if πP = π. For finite irreducible aperiodic chains, π is unique and Pⁿ → 1π as n→∞ (convergence to equilibrium). The detailed balance condition π_i P_{ij} = π_j P_{ji} (reversibility) is sufficient for stationarity. Absorbing Markov chains have absorbing states; the fundamental matrix (I−Q)^{-1} gives expected absorption times. Continuous-time Markov chains have a rate matrix Q with P(t) = e^{Qt}.
Markov chains are the simplest models of sequential randomness — the future depends only on the present, not the past.
Part 2

💰 Martingales — Fair Games and Stopping Times

Fair Games
Martingales — Processes That Are Constant on Average
A martingale is a sequence M_n where E[M_{n+1} | ℱ_n] = M_n — the best prediction of the future is the present (a fair game). The optional stopping theorem (OST): if τ is a stopping time with bounded time or integrability conditions, E[M_τ] = E[M_0]. The Doob decomposition: any adapted integrable process decomposes uniquely as a martingale plus a predictable process. Doob's martingale inequalities: P(max_{k≤n} |M_k| ≥ λ) ≤ E[|M_n|]/λ (maximal inequality) and E[max_{k≤n} M_k²] ≤ 4E[M_n²] (L² inequality). Martingale convergence theorem: if sup_n E[|M_n|] < ∞, then M_n converges a.s. to a limit M_∞. Branching processes (Galton-Watson): the population size Z_n forms a martingale after normalization — extinction probability is the smallest fixed point of the generating function.
Martingales are the backbone of stochastic analysis — they model fair games and converge under mild conditions.
Part 3

🌊 Brownian Motion and Itô Calculus

Continuous Noise
Brownian Motion — The Universal Noise Process
Brownian motion B_t (Wiener process) is a continuous process with independent increments B_t−B_s ~ N(0, t−s). It's nowhere differentiable (paths have fractal dimension 1.5). The quadratic variation [B]_t = t — unlike smooth functions, Brownian motion accumulates finite quadratic variation. Itô's lemma (the stochastic chain rule): for dX = μ dt + σ dB and f ∈ C², df(X) = (f'μ + ½ f''σ²) dt + f'σ dB. The extra ½ f''σ² dt term is the Itô correction coming from quadratic variation. The Black-Scholes equation (∂V/∂t + ½σ²S²∂²V/∂S² + rS∂V/∂S − rV = 0) for option pricing follows from Itô's lemma and a hedging argument. The Girsanov theorem changes the drift of a Brownian motion via an exponential martingale — it's the basis of risk-neutral pricing. Stochastic differential equations (SDEs) dX = μ(X,t)dt + σ(X,t)dB have solutions under Lipschitz conditions.
Itô calculus is the calculus of random noise — it revolutionized finance, physics, and every field with continuous randomness.
Part 4

⚡ Lévy Processes, Ergodic Theory, and Advanced Topics

Jumps and Invariance
Lévy Processes — Adding Jumps to Brownian Motion
A Lévy process has stationary independent increments with càdlàg paths. By the Lévy-Khintchine formula, the characteristic exponent ψ(θ) = log E[e^{iθX_1}] = iμθ − ½σ²θ² + ∫_{ℝ\{0}} (e^{iθx}−1−iθx1_{|x|<1}) ν(dx), where ν is the Lévy measure controlling jumps. The compound Poisson process models random jumps at Poisson times. Stable processes are Lévy processes with power-law tails (α-stable: P(|X_t| > x) ~ cx^{-α}). Ergodic theory studies the long-run behavior of dynamical systems. Birkhoff's ergodic theorem: for a measure-preserving transformation T on a probability space, (1/n) Σ_{k=0}^{n−1} f(T^k x) → E[f | ℐ] a.s., where ℐ is the σ-algebra of invariant sets. Mixing (strong mixing, α-mixing) quantifies how quickly dependence decays. Malliavin calculus is a differential calculus on Wiener space, enabling smoothness of densities for SDE solutions. Rough path theory (Lyons) provides a pathwise approach to SDEs using higher-order iterated integrals.
Lévy processes extend Brownian motion to include jumps; ergodic theory explains why time averages converge to space averages.
