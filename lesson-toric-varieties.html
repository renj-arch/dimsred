Toric Varieties — Fans, Polytopes & Torus Actions

Toric varieties are normal algebraic varieties that contain an algebraic torus (ℂ^*)ⁿ as a dense open subset, with the torus action extending to the whole variety. They are the meeting point of algebraic geometry and combinatorics: every toric variety is described by a fan — a collection of strongly convex rational polyhedral cones in a real vector space N_ℝ ≅ ℝⁿ. The combinatorial data completely determines the geometry: the orbit-cone correspondence bijects cones of the fan with torus orbits, the Cox ring provides a quotient construction, and properties like smoothness, completeness, and projectivity are encoded in simple conditions on the fan. Toric varieties provide the simplest testing ground for deep ideas in algebraic geometry — resolutions of singularities, intersection theory, and mirror symmetry. In particular, Batyrev's construction uses reflexive polytopes to produce mirror pairs of Calabi-Yau hypersurfaces in toric varieties, linking toric geometry to string theory.

"Toric varieties are the algebraic geometer's simplest testing ground."
— William Fulton
Part 1

📏 The Algebraic Torus and Its Characters

Torus
The Algebraic Torus, Character Lattice, and One-Parameter Subgroups
The algebraic torus of dimension n is T ≅ (ℂ^*)ⁿ — a product of n copies of the multiplicative group of the complex numbers. It is a commutative affine algebraic group. Two lattices encode its structure: the character lattice M = Hom(T, ℂ^*) ≅ ℤⁿ consists of algebraic group homomorphisms χᵘ: T → ℂ^*, where for u = (u₁,…,uₙ) ∈ ℤⁿ, we have χᵘ(t₁,…,tₙ) = t₁^u₁ ⋯ tₙ^uₙ. The cocharacter lattice N = Hom(ℂ^*, T) ≅ ℤⁿ consists of one-parameter subgroups λᵛ: ℂ^* → T, where λᵛ(s) = (s^v₁,…,s^vₙ) for v = (v₁,…,vₙ) ∈ ℤⁿ. There is a natural perfect pairing ⟨,⟩ : M × N → ℤ given by ⟨u, v⟩ = Σ u_i v_i, which satisfies χᵘ(λᵛ(s)) = s^{⟨u,v⟩}. A toric variety is a normal algebraic variety X containing T as a Zariski open subset such that the multiplication action of T on itself extends to an algebraic action T × X → X. The simplest examples: projective space ℙⁿ (whose fan has cones generated by subsets of standard basis vectors), affine space 𝔸ⁿ (a single cone), and weighted projective spaces (fans with non-standard lattices).
The algebraic torus T ≅ (ℂ^*)ⁿ is the computational heart of toric geometry; its characters and cocharacters encode all combinatorial data through the lattices M and N.
Part 2

🌍 Fans and the Orbit-Cone Correspondence

Fans
Strongly Convex Rational Polyhedral Cones, Fans, and the Orbit-Cone Correspondence
Let N_ℝ = N ⊗_ℤ ℝ ≅ ℝⁿ. A strongly convex rational polyhedral cone σ ⊂ N_ℝ is a cone generated by finitely many lattice vectors that contains no line through the origin. Its dual cone σ^∨ = {x ∈ M_ℝ : ⟨x, y⟩ ≥ 0 for all y ∈ σ} is also rational polyhedral. The affine toric variety associated to σ is U_σ = Spec ℂ[σ^∨ ∩ M], where ℂ[σ^∨ ∩ M] is the monoid algebra of lattice points in the dual cone — its monomials correspond to characters of T. A fan Δ is a finite collection of such cones satisfying: (i) every face of a cone in Δ is in Δ, and (ii) the intersection of any two cones in Δ is a face of each. The toric variety X(Δ) is constructed by gluing the affine patches U_σ along their intersections U_{σ∩τ}. The orbit-cone correspondence is the central combinatorial-geometric dictionary: for each cone σ ∈ Δ of dimension k, there is a T-orbit O(σ) ≅ (ℂ^*)^{n−k} in X(Δ) whose closure is V(σ) ≅ X(Star(σ)). The fan determines all geometric properties: X(Δ) is complete (compact) iff the support |Δ| = ⋃_{σ∈Δ} σ equals all of N_ℝ; it is smooth iff every cone is generated by a subset of a ℤ-basis of N; it is projective iff Δ is the normal fan of a lattice polytope.
Every fan Δ produces a toric variety X(Δ), and the combinatorial structure of Δ — its cones, faces, and lattice points — perfectly encodes the geometry of X(Δ).
Part 3

🧮 Polytopes and Projective Toric Varieties

Polytopes
Lattice Polytopes, Normal Fans, and the Ehrhart Polynomial
A lattice polytope P ⊂ M_ℝ is the convex hull of finitely many points in the lattice M ≅ ℤⁿ. The normal fan Σ_P of P is the fan whose cones are the inner normal cones to the faces of P. The associated polarized toric variety (X_P, L_P) comes with an ample line bundle L_P whose space of global sections H⁰(X_P, L_P^⊗^k) is canonically identified with the lattice points in the dilated polytope kP ∩ M. The Hilbert polynomial counts these lattice points: dim H⁰(X_P, L_P^⊗^k) = |kP ∩ M| for sufficiently large k. The Ehrhart polynomial L(P, t) = |tP ∩ M| is a polynomial in t of degree n: L(P, t) = Vol(P)·tⁿ + (∂Vol(P)/2)·tⁿ⁻¹ + … + 1, where Vol(P) is the lattice volume. Divisors on toric varieties: the divisor class group Cl(X) ≅ ℤ^{r−n} where r = |Δ(1)| is the number of rays (1-dimensional cones). Each ray ρ corresponds to a T-invariant prime divisor D_ρ. The canonical divisor is K_X = −Σ_{ρ∈Δ(1)} D_ρ, giving K_X = −Σ D_ρ. The Cox ring (or total coordinate ring) is Cox(X) = ℂ[x_ρ : ρ ∈ Δ(1)], graded by the divisor class group Cl(X) via the degree map deg(x_ρ) = [D_ρ] ∈ Cl(X). The toric variety X is realized as a GIT quotient of Spec(Cox(X)) by Hom(Cl(X), ℂ^*).
Lattice polytopes give projective toric varieties; the Ehrhart polynomial counts lattice points, and the Cox ring provides a powerful homogeneous coordinate description.
Part 4

🔥 Mirror Symmetry and Calabi-Yau Hypersurfaces in Toric Varieties

Mirror
Batyrev's Construction, Reflexive Polytopes, and Hodge Number Combinatorics
A lattice polytope P ⊂ M_ℝ containing the origin in its interior is reflexive if its polar polytope P° = {y ∈ N_ℝ : ⟨x,y⟩ ≥ −1 for all x ∈ P} is also a lattice polytope (in the dual lattice N). Reflexive polytopes correspond to Gorenstein toric Fano varieties — the anticanonical divisor −K_X is ample and Cartier. Batyrev's mirror construction: given a reflexive polytope P, the toric variety X_P (from the normal fan of P°) and its mirror X_{P°} (from the normal fan of P) form a mirror pair. A Calabi-Yau hypersurface is defined by the zero set of a generic anticanonical section (a Laurent polynomial whose Newton polytope is P) inside X_P. The Hodge numbers of the Calabi-Yau hypersurface are computed combinatorially: h^{1,1} = l(P) − n − 1 − Σ_{faces} l^*(face) and h^{n-1,1} = l(P°) − n − 1 − Σ_{faces} l^*(face^°), where l(P) counts lattice points in P and l^*(face) counts interior lattice points. The quintic threefold — a degree 5 hypersurface in ℙ⁴ — is the classic Calabi-Yau 3-fold with h^{1,1}=1, h^{2,1}=101. The Batyrev-Borisov construction generalizes to nef-partitions and complete intersections in toric varieties, producing a vast landscape of mirror pairs that have been central to the development of mirror symmetry and string compactifications.
Reflexive polytopes produce mirror Calabi-Yau hypersurfaces in toric varieties; Hodge numbers are computed purely combinatorially, making mirror symmetry concrete and computable.
