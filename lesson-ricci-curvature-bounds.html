Synthetic Ricci Curvature Bounds — CD(K,N) & the Lott–Sturm–Villani Theory

Deep dive into Synthetic Ricci Curvature Bounds — the program of defining Ricci curvature lower bounds on metric measure spaces without a smooth Riemannian structure. Topics covered include the Bakry–Émery Γ₂ calculus (the Γ₂(f) = ½Δ|∇f|² − ⟨∇f, ∇Δf⟩ criterion characterizing Ric ≥ K via Γ₂(f) ≥ K|∇f|²), the equivalence between the Γ₂ criterion and the Wasserstein K-convexity of entropy for Riemannian manifolds, the Lott–Sturm–Villani CD(K,N) condition (the K-convexity of the Rényi entropy S_N along W₂-geodesics in the space of probability measures), the Sturm–von Renesse theorem (CD(K,∞) is equivalent to Ric ≥ K on Riemannian manifolds), the tensorization property (the product of CD(K,Nᵢ) spaces is CD(K, Σ Nᵢ)), the stability under measured Gromov–Hausdorff convergence (limits of Riemannian manifolds with Ric ≥ K satisfy CD(K,N) — this is the foundation for studying limits in the Gromov–Hausdorff sense), the Bishop–Gromov volume comparison theorem (vol(B(p,R)) / vol(B(p,r)) ≤ (R/r)^N for Ric ≥ K(N−1) with N the dimension), the Bonnet–Myers diameter bound (Ric ≥ K > 0 implies diam(M) ≤ π√((N−1)/K)), the Cheeger–Gromoll splitting theorem (a complete manifold with Ric ≥ 0 containing a line splits as ℝ × N), the Gromov–Hausdorff precompactness theorem (a collection of manifolds with uniform Ricci lower bound and diameter upper bound is precompact), and the RCD(K,N) condition (adding the infinitesimally Hilbertian condition to eliminate Finsler-like spaces) with the splitting theorem, rectifiability, and the regular set of full measure.

"Ricci curvature lower bounds are the fundamental geometric control — they constrain volume, diameter, spectrum, and topology."
— Mikhail Gromov
Part 1

📏 Ricci Curvature and Geodesic Convexity of Entropy

Ricci
The Bakry–Émery Γ₂ Calculus, the Wasserstein Interpretation, and the von Renesse–Sturm Theorem
Ricci curvature Ric is a (0,2)-tensor defined as the trace of the Riemann curvature: Ric(X, Y) = Σᵢ ⟨R(X, eᵢ)Y, eᵢ⟩ for an orthonormal frame {eᵢ}. On a Riemannian manifold (M, g), Ric ≥ K means Ric(v, v) ≥ K|v|² for all tangent vectors v. The Bakry–Émery Γ₂ calculus (1984): define Γ(f, g) = ½(Δ(fg) − fΔg − gΔf) = ⟨∇f, ∇g⟩, and Γ₂(f, g) = ½(ΔΓ(f, g) − Γ(f, Δg) − Γ(g, Δf)). For smooth functions, Γ₂(f) = Ric(∇f, ∇f) + ‖Hess f‖². The Γ₂ criterion: Ric ≥ K iff Γ₂(f) ≥ K|∇f|² = K Γ(f) for all smooth f. The Bakry–Émery–Ledoux inequality: if Ric ≥ K > 0, then for any Lipschitz function f, the variance satisfies Var_μ(f) ≤ (1/K) ∫ |∇f|² dμ (the Poincaré inequality), and also the log-Sobolev inequality Ent_μ(f²) ≤ (2/K) ∫ |∇f|² dμ. The von Renesse–Sturm theorem (2005): on a compact Riemannian manifold M, the following are equivalent: (i) Ric ≥ K, (ii) the Boltzmann–Shannon entropy H(μ) = ∫ ρ log ρ dvol is K-convex along Wasserstein-2 geodesics in P(M), (iii) the Monge–Ampère equation has a unique solution and W₂ satisfies the K-convexity inequality: H(μ_t) ≤ (1−t)H(μ₀) + tH(μ₁) − (K/2) t(1−t) W₂²(μ₀, μ₁). This is the bridge between classical Ricci curvature and the synthetic CD condition. The Γ₂ criterion on metric measure spaces: in the RCD setting, Γ₂ can be defined via the heat flow and the carré du champ operator, providing an analytic characterization of curvature bounds.
The equivalence between Ric ≥ K and K-convexity of entropy in the Wasserstein space is the fundamental insight that allows Ricci curvature to be defined synthetically on metric measure spaces without any smooth structure.
Part 2

🌐 The CD(K,N) Condition

Synthetic
The Lott–Sturm–Villani CD(K,N) Condition, Geodesic Convexity, and Displacement Convexity Classes
The CD(K,N) condition (Lott–Sturm–Villani, 2005) is defined for a metric measure space (X, d, m). Let P₂(X, m) be the space of probability measures with finite second moment that are absolutely continuous with respect to m. For μ₀ = ρ₀ m and μ₁ = ρ₁ m in P₂(X, m), define the Rényi entropy S_N(μ) = −∫ ρ^{1−1/N} dm (with N ∈ (1, ∞)) and the Boltzmann–Shannon entropy H(μ) = ∫ ρ log ρ dm for N = ∞. The space satisfies CD(K,N) if for every pair μ₀, μ₁ ∈ P₂(X, m), there exists a W₂-geodesic μ_t (an optimal transport interpolation) such that for all t ∈ [0, 1]: S_N(μ_t) ≤ (1−t) S_N(μ₀) + t S_N(μ₁) − (K/2) t(1−t) W₂²(μ₀, μ₁) in the case N = ∞; for finite N, the inequality involves the distortion coefficients τ_{K,N}^{(t)}(θ) = t^{1/N} (sin(tθ)/sin θ)^{N−1} when K > 0 and the hyperbolic sine when K < 0. The Sturm distortion coefficients τ_{K,N}^{(t)} encode the Jacobi field behavior in model spaces. For K = 0, τ₀,N^{(t)} = t. For K > 0, the coefficients come from spheres S^N_R with Ric = K(N−1). The CD(K,N) condition is stable under measured Gromov–Hausdorff convergence (Sturm, Lott–Villani): if (X_n, d_n, m_n) → (X, d, m) in the measured Gromov–Hausdorff sense and each X_n satisfies CD(K,N), then X satisfies CD(K,N). This stability is crucial for studying limits of Riemannian manifolds. The tensorization property: if (X₁, d₁, m₁) satisfies CD(K,N₁) and (X₂, d₂, m₂) satisfies CD(K,N₂), then the product X₁ × X₂ with the L² metric and product measure satisfies CD(K, N₁+N₂). The local-to-global property (Cavalletti–Milman): if (X, d, m) has the CD(K,N) property locally (every point has a neighborhood that is CD(K,N)), then the global space is CD(K,N). The CD(K,N) condition implies the Bishop–Gromov inequality: for any ball B(x, R), the volume ratio vol(B(x,R))/vol(B(x,r)) ≤ vol(B_K(N−1)(R))/vol(B_K(N−1)(r)) where B_K(N−1)(r) is a ball in the model space of constant curvature K/(N−1).
The CD(K,N) condition is the definitive synthetic notion of Ricci curvature bounded below by K and dimension bounded above by N — it is stable, tensorizable, and recovers all classical geometric consequences of Ricci curvature bounds.
Part 3

🔬 Stability and Tensorization

Consequences
Gromov–Hausdorff Stability, Tensorization, and Geometric Consequences of CD(K,N)
The measured Gromov–Hausdorff convergence (Fukaya, 1985) extends Gromov–Hausdorff convergence to metric measure spaces by including the convergence of measures. A sequence (X_n, d_n, m_n) converges to (X, d, m) if there exist metric isometric embeddings into a common metric space such that the spaces converge in the Hausdorff sense and the pushforward measures converge weakly. The stability theorem (Sturm, Lott–Villani): the CD(K,N) condition is preserved under measured Gromov–Hausdorff convergence. This is the foundation for the Gromov precompactness theorem: a collection of Riemannian manifolds M_n with Ric_{M_n} ≥ K(N−1) and diam(M_n) ≤ D is precompact in the Gromov–Hausdorff topology. The limits are metric measure spaces satisfying CD(K,N) but may have singularities. The Bishop–Gromov volume comparison for CD(K,N) spaces: the function r ↦ m(B(x,r)) / v_{K,N}(r) is non-increasing, where v_{K,N}(r) is the volume of a ball of radius r in the model space (the simply connected space form of constant curvature K/(N−1)). The Bonnet–Myers theorem for CD(K,N): if K > 0, then diam(X) ≤ π√((N−1)/K). The Abresch–Gromoll inequality (a version of the splitting theorem): in an CD(0,N) space with a line, the space splits as ℝ × Y with the product measure. The Lévy–Gromov isoperimetric inequality: for a CD(K,N) space with K > 0, the isoperimetric profile I(θ) ≥ I_{K,N}(θ) where I_{K,N} is the profile of the model space. The Poincaré inequality and the log-Sobolev inequality hold on CD(K,N) spaces when K > 0 with constants determined by K. The spectral gap λ₁ of the Laplacian on a CD(K,N) space satisfies λ₁ ≥ K/(N−1) when K > 0 (the Lichnerowicz estimate).
The geometric consequences of CD(K,N) include the classical Bonnet–Myers, Bishop–Gromov, and Lichnerowicz theorems — proving that the synthetic condition captures the full essence of Ricci curvature bounds.
Part 4

🏛️ RCD Spaces and the Synthetic Program

RCD
The RCD(K,N) Condition, the Splitting Theorem, Rectifiability, and the Future of Synthetic Curvature
The RCD(K,N) condition (Gigli, Ambrosio–Tresoldi, 2010) refines CD(K,N) by adding the requirement that the space is infinitesimally Hilbertian: the Sobolev space W^{1,2}(X) (defined via the Cheeger energy) is a Hilbert space. This excludes Finsler-like metric measure spaces that satisfy CD(K,N) but are not Riemannian — e.g., a finite-dimensional ℓ^p-space with p ≠ 2 satisfies CD(0,N) but not any RCD condition. The splitting theorem (Gigli, 2013): an RCD(0,N) space that contains a line is isometric to ℝ × Y for some RCD(0,N−1) space Y. This is the synthetic analogue of the Cheeger–Gromoll splitting theorem. The rectifiability theorem (Mondino–Naber, 2013): an RCD(K,N) space is rectifiable — there exists a regular set ℛ of full measure such that every point of ℛ has a tangent cone isometric to ℝ^k for some k (the dimension). The regular set is open in the measured Gromov–Hausdorff sense and has a C^{1,α} structure. The essential dimension of an RCD(K,N) space is an integer n ≤ N (the dimension of the regular set). The heat kernel on an RCD(K,N) space exists, is unique, and satisfies Gaussian estimates (the Li–Yau type bounds). The RCD program has led to major advances: the stability of the heat kernel under measured Gromov–Hausdorff convergence (Gigli–Mondino), the topological regularity of RCD spaces (De Philippis–Gigli–Pasqualetto), the Ahlfors regularity of the measure, and the Brenier theorem on RCD spaces (Gigli). The Cheeger–Colding theory of limits of Riemannian manifolds with Ricci bounds (pre-2005) already showed that the limit spaces have a regular-singular decomposition, are rectifiable, and that the singular set has codimension at least 2 — these results are now recovered and extended in the RCD framework. The synthetic program continues to grow: Ricci curvature bounds for metric measure spaces with variable curvature, the CD(K,N) with variable N, the Riemannian cone characterization of RCD spaces, and the connections to the Enriched Ricci flow (Sturm–Lott).
The RCD(K,N) condition is the definitive Riemannian synthetic Ricci curvature bound — it is stable, rectifiable, and carries all the analytic and geometric consequences of classical Ricci curvature, opening the door to understanding highly singular spaces that arise as limits.
