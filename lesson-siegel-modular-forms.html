Siegel Modular Forms — The Siegel Upper Half-Space, Eisenstein Series & the Saito–Kurokawa Lift

Deep dive into Siegel modular forms — the Siegel upper half-space ℃_g = {τ ∈ M_g(ℂ) | τ = τ^T, Im(τ) > 0} (a Hermitian symmetric domain of type III), the symplectic group Sp(2g,ℝ) acting on ℃_g by τ ↦ (Aτ+B)(Cτ+D)⁻¹, Siegel modular forms of weight k (holomorphic functions F: ℃_g → ℂ with F((Aτ+B)(Cτ+D)⁻¹) = det(Cτ+D)^k F(τ)), the Fourier–Jacobi expansion, the Siegel Φ-operator and the theory of cusp forms, Siegel Eisenstein series E_k^{(g)}(τ) = Σ_{(C,D)} det(Cτ+D)⁻^k summed over coprime symmetric pairs, Klingen’s Eisenstein series, the Maass lift (a correspondence between elliptic modular forms and Siegel modular forms of degree 2), the Saito–Kurokawa lift (a lifting from elliptic cusp forms of weight 2k−2 to Siegel cusp forms of weight k for Sp(4,ℝ)), the Ikeda lift (the generalization to higher degrees), and connections to the Langlands functoriality and L-functions.

“Siegel modular forms are the natural generalization of classical modular forms to several variables, living on the symmetric space of the symplectic group.”

Part 1

📐 The Siegel Upper Half-Space and Symplectic Group

Siegel Space
℃_g, the Symplectic Group Sp(2g,ℝ), and the Action
The Siegel upper half-space of genus g is ℃_g = {τ ∈ M_g(ℂ) | τ = τ^T, Im(τ) > 0} where Im(τ) > 0 means positive definite. For g=1, ℃₁ = ℓ (the usual upper half-plane). For g>1, ℃_g is a Hermitian symmetric domain of type III (Cartan’s classification), with automorphism group the real symplectic group Sp(2g,ℝ). The group Sp(2g,ℝ) = {M ∈ GL(2g,ℝ) | M^T J_g M = J_g} where J_g = (0 I_g; −I_g 0) is the standard symplectic matrix. It acts transitively on ℃_g by τ ↦ (Aτ+B)(Cτ+D)⁻¹ with the decomposition M = ((A,B),(C,D)) in g×g blocks. The maximal compact subgroup is K ≅ U(g) fixing τ = iI_g. The discrete subgroup Γ_g = Sp(2g,ℤ) (the Siegel modular group) acts properly discontinuously on ℃_g. The quotient ℃_g/Γ_g is a moduli space of principally polarized abelian varieties (the Satake–Baily–Borel compactification). The boundary components of the Satake compactification correspond to degenerate abelian varieties (products of lower-dimensional ones). The Fourier expansion of a Siegel modular form F(τ) = Σ_{T≥0} a(T) exp(2πi Tr(Tτ)) where T runs over half-integral positive semidefinite symmetric matrices (T = (t_{ij}) with t_{ii} ∈ ℤ and 2t_{ij} ∈ ℤ for i≠j). The theta series θ_S(τ) = Σ_{x∈ℤ^g} exp(πi x^T S x τ) for a positive definite integral quadratic form S gives Siegel modular forms.
The Siegel upper half-space generalizes the Poincaré upper half-plane to genus g, with the symplectic group replacing SL₂(ℝ).
Part 2

🏛 Siegel Modular Forms and Eisenstein Series

Siegel Forms
Siegel Modular Forms, Cusp Forms, the Φ-Operator, and Eisenstein Series
A Siegel modular form of weight k for Γ_g = Sp(2g,ℤ) is a holomorphic function F: ℃_g → ℂ satisfying F((Aτ+B)(Cτ+D)⁻¹) = det(Cτ+D)^k F(τ) for all ((A,B),(C,D)) ∈ Γ_g, with holomorphicity at the cusps (automatic for g>1 by Koecher’s principle). For g=1, we need holomorphicity at i∞. The Siegel Φ-operator (the Siegel analogue of the Ramanujan θ-operator) is defined for g≥2 by (ΦF)(τ') = lim_{t→i∞} F((τ' 0; 0 t)) where τ' ∈ ℃_{g−1}. The image of Φ is a Siegel modular form of degree g−1. The kernel of Φ is the space of Siegel cusp forms S_k(Γ_g). The space M_k(Γ_g) of Siegel modular forms is finite-dimensional. The Siegel Eisenstein series: E_k^{(g)}(τ) = Σ_{(C,D)} det(Cτ+D)⁻^k where the sum is over all coprime symmetric pairs (C,D) of g×g matrices (the set Γ_g,∞\Γ_g divided by the stabilizer of the cusp). The series converges absolutely for k > g+1. The Fourier expansion of E_k^{(g)}(τ) has rational coefficients (the Siegel formula: the constant term involves ζ(1−2k) and ζ(3−2k),…). For g=2, the Siegel Eisenstein series of weight k has Fourier coefficients a(T) = Σ_{d|ε(T)} d^{k−1} σ_{k−2}(ε(T)/d) where σ_r(n) = Σ_{d|n} d^r and ε(T) = gcd of the entries of 2T. The Klingen Eisenstein series lift a cusp form of degree g−1 to a Siegel modular form of degree g by summing over Γ_g. The Maaß space M_k^*(Γ_2) consists of those Siegel modular forms of degree 2 whose Fourier coefficients a(T) depend only on the GL₂(ℤ)-equivalence class of T (the discriminant and the content).
Siegel Eisenstein series are the building blocks of spaces of Siegel modular forms; their Fourier coefficients encode deep arithmetic information.

🌐 The Saito–Kurokawa Lift

Lift
The Saito–Kurokawa Lift, the Maaß Space, and the Ikeda Lift
The Saito–Kurokawa lift (Kurokawa, 1978; Saito, 1977) is a linear map from elliptic cusp forms of weight 2k−2 for SL₂(ℤ) to Siegel cusp forms of weight k for Sp(4,ℤ). More precisely, given a normalized Hecke eigenform f ∈ S_{2k−2}(SL₂(ℤ)) with Fourier coefficients a(n), the lifted form F = Lift(f) ∈ S_k(Sp(4,ℤ)) has Fourier coefficients: for T = ((a,b/2),(b/2,c)) with a,b,c ∈ ℤ, a,b,c ≥ 0, the Fourier coefficient A(T) = Σ_{d|gcd(a,b,c)} d^{k−1} a(4ac−b^2/4d^2). The lift factors through the Maaß space: the image of the Saito–Kurokawa lift is exactly the Maaß subspace M_k^*(Γ_2). Andrianov proved that the Maaß subspace is characterized by the property that its Fourier coefficients a(T) depend only on the GL₂(ℤ)-orbit of T. The L-function of the lifted form F equals L(f, s) · ζ(s−k+1) · ζ(s−k+2). The lift is injective. The Ikeda lift (Ikeda, 2001) generalizes the Saito–Kurokawa lift to higher degrees: given an elliptic cusp form f ∈ S_{2k}(SL₂(ℤ)), the Ikeda lift produces a Siegel cusp form F ∈ S_{k+n−1}(Sp(2n,ℤ)) whose standard L-function is L(f, s) · ∏_{j=1}^{n−1} ζ(2s−2j). The Fourier coefficients of F are given by the Ikeda’s formula involving the Fourier coefficients of f and the Witt’s theorem on representation numbers of quadratic forms. The Ikeda lift is a crucial example of Langlands functoriality: it corresponds to the transfer from GL₂ to the symplectic group Sp(2n) via the symmetric square L-function.
The Saito–Kurokawa lift constructs Siegel modular forms from elliptic modular forms, realizing Langlands functoriality between GL₂ and GSp₄.

🌟 Applications and Further Topics

Applications
L-Functions, the Bloch–Kato Conjecture, and the Gan–Gross–Prasad Conjecture
Siegel modular forms appear throughout number theory. The Andrianov L-function attached to a Siegel modular form of degree 2 is the spinor L-function Z_F(s) = ∏_p Q_p(p⁻^s)⁻¹ where Q_p(X) = (1−α_p X)(1−α_p⁻¹β_p X)(1−β_p⁻¹γ_p X)(1−γ_p X) and α_p,β_p,γ_p arise from Satake parameters. The standard L-function L(F, s, St) relates to the symmetric square L-function of the corresponding elliptic modular form under the Saito–Kurokawa lift. The Bloch–Kato conjecture for motives attached to Siegel modular forms relates special values of L-functions to Selmer groups. The Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture (for symplectic groups) predicts the non-vanishing of periods of automorphic forms on GSpin groups, with applications to the Gross–Zagier formula and the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture. The theta correspondence (Howe duality) pairs Siegel modular forms with metaplectic forms: the Shimura correspondence lifts forms of half-integer weight to integer weight. The lift of Yoshida (the Yoshida lift) constructs Siegel modular forms of degree 2 from pairs of elliptic modular forms. The Siegel–Weil formula connects Siegel Eisenstein series to theta series via the Weil representation; this is the key to the analytic continuation and functional equation of the Siegel Eisenstein series. The Kudla program (Kudla, Millson, Rapoport) relates Fourier coefficients of Siegel Eisenstein series to intersection numbers of special cycles on Shimura varieties, leading to arithmetic applications like the Kudla’s conjecture on central derivatives of L-functions. The paramodular conjecture (Brumer–Kramer) predicts that abelian surfaces with endomorphism ring ℤ correspond to Siegel modular forms of degree 2 with rational eigenvalues.
Siegel modular forms sit at the intersection of automorphic forms, arithmetic geometry, and the Langlands program, with applications to L-functions and cycle theory.
