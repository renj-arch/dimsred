Geometric Langlands — S-duality, Hecke Eigensheaves & the Quantum Geometric Langlands

Deep dive into the Geometric Langlands Program — the geometric reformulation of the classical Langlands correspondence in number theory. Topics covered include the classical Langlands correspondence (a correspondence between Galois representations and automorphic forms for reductive groups over global fields), the geometric reformulation (let X be a smooth projective curve over ℂ; the Galois side becomes the moduli stack of G-bundles with connections, the automorphic side becomes the moduli stack of G'-bundles called the Hitchin moduli space), Hecke eigensheaves (D-modules on the moduli stack of G-bundles satisfying Hecke eigenvalue conditions), the Hecke operators (the geometric analogue of Hecke operators in number theory — correspondences connecting moduli stacks of bundles with different parabolic structures), the Hitchin fibration (the integrable system whose fibers are Abelian varieties — Prym varieties of spectral curves), the Hitchin discriminant locus and the singularities of the fibration, the Langlands duality between the groups G and LG (Langlands dual group, obtained by exchanging roots and coroots), the Beilinson–Drinfeld construction of Hecke eigensheaves using the Hitchin system, the Kapustin–Witten interpretation of geometric Langlands as S-duality in N=4 supersymmetric Yang–Mills theory in four dimensions (a duality between the gauge theory for G and for LG exchanging electric and magnetic degrees of freedom), the quantum geometric Langlands correspondence (the deformation of the correspondence by a parameter k related to the level of the WZW model and the quantum deformation of the Hitchin system), and the connection to the Ngo–Laumon proof of the fundamental lemma (the Langlands–Shelstad fundamental lemma proved using the Hitchin fibration).

"The geometric Langlands program is a vast generalization of the Fourier transform — it intertwines the geometry of moduli spaces of bundles with the representation theory of reductive groups."
— Alexander Beilinson
Part 1

📐 The Classical Langlands Correspondence

Number Theory
Local Fields, Global Fields, the Langlands Conjectures, and the Langlands Dual Group
The classical Langlands correspondence (Langlands, 1967) relates two fundamental objects for a reductive group G over a global field F (e.g., ℚ or 𝔽_q(t)): the automorphic side (automorphic representations of G(𝔸_F) where 𝔸_F is the adele ring) and the Galois side (representations of the absolute Galois group Gal(F̄/F) into the Langlands dual group LG(ℚ̄_ℓ)). The Langlands dual group LG: for a reductive group G, let (X, Δ, X^∨, Δ^∨) be the root data. The dual group LG is the reductive group over ℂ with the dual root data (X^∨, Δ^∨, X, Δ). Examples: LGL(n) = GL(n), LSO(2n+1) = Sp(2n), LSp(2n) = SO(2n+1), LE₆ = E₆, LE₇ = E₇, LE₈ = E₈, LF₄ = F₄, LG₂ = G₂. The local Langlands correspondence for a local field F_v (e.g., ℚ_p): a bijection between irreducible admissible representations of G(F_v) and L-parameters (homomorphisms from the Weil–Deligne group WF_v' → LG(ℂ)) satisfying certain compatibility conditions (the local Langlands conjectures, proven for GL_n by Harris–Taylor and Henniart). The global Langlands correspondence: a correspondence between cuspidal automorphic representations of G(𝔸_F) and global Langlands parameters (Galois representations into LG(ℂ)). The Arthur–Selberg trace formula: a powerful analytic tool linking the spectral side (automorphic representations) with the geometric side (orbital integrals). The fundamental lemma (Langlands–Shelstad): an identity of orbital integrals used in the trace formula to prove Langlands functoriality — proved by Ngŏ Bao Châu (Fields Medal, 2010) using the geometry of the Hitchin fibration. The functoriality principle: for any homomorphism LH₁ → LH₂, there should be a transfer of automorphic representations of H₁ to automorphic representations of H₂ — the overarching conjecture of the Langlands program.
The classical Langlands correspondence is one of the most profound conjectures in mathematics — it bridges Galois theory (number theory) and automorphic forms (harmonic analysis on adele groups), with the Langlands dual group encoding the symmetry.
Part 2

🌀 The Geometric Langlands Correspondence

Geometry
The Moduli Stack of G-Bundles, Hecke Eigensheaves, and the Beilinson–Drinfeld Construction
The geometric Langlands correspondence (Drinfeld, Laumon, Beilinson–Drinfeld) replaces the number field F with the function field of a smooth projective curve X over ℂ. The automorphic side becomes the moduli stack Bun_G(X) of algebraic G-bundles on X. Instead of automorphic functions, we study D-modules (sheaves of modules over the sheaf of differential operators) on Bun_G. The Galois side becomes the moduli stack Loc_{LG}(X) of flat LG-bundles on X (i.e., principal LG-bundles with a connection — local systems). The correspondence: for every flat LG-bundle E ∈ Loc_{LG}(X), there exists a Hecke eigensheaf Aut_E on Bun_G(X) — a D-module with eigenvalue E under the Hecke operators. The Hecke operators are defined via the Hecke correspondence: over Bun_G × X, there is the Hecke stack H = {(P, P', x, φ)} where P, P' ∈ Bun_G and φ: P|_{X\{x}} → P'|_{X\{x}} is an isomorphism. For a representation V of LG (which corresponds to a finite-dimensional representation of G^∨ = LG), the corresponding Hecke operator H_V: D-mod(Bun_G) → D-mod(Bun_G) acts by pullback and pushforward along the Hecke correspondence. The condition that Aut_E is a Hecke eigensheaf with eigenvalue E means: H_V(Aut_E) ≅ E_V ⊠ Aut_E, where E_V is the vector bundle on X associated to E via V. The Beilinson–Drinfeld construction: for a smooth spectral curve (a smooth ramified double cover of X), they constructed Hecke eigensheaves using the Hitchin system — the Hitchin fiber is an Abelian variety (a Prym variety), and the D-module is the Dolbeault–Grothendieck resolution of the sheaf of functions on the Hitchin fiber. The geometric Satake equivalence (Mirković–Vilonen, Ginzburg): the category of LG-equivariant perverse sheaves on the affine Grassmannian Gr_G = G(ℂ((t)))/G(ℂ[[t]]) is equivalent to the tensor category of representations of LG — giving the geometric analogue of the Satake isomorphism linking spherical Hecke algebras to representations of the Langlands dual group.
The geometric Langlands correspondence translates the number-theoretic Langlands program into the language of D-modules on moduli stacks of bundles over curves, where Hecke eigensheaves replace automorphic forms and flat LG-bundles replace Galois representations.
Part 3

🌌 The Hitchin Fibration and Mirror Symmetry

Integrable
The Hitchin System, the Hitchin Fibration, and Hyperkähler Mirror Symmetry
The Hitchin moduli space M_H(G) = {(E, φ)} where E is a principal G-bundle on a curve X and φ ∈ H⁰(X, ad(E) ⊗ K_X) is a Higgs field — a section of the bundle of endomorphisms twisted by the canonical bundle K_X. The Hitchin equations: F_A + [φ, φ†] = 0 and ∂̄_A φ = 0 — these are the equations for a Higgs bundle, which is equivalent to a flat connection on X (the non-Abelian Hodge correspondence of Hitchin–Simpson). The Hitchin fibration h: M_H(G) → B_G = ⊕_{i=1}^r H⁰(X, K_X^{d_i+1}) where the d_i are the degrees of the basic invariant polynomials of G. The map h sends (E, φ) to the characteristic polynomial coefficients of φ (the Hitchin map). The generic fiber of h is a Prym variety — an Abelian variety of dimension dim G (g−1) for a curve X of genus g. The spectral curve Σ: the equation det(φ − t·Id) = 0 defines a ramified cover Σ → X of degree rank G, and the Higgs bundle data is equivalent to a line bundle on Σ (the BNR correspondence of Beauville–Narasimhan–Ramanan). The Hitchin system is a completely integrable system: the fibers are Abelian varieties and the Poisson structure on M_H(G) (coming from the cotangent bundle T*Bun_G) is torus-invariant. The global nilpotent cone h⁻¹(0) — the union of all nilpotent Higgs fields — is a Lagrangian subvariety of M_H(G) and plays a role in the geometric Langlands correspondence. Mirror symmetry (Kapustin–Witten, Hausel–Thaddeus): the Langlands dual groups G and LG correspond under mirror symmetry. M_H(G) and M_H(LG) are mirror partners in the sense of Strominger–Yau–Zaslow: the Hitchin fibration for G and for LG have the same base B, but the generic fibers are dual Abelian varieties (the Prym variety for G and its dual for LG). The topological mirror symmetry: the Hodge numbers of M_H(G) and M_H(LG) are related — the Hodge diamond is rotated, as conjectured by Hausel–Thaddeus and proven by Groechenig–Wyss–Ziegler. The P=W conjecture (de Cataldo–Hausel–Migliorini): the perverse filtration on the cohomology of M_H(G) arising from the Hitchin fibration corresponds to the weight filtration on the cohomology of the character variety (the space of flat connections) via the non-Abelian Hodge correspondence.
The Hitchin system is a completely integrable system whose fibers are Abelian varieties — its geometry encodes the mirror symmetry between dual groups and provides the spectral data needed to construct Hecke eigensheaves in the geometric Langlands program.
Part 4

🔥 S-Duality and the Quantum Geometric Langlands

Duality
The Kapustin–Witten Interpretation, S-Duality in N=4 SYM, and the Quantum Geometric Langlands Correspondence
The Kapustin–Witten interpretation (2006): the geometric Langlands correspondence is interpreted as S-duality in N=4 supersymmetric Yang–Mills theory (SYM) in four dimensions. In N=4 SYM with gauge group G, there is an SL(2, ℤ) duality group generated by the transformation τ → −1/τ (S-duality, exchanging electric and magnetic charges) and τ → τ + 1 (T-duality). Here τ = θ/(2π) + 4πi/g² is the complex coupling constant. S-duality sends G to LG (the Langlands dual group) and interchanges Wilson loops ('t Hooft loops) — electric and magnetic observables. Kapustin and Witten consider the topological twist of N=4 SYM on a 4-manifold of the form Σ × C where Σ is a Riemann surface and C is a smooth curve. The resulting 2-dimensional topological field theory on Σ has a moduli space of vacua given by the Hitchin moduli space M_H(G). The boundary conditions along Σ (or more precisely along the boundaries of the cigar-like geometry) correspond to D-modules on Bun_G. The S-duality transformation sends the G-theory to the LG-theory and exchanges the two types of boundary conditions — giving the geometric Langlands correspondence. The quantum geometric Langlands correspondence (Beilinson–Drinfeld, Gaitsgory–Lurie): a deformation of the geometric Langlands correspondence by a parameter k (the level of the WZW model). On the automorphic side, D-modules on Bun_G are replaced by k-twisted D-modules (modules over the sheaf of differential operators with a twist by the level k). On the Galois side, local systems on X are deformed to k-twisted local systems (flat G-connections with a specific central extension). The quantum Hitchin system: the Hitchin system can be quantized by replacing the Higgs field φ by a differential operator (the quantum Hitchin connection). The quantization parameter is ℏ = 1/(k + h^∨) where h^∨ is the dual Coxeter number. The KZ/WZW correspondence links the quantum Hitchin system to the representation theory of affine Lie algebras. The Tamakian formalism (the theory of tensor categories) reformulates the geometric Langlands in terms of the category of D-modules on Bun_G being equivalent to the category of O-modules on Loc_{LG}(X) — a categorical equivalence. This is the subject of the Gaitsgory–Lurie program (the categorical geometric Langlands conjecture): an equivalence between the derived category of D-modules on Bun_G and the derived category of quasi-coherent sheaves on Loc_{LG}(X). This program uses powerful technology from derived algebraic geometry, ind-coherent sheaves, and the theory of arithmetic D-modules.
S-duality in N=4 SYM provides the physical interpretation of the geometric Langlands correspondence — it is the electromagnetic duality exchanging electric (Wilson) and magnetic ('t Hooft) Wilson loops, with the quantum deformation corresponding to the level k of the WZW model.

The Affine Grassmannian and the Geometric Satake Equivalence

The affine Grassmannian GrG = G(𝒦)/G(𝒪) — where 𝒦 = ℂ((t)) and 𝒪 = ℂ[[t]] — is an ind-scheme that parametrizes G-bundles on the formal disc with a trivialization on the punctured disc. Its geometry provides the local model for the Hecke correspondences used in the geometric Langlands program. The geometric Satake equivalence, proved by Ginzburg, Mirković–Vilonen, and others, establishes a tensor equivalence between the category of spherical perverse sheaves on GrG (with convolution product) and the category of finite-dimensional representations of the Langlands dual group LG. This equivalence is the local geometric counterpart of the classical Satake isomorphism for p-adic groups. The convolution product on perverse sheaves corresponds to the tensor product of representations, and the fiber functor is given by the global sections functor. The affine Grassmannian also carries a natural action of the loop group G(𝒦), and its Schubert varieties are indexed by dominant coweights of G, which correspond to dominant weights of LG. The geometric Satake equivalence is functorial with respect to homomorphisms of reductive groups and compatible with the nearby cycles functor, linking it to the monodromy of the Hitchin system. In the geometric Langlands correspondence, the Hecke operators at a point x ∈ X are defined by modifying a G-bundle at x using the affine Grassmannian; the geometric Satake equivalence identifies the eigenvalues of these Hecke operators with LG-local systems. This local-to-global principle is central: the global Hecke eigensheaf condition is built from the local geometric Satake data. The affine Grassmannian also appears in the construction of the Whittaker category and the geometric Casselman–Shalika formula, which expresses the Whittaker functions of automorphic forms in terms of the geometry of GrG. Recent work by Braverman–Finkelberg–Nakajima extends the geometric Satake to the Coulomb branches of 3d N=4 gauge theories, providing a physical interpretation via mirror symmetry.

The Categorical Geometric Langlands Conjecture and Ind-Coherent Sheaves

The categorical geometric Langlands conjecture, formulated by Gaitsgory and Lurie, asserts an equivalence of derived ∞-categories: D-mod(BunG) ≃ IndCohNilp(LocSysLG), where the right-hand side is the category of ind-coherent sheaves on the stack of LG-local systems with nilpotent singular support. This formulation replaces the earlier naive version using quasi-coherent sheaves on LocSysLG, which fails to capture the correct categorical structure because the stack of local systems is highly singular and non-smooth. Ind-coherent sheaves, introduced by Gaitsgory–Rozenblyum, behave like coherent sheaves on smooth schemes but are well-behaved on arbitrary derived stacks. The nilpotent singular support condition restricts to the global nilpotent cone, which is the image of the Hitchin fibration’s zero fiber under the non-Abelian Hodge correspondence. The equivalence is expected to be compatible with the natural monoidal structures: convolution of D-modules on BunG (coming from the Hecke correspondences) corresponds to the tensor product of ind-coherent sheaves on LocSysLG. The proof strategy uses the theory of chiral algebras and factorization algebras on the Ran space of the curve X. The chiral algebra of D-modules on BunG is identified with the chiral algebra of the affine Kac–Moody algebra at the critical level, which by the Feigin–Frenkel theorem is isomorphic to the algebra of functions on the space of LG-opers. This provides a bridge to the quantum geometric Langlands correspondence. The categorical equivalence also implies the classical geometric Langlands correspondence by taking the category of compact objects and passing to the Grothendieck group. The technology of derived algebraic geometry, in particular the theory of prestacks and the six-functor formalism for ind-coherent sheaves, is essential for the formulation and proof. Recent progress by Gaitsgory, Lurie, and others has established the conjecture for GLn and for tori, and has developed the necessary foundations for the general reductive case.

Worked Example: Hecke Eigensheaf Condition via Geometric Satake
Let G = SL2 and X = ℙ1. Describe how the geometric Satake equivalence for the affine Grassmannian GrSL2 identifies the eigenvalue of the Hecke operator at a point x ∈ X with a rank-2 local system on X.
The affine Grassmannian GrSL2 has Schubert varieties indexed by dominant coweights of SL2, i.e., non-negative integers n. The geometric Satake equivalence sends the intersection cohomology complex ICn of the Schubert variety Grn to the irreducible representation Vn of LG = PGL2 of dimension n+1. The Hecke operator Hx at x acts on a D-module ℱ on BunSL2 by the correspondence: BunSL2 ← Heckex → BunSL2 × X, where the fiber of Heckex over a bundle ℰ is GrSL2. The eigenvalue condition for ℱ to be a Hecke eigensheaf with eigenvalue a local system ℰ on X is that for each n, the action of Hx via ICn yields ℱ ⊠ Vn(ℰ), where Vn(ℰ) is the vector bundle associated to ℰ via the representation Vn. The geometric Satake equivalence guarantees that the convolution of ICn with ℱ corresponds to the tensor product of the corresponding representation of LG with the fiber of ℰ. Thus the eigenvalue local system ℰ is precisely the LG-local system whose monodromy gives the eigenvalues of the Hecke operators.
Exam Strategy: Categorical vs Classical Statements
When a question asks for the geometric Langlands correspondence, distinguish between the classical statement (equivalence of derived categories of D-modules and quasi-coherent sheaves) and the categorical statement (equivalence of D-mod(BunG) with IndCohNilp(LocSysLG)). The categorical version is the correct modern formulation; the classical version is a shadow obtained by taking compact objects. Examiners often test whether you know that quasi-coherent sheaves on LocSysLG are insufficient because LocSys is not smooth. Remember the nilpotent singular support condition and the role of ind-coherent sheaves.

The Quantum Geometric Langlands at Critical Level and the Feigin–Frenkel Center

The quantum geometric Langlands correspondence depends on a parameter ℏ = 1/(k + h∨), where k is the level of the affine Kac–Moody algebra and h∨ is the dual Coxeter number. The critical level corresponds to k = -h∨, i.e., ℏ → ∞. At this level, the center of the completed universal enveloping algebra of the affine Kac–Moody algebra, known as the Feigin–Frenkel center, becomes large and isomorphic to the algebra of functions on the space of LG-opers on the formal disc. An oper is a G-bundle with a connection and a reduction to a Borel subgroup satisfying a transversality condition; the space of LG-opers on X is a finite-dimensional variety that embeds into LocSysLG(X). The Feigin–Frenkel theorem states that the center of the affine vertex algebra at critical level is canonically isomorphic to the algebra of functions on the space of LG-opers on the formal disc. This provides a commutative algebra of quantum Hamiltonians for the quantum Hitchin system. In the quantum geometric Langlands correspondence, the category of k-twisted D-modules on BunG at critical level is expected to be equivalent to the category of modules over the sheaf of chiral differential operators on LocSysLG supported on the oper locus. The classical limit ℏ → 0 (i.e., k → ∞) recovers the classical geometric Langlands correspondence, where the quantum Hitchin Hamiltonians become the classical Hitchin Hamiltonians and the oper locus becomes the global nilpotent cone. The critical level is also where the KZ/WZW correspondence relates the quantum Hitchin connection to the monodromy of the Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov equations. The Bethe ansatz equations for the quantum Gaudin model, which is the quantum Hitchin system for G = SL2, are identified with the condition that a LG-oper has trivial monodromy. This links the quantum geometric Langlands to the theory of quantum integrable systems and the Langlands correspondence for the loop group.

Opers, Miura Transformations, and the Classical Limit

An oper for a reductive group G on a curve X is a triple (ℰ, ∇, ℰB) where ℰ is a G-bundle, ∇ is a connection, and ℰB is a reduction to a Borel subgroup B ⊂ G such that the induced map ℰB ×B (𝔤/𝔟) → ℰ ×G 𝔤 is an isomorphism. The space of G-opers, OpG(X), is a finite-dimensional affine space modeled on the Hitchin base B = ⊕i H0(X, Kdi+1), where di are the exponents of G. The Miura transformation is a map from the space of G-opers to the space of G-local systems with a full flag, given by forgetting the oper transversality condition. The image of the Miura transformation is the space of Miura opers, which are local systems with a flag preserved by the connection. The classical geometric Langlands correspondence relates D-modules on BunG to quasi-coherent sheaves on LocSysLG. In the classical limit of the quantum geometric Langlands, the category of k-twisted D-modules for large k localizes to the oper locus: the quantum Hamiltonians become the classical Hitchin Hamiltonians, and the eigenvalues of the quantum Hitchin system are given by opers. The Feigin–Frenkel center at critical level provides a quantization of the Hitchin Hamiltonians, and its spectrum is the space of LG-opers. The classical limit ℏ → 0 corresponds to the limit where the quantum parameter goes to zero, and the oper locus becomes the global nilpotent cone inside the Hitchin fiber. The Miura transformation then relates the oper locus to the space of local systems with a Borel reduction, which is the fiber of the map from the moduli of local systems with a flag to the Hitchin base. This geometry underlies the construction of Hecke eigensheaves via the Hitchin fibration: the spectral data of a Higgs bundle is a point in the Hitchin base, and the corresponding local system is obtained by solving the oper equations. The work of Beilinson–Drinfeld on the quantization of the Hitchin system and the construction of the quantum Hitchin connection uses the language of D-modules on the moduli stack of G-bundles and the chiral algebra of the affine Kac–Moody algebra.

The Geometric Langlands Correspondence for Parabolic Bundles and Ramification

The geometric Langlands correspondence extends to the setting of parabolic bundles and ramified local systems. A parabolic G-bundle on a curve X with marked points D = {x1, …, xn} is a G-bundle equipped with a reduction to a parabolic subgroup Pi ⊂ G at each xi and a weight in the dominant chamber of the Levi quotient. The moduli stack BunG(X, D) of parabolic bundles is a smooth algebraic stack. On the Galois side, one considers LG-local systems on X D with prescribed monodromy around the punctures, i.e., local systems with regular singularities and fixed conjugacy classes of monodromy. The geometric Langlands correspondence with ramification asserts an equivalence between the derived category of D-modules on BunG(X, D) (with appropriate twists) and the derived category of quasi-coherent sheaves on the stack of LG-local systems with the prescribed ramification data. The Hecke operators are modified at the marked points: they allow modifications of the parabolic structure according to the representation theory of the Levi subgroups. The geometric Satake equivalence for the affine Grassmannian of the parabolic group (or the affine flag variety) provides the local eigenvalues. The Hitchin fibration for parabolic Higgs bundles has a base that includes the residues of the Higgs field at the marked points, and the fibers are again Abelian varieties (Prym varieties of spectral curves with marked points). The quantum geometric Langlands with ramification introduces a level k for each marked point, and the quantum Hitchin system becomes the quantum Gaudin model with inhomogeneities. The Bethe ansatz equations for the Gaudin model correspond to the condition that the oper has the prescribed monodromy. This ramified correspondence is essential for the geometric Langlands program over ℙ1 with three marked points, which is related to the quantum geometric Langlands for the quantum group at a root of unity and to the representation theory of the Virasoro algebra. The work of Frenkel–Gross and others connects the ramified geometric Langlands to the theory of rigid local systems and the geometric Langlands correspondence for the loop group.

  • Common Trap: Confusing the affine Grassmannian with the affine flag variety; the former gives the spherical Hecke category, the latter gives the Iwahori-Hecke category.
  • Common Trap: Assuming the classical geometric Langlands uses quasi-coherent sheaves on the whole LocSys; the correct target is ind-coherent sheaves with nilpotent singular support.
  • Common Trap: Forgetting that the critical level is k = -h∨, not k = 0; the parameter ℏ = 1/(k + h∨) diverges at critical level.
  • Common Trap: Mixing up the Miura transformation (oper → local system with flag) with the non-Abelian Hodge correspondence (Higgs bundle → local system).
  • Common Trap: Thinking the quantum geometric Langlands is only a deformation of the classical one; at critical level it is a completely different categorical equivalence involving chiral algebras.
  • Common Trap: Overlooking the role of the Feigin–Frenkel center as the algebra of quantum Hitchin Hamiltonians; it is the center of the affine vertex algebra at critical level.
  • Common Trap: Neglecting that parabolic structures require modifications of both the automorphic and Galois sides, and that the Hecke operators at marked points involve the affine flag variety of the Levi subgroup.
