Geometric Langlands — S-duality, Hecke Eigensheaves & the Quantum Geometric Langlands

Deep dive into the Geometric Langlands Program — the geometric reformulation of the classical Langlands correspondence in number theory. Topics covered include the classical Langlands correspondence (a correspondence between Galois representations and automorphic forms for reductive groups over global fields), the geometric reformulation (let X be a smooth projective curve over ℂ; the Galois side becomes the moduli stack of G-bundles with connections, the automorphic side becomes the moduli stack of G'-bundles called the Hitchin moduli space), Hecke eigensheaves (D-modules on the moduli stack of G-bundles satisfying Hecke eigenvalue conditions), the Hecke operators (the geometric analogue of Hecke operators in number theory — correspondences connecting moduli stacks of bundles with different parabolic structures), the Hitchin fibration (the integrable system whose fibers are Abelian varieties — Prym varieties of spectral curves), the Hitchin discriminant locus and the singularities of the fibration, the Langlands duality between the groups G and LG (Langlands dual group, obtained by exchanging roots and coroots), the Beilinson–Drinfeld construction of Hecke eigensheaves using the Hitchin system, the Kapustin–Witten interpretation of geometric Langlands as S-duality in N=4 supersymmetric Yang–Mills theory in four dimensions (a duality between the gauge theory for G and for LG exchanging electric and magnetic degrees of freedom), the quantum geometric Langlands correspondence (the deformation of the correspondence by a parameter k related to the level of the WZW model and the quantum deformation of the Hitchin system), and the connection to the Ngo–Laumon proof of the fundamental lemma (the Langlands–Shelstad fundamental lemma proved using the Hitchin fibration).

"The geometric Langlands program is a vast generalization of the Fourier transform — it intertwines the geometry of moduli spaces of bundles with the representation theory of reductive groups."
— Alexander Beilinson
Part 1

📐 The Classical Langlands Correspondence

Number Theory
Local Fields, Global Fields, the Langlands Conjectures, and the Langlands Dual Group
The classical Langlands correspondence (Langlands, 1967) relates two fundamental objects for a reductive group G over a global field F (e.g., ℚ or 𝔽_q(t)): the automorphic side (automorphic representations of G(𝔸_F) where 𝔸_F is the adele ring) and the Galois side (representations of the absolute Galois group Gal(F̄/F) into the Langlands dual group LG(ℚ̄_ℓ)). The Langlands dual group LG: for a reductive group G, let (X, Δ, X^∨, Δ^∨) be the root data. The dual group LG is the reductive group over ℂ with the dual root data (X^∨, Δ^∨, X, Δ). Examples: LGL(n) = GL(n), LSO(2n+1) = Sp(2n), LSp(2n) = SO(2n+1), LE₆ = E₆, LE₇ = E₇, LE₈ = E₈, LF₄ = F₄, LG₂ = G₂. The local Langlands correspondence for a local field F_v (e.g., ℚ_p): a bijection between irreducible admissible representations of G(F_v) and L-parameters (homomorphisms from the Weil–Deligne group WF_v' → LG(ℂ)) satisfying certain compatibility conditions (the local Langlands conjectures, proven for GL_n by Harris–Taylor and Henniart). The global Langlands correspondence: a correspondence between cuspidal automorphic representations of G(𝔸_F) and global Langlands parameters (Galois representations into LG(ℂ)). The Arthur–Selberg trace formula: a powerful analytic tool linking the spectral side (automorphic representations) with the geometric side (orbital integrals). The fundamental lemma (Langlands–Shelstad): an identity of orbital integrals used in the trace formula to prove Langlands functoriality — proved by Ngŏ Bao Châu (Fields Medal, 2010) using the geometry of the Hitchin fibration. The functoriality principle: for any homomorphism LH₁ → LH₂, there should be a transfer of automorphic representations of H₁ to automorphic representations of H₂ — the overarching conjecture of the Langlands program.
The classical Langlands correspondence is one of the most profound conjectures in mathematics — it bridges Galois theory (number theory) and automorphic forms (harmonic analysis on adele groups), with the Langlands dual group encoding the symmetry.
Part 2

🌀 The Geometric Langlands Correspondence

Geometry
The Moduli Stack of G-Bundles, Hecke Eigensheaves, and the Beilinson–Drinfeld Construction
The geometric Langlands correspondence (Drinfeld, Laumon, Beilinson–Drinfeld) replaces the number field F with the function field of a smooth projective curve X over ℂ. The automorphic side becomes the moduli stack Bun_G(X) of algebraic G-bundles on X. Instead of automorphic functions, we study D-modules (sheaves of modules over the sheaf of differential operators) on Bun_G. The Galois side becomes the moduli stack Loc_{LG}(X) of flat LG-bundles on X (i.e., principal LG-bundles with a connection — local systems). The correspondence: for every flat LG-bundle E ∈ Loc_{LG}(X), there exists a Hecke eigensheaf Aut_E on Bun_G(X) — a D-module with eigenvalue E under the Hecke operators. The Hecke operators are defined via the Hecke correspondence: over Bun_G × X, there is the Hecke stack H = {(P, P', x, φ)} where P, P' ∈ Bun_G and φ: P|_{X\{x}} → P'|_{X\{x}} is an isomorphism. For a representation V of LG (which corresponds to a finite-dimensional representation of G^∨ = LG), the corresponding Hecke operator H_V: D-mod(Bun_G) → D-mod(Bun_G) acts by pullback and pushforward along the Hecke correspondence. The condition that Aut_E is a Hecke eigensheaf with eigenvalue E means: H_V(Aut_E) ≅ E_V ⊠ Aut_E, where E_V is the vector bundle on X associated to E via V. The Beilinson–Drinfeld construction: for a smooth spectral curve (a smooth ramified double cover of X), they constructed Hecke eigensheaves using the Hitchin system — the Hitchin fiber is an Abelian variety (a Prym variety), and the D-module is the Dolbeault–Grothendieck resolution of the sheaf of functions on the Hitchin fiber. The geometric Satake equivalence (Mirković–Vilonen, Ginzburg): the category of LG-equivariant perverse sheaves on the affine Grassmannian Gr_G = G(ℂ((t)))/G(ℂ[[t]]) is equivalent to the tensor category of representations of LG — giving the geometric analogue of the Satake isomorphism linking spherical Hecke algebras to representations of the Langlands dual group.
The geometric Langlands correspondence translates the number-theoretic Langlands program into the language of D-modules on moduli stacks of bundles over curves, where Hecke eigensheaves replace automorphic forms and flat LG-bundles replace Galois representations.
Part 3

🌌 The Hitchin Fibration and Mirror Symmetry

Integrable
The Hitchin System, the Hitchin Fibration, and Hyperkähler Mirror Symmetry
The Hitchin moduli space M_H(G) = {(E, φ)} where E is a principal G-bundle on a curve X and φ ∈ H⁰(X, ad(E) ⊗ K_X) is a Higgs field — a section of the bundle of endomorphisms twisted by the canonical bundle K_X. The Hitchin equations: F_A + [φ, φ†] = 0 and ∂̄_A φ = 0 — these are the equations for a Higgs bundle, which is equivalent to a flat connection on X (the non-Abelian Hodge correspondence of Hitchin–Simpson). The Hitchin fibration h: M_H(G) → B_G = ⊕_{i=1}^r H⁰(X, K_X^{d_i+1}) where the d_i are the degrees of the basic invariant polynomials of G. The map h sends (E, φ) to the characteristic polynomial coefficients of φ (the Hitchin map). The generic fiber of h is a Prym variety — an Abelian variety of dimension dim G (g−1) for a curve X of genus g. The spectral curve Σ: the equation det(φ − t·Id) = 0 defines a ramified cover Σ → X of degree rank G, and the Higgs bundle data is equivalent to a line bundle on Σ (the BNR correspondence of Beauville–Narasimhan–Ramanan). The Hitchin system is a completely integrable system: the fibers are Abelian varieties and the Poisson structure on M_H(G) (coming from the cotangent bundle T*Bun_G) is torus-invariant. The global nilpotent cone h⁻¹(0) — the union of all nilpotent Higgs fields — is a Lagrangian subvariety of M_H(G) and plays a role in the geometric Langlands correspondence. Mirror symmetry (Kapustin–Witten, Hausel–Thaddeus): the Langlands dual groups G and LG correspond under mirror symmetry. M_H(G) and M_H(LG) are mirror partners in the sense of Strominger–Yau–Zaslow: the Hitchin fibration for G and for LG have the same base B, but the generic fibers are dual Abelian varieties (the Prym variety for G and its dual for LG). The topological mirror symmetry: the Hodge numbers of M_H(G) and M_H(LG) are related — the Hodge diamond is rotated, as conjectured by Hausel–Thaddeus and proven by Groechenig–Wyss–Ziegler. The P=W conjecture (de Cataldo–Hausel–Migliorini): the perverse filtration on the cohomology of M_H(G) arising from the Hitchin fibration corresponds to the weight filtration on the cohomology of the character variety (the space of flat connections) via the non-Abelian Hodge correspondence.
The Hitchin system is a completely integrable system whose fibers are Abelian varieties — its geometry encodes the mirror symmetry between dual groups and provides the spectral data needed to construct Hecke eigensheaves in the geometric Langlands program.
Part 4

🔥 S-Duality and the Quantum Geometric Langlands

Duality
The Kapustin–Witten Interpretation, S-Duality in N=4 SYM, and the Quantum Geometric Langlands Correspondence
The Kapustin–Witten interpretation (2006): the geometric Langlands correspondence is interpreted as S-duality in N=4 supersymmetric Yang–Mills theory (SYM) in four dimensions. In N=4 SYM with gauge group G, there is an SL(2, ℤ) duality group generated by the transformation τ → −1/τ (S-duality, exchanging electric and magnetic charges) and τ → τ + 1 (T-duality). Here τ = θ/(2π) + 4πi/g² is the complex coupling constant. S-duality sends G to LG (the Langlands dual group) and interchanges Wilson loops ('t Hooft loops) — electric and magnetic observables. Kapustin and Witten consider the topological twist of N=4 SYM on a 4-manifold of the form Σ × C where Σ is a Riemann surface and C is a smooth curve. The resulting 2-dimensional topological field theory on Σ has a moduli space of vacua given by the Hitchin moduli space M_H(G). The boundary conditions along Σ (or more precisely along the boundaries of the cigar-like geometry) correspond to D-modules on Bun_G. The S-duality transformation sends the G-theory to the LG-theory and exchanges the two types of boundary conditions — giving the geometric Langlands correspondence. The quantum geometric Langlands correspondence (Beilinson–Drinfeld, Gaitsgory–Lurie): a deformation of the geometric Langlands correspondence by a parameter k (the level of the WZW model). On the automorphic side, D-modules on Bun_G are replaced by k-twisted D-modules (modules over the sheaf of differential operators with a twist by the level k). On the Galois side, local systems on X are deformed to k-twisted local systems (flat G-connections with a specific central extension). The quantum Hitchin system: the Hitchin system can be quantized by replacing the Higgs field φ by a differential operator (the quantum Hitchin connection). The quantization parameter is ℏ = 1/(k + h^∨) where h^∨ is the dual Coxeter number. The KZ/WZW correspondence links the quantum Hitchin system to the representation theory of affine Lie algebras. The Tamakian formalism (the theory of tensor categories) reformulates the geometric Langlands in terms of the category of D-modules on Bun_G being equivalent to the category of O-modules on Loc_{LG}(X) — a categorical equivalence. This is the subject of the Gaitsgory–Lurie program (the categorical geometric Langlands conjecture): an equivalence between the derived category of D-modules on Bun_G and the derived category of quasi-coherent sheaves on Loc_{LG}(X). This program uses powerful technology from derived algebraic geometry, ind-coherent sheaves, and the theory of arithmetic D-modules.
S-duality in N=4 SYM provides the physical interpretation of the geometric Langlands correspondence — it is the electromagnetic duality exchanging electric (Wilson) and magnetic ('t Hooft) Wilson loops, with the quantum deformation corresponding to the level k of the WZW model.
