C*-algebras — Gelfand Duality, Commutative C*-algebras & the GNS Construction

A C*-algebra is a complex Banach algebra A equipped with an involution *: A → A satisfying (a*)* = a, (ab)* = b*a*, and the crucial C*-identity: ||a*a|| = ||a||² for all a ∈ A. This identity ties the algebraic, topological, and *-structural aspects together. The Gelfand-Naimark theorem (1943) shows that every commutative unital C*-algebra is isometrically *-isomorphic to C(K) for some compact Hausdorff space K — the Gelfand spectrum of characters. This establishes a duality between commutative C*-algebras and compact Hausdorff spaces, the foundation of noncommutative geometry. The GNS construction (Gelfand-Naimark-Segal) builds representations of a C*-algebra from states — positive linear functionals of norm 1 — giving the Gelfand-Naimark theorem that every C*-algebra is isometrically *-isomorphic to a norm-closed *-subalgebra of B(H) for some Hilbert space H. The classification of C*-algebras involves K-theory, the Elliott invariant, and the Baum-Connes conjecture linking K-theory to the geometry of groups.

"C*-algebras are the natural algebra of quantum observables."
— Alain Connes
Part 1

📏 The Definition and the Gelfand-Naimark Theorem

Foundations
C*-algebras, Spectrum, Gelfand Transform, and Functional Calculus
A C*-algebra A is a Banach algebra over ℂ with an involution *: A → A such that (a*)* = a, (ab)* = b*a*, and the C*-identity: ||a*a|| = ||a||² for all a ∈ A. The involution is isometric: ||a*|| = ||a||. An element a ∈ A is normal if a*a = aa*, self-adjoint if a = a*, positive if a = b*b for some b ∈ A, and a projection if a = a* = a². The spectrum σ(a) = {λ ∈ ℂ : a − λ·1 is not invertible} is a nonempty compact subset of ℂ. For a normal element, the spectral radius formula gives r(a) = lim_{n→∞} ||aⁿ||^{1/n} = ||a|| — the norm is determined by the spectrum. The Gelfand-Naimark theorem: for a commutative unital C*-algebra A, the Gelfand transform Γ: A → C(Â) defined by â(φ) = φ(a) is an isometric *-isomorphism, where Â is the character space (nonzero multiplicative linear functionals) equipped with the weak* topology — a compact Hausdorff space. The continuous functional calculus: for a normal element a ∈ A, there exists a unique isometric *-isomorphism C(σ(a)) → C*(a, 1) sending the identity function z ↦ z to a. This allows defining f(a) for any continuous function f on σ(a). The spectral mapping theorem: σ(f(a)) = f(σ(a)).
The Gelfand-Naimark theorem establishes a duality between commutative C*-algebras and compact Hausdorff spaces — every commutative unital C*-algebra is C(K) for a unique compact Hausdorff K.
Part 2

🌍 States, Representations, and the GNS Construction

Representations
States, the GNS Construction, Irreducible Representations, and Nuclearity
A state on a unital C*-algebra A is a positive linear functional φ: A → ℂ with ||φ|| = φ(1) = 1. Positive means φ(a*a) ≥ 0 for all a ∈ A. The state space S(A) is convex and weak* compact. Pure states are the extreme points of S(A). The GNS construction: given a state φ, define N_φ = {a ∈ A : φ(a*a) = 0} (a left ideal), form the pre-Hilbert space A/N_φ with inner product ⟨a + N_φ, b + N_φ⟩ = φ(b*a), complete to H_φ, and define π_φ: A → B(H_φ) by π_φ(a)(b + N_φ) = ab + N_φ. Then π_φ is a *-representation with cyclic vector Ω = 1 + N_φ satisfying φ(a) = ⟨Ω, π_φ(a)Ω⟩. The universal representation is the direct sum ⊕_{φ ∈ S(A)} π_φ — it is faithful (isometric). The Gelfand-Naimark theorem: every C*-algebra is isometrically *-isomorphic to a norm-closed *-subalgebra of B(H) for some H. An irreducible representation has no nontrivial closed invariant subspaces — these correspond precisely to pure states. The C*-algebra K(H) of compact operators on a separable Hilbert space is the only simple C*-algebra (no nontrivial closed ideals) among the C*-algebras acting irreducibly on H. A C*-algebra is nuclear if the minimal and maximal C*-tensor products coincide for every other C*-algebra — nuclearity is a key property for classification, and all commutative C*-algebras, AF-algebras, and group C*-algebras of amenable groups are nuclear.
The GNS construction builds a representation from any state, proving all C*-algebras live inside B(H); pure states yield irreducible representations, the building blocks of representation theory.
Part 3

🧮 AF-Algebras and the Classification Program

Classification
AF-Algebras, Bratteli Diagrams, K-Theory, and the Elliott Invariant
An AF-algebra (approximately finite-dimensional) is the inductive limit of a sequence of finite-dimensional C*-algebras (direct sums of matrix algebras M_n(ℂ)). AF-algebras are classified by their ordered K₀-group with distinguished order unit via the Effros-Handelman-Shen (EHS) theorem: every countable, unperforated, ordered abelian group with the Riesz interpolation property arises as K₀(A) for some AF-algebra A. Bratteli diagrams give a graphical description of the inclusion sequence — a versatile combinatorial invariant. K-theory for C*-algebras: K₀(A) is the Grothendieck group of equivalence classes of projections (idempotents) under Murray-von Neumann equivalence; K₁(A) consists of unitary elements modulo path components. The six-term exact sequence in K-theory relates the K-groups of a C*-algebra, an ideal, and the quotient. The Elliott classification program aims to classify separable, nuclear, simple, unital C*-algebras in the UCT class by the Elliott invariant: the ordered K₀-group, the K₁-group, the tracial state space (with pairing), and the natural map from K₀ to the affine functions on traces. The Jiang-Su algebra Z is a simple, nuclear, infinite-dimensional C*-algebra that absorbs itself tensorially; a C*-algebra is Z-stable if A ⊗ Z ≅ A. Z-stability is a key regularity property in the classification of nuclear C*-algebras. UHF-algebras (uniformly hyperfinite) are AF-algebras with K₀ isomorphic to the group of dyadic rationals — classified by supernatural numbers.
AF-algebras are classified by their K₀-group (EHS theorem); the Elliott program extends this to all separable nuclear C*-algebras using K-theory and traces.
Part 4

🔥 Baum-Connes Conjecture and Noncommutative Geometry

Noncommutative
Baum-Connes Conjecture, KK-Theory, and Noncommutative Geometry
The Baum-Connes conjecture (1982, Paul Baum and Alain Connes) states that for a discrete group G, the assembly map μ: K^G_*(EG) → K_*(C*(G)) from the equivariant K-homology of the classifying space EG to the K-theory of the reduced group C*-algebra is an isomorphism. The left-hand side is the K-homology of the classifying space, which encodes geometric/topological data; the right-hand side is the K-theory of C*(G), encoding the representation theory of G. The conjecture implies the Novikov conjecture on the homotopy invariance of higher signatures and the Gromov-Lawson-Rosenberg conjecture on the existence of metrics of positive scalar curvature. The Baum-Connes conjecture has been proved for a large class of groups: a-T-menable (Higson-Kasparov), hyperbolic groups (Lafforgue), groups acting on trees, and many others. Counterexamples were found by Higson-Lafforgue-Skandalis (2002) for certain expander groups (Gromov monster groups) containing expander graphs. The Connes-Kasparov conjecture is the special case for connected Lie groups. The categorical framework is Kasparov's KK-theory and the Dirac-dual Dirac method. In noncommutative geometry (Connes), C*-algebras serve as algebras of functions on "noncommutative spaces": the noncommutative torus (the irrational rotation algebra A_θ), the noncommutative standard model of particle physics, the spectral triple (A, H, D) encoding the geometry, and the spectral action principle deriving the Einstein-Hilbert action from the trace of f(D/Λ). von Neumann algebras arise as bicommutants of representations: a *-subalgebra M ⊆ B(H) is a von Neumann algebra if M = M'' (the bicommutant). The type classification of factors (Murray-von Neumann): type I (full matrix algebra), type II₁ (finite trace, e.g., the hyperfinite II₁ factor R), type II_∞ (semi-finite, product of II₁ with I_∞), and type III (purely infinite, no nontrivial trace). Tomita-Takesaki modular theory associates a modular automorphism group σ_t^φ to a faithful normal state φ on a von Neumann algebra — the core of the structure theory for type III factors. Connes' classification of type III factors uses the modular spectrum S(M) and the Connes invariant τ(M) to subclassify into type III_λ for 0 ≤ λ ≤ 1.
The Baum-Connes conjecture links K-theory to geometry and topology; its proofs and counterexamples reveal deep connections between C*-algebras, group theory, and geometry.
