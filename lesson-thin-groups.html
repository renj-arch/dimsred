Thin Groups & Superstrong Approximation — Thin Subgroups, Expanders & the Superstrong Approximation

Deep dive into Thin Groups & Superstrong Approximation — a thin subgroup Γ of an algebraic group G(ℤ) is a finitely generated Zariski-dense subgroup of infinite index (so it is not a congruence subgroup), the strong approximation theorem (the congruence completion of a Zariski-dense subgroup of SL_n(ℤ) maps onto SL_n(ℤ/mℤ) for all m), the superstrong approximation theorem (Bourgain–Gamburd–Sarnak): the Cayley graphs Cay(SL_n(ℤ/qℤ), S mod q) for a Zariski-dense subgroup Γ = ⟨S⟩ form an expander family as q varies over square-free integers, the affine sieve (the prime orbit counting and the saturation problem for thin orbits — determining which integers are represented by the norm form of a thin group), the classification of thin subgroups of SL_n(ℤ) (thinness vs. thickness, the ping-pong lemma for constructing free subgroups), property (τ) for congruence subgroups and the Selberg property (λ_1 ≥ 3/16 for congruence subgroups of SL₂(ℤ)), the representation theory of SL₂(ℤ/pℤ) and the Ramanujan bound for Cayley graphs (the Selberg–Kloosterman–Sarnak approach), and applications to Apollonian circle packings (the integrality of curvatures and the local–global principle).

Part 1

🧩 Thin Subgroups of Algebraic Groups

Algebraic Groups
Zariski Density, Thin vs. Congruence Subgroups, and the Tits Alternative
Let G be an algebraic group defined over ℚ (e.g., G = SL_n, Sp_{2n}, SO_n). A finitely generated subgroup Γ ⊂ G(ℤ) is Zariski-dense if its Zariski closure in G is G itself. Γ is thin if it is Zariski-dense in G but has infinite index in G(ℤ) — it is a non-arithmetic subgroup that is not a congruence subgroup. The congruence subgroup problem asks whether every finite-index subgroup of SL_n(ℤ) is a congruence subgroup (true for n ≥ 3 by Bass–Milnor–Serre, false for n = 2). Thin subgroups are those finite-index subgroups not containing a congruence subgroup — they are sparse but Zariski-dense. The Tits alternative: a finitely generated linear group either contains a free non-abelian subgroup or is virtually solvable. For thin subgroups, the free non-abelian case is generic. The ping-pong lemma gives a criterion for two matrices to generate a free group: find disjoint subsets A, B such that g(A) ⊂ B and h(B) ⊂ A. The Schottky construction: two hyperbolic matrices with disjoint attracting/repelling limit sets generate a free group. Thin subgroups are abundant: the Helfgott–Lindenstrauss–Tao product theorem in approximate groups shows that thin subgroups have a rich combinatorial structure.
Thin subgroups are Zariski-dense but sparse in the ambient arithmetic group — they are the non-arithmetic cousins of congruence subgroups, abundant and rich in structure.
Part 2

📐 Superstrong Approximation and Expander Graphs

Approximation
The Strong Approximation Theorem, Bourgain–Gamburd Expander Criterion, and Superstrong Approximation
Strong approximation: if Γ ⊂ SL_n(ℤ) is Zariski-dense, then the reduction map Γ → SL_n(ℤ/qℤ) is surjective for all sufficiently large square-free integers q (Nori–Weisfeiler theorem). The expansion coefficient of a finite graph X is h(X) = min_{|A|≤|V|/2} |∂A|/|A|. A sequence of q-regular graphs forms an expander family if h(X_n) ≥ ε > 0 uniformly. Superstrong approximation (Bourgain–Gamburd–Sarnak, 2005): if Γ = ⟨S⟩ ⊂ SL_n(ℤ) is Zariski-dense and the eigenvalues of each s ∈ S are all real (a technical condition eliminated by later work), then the Cayley graphs Cay(Γ_q, S_q) (where Γ_q = image of Γ mod q and S_q = image of S) form an expander family as q ranges over square-free integers. The proof uses the Bourgain–Gamburd sum-product theorem: a subset of SL₂(p) either has small growth or is essentially the whole group, plus Helfgott's product theorem in SL_n(p) and the flattening lemma. The expander mixing lemma relates the expansion of a Cayley graph to the spectral gap: λ_2 < 1 − ε implies expansion. The Lubotzky–Phillips–Sarnak construction (1987) gave explicit Ramanujan graphs using quaternion groups: Cayley graphs of PSL₂(q) with generating sets from the Selberg trace formula, achieving the optimal Ramanujan bound λ_2 ≤ 2√(d−1)/d.
Superstrong approximation shows that congruence quotients of Zariski-dense subgroups produce expander families — a deep connection between algebraic groups and graph theory.
Part 3

⚡ The Affine Sieve and Prime Orbit Counting

Sieve Theory
The Affine Linear Sieve, the Saturation Problem, and the Local–Global Conjecture
The affine sieve (Bourgain–Gamburd–Sarnak, 2006) studies arithmetic properties of thin orbits: given a finitely generated subgroup Γ ⊂ GL_n(ℚ) and a base point x₀ ∈ ℤ^n, the orbit O = Γ·x₀ is a set of integral vectors. Let f be an integral polynomial (or more generally a polynomial in several variables). The saturation problem asks: what are the prime values taken by f on the orbit O? The key result: for a Zariski-dense Γ and a non-constant polynomial f (square-free on the orbit), the set {f(v): v ∈ O} contains integers with arbitrarily many prime factors, and the number of primitive (square-free) integers in this set grows like cX/(log X)^{1−γ} for some γ > 0. The local–global conjecture: every integer satisfying local congruence conditions (congruences modulo all primes) appears in the primitive orbit — this is known for Apollonian circle packings (Bourgain–Kontorovich, 2010). The orbit counting function N_O(X) = #{v ∈ O: |v| ≤ X} grows like c_X^{δ} where δ is the Hausdorff dimension of the limit set of Γ (the critical exponent of the Poincaré series). The Selberg zeta function Z_Γ(s) for Schottky groups encodes the spectral theory of the Laplacian on the hyperbolic surface ℍ²/Γ — the resonances are the zeros of Z_Γ(s).
The affine sieve extends classical sieve theory to thin groups, detecting primes and almost-primes in Zariski-dense orbits — with applications from circle packings to integral Cayley graphs.
Part 4

🔥 Property (τ), Selberg's Theorem, and Apollonian Packings

Spectral
Property (τ), the Selberg 3/16 Theorem, and Apollonian Circle Packings
Property (τ) for a group Γ with a family of finite-index subgroups Γ_q is the condition that the trivial representation is isolated in the unitary dual: there is a uniform spectral gap for the Laplacian on the quotients ℍ²/Γ_q. The Selberg theorem (1965): for congruence subgroups Γ(q) ⊂ SL₂(ℤ), the smallest eigenvalue of the Laplacian on ℍ²/Γ(q) satisfies λ₁ ≥ 3/16 = 0.1875 (the Selberg 3/16 theorem). The Ramanujan–Petersson conjecture (Deligne) gives the optimal bound λ₁ ≥ 1/4. For thin subgroups, the spectral gap is given by the Patterson–Sullivan theory: the base eigenvalue δ(1−δ) of the Laplacian on the hyperbolic surface ℍ²/Γ is related to the Hausdorff dimension δ of the limit set. The Kloosterman sums K(a,b;c) = Σ_{x mod c, (x,c)=1} e^{2πi(ax + b\bar{x})/c} and the Kuznetsov formula relate the Selberg eigenvalue problem to bounds on Kloosterman sums. The Apollonian circle packing — the integral curvatures (1/a, 1/b, etc.) form a thin orbit under the Apollonian group (a Coxeter group generated by four reflections). The local–global principle for Apollonian packings (Bourgain–Kontorovich, 2011): every sufficiently large integer satisfying certain congruence conditions appears as a curvature in a given integral Apollonian packing. The Sarnak–Kontorovich theorem on positive density of prime curvatures: primes appear with positive density in the curvatures of the packing.
The interplay between spectral theory, number theory, and thin groups reaches its peak in Apollonian circle packings — a beautiful and accessible instance of deep mathematics.
