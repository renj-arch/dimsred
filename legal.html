Constitutional remedies32 (SC), 226 (HC)Writs: habeas corpus (produce the person), mandamus (do your duty), certiorari (quash), prohibition (stop), quo warranto (by what authority).

Reasonable restrictions: Article 19 rights are subject to limits such as public order, decency, morality, security of the State and contempt of court.

If you are arrested — your rights

  • Grounds of arrest must be communicated (Art. 22(1)); a relative/friend must be informed (BNSS).
  • Produced before a magistrate within 24 hours (Art. 22(2)), excluding travel time.
  • Right to a lawyer of choice; free legal aid if you cannot afford one (Art. 22(3), 39A).
  • No self-incrimination (Art. 20(3)); no double jeopardy (Art. 20(2)).
  • D.K. Basu guidelines: memo of arrest, visible name tag, arrest memo signed by a witness, medical examination every 48 hours, entry in the station diary.
  • Women cannot be arrested after sunset and before sunrise except with special permission of a magistrate, and only a woman officer may arrest.
  • Zero FIR: any police station must register it, even outside its area, and transfer it.
  • Default bail if the charge-sheet is not filed within the statutory period (BNSS timelines).

Women's safety and rights

  • Domestic Violence Act 2005 — protection, residence, monetary and custody orders; complaint before a Protection Officer or magistrate.
  • POSH Act 2013 — every workplace with 10+ staff must have an Internal Committee; complaint within 3 months (extendable).
  • Cruelty (BNS 85 / IPC 498A); dowry under the Dowry Prohibition Act 1961.
  • MTP Act 2021 — abortion up to 24 weeks for specified categories; PCPNDT Act bans sex determination.
  • Maternity Benefit Act — 26 weeks paid leave for the first two children.
  • Free legal aid for all women (NALSA).

Children's rights

  • POCSO Act 2012 — sexual offences against children; mandatory reporting; child-friendly courts.
  • Juvenile Justice Act 2015 — children in conflict with law and children in need of care; Juvenile Justice Boards.
  • RTE Act 2009 — free and compulsory education 6–14 years; 25% reservation in private schools.
  • Child Labour (Prohibition & Regulation) Act 2016 — no employment below 14; hazardous work barred 14–18.
  • Child helpline 1098.

Consumer rights

Consumer Protection Act 2019 gives six rights: safety, information, choice, be heard, redressal, and consumer education. It covers goods, services and e-commerce.

  • Where: District Commission (up to ₹50 lakh), State (₹50 lakh–₹2 crore), National (above ₹2 crore).
  • How: e-Daakhil portal; no lawyer needed; limitation 2 years from the cause of action.
  • Product liability against manufacturer/seller; unfair trade practices and misleading ads actionable.
  • CCPA (Central Consumer Protection Authority) can recall products and penalise misleading advertisements.

Cyber law and data

  • IT Act 2000: identity theft (66C), cheating by personation (66D), privacy violation (66E), obscene material (67), unauthorised access (66).
  • Report: cybercrime.gov.in, helpline 1930 (financial fraud — report within the golden hour).
  • DPDP Act 2023: consent-based processing, data-principal rights, Data Protection Board.
  • Intermediary rules: platforms must act on unlawful content on notice/grievance officer.

Labour law (four codes)

  • Code on Wages 2019 — minimum wages, timely payment, equal remuneration.
  • Industrial Relations Code 2020 — unions, standing orders, retrenchment.
  • Social Security Code 2020 — provident fund, ESIC, gig/platform workers.
  • OSH Code 2020 — workplace safety and working conditions.
  • Enforcement is by the Labour Commissioner; state Shops & Establishments Acts still govern many service jobs.

Environmental law

  • Environment (Protection) Act 1986 — umbrella law; EIA Notification 2006.
  • Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972; Forest (Conservation) Act 1980; Water Act 1974; Air Act 1981.
  • NGT Act 2010 — environmental disputes; CAMPA for compensatory afforestation.
  • Biological Diversity Act 2002; Forest Rights Act 2006.

RTI and anti-corruption

  • RTI Act 2005 — apply to the Public Information Officer (₹10, no fee for BPL); 30-day reply; first appeal in 30 days; second appeal to the Information Commission.
  • Whistle Blowers Protection Act 2014; Lokpal & Lokayuktas Act 2013.
  • Prevention of Corruption Act 1988 — bribery, criminal misconduct; prior sanction needed to prosecute public servants.

Legal aid and going to court

  • Free legal aid: NALSA / District Legal Services Authority (DLSA) at every district court; eligible categories include women, children, SC/ST, disabled, industrial workmen, victims of trafficking/disaster, and low-income persons.
  • Lok Adalat — compromise settlement, no court fee, award is final and binding.
  • PIL — any public-spirited person may approach the High Court (Art. 226) or Supreme Court (Art. 32) for a public wrong.
  • e-Courts — case status, orders and virtual hearings via the e-Courts portal.

Case files: what charges would apply?

How a real incident becomes sections, what the court weighs, and the sentence that usually follows. Numbers are BNS 2023 with the old IPC in brackets.

How a criminal case moves

  1. FIR / Zero FIR (BNSS) → investigation; arrest safeguards apply.
  2. Case diary, forensic/medical reports, witness statements, digital evidence.
  3. Charge-sheet within the statutory period, else default bail.
  4. Charges framed; trial; the prosecution must prove guilt beyond reasonable doubt (BSA).
  5. Conviction → sentencing hearing → appeal (Sessions → High Court → Supreme Court).

1. A person kills another and loots money

Likely charges

  • Murder — BNS 103(1) [IPC 302]: death or imprisonment for life, and fine.
  • Robbery — BNS 309 [IPC 392]: rigorous imprisonment up to 10 years and fine; where hurt is caused in the robbery, the aggravated provision applies [IPC 394].
  • Five or more persons acting together → dacoity BNS 310 [IPC 395]; dacoity with murder carries death or life [IPC 396].
  • Others who shared the intention → BNS 3(5) [IPC 34]; a planned conspiracy → BNS 61 [IPC 120B].

What the court decides: murder (intention/knowledge) or culpable homicide BNS 105 [IPC 304]; robbery (force or fear) rather than mere theft BNS 303 [IPC 379].

Sentence: life imprisonment is the rule for murder; death is the exception, only in the "rarest of rare" case (Bachan Singh 1980; Machhi Singh 1983). Aggravating: premeditation, brutality, helpless victim, murder for gain. Mitigating: youth, mental illness, remorse, no prior record, chance of reform. The robbery sentence normally runs consecutively with the murder sentence.

Comparable outcomes

  • Mukesh v State (NCT of Delhi) — Nirbhaya gang rape and murder; death penalty confirmed (2020).
  • Dhananjoy Chatterjee v State of W.B. — security guard convicted of rape and murder of a young girl; death affirmed.
  • Shabnam v State of U.P. — multiple murders for family honour; death affirmed.
  • Ordinary robbery-murder by a single assailant, without exceptional brutality, usually ends in life imprisonment, not death.

2. Gang dacoity with murder

Five or more persons, a common object, loot and a killing: dacoity with murder [IPC 396 / BNS 310] — death or life, plus fine. Every member of the assembly can face the dacoity charge even if he did not strike the blow, because of the common object.

3. Hit-and-run death

Rash or negligent driving causing death: BNS 106(1) [IPC 304A] — up to 5 years and fine. The hit-and-run aggravated offence, BNS 106(2), punishes fleeing without reporting to the police or a magistrate with up to 10 years and fine. Alister Anthony Pareira and the BMW case (State v Sanjeev Nanda) illustrate negligence versus knowledge.

4. Dowry death

Death of a woman within seven years of marriage after cruelty or a dowry demand: BNS 80 [IPC 304B] — minimum 7 years to life. Once the ingredients are shown, the law raises a presumption of dowry death (old IPC 113B). Related: cruelty BNS 85 [498A]; dowry demand under the Dowry Prohibition Act 1961.

5. Cheating / investment fraud

Cheating BNS 318 [IPC 415/420] — up to 7 years and fine where property is dishonestly induced; criminal breach of trust BNS 316 [IPC 406]; conspiracy BNS 61 [120B]. Deposit schemes may also attract the MPID Act and SEBI/RBI rules; public servants, the Prevention of Corruption Act 1988.

6. Acid attack

BNS 124 [IPC 326A] — minimum 10 years to life and fine; the court may order compensation for treatment.

7. Rape

Definition BNS 63 [IPC 375]; punishment BNS 64 [IPC 376] — minimum 10 years to life. Aggravated forms (gang rape, victim under 12, rape during communal violence) carry higher minimums and, in the gravest cases, death (BNS 65; POCSO for children).

8. Online fraud

Cheating by personation BNS 318 [IPC 420] plus IT Act 66C/66D. Report at cybercrime.gov.in or call 1930 at once — the first hours decide whether the money can be frozen.

What usually decides the verdict

StageWhat the court asks
ChargeDoes the act fit the section — intention, knowledge, force, dishonesty?
TrialIs the evidence beyond reasonable doubt (BSA)? Any doubt goes to the accused.
ConvictionWhich offence is proved — often a lesser included offence.
SentenceAggravating versus mitigating circumstances; proportionality; death only if rarest of rare.
AppealSessions → High Court → Supreme Court; bail or suspension of sentence may be sought.

These are illustrative analyses for study and awareness, not legal advice. A real case turns on its evidence.

Landmark cases worth knowing

  • Kesavananda Bharati (1973) — basic structure doctrine.
  • Maneka Gandhi (1978) — procedure must be fair, just and reasonable (Art. 21).
  • D.K. Basu (1997) — arrest and custody safeguards.
  • Vishaka (1997) — workplace sexual harassment guidelines (before POSH).
  • Puttaswamy (2017) — privacy as a fundamental right.
  • Navtej Singh Johar (2018) — decriminalised consensual same-sex relations.
  • Shayara Bano (2017) — triple talaq struck down.
  • Joseph Shine (2018) — adultery struck down.
