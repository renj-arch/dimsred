Japanese (日本語)

Three scripts in one sentence, no spaces, verbs at the end, and politeness baked into every verb ending. Start with the 46 kana, add kanji daily, and the whole language opens up.

Languages Hub → Japanese

🌍 Overview & the Three Scripts

  • Family: Japonic — a language isolate (with Ryukyuan relatives); centuries of work has failed to link it firmly to Korean or anything else.
  • Speakers: ~125M.
  • Hiragana (ひらがな): the phonetic "native" script — grammar particles, verb endings, and words with no kanji.
  • Katakana (カタカナ): the phonetic "foreign" script — loanwords, onomatopoeia, emphasis. コーヒー (kōhī) = coffee.
  • Kanji (漢字): borrowed Chinese characters with Japanese readings — ~2,100 needed for literacy (the jōyō set).
  • No spaces: Japanese text runs together; you learn to see word boundaries by recognizing kana endings and kanji.

🔤 Hiragana chart (46 basic kana)

Each kana is a whole syllable (consonant + vowel). Master reading them — this is the fastest possible start:

Plus ん (n) and the diacritics: ゛(voicing: か→が ga, た→だ da) and ゜(は→ぱ pa). Katakana uses the same grid with different shapes: ア イ ウ エ オ … ン.

💡 Reading hint: し = shi (not "si"), ち = chi, つ = tsu, ふ = fu. These four are the kana where the romanisation keeps the true sound.

🗣 Sound & Pitch Accent

  • Syllables are simple: mostly consonant + vowel, plus standalone vowels and ん. There are no consonant clusters like "str".
  • Pitch accent: a syllable is high or low. はし can be hashi "chopsticks" (H-L) or hashi "bridge" (L-H) — same sounds, different tune.
  • Vowels can vanish: in です (desu) and ます (masu), the final u is often whispered to almost nothing.
  • Double consonants (っ) and long vowels (ー) change meaning: おばさん obasan (aunt) vs おばあさん obaasan (grandmother).

🧩 Grammar Essentials

SOV + particles

Watashi wa sushi o tabemasu — "I (topic) sushi (object) eat (polite)." Particles do the grammar work:

ParticleJobExample
は (wa)topic私は watashi wa
が (ga)subject / emphasisねこが neko ga
を (o)objectみずを mizu o
に / へ (ni/e)to / atがっこうへ gakkō e
の (no)possessiveわたしの watashi no
で (de)by means ofバスで basu de

Politeness: plain vs. polite

Verbs switch ending: taberu (plain) → tabemasu (polite, -ます). Learn polite forms for strangers; plain forms for friends, anime, and reading.

Counter words

Counting requires a counter noun for the thing counted: 3 apples = ringo san-ko (generic -ko), 3 people = san-nin (-nin), 3 thin sheets = san-mai, 3 books = san-satsu. Yes, you must memorize them.

No articles, minimal plurals

Neko = cat/the cat/cats. Context does the work.

🧠 Quick Facts & Cultural Notes

  • Kanji have two readings: on-yomi (Chinese-derived) and kun-yomi (Japanese) — 生 alone reads differently in 先生 (sensei, teacher), 生ビール (nama-bīru, draft beer), and 生きる (ikiru, to live).
  • Kana shapes are related: katakana ア derives from the top strokes of kanji 阿; hiragana are cursive simplifications.
  • Text runs vertically or horizontally — novels go top-to-bottom; web pages left-to-right.
  • Japanese drops the subject constantly: Tabemasu "eat" — who? Context. (Same habit as its lack of plurals and articles.)
  • Wago vs. gairaigo: native words (水 mizu water) sit beside English loanwords (コーヒー kōhī coffee) in the same sentence.
  • Word order is almost the reverse of English: "I from Osaka came" (大阪から来ました) — the verb is the grand finale of every sentence.

📚 Grammar Reference — the full skeleton

1 · Word order: everything ends in a verb

私は毎朝駅まで歩きます — "I every-morning to-the-station walk." Topic first, time where it pleases, verb last. The verb is the whole engine of the sentence.

2 · The core particles

ParticleJobExample
はtopic ("as for…")私は学生です (as for me, I'm a student)
がsubject / new info雨が降っています (rain is falling)
をobject (said "o")本を読みます (I read a book)
にat / to / in (point)九時に寝ます (I sleep at nine)
へtoward (direction)大阪へ行きます (I go to Osaka)
でby means of / at (action site)電車で行きます (I go by train)
のpossessive "of"日本の音楽 (Japanese music)
とwith / and友達と話します (I talk with a friend)

3 · Verbs — three groups, one rule each

GroupDictionary-masu (polite)-te (joining)-nai (negative)-ta (past)
ichidan (ru-verbs)食べる食べます食べて食べない食べた
godan (u-verbs)行く行きます行って行かない行った
irregularする / 来るします / 来ますして / 来てしない / 来ないした / 来た

Patterns: ichidan just drops る and adds the ending; godan shifts the final u-sound across the kana row (行く → 行きます, k→ki). Learn one verb from each group and you unlock the conjugation table.

4 · Politeness levels

LevelEndingWho it's forExample
polite -masu/です行きますstrangers, shops, workyour everyday default
plain行くfriends, family, readingwhat manga & novels use
humble/keigo参りますabout yourself to a superiorお...する / まいる
respectfulいらっしゃるabout the superiorお...になる / られる

5 · Existence: ある vs いる

いる = living things: 犬がいます (there is a dog). ある = objects: 本があります (there is a book). Japanese respects this line strictly.

6 · Joining clauses: the te-form is the glue

朝ご飯を食べて、会社に行きます — "eat-breakfast-(te), go-to-work." Verbs chain with て; reasons with から (creator) or ので (softer); "but" with けど; "if" with -たら/-れば. A long Japanese sentence is really a train of verbs pulling one final carriage.

7 · Adjectives decide their own verb

i-adjectives (quick: 高い) — the adjective itself conjugates: 高かった (was expensive), 高くない (not expensive). na-adjectives (quiet: 静かな) borrow です's job: 静かではありません. Never mix them up.

8 · Counters

一枚 (one flat thing), 一本 (one long thing), 一匹 (one animal), 一人 (one person), 一台 (one machine), 一回 (once). The numbers 1-10 change their reading before many counters: いっぴき (one animal) not "ichi-hiki".

9 · が is the star-turn particle

Use が for the "new information" the listener doesn't know yet, は for the topic everyone knows. 私はコーヒーが好きです — "As for me (は), coffee (が) is liked." Topic + subject both present, each doing its own job.
