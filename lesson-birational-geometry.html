Birational Geometry — the Minimal Model Program, Flips & Singularities

Birational geometry studies algebraic varieties up to birational equivalence — a rational map with a rational inverse, i.e., an isomorphism on Zariski open sets. Two varieties are birationally equivalent if they share a common dense open subset. The central goal is the Minimal Model Program (MMP, or Mori's program): given a smooth projective variety X, find a birational model Xmin with KXmin nef (non-negative on all curves). The program proceeds by contracting extremal rays and, when the contraction is small (codimension ≥ 2), performing a flip — a surgery replacing a K-negative contraction by a K-positive one. The existence of 3-fold flips was proved by Mori (1988); the existence of all flips by Birkar-Cascini-Hacon-McKernan (2010). Singularities (terminal, canonical, log terminal, log canonical) are classified by their discrepancies. The abundance conjecture asserts that when KX is nef, it is semiample. The boundedness of Fano varieties (Birkar, 2018) shows there are finitely many families in each dimension.

"Birational geometry is the geography of algebraic varieties."
— Shigefumi Mori
Part 1

📏 The Classification of Surfaces

Surfaces
The Enriques-Kodaira Classification and Minimal Models for Surfaces
Let X be a smooth projective surface (2-fold). The canonical divisor KX is the divisor of a meromorphic 2-form. The intersection pairing gives an integer (C·D) for curves C, D on X. The adjunction formula KC = (KX + C)|C relates the canonical divisor of a curve C to that of X. The Kodaira dimension κ(X) = limsup log dim H⁰(X, nKX)/log n takes values −∞, 0, 1, or 2. The Enriques-Kodaira classification: κ = −∞: rational surfaces (ℙ², ruled surfaces); κ = 0: K3 surfaces, Enriques surfaces, abelian surfaces, hyperelliptic surfaces; κ = 1: elliptic surfaces (fibered over a curve with elliptic fibers); κ = 2: surfaces of general type. A surface is minimal if it contains no (−1)-curves (a smooth rational curve with self-intersection −1). Castelnuovo's contractibility criterion: a (−1)-curve can be blown down to a smooth point. Every surface has a unique minimal model (obtained by successively blowing down (−1)-curves). The Mori cone NE(X) = {Σ ai Ci: ai ≥ 0, Ci curves} and the Cone Theorem: NE(X) = NEKX≥0 + Σ ℝ₊[Ci] for extremal rational curves Ci with −(KX·Ci) ≤ dim X + 1.
The classification of surfaces by Kodaira dimension and minimal models via (−1)-curve contraction is the blueprint for the higher-dimensional Minimal Model Program.
Part 2

🌍 The Minimal Model Program

MMP
Mori's Program: Contraction Theorems, Flips, and Termination
The Minimal Model Program aims to find for any smooth projective variety X a birational model Xmin with KXmin nef (numerically effective: K·C ≥ 0 for all curves C). The steps: (1) If KX is not nef, find an extremal ray R = ℝ₊[C] with KX·C < 0 using the Cone Theorem. (2) Contract the extremal ray: φR: X → Y. This contraction is either a Fano contraction (dim Y < dim X) or a divisorial contraction (exceptional divisor is a divisor). (3) If the contraction is small (the exceptional locus has codimension ≥ 2), perform a flip: the rational map X ⇢ X⁺ factors through Z where φ: X → Z is small KX-negative and φ⁺: X⁺ → Z is small KX⁺-positive. (4) Replace X with Y or X⁺ and repeat. Termination: Mori proved the MMP terminates in dimension 3. Birkar-Cascini-Hacon-McKernan (2010) proved the MMP terminates for all dimensions for varieties of general type. The Basepoint-Free Theorem (Kawamata-Shokurov): if KX is nef and big, then |mKX| is basepoint-free for sufficiently large m.
The MMP reduces the study of birational geometry to three building blocks: Fano varieties (K negative), Calabi-Yau varieties (K ≡ 0), and varieties of general type (K positive).
Part 3

🧮 Flips and Singularities

Flips
The Existence of Flips, Discrepancies, and Log Canonical Pairs
A flip is a birational map X ⇢ X⁺ factoring through Z such that φ: X → Z is a small contraction (KX-negative) and φ⁺: X⁺ → Z is a small contraction (KX⁺-positive). The existence of 3-fold flips was proved by Mori (1988) using the Hilbert scheme and explicit classification. The Hacon-McKernan existence theorem (2010): flips exist for all log canonical pairs in all dimensions, building on the work of Birkar-Cascini-Hacon-McKernan. Singularities in the MMP: consider a pair (X, Δ) where Δ is an effective ℚ-divisor. Write KY + ΔY = f*(KX + Δ) + Σ ai Ei for a resolution f: Y → X. The numbers ai are discrepancies. The pair (X, Δ) is: terminal if ai > 0 for all i; canonical if ai ≥ 0; log terminal (klt) if ai > −1; log canonical (lc) if ai ≥ −1. The minimal model for a pair (X, Δ) is called a log canonical model. The ACC conjecture (Alexeev-Birkar-Hacon-McKernan): the set of log canonical thresholds in a fixed dimension satisfies the ascending chain condition — i.e., there is no infinite increasing sequence. This was proved by Hacon-McKernan-Xu (2014) and is a cornerstone of birational geometry.
Flips are the key surgery of the MMP; discrepancies classify singularities along a graded scale from terminal (mildest) to log canonical (most general).
Part 4

🔥 Fano Varieties and the Boundedness Theorem

Fano
Fano Varieties, Birkar's Boundedness Theorem, and the Abundance Conjecture
A Fano variety is a projective variety X with −KX ample — the anticanonical bundle is positive. Examples: del Pezzo surfaces (blow-ups of ℙ² at up to 8 points). The boundedness of Fano varieties: there are only finitely many families of Fano varieties in each dimension. Birkar's proof (2018, awarded the Fields Medal): using the MMP and the theory of complements, Birkar proved the boundedness of Fano varieties with klt singularities and given volume. Earlier work: Kollár-Miyaoka-Mori (1992) proved boundedness for smooth Fano varieties. Batyrev's classification classified all toric Fano varieties. The Iitaka conjecture (Cnm conjecture): for a smooth projective fibration f: X → Y with general fiber F, κ(X) ≥ κ(Y) + κ(F). Proved in many cases (Kawamata, Viehweg, Kollár) but open in general. The abundance conjecture: if KX is nef, then it is semiample — some positive multiple mKX is basepoint-free. Known for surfaces (classical), 3-folds (Kawamata, Miyaoka, etc.), and for varieties with κ = 0 (Calabi-Yau case, Kawamata). The Iitaka dimension κ(X, L) of a line bundle L generalizes the Kodaira dimension: it is the maximal dimension of the image of the rational map defined by |mL|.
Fano varieties are the building blocks of the MMP with negative curvature; their boundedness, proved by Birkar, is a crowning achievement of modern birational geometry.
