1. The current affairs window

APFC papers typically ask current affairs from roughly the last 6–12 months before the exam. Because the Recruitment Test is scheduled once a year (notification usually mid-year, exam late in the year), the practical window for a December attempt is approximately January–November of that year. Always check the latest notification for the exact cutoff — it is usually stated or implied by the exam date.

The #1 mistake
Studying "current affairs" as everything that happened this year. The exam asks a thin slice: major national events, government schemes and appointments, international summits involving India, defence/science milestones, and economic/banking decisions. A student who reads three newspapers daily often knows less exam-relevant CA than one who keeps a structured weekly list.

2. What APFC actually asks

From previous APFC and comparable papers (SSC, RBI Grade B — same question families), the CA section splits roughly:

Current affairs categories and their weight
CategoryWeightExamples of what's asked
National events & government decisions~30%Major bills, policy launches, census/data releases, court verdicts in news
Government schemes & missions~20%New or expanded schemes — name + ministry + objective
Appointments & awards~15%New CEC, CVC, chiefs of services, RBI Governor, Padma awards, sports awards
International summits & visits~12%India's role in G20/BRICS/SCO, bilateral visits, agreements signed
Defence, space & science~10%Missile tests, satellite launches, ISRO/DRDO milestones, nuclear/defence deals
Economy & banking~8%Repo rate decisions, budget highlights, RBI policies, ratings
Sports & culture in news~5%India winning events, UNESCO/new heritage tags, booker-type awards

3. Category-by-category coverage

National events & schemes

For every major scheme or mission in the news, record four facts: full form, launching ministry, objective in one line, target beneficiary. The exam asks "which ministry launched X" or "X aims at" more often than "when was X launched".

Appointments & awards

Maintain a running table: position → person → month/year. Constitutional posts (President, VP, PM, CJI, CEC, CVC, CAG, RBI Governor, CDS, service chiefs) change rarely but are asked the moment they change. Award questions ask "who got X award this year" — keep the current-year Padma list and major international awards (Nobel, Booker, Fields, etc.) handy.

International

India's participation in summits (G20 presidency year, BRICS, SCO, Quad) and major bilateral visits/deals. The question is usually "India signed/hosted X with Y" — record the partner and the agreement's subject.

Defence & space

Missile test names (Agni, Prithvi, Akash, Nirbhay), satellite missions (Chandrayaan, Gaganyaan, Aditya-L1), defence deals (which country, which system), and DRDO/ISRO leadership changes.

Economy & banking

RBI monetary policy decisions (repo rate changes with dates), Union Budget headline numbers (fiscal deficit target, capex outlay), major economic data releases (GDP growth, CPI inflation, forex reserves), and banking sector news (mergers, new schemes).

4. The daily note method

15 minutes a day beats 3 hours on Sunday. Each evening, write one line per item in a notebook or phone note, in this format:

[Date] [Category] Fact — Person/Place/Number/Agreement

Examples:

  • "Mar 2026 | Scheme | PM-Dhan-Dhaanya Krishi Yojana launched by Agriculture Ministry for pulse production"
  • "Jun 2026 | Appointment | R. Rao appointed RBI Deputy Governor for markets"
  • "Aug 2026 | Defence | Successful test of Agni-P missile from APJ Abdul Kalam Island"
  • "Sep 2026 | Summit | India-ASEAN Trade Ministers meet in New Delhi; review FTA"

Weekly: convert the week's notes into 10–15 self-made MCQs. Monthly: merge the four weekly lists into one master list — this becomes your revision sheet. In the last 3 weeks before the exam, revise the master list daily instead of reading news.

Pro Tip
Use a standard monthly current-affairs magazine (or the site's own current-affairs practice pool) as your backbone, then layer your daily notes on top for the freshest events the magazine missed. The exam rarely asks something neither source covered — it asks the intersection of "important" and "recent".

5. Practice plan

Daily: 15-min note + 5 CA MCQs from the bank.

Weekly: one 15-question CA timed set + review every miss against your notes.

Last month: stop reading news; revise the master list + solve CA sections from 3–4 previous mocks. Current affairs reward recall under exam conditions, not last-minute reading.
