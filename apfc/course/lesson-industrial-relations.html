1. Why this section exists

Industrial Relations & Labour Codes carries 15 questions — the most specialised section in the paper. Generic GK aspirants skip it; that's your opportunity. The questions are factual: which Act covers what, which code replaced which laws, definitions, thresholds, and recent amendments. There is no interpretation — just accurate knowledge of a small, well-defined body of law.

Context that helps
APFC = Assistant Provident Fund Commissioner. The exam tests labour and social-security law because the job administers EPFO, pension schemes and workers' welfare. Knowing why the section exists makes the facts easier to retain — you're not memorising abstract law, you're learning your own employer's framework.

2. The four labour codes

Between 2019 and 2020, Parliament replaced 29 central labour laws with four comprehensive codes. This is the single most-asked theme in the section:

The four labour codes
CodeYear (enacted)CoversKey features
Code on Wages, 20192019Minimum wages, payment of wages, bonusUnified definition of "wages"; floor wage concept; applies to ALL establishments (organised + unorganised)
Industrial Relations Code, 20202020Trade unions, standing orders, retrenchment, layoffs, closures, strikesReplaced ID Act 1947 + Trade Unions Act 1926 + Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act 1946; threshold for standing orders raised to 300 workers; notice period for strike = 60 days
Code on Social Security, 20202020EPF, ESI, gratuity, maternity benefit, gig/platform workersFirst law covering gig & platform workers; merged 8 social security laws; Aadhar-linked benefits
Occupational Safety, Health & Working Conditions Code, 20202020Factories, mines, construction, working hours, leaveReplaced 13 laws incl. Factories Act 1948; safety obligations on all establishments with 10+ workers

Important nuance: the codes were enacted but many states are still framing rules — exam questions may ask "which code deals with X" rather than implementation details.

3. Core Acts you must know

Industrial Disputes Act, 1947

  • Applies to: "workmen" — any person employed in any industry to do manual, supervisory, technical or clerical work for hire/reward. Excludes people in managerial/administrative capacity earning above a threshold.
  • Key sections: Section 2(k) defines workman; Section 10 lists matters that are industrial disputes; Section 25F requires permission from government for layoff/retirement/retrenchment in establishments with 100+ workers; Section 25N lays down the compensation formula for retrenchment; Section 25O requires one month's notice or pay in lieu for retrenchment; Section 18 defines "appropriate government".
  • Strike rules: public utility services require 6 weeks' notice (14 days for others); illegal strikes attract penalties.
  • Retrenchment compensation: 15 days' average pay per completed year of service.

Trade Unions Act, 1926

  • Registered trade unions get legal status; minimum 7 members to form a union (in an establishment).
  • Registration gives immunity from civil suits for lawful trade-union activities; immunity lost if activities turn criminal.
  • "Recognised" unions get additional privileges (check-off facility, consultation rights).

Standing Orders — Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act, 1946

  • Requires employers with 100+ workers (now 300 under the IR Code) to certify standing orders — rules on classification of workers, hours, holidays, misconduct, disciplinary action.
  • Model standing orders are certified by conciliation officers / labour courts if employer and workers disagree.

Social security laws (pre-code, still tested)

  • EPF & MP Act 1952: Employees' Provident Fund — 12% employee + 12% employer contribution on "basic + DA"; EPFO administers; pension via EPS 1995 (8.33% of employer contribution goes to pension).
  • ESI Act 1948: Employees' State Insurance — medical + cash benefits; 0.75% employee + 3.25% employer of wages; applies to establishments with 10+ workers.
  • Payment of Gratuity Act 1972: 15 days' wages per completed year (max 20 lakh); applies to establishments with 10+ workers; 5-year minimum service (waived on death/disability).
  • Maternity Benefit Act 1961: 26 weeks paid maternity leave (amended 2017).
  • Minimum Wages Act 1948: state/federal minimum wages by scheduled employment.
  • Payment of Bonus Act 1965: statutory bonus 8.33%–20% for eligible workers.
  • Contract Labour (Regulation & Abolition) Act 1970: regulates contractors; abolished in some establishments/notifications.

Verification note
Labour law thresholds and code provisions have been amended several times (notably 2020 codes). Before the exam, verify the current thresholds against the latest official text or a recent monthly magazine — numbers like "300 workers" and maternity-leave weeks are exactly what papers test and exactly what changes.

5. Practice plan

Approach: this is a memorisation section — treat it like GK lists. Make three tables: (1) the four codes → what each replaces → key threshold; (2) the core Acts → year → one-line coverage; (3) the numbers table above.

Drill: 10 IR questions daily from the APFC/micro-test pool; every wrong answer goes into the list with the correct fact. In the last two weeks, revise only the three tables — 15 questions is small enough to fully cover.

Why it pays: most candidates score 4–7/15 here because they skipped it; a prepared candidate can reliably get 11–13. On a 160-question paper, that's a ~5-mark swing — often the difference between merit and miss.
