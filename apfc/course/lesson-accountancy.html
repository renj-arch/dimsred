1. The accountancy section pattern

Accountancy, Auditing & Insurance carries 10 questions — the smallest but most specialised section. Questions are factual and job-relevant: basic accounting terms, audit principles, insurance types and Indian financial/social-security institutions. Like IR, it's a memorisation section where preparation converts directly into marks.

2. Accounting fundamentals

The accounting equation

Assets = Liabilities + Capital (Owner's Equity). Every transaction preserves this equation. APFC questions may ask which element a given item belongs to:

  • Assets: cash, bank, debtors, stock, machinery, buildings — things the business owns.
  • Liabilities: creditors, loans, outstanding expenses — obligations owed.
  • Capital: owner's investment + retained profits.

Key terms

Accounting vocabulary
TermMeaning
JournalFirst book of record — every transaction entered with debit & credit
LedgerClassification of journal entries by account
Trial BalanceList of all ledger balances; total debits = total credits
Profit & Loss AccountRevenue − expenses for a period = net profit/loss
Balance SheetStatement of assets, liabilities, capital at a point in time
DepreciationSystematic allocation of asset cost over useful life
GoodwillPremium paid over net asset value in a business acquisition
Accounts Receivable/PayableDebtors (owed to you) / Creditors (you owe)

Basic principles

  • Double-entry: every transaction has equal debit and credit.
  • Going concern: business assumed to continue indefinitely.
  • Matching: expenses matched to the revenue they generate.
  • Materiality: only items affecting decisions are reported.
  • Consistency: same methods applied period to period.
  • Realisation: revenue recognised when earned, not when cash received.

3. Audit and insurance concepts

Audit

  • Statutory audit: legally required annual audit of financial statements (Companies Act, Income Tax, societies/cooperative laws).
  • Internal audit: ongoing internal check by the organisation's own staff — fraud prevention, not statutory.
  • External audit: independent auditor (CA firm) expresses opinion on fair presentation.
  • Audit evidence: information the auditor uses to reach conclusions — documents, confirmations, physical verification.
  • Qualified opinion: auditor's report flagging material misstatement or scope limitation.
  • CARO: Companies (Auditor's Report) Order — specifies extra reporting matters.

Insurance

  • Life insurance: covers human life — LIC, private life insurers; term, endowment, ULIP, pension plans.
  • General insurance: non-life — fire, motor, health, marine, crop; IRDAI regulates.
  • Social insurance: government-mandated — ESI, EPF (though EPF is a provident fund, administered as social security).
  • Key terms: premium (payment for cover), policy (contract), insured (covered person), sum assured (payout), nominee (beneficiary), underwriting (risk assessment).
  • IRDAI: Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India — established 1999, regulates insurers.

4. Indian institutions to know

  • EPFO: Employees' Provident Fund Organisation — administers EPF, EPS (pension), EDLI (deposits-linked insurance); under Ministry of Labour & Employment.
  • LIC: Life Insurance Corporation — India's largest life insurer, nationalised 1956.
  • GIC: General Insurance Corporation — nationalised general insurer 1973; subsidiaries = National, New India, Oriental, United India.
  • NHB: National Housing Bank — apex housing finance regulator.
  • NABARD: National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development — rural credit apex.
  • SIDBI: Small Industries Development Bank of India — MSME finance.
  • ICAI: Institute of Chartered Accountants of India — statutory body for CA profession, established 1949.
  • SEBI: Securities and Exchange Board — capital markets regulator (not insurance — common distractor).
Pro Tip
The exam loves "which institution regulates X" and "which Act established Y". Build the regulator↔sector map: RBI→banks, SEBI→capital markets, IRDAI→insurance, PFRDA→pension, EPFO→provident fund, NABARD→rural credit. Ten pairs, ~4 questions.

5. Practice plan

Approach: this section rewards a single consolidated list — accounting equation + 8 terms, audit types, insurance types, and the institution map. One evening of list-making covers most of the 10 questions.

Drill: 5 accountancy questions daily in the last month; combine with the IR list for a single "specialist section" revision sheet. If you can answer 8/10 consistently here plus 12/15 in IR, you've neutralised the two weakest sections for most candidates.
