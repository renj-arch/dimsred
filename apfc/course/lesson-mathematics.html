1. The APFC maths pattern

The APFC Recruitment Test Mathematics section carries 25 questions — roughly 16% of the paper. The level is class 10: no calculus, no trigonometry identities beyond basics, no heavy mensuration. What separates a 15/25 scorer from a 22/25 scorer is speed and accuracy on repetitive templates, not mathematical brilliance.

The recurring families, in rough order of frequency:

  1. Percentages and successive change
  2. Ratio and proportion (including partnership and mixtures)
  3. Simple and compound interest
  4. Time, work and pipes
  5. Speed, distance and trains
  6. Profit, loss and discount
  7. Averages and mixtures
  8. Number system (divisibility, remainders, unit digits)
  9. Mensuration and basic geometry
  10. Data interpretation (one table or chart set)

Strategy: master the formula sheet below until it is reflex, then drill each family until you solve in under 60 seconds.

3. Topic-by-topic methods

Percentages and successive change

Two rules cover 90% of questions. First, convert everything to fractions where possible — 20% = 1/5 makes successive changes trivial: +20% then −20% on 100 → 120 → 96, i.e. −4%. Second, for population/final-value problems, fix the initial value at 100 and compute forward — the ratio of final to initial is the answer regardless of the actual number.

Worked example
A's salary is increased by 25% and then decreased by 20%. If the final salary is Rs 45,000, find the original.
Solution: Assume original = 100. After +25% → 125. After −20% → 125 × 0.8 = 100. Net change = 0%! So original = Rs 45,000. The trap is assuming it changed — equal percentage up/down does not return to start unless percentages match exactly (here 25×20/100 = 5% net... recheck: 100×1.25×0.8 = 100. Yes, zero change — the classic trap).

Interest — the SI/CI trap

For 2 years at r%: CI − SI = P(r/100)². For 3 years at 10%: CI = SI + interest on year-1 CI + interest on year-2 CI. Most APFC questions give amount after t years and ask rate or principal — work backwards: subtract simple interest each year mentally or set P = amount/(1+r/100)t for nice rates like 10% (×1.21 for 2y, ×1.331 for 3y).

Time, work and pipes

LCM method: if A takes 12 days and B takes 18, take 36 units of work. A does 3 units/day, B does 2/day, together 5/day → 36/5 = 7.2 days. For pipes, assign filling rates positive and emptying negative; if the tank fills in 6h and empties in 12h, net = 1/6 − 1/12 = 1/12 → fills in 12h. Trap: "A and B together" vs "A alone after B leaves" — split the timeline.

Speed and trains

Three distances matter: crossing a pole/standing man = train length; crossing a platform = train + platform; two trains crossing = sum of lengths (opposite direction) or difference (same direction). Convert km/h ↔ m/s at the start, not mid-solution. Average speed trap: never average the two speeds unless distances are equal — use 2ab/(a+b) for equal distances, total D/total T otherwise.

Averages and mixtures

Averages: if the average of n numbers is A and one number x is excluded, new average = (nA − x)/(n−1). Mixtures: alligation gives the mixing ratio directly — a 60L mixture of milk (cost 30/L) and water replaced... for replacement problems, remaining original = total × (1 − replaced/total)n.

Number system

Divisibility: 3 → digit sum; 9 → digit sum; 11 → alternating sum; 4/8 → last 2/3 digits; 5/10 → last digit. Unit digits cycle with period 4 (bases ending 2,3,7,8,9,4,6,8...). Remainder problems: (an mod m) — use cyclicity. HCF×LCM = product — instant check answers.

4. Data interpretation strategy

DI is 1–2 questions per set at APFC level — a table, bar chart or pie chart followed by 2–3 computations. Rules:

  • Read the question first, then look at the chart for only the needed figures — do not study the whole chart.
  • Keep numbers approximate: 487 ≈ 500 when options are far apart.
  • Pie charts: value = (angle/360) × total; percentage = angle/3.6.
  • Table DI: compute row/column totals first — most questions ask for a share or difference.
  • If a question needs three chained calculations, estimate first and verify only if the estimate is close to two options.

5. Examiner traps

Traps that cost marks
  • SI vs CI: questions say "compound" in one word — read carefully; the difference is the whole question.
  • Average speed: (a+b)/2 is wrong except for equal times, not equal distances.
  • Successive %: +x% then −x% is always a net loss of x²/100%.
  • Time-work: "A alone for x days then B joins" — compute work done in phase 1 first.
  • Profit on marked price: discount applies to MP, profit on CP — chain both percentages.
  • Unit conversion: trains in km/h but lengths in metres — convert before multiplying.
  • Ratio direction: "A is twice B" → A:B = 2:1, not 1:2.

6. Practice plan

Week 1: Memorise the formula sheet — write it from memory daily. Drill percentages, SI/CI, time-work (10 questions each, timed).

Week 2: Speed/trains, profit-loss, averages, mixtures, number system. Aim for 60-second solves.

Week 3: Geometry/mensuration + DI sets. Take one mixed section (25 questions, 30 minutes) every other day.

Final week: Only timed mocks and error-log revision — re-solve every question you got wrong, write the trap in one line.

Pro Tip
In the APFC paper there is no negative marking mentioned for unanswered, but wrong answers do attract −1/3. Attempt every question you can eliminate to two options — expected value is positive. Never leave a section half-attempted: 25 maths questions at ~1 min each is a 25-minute investment that is easy marks compared to IR or Accountancy.
