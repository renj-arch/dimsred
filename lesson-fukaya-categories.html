Fukaya Categories — the A∞-Category, Lagrangian Floer Theory & Homological Mirror Symmetry

The Fukaya category F(X) of a symplectic manifold (X, ω) is an A∞-category whose objects are Lagrangian submanifolds L ⊂ X (equipped with spin structures, gradings, and bounding cochains when necessary). The morphism spaces are Floer cohomology groups HF^*(L₀, L₁) generated by intersection points after a Hamiltonian perturbation. The A∞-structure maps m_k count pseudoholomorphic polygons with boundary on the Lagrangians — these counts are the symplectic analogue of Gromov-Witten invariants. The construction requires sophisticated virtual perturbation techniques (the Kuranishi space of Fukaya-Oh-Ohta-Ono) to achieve transversality. The theory carries an intrinsic obstruction — the curvature m₀ = const·[L] — which can be cancelled by a bounding cochain b ∈ H^1(L; ℂ) satisfying the Maurer-Cartan equation. Kontsevich's homological mirror symmetry conjecture (1994) asserts that for a mirror pair (X, ̂X), the derived Fukaya category of X is equivalent to the derived category of coherent sheaves on ̂X. This conjecture has been verified for elliptic curves (Polishchuk-Zaslow), the quintic threefold (Sheridan), and many toric varieties.

"The Fukaya category is the algebraic skeleton of symplectic topology."
— Kenji Fukaya
Part 1

📏 Lagrangian Submanifolds and Floer Cohomology

Lagrangians
Lagrangian Submanifolds, Maslov Index, and Floer Cohomology
A Lagrangian submanifold L ⊂ (X^{2n}, ω) satisfies ω|_L = 0 and dim L = n. The Maslov index μ: π₂(X, L) → ℤ measures the winding of the tangent spaces along a disk. Floer cohomology HF^*(L₀, L₁) is defined for a pair of Lagrangians: after a Hamiltonian perturbation to achieve transversality, the chain complex is generated by intersection points p ∈ L₀ ⋔ L₁. The differential ∂p = Σ_{q} #ℳ(p, q; J)·q counts pseudoholomorphic strips u: ℝ×[0,1] → X with u(s,0) ∈ L₀, u(s,1) ∈ L₁, finite energy, asymptotic to p and q. The equation is ∂̄_J u = 0 for a generic almost complex structure J compatible with ω. Achieving transversality for the moduli spaces of strips requires the Kuranishi structure (Fukaya-Oh-Ohta-Ono): a virtual perturbation technique that constructs a smooth finite-dimensional reduction. The product m₂: HF(L₀, L₁)⊗HF(L₁, L₂) → HF(L₀, L₂) counts holomorphic triangles with boundary on three Lagrangians. Higher products m_k count holomorphic (k+1)-gons, giving the A∞-structure. The energy zero contribution gives the classical intersection product; bubbling phenomena encode disk invariants.
Floer cohomology is the intersection theory of Lagrangian submanifolds, with quantum corrections from pseudoholomorphic disks and strips counted via virtual perturbation techniques.
Part 2

🌍 The A∞-Category Structure

A∞-Category
A∞-Categories, the Fukaya Category F(X), and Obstruction Theory
An A∞-category over a field 𝕂 consists of objects X,Y,… with ℤ-graded morphism spaces Hom^*(X,Y) and structure maps m_k: Hom(X₀,X₁)⊗⋯⊗Hom(X_{k−1},X_k) → Hom(X₀,X_k)[2−k] for k ≥ 1 (plus m₀: 𝕂 → Hom(X,X)[2] for each object). The A∞-relations hold: Σ_{i+j=n} (−1)^{ε} m_i(…, m_j(…), …) = 0 for all n ≥ 1, where ε = Σ_{p=1}^{i−1} |a_p| + i·j. The Fukaya category F(X) has objects = Lagrangian submanifolds equipped with a spin structure, a grading (a lift of the Lagrangian to the Maslov cover), and a bounding cochain b ∈ H^1(L; ℂ) if the Lagrangian is obstructed. The morphism space Hom(L₀, L₁) = CF^*(L₀, L₁) (the Floer complex). The m₀ term m₀(1) = c·[L] ∈ CF^*(L, L) represents the obstruction: it is the count of holomorphic disks with boundary on L. A Lagrangian is unobstructed if there exists b ∈ H^1(L; ℂ) (the bounding cochain) satisfying the Maurer-Cartan equation: (m₀ + m₁(b) + m₂(b⊗b) + m₃(b⊗b⊗b) + …) = 0. The curved A∞-structure then becomes a genuine A∞-structure on the deformed Floer complex. The category of unobstructed Lagrangians F^{unob}(X) is the full subcategory whose objects are unobstructed. The Floer cohomology algebra HF^*(L, L) is a unital A∞-algebra; its cohomology is isomorphic to H^*(L; ℂ) for the trivial bounding cochain when L is monotone or exact.
The Fukaya category organizes Lagrangian submanifolds into an A∞-category; the obstruction theory via bounding cochains determines which Lagrangians contribute to the derived category.
Part 3

🧮 The Fukaya-Seidel Category

Fukaya-Seidel
Lefschetz Fibrations, Vanishing Cycles, and the Directed Fukaya Category
A Lefschetz fibration π: E → ℂ is a symplectic fiber bundle with isolated non-degenerate critical points. At each critical point, a vanishing cycle L_γ ⊂ E is a Lagrangian sphere that vanishes in the singular fiber. Seidel's directed Fukaya category (or the Fukaya-Seidel category) F_→(π) is an A∞-category whose objects are the vanishing cycles (ordered by the critical values). The morphism spaces are Floer cohomology groups HF^*(L_i, L_j) for i < j. The composition maps m_k count pseudoholomorphic polygons with boundary on the vanishing cycles, where the ordering of objects dictates the direction of morphisms (this is a directed A∞-category). The Seidel representation gives an action of the mapping class group of the base on the derived Fukaya category via Dehn twists along vanishing cycles — the Picard-Lefschetz theory in symplectic topology. The Piunikhin-Salamon-Schwarz (PSS) map relates the quantum cohomology QH^*(E) to the Hochschild cohomology HH^*(F_→(π)). The Seidel exact sequence relates the Fukaya category of the total space to the Fukaya-Seidel category of the fibration: there is a spectral sequence converging to HF^*(L, L) for a Lagrangian L ⊂ E whose E_1 page involves the Floer cohomology of the vanishing cycles. The Fukaya-Seidel category of a Lefschetz fibration is the symplectic analogue of the category of matrix factorizations in algebraic geometry — a key ingredient in the proof of homological mirror symmetry for Fano varieties.
The Fukaya-Seidel category extracts the symplectic topology of a Lefschetz fibration as a directed A∞-category of vanishing cycles, linking Dehn twists to auto-equivalences.

🔥 Homological Mirror Symmetry

HMS
Kontsevich's Conjecture, Verifications, and Applications
Kontsevich's homological mirror symmetry (1994, ICM talk): for a mirror pair (X, ̂X) of Calabi-Yau manifolds (more generally, for any mirror pair), there is an equivalence of triangulated categories D^b F(X) ≅ D^b Coh(̂X). Here D^b F(X) is the derived Fukaya category (the triangulated envelope of the Fukaya category) and D^b Coh(̂X) is the derived category of coherent sheaves on the mirror. The mirror of the Fano toric variety ℙ^n is an affine variety with an exact symplectic structure (the Landau-Ginzburg model). Polishchuk and Zaslow (1998) proved HMS for the elliptic curve: F(T²) ≅ D^b Coh(Ê) where Ê is the dual elliptic curve — the objects are Lagrangian tori on T² corresponding to line bundles on Ê. Sheridan (2015) proved HMS for the quintic threefold: the derived Fukaya category of the quintic Calabi-Yau 3-fold in ℙ⁴ is equivalent to the derived category of coherent sheaves on the mirror quintic. The proof constructs a split-generator of the Fukaya category (the Lagrangian torus fibration) and matches it with the structure sheaf via the SYZ construction (Strominger-Yau-Zaslow). Abouzaid's generation criterion gives conditions under which a collection of Lagrangians generates the Fukaya category — used to prove that the wrapped Fukaya category of T^*M is equivalent to the category of modules over the ring of functions on the mirror affine variety. Nadler's theorem shows that the Fukaya category of the cotangent bundle T^*M is equivalent to the derived category of constructible sheaves on M. The wrapped Fukaya category of a Liouville domain (an exact symplectic manifold with conical end) provides the symplectic analogue of the module category over the ring of functions on the mirror affine variety — central to the Kontsevich-Soibelman approach to mirror symmetry via non-Archimedean geometry.
Homological mirror symmetry is a deep equivalence between symplectic and algebraic geometries: the symplectic side (Fukaya category) mirrors the algebraic side (coherent sheaves) under the exchange of the symplectic and complex structures of mirror pairs.
