Fukaya Categories — the A∞-Category, Lagrangian Floer Theory & Homological Mirror Symmetry

The Fukaya category F(X) of a symplectic manifold (X, ω) is an A∞-category whose objects are Lagrangian submanifolds L ⊂ X (equipped with spin structures, gradings, and bounding cochains when necessary). The morphism spaces are Floer cohomology groups HF^*(L₀, L₁) generated by intersection points after a Hamiltonian perturbation. The A∞-structure maps m_k count pseudoholomorphic polygons with boundary on the Lagrangians — these counts are the symplectic analogue of Gromov-Witten invariants. The construction requires sophisticated virtual perturbation techniques (the Kuranishi space of Fukaya-Oh-Ohta-Ono) to achieve transversality. The theory carries an intrinsic obstruction — the curvature m₀ = const·[L] — which can be cancelled by a bounding cochain b ∈ H^1(L; ℂ) satisfying the Maurer-Cartan equation. Kontsevich's homological mirror symmetry conjecture (1994) asserts that for a mirror pair (X, ̂X), the derived Fukaya category of X is equivalent to the derived category of coherent sheaves on ̂X. This conjecture has been verified for elliptic curves (Polishchuk-Zaslow), the quintic threefold (Sheridan), and many toric varieties.

Part 1

📏 Lagrangian Submanifolds and Floer Cohomology

Lagrangians
Lagrangian Submanifolds, Maslov Index, and Floer Cohomology
A Lagrangian submanifold L ⊂ (X^{2n}, ω) satisfies ω|_L = 0 and dim L = n. The Maslov index μ: π₂(X, L) → ℤ measures the winding of the tangent spaces along a disk. Floer cohomology HF^*(L₀, L₁) is defined for a pair of Lagrangians: after a Hamiltonian perturbation to achieve transversality, the chain complex is generated by intersection points p ∈ L₀ ⋔ L₁. The differential ∂p = Σ_{q} #ℳ(p, q; J)·q counts pseudoholomorphic strips u: ℝ×[0,1] → X with u(s,0) ∈ L₀, u(s,1) ∈ L₁, finite energy, asymptotic to p and q. The equation is ∂̄_J u = 0 for a generic almost complex structure J compatible with ω. Achieving transversality for the moduli spaces of strips requires the Kuranishi structure (Fukaya-Oh-Ohta-Ono): a virtual perturbation technique that constructs a smooth finite-dimensional reduction. The product m₂: HF(L₀, L₁)⊗HF(L₁, L₂) → HF(L₀, L₂) counts holomorphic triangles with boundary on three Lagrangians. Higher products m_k count holomorphic (k+1)-gons, giving the A∞-structure. The energy zero contribution gives the classical intersection product; bubbling phenomena encode disk invariants.
Floer cohomology is the intersection theory of Lagrangian submanifolds, with quantum corrections from pseudoholomorphic disks and strips counted via virtual perturbation techniques.

🌍 The A∞-Category Structure

A∞-Category
A∞-Categories, the Fukaya Category F(X), and Obstruction Theory
An A∞-category over a field 𝕂 consists of objects X,Y,… with ℤ-graded morphism spaces Hom^*(X,Y) and structure maps m_k: Hom(X₀,X₁)⊗⋯⊗Hom(X_{k−1},X_k) → Hom(X₀,X_k)[2−k] for k ≥ 1 (plus m₀: 𝕂 → Hom(X,X)[2] for each object). The A∞-relations hold: Σ_{i+j=n} (−1)^{ε} m_i(…, m_j(…), …) = 0 for all n ≥ 1, where ε = Σ_{p=1}^{i−1} |a_p| + i·j. The Fukaya category F(X) has objects = Lagrangian submanifolds equipped with a spin structure, a grading (a lift of the Lagrangian to the Maslov cover), and a bounding cochain b ∈ H^1(L; ℂ) if the Lagrangian is obstructed. The morphism space Hom(L₀, L₁) = CF^*(L₀, L₁) (the Floer complex). The m₀ term m₀(1) = c·[L] ∈ CF^*(L, L) represents the obstruction: it is the count of holomorphic disks with boundary on L. A Lagrangian is unobstructed if there exists b ∈ H^1(L; ℂ) (the bounding cochain) satisfying the Maurer-Cartan equation: (m₀ + m₁(b) + m₂(b⊗b) + m₃(b⊗b⊗b) + …) = 0. The curved A∞-structure then becomes a genuine A∞-structure on the deformed Floer complex. The category of unobstructed Lagrangians F^{unob}(X) is the full subcategory whose objects are unobstructed. The Floer cohomology algebra HF^*(L, L) is a unital A∞-algebra; its cohomology is isomorphic to H^*(L; ℂ) for the trivial bounding cochain when L is monotone or exact.
The Fukaya category organizes Lagrangian submanifolds into an A∞-category; the obstruction theory via bounding cochains determines which Lagrangians contribute to the derived category.

🧮 The Fukaya-Seidel Category

Fukaya-Seidel
Lefschetz Fibrations, Vanishing Cycles, and the Directed Fukaya Category
A Lefschetz fibration π: E → ℂ is a symplectic fiber bundle with isolated non-degenerate critical points. At each critical point, a vanishing cycle L_γ ⊂ E is a Lagrangian sphere that vanishes in the singular fiber. Seidel's directed Fukaya category (or the Fukaya-Seidel category) F_→(π) is an A∞-category whose objects are the vanishing cycles (ordered by the critical values). The morphism spaces are Floer cohomology groups HF^*(L_i, L_j) for i < j. The composition maps m_k count pseudoholomorphic polygons with boundary on the vanishing cycles, where the ordering of objects dictates the direction of morphisms (this is a directed A∞-category). The Seidel representation gives an action of the mapping class group of the base on the derived Fukaya category via Dehn twists along vanishing cycles — the Picard-Lefschetz theory in symplectic topology. The Piunikhin-Salamon-Schwarz (PSS) map relates the quantum cohomology QH^*(E) to the Hochschild cohomology HH^*(F_→(π)). The Seidel exact sequence relates the Fukaya category of the total space to the Fukaya-Seidel category of the fibration: there is a spectral sequence converging to HF^*(L, L) for a Lagrangian L ⊂ E whose E_1 page involves the Floer cohomology of the vanishing cycles. The Fukaya-Seidel category of a Lefschetz fibration is the symplectic analogue of the category of matrix factorizations in algebraic geometry — a key ingredient in the proof of homological mirror symmetry for Fano varieties.
The Fukaya-Seidel category extracts the symplectic topology of a Lefschetz fibration as a directed A∞-category of vanishing cycles, linking Dehn twists to auto-equivalences.

🔥 Homological Mirror Symmetry

HMS
Kontsevich's Conjecture, Verifications, and Applications
Kontsevich's homological mirror symmetry (1994, ICM talk): for a mirror pair (X, ̂X) of Calabi-Yau manifolds (more generally, for any mirror pair), there is an equivalence of triangulated categories D^b F(X) ≅ D^b Coh(̂X). Here D^b F(X) is the derived Fukaya category (the triangulated envelope of the Fukaya category) and D^b Coh(̂X) is the derived category of coherent sheaves on the mirror. The mirror of the Fano toric variety ℙ^n is an affine variety with an exact symplectic structure (the Landau-Ginzburg model). Polishchuk and Zaslow (1998) proved HMS for the elliptic curve: F(T²) ≅ D^b Coh(Ê) where Ê is the dual elliptic curve — the objects are Lagrangian tori on T² corresponding to line bundles on Ê. Sheridan (2015) proved HMS for the quintic threefold: the derived Fukaya category of the quintic Calabi-Yau 3-fold in ℙ⁴ is equivalent to the derived category of coherent sheaves on the mirror quintic. The proof constructs a split-generator of the Fukaya category (the Lagrangian torus fibration) and matches it with the structure sheaf via the SYZ construction (Strominger-Yau-Zaslow). Abouzaid's generation criterion gives conditions under which a collection of Lagrangians generates the Fukaya category — used to prove that the wrapped Fukaya category of T^*M is equivalent to the category of modules over the ring of functions on the mirror affine variety. Nadler's theorem shows that the Fukaya category of the cotangent bundle T^*M is equivalent to the derived category of constructible sheaves on M. The wrapped Fukaya category of a Liouville domain (an exact symplectic manifold with conical end) provides the symplectic analogue of the module category over the ring of functions on the mirror affine variety — central to the Kontsevich-Soibelman approach to mirror symmetry via non-Archimedean geometry.
Homological mirror symmetry is a deep equivalence between symplectic and algebraic geometries: the symplectic side (Fukaya category) mirrors the algebraic side (coherent sheaves) under the exchange of the symplectic and complex structures of mirror pairs.

Obstruction Theory and Bounding Cochains in the Fukaya Category

The Fukaya category of a symplectic manifold X is defined only after one resolves the obstruction to defining the A∞‑operations mₖ on the Floer complexes CF*(L₀,L₁). For a Lagrangian submanifold L, the obstruction lives in the degree‑2 Floer cohomology HF²(L,L) and is represented by the curvature term m₀ ∈ CF²(L,L). When m₀ ≠ 0 the A∞‑relations fail at the first level: m₁² = m₂(m₀⊗id) + m₂(id⊗m₀). The modern solution, due to Fukaya‑Oh‑Ohta‑Ono, introduces a bounding cochain b ∈ CF¹(L,L) (or more generally b ∈ H¹(L;Λ₀) where Λ₀ is the Novikov ring) and deforms the operations by the Maurer‑Cartan series m₀ᵇ = m₀ + m₁(b) + m₂(b⊗b) + m₃(b⊗b⊗b) + … . The condition m₀ᵇ = 0 is the Maurer‑Cartan equation; a solution b makes the deformed complex (CF*(L,L), m₁ᵇ) a genuine differential (m₁ᵇ² = 0) and yields a well‑defined A∞‑structure on the deformed morphism spaces. If such a b exists, L is called unobstructed; otherwise L is obstructed and does not define an object of the Fukaya category. For monotone or exact Lagrangians the trivial cochain b = 0 solves the equation, so HF*(L,L) ≅ H*(L;Λ₀) as algebras. In the general case one must work with the curved A∞‑category and then pass to the category of unobstructed objects Funob(X). The deformation theory of bounding cochains is governed by the obstruction class [m₀] ∈ HF²(L,L); its vanishing is necessary and sufficient for the existence of a bounding cochain after a suitable gauge transformation. This framework also explains why the Fukaya category is only defined over the Novikov field: the energy filtration on pseudoholomorphic disks forces the use of formal power series in the symplectic area variable T.

Wrapped Fukaya Categories and Liouville Domains

For non‑compact exact symplectic manifolds with cylindrical ends — Liouville domains — the ordinary Fukaya category is too small because Hamiltonian chords that escape to infinity are not counted. The wrapped Fukaya category W(X) remedies this by allowing Hamiltonians that grow quadratically at infinity, so that the time‑1 flow wraps Lagrangians around the cylindrical end. Objects are exact Lagrangian submanifolds that are cylindrical outside a compact set; morphism complexes are generated by Hamiltonian chords of the wrapped Hamiltonian. The A∞‑operations count pseudoholomorphic polygons with boundary on the wrapped Lagrangians, and the wrapping ensures that every intersection point appears after sufficiently many wraps. A fundamental theorem of Abouzaid (2010) states that for a Weinstein manifold X (a Liouville domain with a Morse function compatible with the Liouville flow) the wrapped Fukaya category is generated by the cocores of the critical handles. Moreover, the wrapped category is invariant under Liouville deformation and satisfies a Künneth formula W(X × Y) ≃ W(X) ⊗ W(Y). The wrapped category of the cotangent bundle T*M is quasi‑equivalent to the derived category of modules over the singular chains on the based loop space ΩM, and via the work of Nadler‑Zaslow it is also equivalent to the derived category of constructible sheaves on M. This equivalence is a cornerstone of the Kontsevich‑Soibelman approach to mirror symmetry for affine varieties: the mirror of a Liouville domain is an affine variety whose category of coherent sheaves (or modules over its coordinate ring) matches W(X). The wrapped category also carries a natural Calabi‑Yau structure of dimension n = dim X, given by a non‑degenerate cyclic pairing on Hochschild homology.

Worked Example: Obstruction Vanishing for a Monotone Lagrangian Torus
Let L ⊂ ℂP² be the Clifford torus, which is monotone with minimal Maslov number 2. Show that the curvature term m₀ ∈ CF²(L,L) vanishes for the trivial bounding cochain b = 0, and conclude that L is an object of the Fukaya category F(ℂP²).
For a monotone Lagrangian the symplectic area of any pseudoholomorphic disk with boundary on L is proportional to its Maslov index. The Clifford torus in ℂP² bounds exactly three families of Maslov‑2 disks, each contributing to m₀ with coefficient Tω/2 where ω is the symplectic area of the generator of H₂(ℂP²). The signed count of these disks is zero because the three families are related by the torus symmetry and cancel in the Floer complex over the Novikov field. Hence m₀ = 0 in CF²(L,L). With b = 0 the Maurer‑Cartan equation m₀ + m₁(b) + … = 0 holds trivially. Therefore L is unobstructed and defines an object of F(ℂP²). Its Floer cohomology HF*(L,L) is isomorphic to H*(L;Λ₀) ≅ Λ₀ ⊕ Λ₀[1]² ⊕ Λ₀[2] as a graded vector space, and the product structure recovers the quantum cohomology of ℂP² restricted to the torus.
Exam Strategy: Recognising Unobstructed Lagrangians
When a question asks whether a given Lagrangian defines an object of the Fukaya category, first check monotonicity or exactness. If the Lagrangian is monotone with minimal Maslov number ≥ 2, the curvature m₀ often vanishes by symmetry or by a degree argument (no Maslov‑0 disks). For exact Lagrangians in a Liouville domain, m₀ = 0 automatically because there are no closed pseudoholomorphic disks. If the Lagrangian is neither monotone nor exact, look for a bounding cochain solving the Maurer‑Cartan equation; the existence of such a cochain is equivalent to the vanishing of the obstruction class in HF². In multiple‑choice settings, the answer “unobstructed” is correct precisely when the obstruction class vanishes.

Generation Criteria and Split‑Generators

A central problem in Fukaya category theory is to determine when a collection of Lagrangians generates the whole category. Abouzaid’s generation criterion (2010) gives a practical test: a set of objects {Lᵢ} in a triangulated A∞‑category C split‑generates C if and only if the induced map on Hochschild homology HH*(C) → ⊕ HH*(Lᵢ,Lᵢ) is surjective. In the wrapped Fukaya category of a Liouville domain, the cocores of the critical handles form a split‑generating set; this is the wrapped analogue of the fact that the structure sheaf generates the derived category of coherent sheaves on an affine variety. For compact symplectic manifolds, the situation is subtler. Sheridan’s proof of homological mirror symmetry for the quintic threefold constructs a split‑generator from the Lagrangian torus fibration given by the SYZ construction. The generator is a formal direct sum of Lagrangian tori with appropriate bounding cochains, and its endomorphism algebra is quasi‑isomorphic to the homogeneous coordinate ring of the mirror quintic. The generation criterion is also used to prove that the Fukaya category of a Fano toric variety is generated by the Lagrangian torus fibers of the moment map; the mirror is the Landau‑Ginzburg model whose category of matrix factorizations is generated by the structure sheaf. In practice, to show that a given Lagrangian L split‑generates, one often computes the open‑closed map OC: HH*(F(X)) → QH*(X) and checks that the unit in quantum cohomology lies in the image of the composition HH*(F(X)) → HH*(L,L) → QH*(X). If the unit is hit, then L split‑generates by a theorem of Abouzaid‑Fukaya‑Oh‑Ohta‑Ono.

SYZ Fibrations and Mirror Construction

The Strominger‑Yau‑Zaslow (SYZ) conjecture (1996) proposes that mirror symmetry for Calabi‑Yau manifolds can be understood geometrically as a duality between special Lagrangian torus fibrations. A special Lagrangian submanifold L in a Calabi‑Yau n‑fold X is a Lagrangian calibrated by the real part of the holomorphic volume form Ω; it has minimal volume in its homology class and its Maslov class vanishes. An SYZ fibration is a map π: X → B whose generic fibers are special Lagrangian n‑tori, with a discriminant locus Δ ⊂ B where the fibers degenerate. The mirror ̂X is constructed by dualizing the torus fibers: ̂X = ⋃b∈B\Δ H¹(L_b;U(1)) / H¹(L_b;ℤ), equipped with a complex structure determined by the affine structure on B\Δ coming from the periods of the symplectic form and the holomorphic volume form. The SYZ picture explains why the Fukaya category of X should be equivalent to the derived category of coherent sheaves on ̂X: a Lagrangian section of the fibration corresponds to a skyscraper sheaf on ̂X, while a Lagrangian multisection corresponds to a vector bundle. The Fukaya‑Seidel category of a Lefschetz fibration provides a local model for the SYZ fibration near a focus‑focus singularity; the vanishing cycles are the Lagrangian spheres that collapse at the singular fiber, and their Dehn twists generate the monodromy of the fibration. Gross‑Siebert’s program (2000s) gives an algebraic reconstruction of the mirror from the tropical geometry of the base B, using scattering diagrams to correct the naive dual torus bundle. This program has been used to prove mirror symmetry for many toric Calabi‑Yau varieties and for K3 surfaces. In the context of the Fukaya category, the SYZ fibration provides a geometric source of split‑generators: the collection of all Lagrangian torus fibers (with appropriate bounding cochains) is expected to split‑generate F(X). This expectation underlies many recent proofs of homological mirror symmetry for Fano and Calabi‑Yau manifolds.

Dehn Twist Autoequivalences and the Seidel Representation

In a symplectic manifold X, a Lagrangian sphere S ⊂ X of dimension n admits a generalized Dehn twist τ_S: X → X, a symplectomorphism supported in a neighbourhood of S that acts as a reflection on the normal directions. On the Fukaya category, τ_S induces an A∞‑autoequivalence Φ_S: F(X) → F(X) given by the spherical twist functor. For any Lagrangian L, the morphism complex CF*(L,S) carries a natural action of the spherical twist, and the effect on objects is expressed by the exact triangle Φ_S(L) → L → CF*(L,S) ⊗ S in the derived Fukaya category. Seidel’s representation theorem states that for a Lefschetz fibration π: E → ℂ with vanishing cycles V₁,…,V_m, the mapping class group of the base (the braid group on m strands) acts on the derived Fukaya‑Seidel category D^b F_→(π) via the spherical twists along the vanishing cycles. This action categorifies the classical Picard‑Lefschetz monodromy on the middle homology of the fiber. In the case of the Am‑1 Milnor fiber, the derived Fukaya‑Seidel category is equivalent to the derived category of representations of the Am‑1 quiver, and the braid group action corresponds to the derived autoequivalences given by reflection functors. For the quintic threefold, the Seidel representation provides a large subgroup of the autoequivalence group of D^b Coh(̂X) on the mirror side, matching the expected mirror symmetry of autoequivalences. The spherical twist functors also satisfy the braid relations: if two Lagrangian spheres intersect transversely in a single point, their twists satisfy the braid relation τ_S τ_T τ_S ≃ τ_T τ_S τ_T. This categorical braid group action is a powerful tool for constructing new objects in the Fukaya category and for proving generation results.

Higher Massey Products and the A∞-Structure

The A∞-category structure on the Fukaya category is encoded by a sequence of multilinear operations μ^k: CF^*(Lk-1,L_k) ⊗ … ⊗ CF^*(L0,L1) → CF^*(L0,L_k) of degree 2−k, satisfying the A∞-relations. For k=1 the operation is the Floer differential μ1 = ∂, for k=2 it is the product μ2 = ⋅, and for k≥3 the operations measure the failure of associativity of μ2 up to coherent homotopy. These higher operations are precisely the higher Massey products in Floer cohomology: given cohomology classes a1,…,a_k with vanishing lower Massey products, the k-th Massey product ⟨a1,…,a_k⟩ is a subset of HF^*(L0,L_k) represented by the output of μ^k on any lifts of the a_i to the chain level. The geometric definition of μ^k counts rigid pseudoholomorphic (k+1)-gons with boundary on the Lagrangians L0,…,L_k and with one output marked point and k input marked points ordered cyclically. The A∞-relations follow from the compactification of the 1-dimensional moduli spaces of such polygons, whose boundary strata correspond to splittings into two polygons, exactly mirroring the Stasheff associahedron relations. In the monotone or exact setting, the operations are defined over the Novikov field Λ, and the resulting A∞-category is strictly unital up to homotopy. A fundamental result of Fukaya–Oh–Ohta–Ono shows that when all Lagrangians are equipped with bounding cochains solving the Maurer–Cartan equation, the deformed differential μ1_b squares to zero and the higher operations μ^k_b satisfy the A∞-relations on the nose, yielding a genuine A∞-category whose cohomology category is the derived Fukaya category. The higher Massey products carry enumerative information: for instance, the operation μ3 on a triple of Lagrangian spheres in a Lefschetz fibration computes the triple Massey product that detects the non‑triviality of the spherical twist autoequivalence. In mirror symmetry, the higher products on the Fukaya side correspond under homological mirror symmetry to the higher Ext groups and the Yoneda product on the derived category of coherent sheaves, providing a categorical refinement of the classical mirror map.

The Open-Closed String Map and Hochschild Cohomology

The open‑closed string map OC: HH^*(F(X),F(X)) → QH^*(X) (or to the quantum cohomology of X) is a morphism from the Hochschild cohomology of the Fukaya A∞-category to the quantum cohomology ring of the symplectic manifold. On the chain level it is defined by counting pseudoholomorphic disks with one interior marked point mapping to a cycle in X and boundary on a single Lagrangian, or more generally by the closed‑open map CO: QH^*(X) → HH^*(F(X),F(X)) which inserts a closed string puncture into the disk. The composition OC ∘ CO is the quantum cup product with the fundamental class, and the pair (CO,OC) makes the Fukaya category into a Calabi‑Yau A∞-category of dimension n when X is a Calabi‑Yau n‑fold. The Hochschild cohomology HH^*(F(X)) carries a Gerstenhaber algebra structure, and the open‑closed map is a morphism of BV algebras when X is exact or monotone. A key theorem of Abouzaid (2010) states that for a Liouville domain X, the wrapped Fukaya category W(X) is generated by the cocore disks, and the open‑closed map induces an isomorphism HH^*(W(X)) ≅ SH^*(X) (symplectic cohomology). In the compact Calabi‑Yau case, the open‑closed map is conjecturally an isomorphism after suitable completion, and its injectivity is equivalent to the split‑generation of the Fukaya category by a given collection of Lagrangians. The closed‑open map CO sends the unit 1 ∈ QH0(X) to the identity element in HH0(F(X)), and the condition that 1 lies in the image of OC is precisely the condition that the Fukaya category is homologically smooth and proper. The open‑closed map also controls deformations of the Fukaya category: a class in HH2(F(X)) corresponds to a formal deformation of the A∞-structure, and its image under OC is the corresponding deformation of the symplectic form. This interplay underlies the proof of homological mirror symmetry for many toric varieties, where the open‑closed map on the A‑model is matched with the Hochschild‑Kostant‑Rosenberg isomorphism on the B‑model.

Worked Example: Computing a Higher Massey Product
Let L0, L1, L2 be three Lagrangian spheres in a Lefschetz fibration such that HF^*(L_i,Li+1) is one‑dimensional generated by a degree‑0 class a_i for i=0,1, and HF^*(L0,L2)=0. Show that the triple Massey product ⟨a0,a1,a2⟩ is well defined and compute its value in terms of the A∞-operation μ3.
Because HF^*(L0,L2)=0, the pairwise products a0⋅a1 and a1⋅a2 vanish in cohomology, so the triple Massey product is defined. Choose chain‑level representatives α_i ∈ CF0(L_i,Li+1) with ∂α_i=0 and [α_i]=a_i. Since a0⋅a1=0, there exists β0 ∈ CF-1(L0,L2) with ∂β0 = μ2(α0,α1). Similarly choose β1 with ∂β1 = μ2(α1,α2). The Massey product is represented by the cohomology class of μ2(α0,β1) + (-1)|α0| μ2(β0,α2) + μ3(α0,α1,α2). In the Fukaya category of a Lefschetz fibration, the only rigid polygons contributing to μ3 are the triangles with vertices on the three vanishing cycles; their count equals the intersection number of the corresponding thimbles. For the standard A2 Milnor fiber, μ3(α0,α1,α2) is a generator of HF0(L0,L2) (which is zero), so the Massey product vanishes. In higher A_m cases, the non‑vanishing of μ3 detects the non‑triviality of the braid group action on the derived Fukaya‑Seidel category.
Exam Strategy
When a question asks for the relationship between higher A∞-operations and Massey products, write the defining formula for μ^k in terms of polygon counts, then state the inductive construction of Massey products using the A∞-relations. Remember that the vanishing of lower Massey products is exactly the condition that the relevant μ^j for j<k have cohomologically trivial outputs. Mirror Symmetry for Toric Fano Varieties and the Landau-Ginzburg Model

For a toric Fano variety X, the mirror is a Landau‑Ginzburg model (Ŷ,W) where Ŷ = (ℂ^*)^n is the algebraic torus and W: Ŷ → ℂ is the superpotential given by the sum of monomials corresponding to the toric divisors. The Fukaya category of X (or more precisely the Fukaya‑Seidel category of the Lefschetz fibration W) is expected to be equivalent to the derived category of matrix factorizations MF(Ŷ,W). The superpotential W = Σi=1^m zv_i where v_i are the primitive generators of the rays of the fan of X. The critical points of W correspond to the eigenvalues of the quantum product by the anticanonical class, and the number of critical points equals the rank of the quantum cohomology. The A∞-structure on the Fukaya‑Seidel category is computed by counting pseudoholomorphic polygons in the fiber of W with boundary on vanishing cycles; these counts match the Yoneda products in MF(Ŷ,W) via the mirror map identifying the Novikov parameter with the complexified Kähler parameter. Abouzaid's generation criterion (2010) proves that the collection of Lagrangian thimbles associated to a distinguished basis of vanishing paths split‑generates the Fukaya‑Seidel category, and the open‑closed map identifies the Hochschild cohomology of this category with the Jacobian ring ℂ[z1±1,…,z_n±1]/(∂W). The mirror symmetry isomorphism of Frobenius manifolds identifies the quantum cohomology QH^*(X) with the Saito Frobenius structure on the Jacobian ring. For toric Fano surfaces, the equivalence of categories was established by Abouzaid–Fukaya–Oh–Ohta–Ono (2013) using family Floer theory and the SYZ transform. The Landau‑Ginzburg model also provides a natural setting for the closed‑open map: the unit in QH^*(X) corresponds to the residue class 1 in the Jacobian ring, and the condition that the unit lies in the image of the open‑closed map is equivalent to the non‑degeneracy of the superpotential (i.e., W has only isolated critical points). This framework extends to toric Calabi‑Yau varieties where the mirror is a Landau‑Ginzburg model on a non‑compact Calabi‑Yau manifold, and the wrapped Fukaya category of the mirror is equivalent to the derived category of coherent sheaves on the original toric variety.

Categorical Enumerative Invariants: Gromov-Witten and Donaldson-Thomas via Fukaya Categories

The Fukaya category provides a categorical framework for defining and computing enumerative invariants such as Gromov‑Witten invariants and Donaldson‑Thomas invariants. The open‑closed map OC: HH_*(F(X)) → QH_*(X) (on Hochschild homology) sends the fundamental class of a Lagrangian to the Gromov‑Witten invariant counting disks with boundary on that Lagrangian. More generally, the cyclic homology HC_*(F(X)) carries a Connes operator B, and the composition OC ∘ B recovers the closed‑string Gromov‑Witten potential. On the B‑model side, the Hochschild homology of the derived category of coherent sheaves is isomorphic to the Hodge cohomology ⊕ Hp,q(X), and the closed‑open map corresponds to the Mukai pairing. The categorical approach to Donaldson‑Thomas invariants uses the fact that the derived Fukaya category of a Calabi‑Yau 3‑fold is a 3‑Calabi‑Yau A∞-category; its numerical Grothendieck group carries a Euler form, and the Kontsevich‑Soibelman wall‑crossing formula for stability conditions on the Fukaya category reproduces the DT wall‑crossing. In the work of Joyce and others, the Fukaya category is equipped with a stability condition (Bridgeland stability) whose central charge is given by the period integrals of the holomorphic volume form. The DT invariants are then defined as the weighted Euler characteristics of the moduli spaces of stable objects in the Fukaya category, and the wall‑crossing formula follows from the associativity of the Hall algebra of the category. For local Calabi‑Yau geometries (e.g., the resolved conifold), the Fukaya category is equivalent to the derived category of representations of a quiver with potential, and the DT invariants coincide with the numerical DT invariants of the quiver computed via the motivic Hall algebra. The categorical viewpoint also clarifies the relationship between open and closed Gromov‑Witten invariants: the open invariants are the coefficients of the disk potential, which is the generating function of μ^k operations on a chosen Lagrangian, while the closed invariants are obtained by the open‑closed map applied to the cyclic homology class represented by the disk potential. This package has been used to prove the remodeling conjecture for toric Calabi‑Yau threefolds, where the higher genus Gromov‑Witten invariants are reconstructed from the spectral curve of the mirror Landau‑Ginzburg model via the Eynard‑Orantin topological recursion.

Fukaya Categories of Symplectic Manifolds with Boundary and Relative Fukaya Categories

When a symplectic manifold X has contact type boundary ∂X, one can define a relative Fukaya category whose objects are exact Lagrangian submanifolds with Legendrian boundary in ∂X, and whose morphism spaces are generated by intersection points in the interior together with Reeb chords on the boundary. The differential counts pseudoholomorphic disks with boundary on the Lagrangians and with corners at Reeb chords, satisfying Lagrangian boundary conditions. This construction, due to Ekholm–Etnyre–Sullivan and developed by Ganatra–Pardon–Shende, yields an A∞-category that is quasi‑equivalent to the wrapped Fukaya category of the completion of X. The relative Fukaya category fits into a long exact sequence relating the Fukaya category of the closed manifold obtained by capping off the boundary with the symplectic cohomology of the filling. In particular, for a Liouville domain X, the wrapped Fukaya category W(X) is generated by the cocore disks, and the relative Fukaya category of the completion X̂ is equivalent to the category of modules over the Chekanov‑Eliashberg DGA of the Legendrian link ∂X. The Legendrian contact homology DGA appears as the endomorphism algebra of the zero section in the relative Fukaya category. This perspective underlies the proof of the Arnold conjecture for Legendrian submanifolds and the classification of fillings of contact manifolds via the wrapped Fukaya category. The relative Fukaya category also provides a natural setting for the surgery formula: a Legendrian surgery on ∂X corresponds to attaching a Weinstein handle to X, and the effect on the wrapped Fukaya category is given by a categorical localization at the spherical object associated to the attaching sphere. This localization is precisely the spherical twist autoequivalence discussed in the context of Dehn twists. The relative theory is essential for the proof of homological mirror symmetry for log Calabi‑Yau pairs (X,D), where the mirror is a Landau‑Ginzburg model with a superpotential having poles along the divisor D. The Fukaya category of the complement XD is equivalent to the category of matrix factorizations of the superpotential, and the relative Fukaya category of (X,D) captures the monodromy of the superpotential around the poles.

Fukaya Categories with Bulk Deformations and the Novikov Ring

The standard Fukaya category F(X) of a compact symplectic manifold X is defined over the Novikov ring Λ = {Σi=0^∞ a_i Tλ_i mid a_i ∈ K, λ_i ∈ R≥ 0, λ_i → ∞}, where T is a formal variable tracking symplectic area. When Lagrangians are not exact, the A_∞-operations μ^k involve sums over pseudoholomorphic polygons weighted by Tω(u) where ω(u) is the symplectic area of the polygon u. The Novikov ring ensures convergence of these infinite sums by filtering by area. Bulk deformations, introduced by Fukaya–Oh–Ohta–Ono, extend this by allowing insertions of cohomology classes b ∈ H^*(X; Λ_+) at interior marked points of the polygons. The deformed operations μ^k_b count polygons with interior marked points constrained to cycles representing b, weighted by Tω(u) Π ev_j^*(b). The bulk-deformed A_∞-relations hold, and the resulting category F(X; b) is quasi-equivalent to the original when b is a coboundary. The Maurer–Cartan equation for a bounding cochain b on a Lagrangian L becomes Σk=0^∞ μ^k_b(b, …, b) = 0, and its solutions parametrize the A_∞-deformations of L. The potential function W^b_L is the generating function of μ0_b; critical points of W^b_L correspond to unobstructed bulk-deformed Lagrangians. This framework is essential for defining the Fukaya category of non-exact Lagrangians in monotone symplectic manifolds, where the minimal Chern number N ≥ 2 prevents sphere bubbling from obstructing the A_∞-relations. The bulk deformation parameter b also encodes the quantum cohomology ring QH^*(X) via the open-closed map: the image of the fundamental class [L] under the open-closed map OC: HH_*(F(X)) → QH^*(X) recovers the quantum product structure. In the monotone setting, the Novikov ring can be replaced by the universal Novikov field Λuniv or by working over C with a fixed valuation, but the area filtration remains crucial for defining the A_∞-structure rigorously.

The Nadler–Zaslow Correspondence and Microlocal Sheaf Theory

The Nadler–Zaslow theorem establishes an equivalence between the wrapped Fukaya category W(T^*M) of the cotangent bundle of a smooth manifold M and the derived category of constructible sheaves Sh_c(M) on M. Objects in W(T^*M) are exact Lagrangian submanifolds L ⊂ T^*M that are conical at infinity, i.e., invariant under the Liouville flow outside a compact set. The correspondence sends a Lagrangian L to its microlocal sheaf μ_L, a constructible complex whose singular support SS(μ_L) is contained in the Legendrian lift of L ∩ ∂_∞ T^*M in the contact boundary. Morphisms in W(T^*M) are given by wrapped Floer cohomology HW^*(L0, L1), which counts pseudoholomorphic strips with one end at a Hamiltonian chord and the other at an intersection point. On the sheaf side, morphisms are Hom^*(μL0, μL1) in the derived category. The equivalence is constructed via the microlocalization functor μ: Sh_c(M) → W(T^*M) and its inverse, the sheafification functor. A key technical tool is the Guillermou–Kashiwara–Schapira sheaf quantization of contact transformations, which identifies the monodromy of the wrapped Fukaya category with the monodromy of constructible sheaves under the action of the fundamental group of the space of contactomorphisms. For M = S1, the wrapped Fukaya category of T^*S1 ≅ S1 × R is generated by the zero section and the cotangent fiber, and the equivalence recovers the classical fact that Sh_c(S1) is generated by the constant sheaf and the skyscraper sheaf. For higher-dimensional M, the correspondence implies that the wrapped Fukaya category is invariant under Hamiltonian isotopies of the Liouville structure, and it provides a geometric interpretation of the A_∞-operations in terms of sheaf-theoretic convolution. The Nadler–Zaslow correspondence has been extended to singular Lagrangians by Nadler, to relative settings by Ganatra–Pardon–Shende, and to the partially wrapped setting by Sylvan, where one restricts to Lagrangians with prescribed asymptotic behavior at a subset of the contact boundary. This sheaf-theoretic perspective is indispensable for computations: the wrapped Floer cohomology of two cotangent fibers T^*q0M and T^*q1M is isomorphic to the singular cohomology of the based loop space H^*(Ωq0,q1M), and the A_∞-structure corresponds to the Pontryagin product on loops.

Worked Computation: Wrapped Floer Cohomology of Cotangent Fibers
Let M = R^n and consider the cotangent fibers L0 = T^*0R^n and L1 = T^*qR^n for q ≠ 0. Compute HW^*(L0, L1) using the Nadler–Zaslow correspondence and identify the A_∞-product.
Under the Nadler–Zaslow equivalence, L0 corresponds to the constant sheaf K_M and L1 corresponds to the skyscraper sheaf K_q shifted by n (since the fiber is Lagrangian of dimension n). The wrapped Floer cohomology HW^*(L0, L1) is isomorphic to Hom^*(K_M, K_q[n]) in the derived category of constructible sheaves. This Hom group is the stalk cohomology of K_M at q shifted by n, which is H^*(M, M ∖ {q})[n] ≅ K[-n][n] = K concentrated in degree 0. Thus HW^*(L0, L1) ≅ K in degree 0. The A_∞-product μ2: HW^*(L1, L2) ⊗ HW^*(L0, L1) → HW^*(L0, L2) corresponds under the equivalence to the composition of morphisms in the derived category, which is the usual composition of sheaf maps. For three fibers L0, L1, L2 at distinct points, the product is the identity map K ⊗ K → K. Higher products μ^k for k ≥ 3 vanish because the corresponding sheaf compositions are strictly associative. This matches the geometric fact that in T^*R^n, there are no non-constant pseudoholomorphic polygons with boundary on cotangent fibers, so all higher A_∞-operations vanish and the wrapped Fukaya category is formal.

When a question asks about the wrapped Fukaya category of a cotangent bundle, immediately translate to constructible sheaves via Nadler–Zaslow. The zero section corresponds to the constant sheaf; cotangent fibers correspond to skyscraper sheaves (shifted by dimension). Conormal bundles to submanifolds correspond to pushforwards of constant sheaves. The wrapped Floer cohomology HW^*(L0, L1) is the sheaf Hom Hom^*(F0, F1). The A_∞-structure is the Yoneda product. If the question involves a Legendrian link in the contact boundary, think of the Chekanov–Eliashberg DGA as the endomorphism algebra of the corresponding microlocal sheaf. This dictionary converts hard symplectic geometry computations into standard sheaf theory.

Lefschetz Fibrations, Vanishing Cycles, and the Seidel Functor

A Lefschetz fibration π: X → C is a proper symplectic map with isolated non-degenerate critical points, each modeled on (z1, …, z_n) ↦ z12 + … + z_n2. The regular fiber F = π-1(t) for a regular value t is a symplectic manifold with boundary, and the critical fibers contain a vanishing cycle V ⊂ F, a Lagrangian sphere that collapses at the critical point. Parallel transport along a path in the base avoiding critical values gives a symplectomorphism of the fiber, well-defined up to Hamiltonian isotopy. A distinguished basis of vanishing cycles V1, …, V_m is obtained by choosing paths from a basepoint to each critical value that intersect only at the basepoint. The ordered collection determines the monodromy of the fibration: the product of Dehn twists τV_m ° … ° τV1 is the monodromy around all critical values. Seidel constructed a functor from the Fukaya category of the total space F(X) to the Fukaya category of the fiber F(F) by associating to a Lagrangian L ⊂ X its intersection with the fiber, which is a Lagrangian in F with boundary on the vanishing cycles. More precisely, the Seidel functor S: F(X) → Tw F(F) lands in the category of twisted complexes over the fiber Fukaya category, because the intersection L ∩ F may have boundary on the vanishing cycles, which are objects in F(F). The functor is fully faithful when the vanishing cycles split-generate F(F). This is the basis of Seidel's proof of homological mirror symmetry for the quartic surface: the mirror is a Landau–Ginzburg model whose category of matrix factorizations is equivalent to the Fukaya category of the total space of a Lefschetz fibration with fiber a punctured torus. The vanishing cycles in this case are the A and B cycles of the torus, and their Dehn twists generate the mapping class group SL(2, Z). The Seidel functor intertwines the A_∞-operations on the total space with the A_∞-operations on the fiber twisted by the monodromy. In particular, the quantum product on QH^*(X) is recovered from the open-closed map applied to the Hochschild homology of the fiber category with the monodromy action. The Seidel functor also provides a categorical interpretation of the Picard–Lefschetz formula: the action of the monodromy on the Fukaya category of the fiber is given by the spherical twist autoequivalences associated to the vanishing cycles. This perspective generalizes to Lefschetz–Bott fibrations, where the critical locus is a submanifold and the vanishing cycles form a spherical fibration, and to the relative setting where the base has boundary and the Fukaya category of the total space is related to the wrapped Fukaya category of the fiber.

The Cardy Relation and the Closed-Open String Map in Topological Conformal Field Theory

The closed-open string map CO: QH^*(X) → HH^*(F(X), F(X)) and the open-closed string map OC: HH_*(F(X), F(X)) → QH^*(X) are the algebraic avatars of the sewing operations in topological conformal field theory. The Cardy relation, formulated by Costello and proved in the symplectic context by Ganatra, states that the composition OC ° CO: QH^*(X) → QH^*(X) is the quantum product with the fundamental class [X], i.e., the identity map. Equivalently, the pair (QH^*(X), F(X)) forms a Calabi–Yau A_∞-category with a cyclic inner product, and the Cardy condition is the statement that the closed sector is the Hochschild homology of the open sector. The map CO is defined by counting pseudoholomorphic disks with one interior marked point constrained to a cycle representing a class in QH^*(X) and boundary on a Lagrangian L; the output is a Hochschild cochain, i.e., a collection of maps CF^*(L0, L1) → CF^*(L0, L1) compatible with the A_∞-structure. The map OC counts disks with one interior marked point mapping to a cycle in X and boundary on a sequence of Lagrangians; the output is a class in QH^*(X). The Cardy relation implies that the quantum cohomology ring is recovered as the center of the Fukaya category: QH^*(X) ≅ Z(F(X)) = HH^*(F(X), F(X)) when F(X) is split-generated by a single object. For a general Fukaya category, the closed-open map is injective but not surjective; its image is the subring of quantum cohomology that acts non-trivially on the Fukaya category. The Cardy relation is the categorical shadow of the sewing axiom for the pair of pants: gluing two disks along a boundary interval corresponds to the composition of open-closed and closed-open maps. In the wrapped Fukaya category of a Liouville domain, the Cardy relation holds without split-generation assumptions because the wrapped category is generated by the cocore disks, and the closed sector is the symplectic cohomology SH^*(X). The relation OC ° CO = id on SH^*(X) is a theorem of Ganatra–Pardon–Shende and implies that the symplectic cohomology is the Hochschild homology of the wrapped Fukaya category. This result is the foundation of the sheaf-theoretic approach to symplectic cohomology via the Nadler–Zaslow correspondence, where SH^*(T^*M) is identified with the homology of the free loop space H_*(LM).

Fukaya Categories and Cluster Algebras from Triangulated Surfaces

The Fukaya category of a punctured surface S with boundary is intimately connected to the cluster algebra structure on the coordinate ring of the moduli space of GL_n-local systems on S. For n=2, the cluster algebra is of finite type if and only if S is a disk with at most 3 punctures, an annulus with at most 1 puncture, or a pair of pants. In general, the cluster variables correspond to arcs on S, and the exchange relations correspond to flips of triangulations. The wrapped Fukaya category W(T^*S) is equivalent to the category of modules over the Ginzburg dg-algebra associated to the quiver with potential coming from a triangulation of S. The objects are Lagrangian arcs (properly embedded intervals with endpoints on the boundary or punctures), and the morphisms are given by wrapped Floer cohomology, which counts intersections of arcs with winding at the punctures. The A_∞-structure is determined by the combinatorics of the triangulation: the product μ2 corresponds to concatenation of arcs, and higher products μ^k for k ≥ 3 correspond to polygons in the triangulation. The cluster mutation at a vertex of the quiver corresponds to the spherical twist autoequivalence around the corresponding Lagrangian arc. This equivalence, established by Labardini-Fragoso, Haiden–Katzarkov–Kontsevich, and others, provides a concrete combinatorial model for the Fukaya category of surfaces. The cluster algebra structure on the Hochschild cohomology HH^*(W(T^*S)) is the quantum cluster algebra of the surface, and the cluster variables are the trace functions of the corresponding local systems. For higher rank n > 2, the Fukaya category of the moduli space of GL_n-local systems (the character variety) is related to the cluster algebra structure on the coordinate ring via the Fock–Goncharov duality. The wrapped Fukaya category of the cotangent bundle of the moduli space is equivalent to the category of constructible sheaves on the moduli space, and the cluster structure arises from the stratification by the Harder–Narasimhan filtration. This circle of ideas connects the Fukaya category to representation theory, Teichmüller theory, and the geometric Langlands program. In particular, the spherical twists in the Fukaya category generate the mapping class group action on the cluster algebra, and the cluster modular group is identified with the autoequivalence group of the Fukaya category.

Graded Lagrangians and the Grading Shift in the Fukaya Category

A graded Lagrangian submanifold in a symplectic manifold X equipped with a trivialization of the canonical bundle KX is a Lagrangian L together with a lift of the phase map L → S1 to the real line. This lift, called a grading, assigns to each point of L a real number modulo 2 that records the argument of the complex volume form restricted to L. The existence of a grading is equivalent to the vanishing of the Maslov class of L in H1(L; Z). When L is graded, the Floer cohomology groups HF*(L0, L1) acquire an absolute Z-grading (or Z/2-grading if only a relative grading is fixed) determined by the difference of the gradings at intersection points. The grading shift functor [k] acts on objects by adding k to the grading, and on morphisms by shifting the cohomological degree by k. In the A∞-structure, the higher products μk have degree 2−k with respect to this grading, a fact that follows from the index formula for pseudoholomorphic polygons. The grading shift also appears in the spherical twist formula: for a spherical object S of dimension n, the twist TS(X) = Cone(Hom*(S, X) ⊗ S → X) involves a shift of the Hom complex by n to preserve the Calabi–Yau property. If the grading is not respected, the A∞-relations fail because the signs coming from the orientation of moduli spaces no longer match the Koszul sign rule. In practice, one often works with Z-graded Fukaya categories over the Novikov field, where the grading is defined modulo the minimal Maslov number of the ambient manifold. For monotone symplectic manifolds, the grading can be taken modulo 2N where N is the minimal Chern number. The choice of grading affects the split-generation criteria: a collection of graded Lagrangians split-generates the graded Fukaya category if and only if their underlying ungraded Lagrangians split-generate the ungraded category and the gradings are compatible with the grading on the diagonal bimodule. This compatibility is checked by verifying that the open-closed string map hits the identity in Hochschild cohomology with the correct degree shift.

Spin Structures and Orientation Data for Fukaya Categories

The construction of the Fukaya category over coefficient rings other than Z/2 requires a coherent choice of orientations for the moduli spaces of pseudoholomorphic polygons with boundary on a collection of Lagrangians. An orientation of the determinant line of the linearized Cauchy–Riemann operator at a solution is equivalent to a trivialization of the orientation bundle of the moduli space. For a Lagrangian L, a spin structure (or more generally a relative spin structure with respect to the ambient manifold) provides a canonical trivialization of the determinant line of the index bundle for disks with boundary on L. When L is not spin, one can still define the Fukaya category over Z/2, but the A∞-operations over Z or Q are obstructed by the second Stiefel–Whitney class w2(L). In the relative setting, a relative spin structure for a pair (L, X) is a lift of the structure group of TL ⊕ TX|L from SO to Spin. This data allows one to orient the moduli spaces of polygons with multiple boundary components, each mapping to a different Lagrangian, by comparing the given relative spin structures to a fixed reference orientation on the universal curve. The resulting signs satisfy the A∞-relations because the gluing of polygons corresponds to the concatenation of the orientation data, which is associative up to the Koszul sign rule. For non-orientable Lagrangians, one uses Pin− structures instead of spin structures; the orientation lines are then defined over Z/2, and the Fukaya category can be defined over Z/2 with a twisted coefficient system. The choice of spin structure is not unique: different choices differ by an element of H1(L; Z/2), and this ambiguity translates into an autoequivalence of the Fukaya category given by tensoring with a flat Z/2-local system. In the presence of bulk deformations, the orientation data must also be compatible with the choice of fundamental chain for the ambient manifold, which is part of the data of a cyclic A∞-structure. The cyclic symmetry of the A∞-operations is then equivalent to the existence of a non-degenerate pairing on morphism spaces that is invariant under the cyclic permutation of inputs, a property that relies crucially on the chosen orientation data.

Worked Example: Grading Shift in a Spherical Twist
Let S be a graded spherical Lagrangian sphere of dimension 2 in a Calabi–Yau surface X. The graded Floer cohomology HF*(S, S) is isomorphic to H*(S2; Z) with degrees shifted by the grading of S. Compute the effect of the spherical twist TS on a graded Lagrangian L with HF0(S, L) = Z and HF1(S, L) = 0. Determine the grading of the cone TS(L).
The spherical twist is defined as the mapping cone of the evaluation map ev: HF*(S, L) ⊗ S → L. Since S has dimension 2, the evaluation map has degree 0. The Hom complex HF*(S, L) is concentrated in degree 0, so the tensor product HF0(S, L) ⊗ S sits in degree equal to the grading of S. The cone shifts the source by +1, so the summand coming from S appears in degree (grading of S) + 1. The target L sits in its original grading. Therefore TS(L) is a twisted complex with two summands: L in its original grading and S[1] (i.e. S shifted by +1). The resulting object is graded provided the grading of S is chosen so that the evaluation map is degree 0, which is exactly the condition that the grading of S differs from the grading of L by the Maslov index of the intersection points. In this example the Maslov index is 0, so the grading shift is consistent.
Exam Strategy
When a question asks for the degree of a morphism in a graded Fukaya category, always trace the grading shift through the definition of the A∞-operations: μk has degree 2−k. For spherical twists, remember the shift by the dimension of the spherical object. Write down the grading of each summand in a twisted complex explicitly; this avoids sign errors in the A∞-relations.

Partially Wrapped Fukaya Categories and Sectorial Stops

A partially wrapped Fukaya category is defined for a Liouville sector, which is a Liouville manifold with a prescribed stop on its ideal contact boundary. The stop is a closed Legendrian submanifold (or a more general isotropic subset) that prevents Reeb chords from wrapping past it. Objects are exact Lagrangian branes that are disjoint from the stop and are allowed to wrap only in the complement of the stop. Morphism complexes are generated by Reeb chords that do not intersect the stop, together with Hamiltonian chords in the interior. The A∞-operations count pseudoholomorphic polygons whose boundary arcs are allowed to run along the stop only in the direction prescribed by the sectorial condition. This construction interpolates between the compact Fukaya category (stop = entire boundary) and the fully wrapped Fukaya category (stop = ∅). Sectorial stops arise naturally in mirror symmetry for log Calabi–Yau varieties: the mirror of a log Calabi–Yau pair (Y, D) is a Landau–Ginzburg model (X, W) where the stop is the fiber of W at infinity. The partially wrapped Fukaya category of the sector is then equivalent to the category of matrix factorizations of W with support away from the stop. The localization functor from the partially wrapped category to the fully wrapped category is induced by forgetting the stop; it is a quotient by the subcategory generated by Lagrangians that intersect the stop. This functor admits a right adjoint given by the inclusion of the stop as a Lagrangian skeleton. The existence of a stop also allows one to define a relative Fukaya category for a pair (X, Λ) where Λ is a Legendrian stop, and the resulting category carries a natural action of the monoid of Reeb flows that preserve the stop. In the setting of cluster algebras from surfaces, the stop corresponds to a collection of punctures, and the partially wrapped category recovers the cluster category of the surface. The sectorial formalism also provides a clean framework for the gluing of Fukaya categories along common stops, which underlies the proof of the Künneth theorem for wrapped Fukaya categories and the construction of the Fukaya category of a Weinstein manifold from its Weinstein sectors.

Fukaya Categories of Weinstein Manifolds and Symplectic Cohomology

A Weinstein manifold is a Liouville manifold equipped with an exhausting Morse function that is plurisubharmonic with respect to the complex structure induced by the Liouville flow. The wrapped Fukaya category W(X) of a Weinstein manifold X is defined using Lagrangians that are conical at infinity with respect to the Liouville flow. The symplectic cohomology SH*(X) is the Hamiltonian Floer cohomology of a quadratic Hamiltonian that grows linearly at infinity; it can be computed as the direct limit of Floer cohomology groups for Hamiltonians with increasing slope. A fundamental theorem of Ganatra–Pardon–Shende identifies the Hochschild homology of W(X) with SH*(X) and the Hochschild cohomology with the symplectic cohomology of the product X × X relative to the diagonal. The Viterbo functor gives a map from the compact Fukaya category of a Liouville domain to the wrapped Fukaya category of its completion, and on Hochschild homology this map coincides with the Viterbo transfer map SH*(X) → H*+n(X, ∂X). For a Weinstein manifold that is the cotangent bundle T*M of a closed manifold M, the wrapped Fukaya category is equivalent to the category of constructible sheaves on M (Nadler–Zaslow), and symplectic cohomology is isomorphic to the homology of the free loop space of M. The symplectic cohomology ring carries a Batalin–Vilkovisky algebra structure that corresponds to the string topology operations on the loop space. In mirror symmetry, the symplectic cohomology of a Weinstein manifold is mirror to the Hochschild cohomology of the mirror category of coherent sheaves, and the closed-open string map SH*(X) → HH*(W(X)) is mirror to the Hochschild–Kostant–Rosenberg isomorphism. The generation criterion of Abouzaid says that a collection of Lagrangians split-generates W(X) if and only if the open-closed map hits the unit in SH*(X). This criterion is often checked by computing the symplectic cohomology via a Morse–Bott model on the contact boundary and verifying that the Reeb orbits corresponding to the Lagrangians generate the homology of the contact manifold.

Fukaya Categories and Mirror Symmetry for Log Calabi–Yau Varieties

A log Calabi–Yau variety is a pair (Y, D) where Y is a smooth algebraic variety and D is a reduced divisor with simple normal crossings such that the canonical bundle KY ⊗ O(D) is trivial. The mirror of a log Calabi–Yau pair is a Landau–Ginzburg model (X, W) where X is a non-compact Calabi–Yau manifold and W: X → C is a proper holomorphic function with critical points corresponding to the strata of D. Homological mirror symmetry in this setting predicts an equivalence between the derived Fukaya–Seidel category FS(X, W) of vanishing cycles for W and the derived category of coherent sheaves on Y with support on D. The Fukaya–Seidel category is a directed A∞-category whose objects are graded Lagrangian thimbles (Lefschetz thimbles) associated to a choice of distinguished basis of vanishing paths. The morphism spaces are given by Floer cohomology of thimbles, which can be computed combinatorially from the intersection pattern of the vanishing cycles in a generic fiber. The A∞-structure is determined by counts of pseudoholomorphic polygons with boundary on the thimbles and with one interior puncture mapping to a critical point of W. On the B-model side, the category of coherent sheaves on Y with support on D is equivalent to the category of matrix factorizations of the superpotential W on the mirror X. The equivalence is constructed by associating to each vanishing cycle a spherical object in the derived category, and the spherical twists around these objects generate the autoequivalence group corresponding to the monodromy of the fibration W. The log Calabi–Yau condition ensures that the superpotential has a well-defined critical locus and that the Fukaya–Seidel category is Calabi–Yau of dimension n = dimC Y. In the case where Y is a toric variety and D is the toric boundary, the mirror is the Hori–Vafa Landau–Ginzburg model, and the equivalence reduces to the statement that the wrapped Fukaya category of the mirror is equivalent to the derived category of coherent sheaves on Y. For more general log Calabi–Yau pairs, the mirror is constructed via the Gross–Siebert program using tropical geometry, and the Fukaya category of the mirror is built from a Lagrangian torus fibration with singular fibers. The sectorial stop on the mirror corresponds to the divisor D, and the partially wrapped Fukaya category of the sector is equivalent to the category of coherent sheaves on Y with support on D. This provides a geometric realization of the cluster algebra structure on the coordinate ring of the moduli space of local systems on Y D.

  • Assuming every Lagrangian admits a spin structure; non-spin Lagrangians force coefficients to be Z/2 or require a twisted coefficient system.
  • Treating the stop in a partially wrapped category as a Lagrangian submanifold rather than a Legendrian subset of the ideal boundary.
  • Believing the Viterbo functor is fully faithful for all Liouville domains; it is fully faithful only when the domain is subcritical or the stop is empty.

Lagrangian Correspondences and Functoriality in the Fukaya Category

A Lagrangian correspondence from a symplectic manifold X to a symplectic manifold Y is a Lagrangian submanifold L ⊂ X⁻ × Y, where X⁻ denotes X with the opposite symplectic form. Such correspondences serve as morphisms in a 2‑category whose objects are symplectic manifolds and whose 1‑morphisms are Lagrangian correspondences. Composition of correspondences L₁₂ ⊂ X₁⁻ × X₂ and L₂₃ ⊂ X₂⁻ × X₃ is defined by the fiber product L₁₂ ×X₂ L₂₃, which is a Lagrangian correspondence from X₁ to X₃ after a Hamiltonian perturbation to achieve transversality. Wehrheim and Woodward developed a quilted Floer theory that assigns to each correspondence an A∞‑functor Fuk(X) → Fuk(Y) on the level of Fukaya categories. The functor is constructed by counting pseudoholomorphic quilts — maps from a strip with a seam mapping to the correspondence — with boundary conditions given by Lagrangians in X and Y. The A∞‑relations follow from the compactification of the moduli spaces of quilts, where boundary strata correspond to breaking of quilted strips and to the A∞‑operations in the source and target categories. A key result is that the identity correspondence (the diagonal Δ ⊂ X⁻ × X) induces the identity functor, and that geometric composition of correspondences is homotopic to composition of the associated functors. This functoriality underlies the statement that the Fukaya category is a symplectic invariant: a symplectomorphism φ : X → Y gives the graph correspondence Γφ ⊂ X⁻ × Y, and the induced functor is an A∞‑equivalence. The theory also extends to correspondences with stops, yielding functors between partially wrapped Fukaya categories. In mirror symmetry, a Lagrangian correspondence between mirror pairs corresponds to a Fourier–Mukai kernel on the B‑model side, providing a geometric realization of derived equivalences.

Lagrangian Cobordisms and Induced A∞‑Functors

A Lagrangian cobordism between two Lagrangian submanifolds L₀, L₁ in a symplectic manifold X is a Lagrangian submanifold V ⊂ X × ℂ (or X × ℝ²) whose ends are cylindrical over L₀ and L₁ at the negative and positive ends of the ℂ factor. Biran and Cornea showed that an exact Lagrangian cobordism induces an A∞‑functor between the Fukaya categories of the ends, and more generally a morphism in the derived Fukaya category. The construction uses a stretched neck limit: one considers Floer complexes for Lagrangians in X with a long cylindrical region modeled on the cobordism, and counts pseudoholomorphic curves with boundary on the Lagrangians and with one interior puncture mapping to the cobordism. The resulting map on morphism spaces satisfies the A∞‑functor equations because the boundary of the 1‑dimensional moduli space of such curves corresponds to the A∞‑relations. A fundamental exact triangle appears when the cobordism is obtained by Lagrangian surgery: if L₁ is the result of surgery on L₀ along a transverse intersection point p, then there is an exact triangle L₀ → L₁ → C → L₀[1] in the derived Fukaya category, where C is the mapping cone of the morphism represented by p. This triangle is the categorical shadow of the surgery exact sequence in symplectic homology. Cobordisms also provide a geometric realization of Dehn twists: the Lagrangian suspension of a Dehn twist along a sphere S is a cobordism from the identity to the twist, and the induced functor is the spherical twist autoequivalence. The functoriality of cobordisms is compatible with composition: gluing cobordisms end‑to‑end corresponds to composition of the associated A∞‑functors up to homotopy. This framework has been extended to immersed Lagrangian cobordisms and to cobordisms with stops, linking the wrapped Fukaya category to the partially wrapped category via the Viterbo restriction functor.

Worked Example: Cobordism Exact Triangle
Let L₀ and L₁ be exact Lagrangians in a Liouville domain X intersecting transversely at a single point p. Let L₁ be obtained from L₀ by Polterovich surgery at p. Describe the exact triangle in the derived Fukaya category DπFuk(X) induced by the surgery cobordism, and identify the morphism L₀ → L₁.
The surgery cobordism V ⊂ X × ℂ has negative end L₀ and positive end L₁. The induced A∞‑functor yields a morphism f ∈ CF⁰(L₀, L₁) represented by the intersection point p. The mapping cone Cone(f) is an object in the derived category fitting into the exact triangle L₀ → L₁ → Cone(f) → L₀[1]. In this setting Cone(f) is quasi‑isomorphic to the Lagrangian L₀ with a local system determined by the surgery parameter; when the parameter is generic the cone is a new Lagrangian L₂ which is the result of a second surgery. The triangle reflects the fact that the surgery cobordism is a Lagrangian correspondence whose associated functor is the cone construction.

Lagrangian Surgery and the Cone Construction in the Fukaya Category

Polterovich’s Lagrangian surgery replaces a transverse intersection point p of two exact Lagrangians L₀, L₁ in a symplectic manifold X by a small neck, producing a new exact Lagrangian L₁#pL₀ (often denoted L₀ ∪p L₁). Seidel proved that in the derived Fukaya category DπFuk(X) there is an exact triangle L₀ → L₁ → L₀ ∪p L₁ → L₀[1], where the first map is the morphism represented by p in Floer cohomology HF⁰(L₀, L₁). The triangle is a categorical incarnation of the surgery exact sequence in symplectic homology and of the mutation formula in cluster algebras arising from triangulated surfaces. The construction of the triangle uses the surgery cobordism described in the previous section: the cobordism gives an A∞‑functor whose mapping cone is quasi‑isomorphic to the surgered Lagrangian. The parameter of the surgery (the size of the neck) corresponds to the choice of a bounding cochain on the cone; varying the parameter yields a family of objects related by gauge transformations. When L₀ and L₁ are graded and spin, the surgery inherits a grading and a spin structure determined by the grading of p and the relative spin structures. The cone construction is functorial with respect to Lagrangian correspondences: a correspondence from X to Y sends the surgery triangle in X to a surgery triangle in Y. In mirror symmetry for toric Fano varieties, Lagrangian surgery corresponds to mutation of the associated cluster variables, and the exact triangle matches the exchange relation in the cluster algebra. A common pitfall is to confuse the direction of the morphism: the triangle reads L₀ → L₁ → L₀#pL₁ → L₀[1], not the reverse. The shift [1] is the cohomological shift in the derived category, not the grading shift of the Lagrangian.

Immersed Lagrangians and the Extended Fukaya Category

Akaho and Joyce extended Floer theory to immersed Lagrangians with clean self‑intersections. An immersed Lagrangian L : L̃ → X is a Lagrangian immersion with transverse double points. The Floer complex CF*(L, L) is generated by the self‑intersection points, each equipped with a degree determined by the Conley–Zehnder index of the corresponding Reeb chord in the normal bundle. The differential counts pseudoholomorphic polygons with boundary on L and with corners at the self‑intersection points; the A∞‑operations are defined analogously. A crucial new feature is that the immersion may have a non‑trivial Maslov class, and the minimal Maslov number of the immersion controls the grading. To obtain a well‑defined A∞‑category one must add bounding cochains — solutions of the Maurer–Cartan equation in the Floer complex — which deform the differential and higher products. The extended Fukaya category includes both embedded and immersed Lagrangians as objects; morphisms between an immersed and an embedded Lagrangian are defined by counting polygons with one boundary component on the immersed Lagrangian and the other on the embedded one. This theory is essential for mirror symmetry of orbifolds and for the homological mirror symmetry of weighted projective spaces, where the mirror Landau–Ginzburg model has critical points corresponding to immersed Lagrangian thimbles. The immersed Fukaya category also appears in the study of Lagrangian correspondences: the composition of two correspondences often yields an immersed Lagrangian, and the quilted Floer theory of Wehrheim–Woodward naturally produces immersed objects. A subtle point is that the self‑intersection points contribute to the obstruction class m₀; a bounding cochain can cancel this obstruction only if the immersion is unobstructed in the sense of Fukaya–Oh–Ohta–Ono. The extended category is not strictly larger than the embedded one: every immersed Lagrangian is quasi‑isomorphic to a formal direct sum of embedded Lagrangians (the “resolution” by surgery at the double points), but the immersed description is often more computable.

Monotone Fukaya Categories and Minimal Maslov Number

A symplectic manifold X is monotone if the symplectic form ω and the first Chern class c₁ are proportional on π₂(X): [ω] = λ c₁ for some λ > 0. A Lagrangian L ⊂ X is monotone if the area and Maslov index of any disk with boundary on L satisfy the same proportionality. The minimal Maslov number NL is the positive generator of the image of the Maslov index homomorphism π₂(X, L) → ℤ. For monotone Lagrangians with NL ≥ 2, the Floer differential can be defined over the Novikov ring with a single formal variable q of degree 2, because all holomorphic disks have area a multiple of a fixed constant. Oh constructed the monotone Fukaya category Fukmon(X) whose objects are monotone Lagrangians with NL ≥ 2 and whose morphism spaces are Floer cohomology groups with coefficients in the Novikov field Λ = ℂ((q)). The A∞‑operations count pseudoholomorphic polygons with a weight qA/λ where A is the symplectic area. The monotone condition guarantees that the energy of a polygon is determined by its Maslov index, so the q‑adic valuation provides a filtration compatible with the A∞‑relations. A key difference from the exact case is the presence of a non‑zero curvature term m₀ ∈ CF²(L, L) coming from Maslov‑index‑2 disks; this curvature is a multiple of the fundamental class of L and equals the quantum cohomology class of the point. The obstruction theory for bounding cochains in the monotone setting is governed by the potential function W = m₀, and the critical points of W correspond to objects of the Fukaya category. The monotone Fukaya category is closely related to the quantum cohomology ring QH*(X) via the closed‑open string map; for a monotone symplectic manifold the split‑generation criterion of Abouzaid still holds, but one must work over the Novikov field rather than the universal Novikov ring. When NL = 1, Maslov‑index‑1 disks appear and the Floer differential may not square to zero without additional data (e.g., a spin structure and a choice of orientation). The monotone Fukaya category of ℂPⁿ with the Clifford torus as a generator recovers the derived category of coherent sheaves on the mirror Landau–Ginzburg model, illustrating homological mirror symmetry in the monotone setting.

  • Treating a Lagrangian correspondence as an ordinary Lagrangian in the product; it is a morphism in a 2‑category and its composition uses fiber products, not ordinary intersection.
  • Assuming every Lagrangian cobordism induces a functor on the wrapped Fukaya category; the Viterbo restriction functor is fully faithful only for subcritical domains or empty stops.
  • Neglecting the bounding cochain when working with immersed Lagrangians; without a solution of the Maurer–Cartan equation the A∞‑structure is not well defined.
  • Mixing the minimal Maslov number of a monotone Lagrangian with the grading shift in the spherical twist; the shift is by the dimension of the object, not by NL.
  • Believing the monotone Fukaya category is defined over the universal Novikov ring; it requires the specialized Novikov field ℂ((q)) because all disk areas are multiples of a fixed constant.
  • Overlooking that the surgery parameter corresponds to a bounding cochain on the mapping cone; varying the parameter changes the object only up to gauge equivalence.

Equivariant Fukaya Categories and Hamiltonian Group Actions

When a compact Lie group G acts on a symplectic manifold X by Hamiltonian symplectomorphisms, the ordinary Fukaya category does not retain the symmetry. The equivariant Fukaya category FukG(X) is constructed by enriching the Floer chain complexes with the group action: objects are G-equivariant Lagrangian branes (Lagrangian submanifolds equipped with a lift of the G-action to the grading and spin structures), and morphism spaces are the G-equivariant Floer cohomology groups HFG*(L₀,L₁). The A∞-operations count G-invariant pseudoholomorphic polygons, which can be organized using the Borel construction XG = EG ×G X. This yields a category that is naturally a module over the equivariant quantum cohomology QHG*(X). A fundamental result of Seidel shows that for a Hamiltonian torus action the equivariant Fukaya category is quasi‑equivalent to the Fukaya category of the symplectic quotient X//G after a suitable localization at the equivariant parameters. The localization theorem identifies the equivariant Floer differential with the ordinary differential on the quotient plus correction terms coming from the moment map. In practice, one often works with the G-equivariant Novikov ring ΛG where the formal variables record both symplectic area and the weights of the G-action. The equivariant closed‑open string map QHG*(X) → HH*(FukG(X)) is a ring homomorphism that plays the same role as its non‑equivariant counterpart, and its image controls split‑generation in the equivariant setting. Applications include the computation of Fukaya categories of toric Fano varieties via the mirror Landau–Ginzburg model with a torus action, and the study of symplectic cohomology of Hamiltonian G-manifolds through the equivariant Viterbo functor.

Fukaya Categories with Non‑Trivial Local Systems and Higher Rank Branes

In the standard Fukaya category objects are Lagrangian submanifolds equipped with a rank‑one local system (a flat U(1) connection). To capture richer geometric data one allows higher rank flat vector bundles E → L with structure group GL(r,ℂ) (or a compact form). The resulting A∞-category Fuk(X;ℒ) has morphism spaces CF*(L₀,E₀; L₁,E₁) = CF*(L₀,L₁) ⊗ Hom(E₀|L₀∩L₁, E₁|L₀∩L₁). The A∞-operations incorporate parallel transport of the local systems along the boundary edges of pseudoholomorphic polygons. The curvature term m₀ now takes values in CF²(L,E) ⊗ End(E) and the Maurer–Cartan equation becomes a matrix‑valued equation. A key theorem of Abouzaid and Fukaya–Oh–Ohta–Ono states that if (L,E) is an object with a bounding cochain solving the Maurer–Cartan equation, then the deformed Floer cohomology HF*((L,E), (L,E)) is isomorphic to the cohomology of L with coefficients in the endomorphism bundle End(E). This identification underpins the generation criterion: a collection of branes with local systems split‑generates the Fukaya category if and only if their endomorphism algebras generate the Hochschild cohomology. Higher rank branes appear naturally in mirror symmetry for non‑abelian gauge theories; on the mirror side they correspond to vector bundles or complexes of coherent sheaves of higher rank. The extended category also admits a natural action of the derived category of representations of the fundamental group of L, linking it to the representation theory of the Lagrangian skeleton.

Technique
Explain how a rank‑two local system on a Lagrangian torus in a symplectic four‑manifold modifies the Floer differential and the obstruction class m₀.
A rank‑two local system E on a torus L ≅ T² is given by a pair of commuting matrices (A,B) ∈ GL(2,ℂ)² representing holonomies around the two cycles. The Floer complex CF*(L,E; L,E) is the tensor product of the ordinary Morse complex of L with End(E) ≅ Mat₂(ℂ). The differential m₁ acts as m₁ ⊗ id + id ⊗ δ where δ is the covariant derivative along gradient flow lines; because E is flat, δ = 0 on constant paths, so m₁ reduces to the ordinary Morse differential tensored with the identity on End(E). The obstruction class m₀ receives contributions from Maslov‑index‑two disks with boundary on L. Each such disk u contributes a term Hol∂u(E) ⊗ [pt] where Hol∂u(E) is the holonomy of E around the boundary loop. Summing over all disks yields m₀ = (∑ qA(u) Hol∂u(E)) ⊗ [pt]. If the holonomies are generic, m₀ is a non‑scalar matrix; a bounding cochain b ∈ CF¹(L,E) ⊗ End(E) must satisfy the matrix Maurer–Cartan equation m₀ + m₁(b) + m₂(b,b) + … = 0. Solving this equation deforms the differential to m₁^b = m₁ + [m₂(b,·)] + … and the resulting Floer cohomology is isomorphic to H*(L; End(E)).
Exam‑day strategy
When a question asks for the effect of a higher rank local system, first write the tensor product decomposition of the Floer complex, then identify which A∞-operations acquire matrix‑valued holonomy factors. Remember that flatness kills the covariant derivative on constant paths, so the only new contributions appear in m₀ and higher mk with k ≥ 2. Practice computing m₀ for a torus with diagonal holonomies; the eigenvalues of the resulting matrix are the critical points of the deformed superpotential.

Curved A∞‑Structures and the Fukaya Category of Non‑Exact Lagrangians

For a non‑exact symplectic manifold X (or for Lagrangians with non‑zero symplectic area disks) the A∞-operations do not satisfy the strict A∞-relations; instead they satisfy the curved A∞-relations where the curvature term m₀ ∈ CF²(L,L) is non‑zero. A curved A∞-category has objects (L,b) consisting of a Lagrangian L together with a bounding cochain b ∈ CF¹(L,L) ⊗ Λ₊ solving the Maurer–Cartan equation ∑ mk(b,…,b) = 0. The deformed differential m₁^b(x) = ∑ mk+1(b,…,b,x) then squares to zero, and the resulting Floer cohomology HF*((L,b),(L,b)) is well defined. The curvature m₀ is a sum over Maslov‑index‑two pseudoholomorphic disks weighted by their symplectic area; in the monotone case it is a multiple of the fundamental class and equals the quantum cohomology class of a point. The space of solutions of the Maurer–Cartan equation modulo gauge equivalence is the critical locus of the Landau–Ginzburg superpotential W(b) = ∑ (1/(k+1)) ⟨mk(b,…,b), b⟩. This perspective, developed by Fukaya–Oh–Ohta–Ono and by Seidel, shows that the Fukaya category of a non‑exact symplectic manifold is a category of matrix factorizations of W when W is a scalar. For general W taking values in CF²(L,L) one obtains a curved A∞-category that is quasi‑equivalent to the category of curved matrix factorizations. The closed‑open string map QH*(X) → HH*(Fuk(X)) factors through the Jacobian ring of W, providing a powerful computational tool. In the monotone setting the Novikov field ℂ((q)) replaces the universal Novikov ring because all disk areas are integer multiples of a fixed constant, simplifying the convergence analysis.

Fukaya Categories of Symplectic Manifolds with Corners and Relative Fukaya Categories

Symplectic manifolds with corners arise naturally as Liouville sectors, as the total spaces of Lefschetz fibrations over a polygon, or as the symplectic reductions of Hamiltonian torus actions with non‑free moment maps. A corner of codimension k is locally modeled on (ℝ₊)ᵏ × ℝ2n−k with the standard symplectic form. The Fukaya category Fuk(X) for such a space is defined by allowing Lagrangians that meet the boundary strata transversely and are conical near the corners. The morphism spaces are defined using Floer theory with moving boundary conditions: the boundary of a pseudoholomorphic polygon is allowed to slide along the corner strata according to a prescribed sectorial stop. This leads to the notion of a partially wrapped Fukaya category with stops on the corner strata, developed by Ganatra–Pardon–Shende. The relative Fukaya category Fuk(X, D) for a divisor D (or a collection of boundary components) is the subcategory of objects disjoint from D; its wrapped version is obtained by allowing wrapping only in directions transverse to D. A fundamental result is the sectorial gluing formula: if X = X₁ ∪Y X₂ is a decomposition along a contact hypersurface Y, then the wrapped Fukaya category of X is the categorical pushout of the wrapped Fukaya categories of X₁ and X₂ over the partially wrapped category of Y. This gluing underlies the proof of homological mirror symmetry for log Calabi–Yau varieties and for cluster varieties from triangulated surfaces. The corner structure also enters the definition of the A∞-operations: the count of polygons with corners on the boundary strata contributes higher A∞-operations that encode the monodromy of the symplectic connection around the corners.

Fukaya Categories and the Categorical Crepant Resolution Conjecture

The categorical crepant resolution conjecture predicts an equivalence between the derived Fukaya category of a symplectic orbifold [X/G] (or of a symplectic manifold with a finite group action) and the derived category of coherent sheaves on a crepant resolution Ỹ → X/G. On the symplectic side one works with the G-equivariant Fukaya category FukG(X) and then takes the G-invariant part, or equivalently the Fukaya category of the orbifold [X/G] defined via twisted sectors. The objects are Lagrangian branes in the untwisted sector together with branes supported on the inertia orbifold components. The A∞-operations count orbifold pseudoholomorphic polygons, i.e., maps from orbifold Riemann surfaces with marked points mapping to twisted sectors. The curvature term m₀ receives contributions from disks with boundary on a twisted sector; these are weighted by the age grading. The conjecture, proved in many toric cases by Abouzaid–Fukaya–Oh–Ohta–Ono and by Sheridan, identifies the orbifold Fukaya category with the derived category of the crepant resolution via a Fourier–Mukai transform whose kernel is the universal family of Lagrangian thimbles. In the mirror Landau–Ginzburg model the crepant resolution corresponds to a partial smoothing of the superpotential, and the equivalence matches the category of matrix factorizations of the orbifold superpotential with the category of singularities of the smoothed potential. This framework extends to non‑abelian quotients and to the derived category of coherent sheaves on the GIT quotient, providing a categorical interpretation of the classical crepant resolution conjecture of Reid and Ruan.

  • Neglecting the matrix‑valued nature of the Maurer–Cartan equation for non‑abelian local systems; a scalar bounding cochain is insufficient to cancel a non‑scalar m₀.
  • Treating the curved A∞-relations as strict A∞-relations; the presence of m₀ means m₁² = [m₀,·], not zero.
  • Overlooking the role of sectorial stops in the partially wrapped category of a Liouville sector; the stop determines which Reeb chords are allowed to wrap and changes the Hochschild cohomology.
  • Believing the categorical crepant resolution conjecture holds for any finite group action without the crepant condition; the age grading must match the discrepancy of the resolution.
  • These traps highlight the subtle points that often appear in advanced examinations.

Viterbo Functor and Symplectic Cohomology as Hochschild Homology of the Wrapped Fukaya Category

The Viterbo functor provides a canonical isomorphism between the symplectic cohomology SH*(X) of a Liouville domain X and the Hochschild homology HH*(W(X)) of its wrapped Fukaya category W(X). This result, proved by Ganatra, identifies the pair‑of‑pants product on SH* with the Connes‑B operator on HH* and shows that the Viterbo transfer map is a unital ring homomorphism. The construction uses the wrapped Floer complex CW*(L,L) for a Lagrangian brane L and the cyclic bar complex of the A∞‑category W(X). The isomorphism is natural with respect to Liouville embeddings and respects the module structure over the quantum cohomology of the completion of X. A key consequence is the generation criterion: a collection of Lagrangians split‑generates W(X) if and only if the induced map SH*(X) → ⊕ HW*(Li,Li) is surjective. This criterion underlies many proofs of homological mirror symmetry for affine varieties, where the mirror is a Landau–Ginzburg model and the wrapped category is generated by a set of thimbles. The Viterbo isomorphism also implies the non‑commutative Hodge‑to‑de Rham degeneration for W(X), linking the cyclic homology of the Fukaya category to the symplectic cohomology of the filling.

Spherical Twists, Braid Group Actions, and the Categorical Mapping Class Group

For a Lagrangian sphere L in a symplectic manifold X of dimension 2n, the generalized Dehn twist τL induces an autoequivalence of the Fukaya category called the spherical twist TL. Following Seidel–Thomas, an object L is spherical if its endomorphism algebra is isomorphic to the cohomology of an n‑sphere; in the Fukaya category this holds precisely for Lagrangian spheres with appropriate grading and spin structure. The spherical twist acts on an object M by the mapping cone of the evaluation map hom(L,M) ⊗ L → M. When two Lagrangian spheres L1, L2 intersect transversely in a single point, their spherical twists satisfy the braid relation TL1 TL2 TL1 ≅ TL2 TL1 TL2. This yields an action of the braid group Bk on the Fukaya category for any configuration of k pairwise once‑intersecting spheres. For a symplectic surface Σg, the mapping class group Γg is generated by Dehn twists about simple closed curves; the induced action on Fuk(Σg) factors through the braid group action on the corresponding Lagrangian spheres. This categorical representation is used to prove homological mirror symmetry for punctured surfaces, where the mirror is a cluster variety and the braid group action corresponds to cluster mutations. The spherical twist also appears in the study of stability conditions on Fukaya categories, where the space of stability conditions carries a natural action of the autoequivalence group generated by spherical twists.

Technique: Computing a Spherical Twist on a Pair of Intersecting Spheres
Let L1, L2 be graded Lagrangian 2‑spheres in a symplectic 4‑manifold intersecting transversely at a single point p. Describe the effect of the spherical twist TL1 on the object L2 in the Fukaya category, and verify the braid relation TL1 TL2 TL1(L1) ≅ TL2 TL1 TL2(L1).
The morphism space hom(L1,L2) is generated by the intersection point p, placed in degree 1 by the grading. The evaluation map hom(L1,L2) ⊗ L1 → L2 is the inclusion of the cone summand. The spherical twist TL1(L2) is the mapping cone of this evaluation, which is quasi‑isomorphic to the Lagrangian obtained by surgery of L2 along L1 at p; this surgery yields a Lagrangian sphere L12 isotopic to the image of L2 under the geometric Dehn twist τL1. Applying TL2 to L12 gives the surgery of L12 along L2, which is the sphere L1 again. The triple composition TL1 TL2 TL1(L1) therefore returns L1 up to isomorphism. By symmetry the same holds for TL2 TL1 TL2(L1), establishing the braid relation in the categorical setting.
Exam‑Day Strategy
When a question asks for the action of a Dehn twist on the Fukaya category, first identify whether the Lagrangian is a sphere (spherical object). If it is, the autoequivalence is the spherical twist defined by the mapping cone of the evaluation map. For braid relations, check the intersection pattern: once‑intersecting spheres give the standard braid relation; higher intersections produce more complicated relations involving higher A∞‑operations. Remember that the Seidel representation of the mapping class group factors through the braid group action on the Fukaya category of a surface.

Ganatra–Pardon–Shende Equivalence: Fukaya Categories of Weinstein Manifolds and Constructible Sheaves on Skeletons

The Nadler–Zaslow correspondence identifies the wrapped Fukaya category of a cotangent bundle T*M with the category of constructible sheaves on M. Ganatra, Pardon, and Shende extended this equivalence to arbitrary Weinstein manifolds. A Weinstein manifold X admits a Liouville vector field whose flow defines a Lagrangian skeleton Λ, a Lagrangian CW complex obtained as the union of stable manifolds of the critical points of a Morse function. The wrapped Fukaya category W(X) is equivalent to the category ShΛ(X) of sheaves on X with microsupport contained in Λ. The equivalence is constructed by sending a Lagrangian brane L to its microlocalization μL, a constructible sheaf on Λ whose stalk at a point x ∈ Λ is the wrapped Floer cohomology HW*(L, Lx) where Lx is a cotangent fiber at x. This functor is fully faithful and essentially surjective, and it intertwines the wrapped Floer product with the convolution product of sheaves. A major consequence is that W(X) is invariant under Weinstein homotopy, because the skeleton Λ changes by elementary expansions and collapses that do not affect the category of constructible sheaves. The equivalence also provides a combinatorial model for computations: the skeleton Λ can be presented as a Legendrian link in the contact boundary, and the wrapped Fukaya category is equivalent to the category of modules over the Chekanov–Eliashberg dg‑algebra of that link. This perspective underlies the sectorial gluing formula for Liouville sectors, where the partially wrapped category of a sector is identified with sheaves on a skeleton with stops.

Calabi–Yau A∞‑Structures on Fukaya Categories and Categorical Mirror Symmetry

A Calabi–Yau A∞‑category of dimension d is an A∞‑category equipped with a non‑degenerate cyclic inner product ⟨·,·⟩ of degree d satisfying ⟨mk(a1,…,ak), a0⟩ = (−1)‖ ⟨mk(a0,a1,…,ak−1), ak⟩ for all sequences of morphisms. For a compact Calabi–Yau symplectic manifold X of complex dimension n, the Fukaya category Fuk(X) carries a Calabi–Yau A∞‑structure of dimension n. The cyclic inner product is given by the Poincaré duality pairing on Floer cohomology: ⟨α,β⟩ = ∫L0∩L1 α ∪ β, where the integral is the evaluation of the cup product on the fundamental class of the intersection. This structure is essential for the categorical formulation of homological mirror symmetry: the equivalence DπFuk(X) ≃ DbCoh(Y) is required to be an equivalence of Calabi–Yau A∞‑categories, meaning it preserves the cyclic inner product up to homotopy. On the coherent side, the Calabi–Yau structure corresponds to the Serre functor S(·) = (·) ⊗ ωY[n]. The Calabi–Yau structure also controls higher genus Gromov–Witten invariants via Costello’s construction of a topological conformal field theory from a Calabi–Yau A∞‑category. For non‑compact Calabi–Yau manifolds (e.g., Liouville manifolds), a Calabi–Yau structure on the wrapped Fukaya category requires a choice of a volume form on the skeleton and a grading; the existence of such a structure is equivalent to the vanishing of the first Chern class of the symplectic manifold.

Quantum Cap Action and the Seidel Representation on the Fukaya Category

The quantum cap action is a homomorphism from the quantum cohomology ring QH*(X) of a closed symplectic manifold X to the Hochschild cohomology HH*(Fuk(X)) of its Fukaya category, constructed by Seidel. For a class a ∈ QH*(X), the quantum cap action assigns a natural transformation cap(a) of the identity functor on Fuk(X). On an object L, the component cap(a)L is defined by counting pseudoholomorphic disks with one interior marked point constrained to represent a and boundary on L, weighted by the holonomy of the local system. This action refines the closed‑open string map CO: QH*(X) → HH*(Fuk(X)) by incorporating the A∞‑structure: cap(a) satisfies the Maurer–Cartan equation in the Hochschild complex and defines an autoequivalence of Fuk(X) when a is invertible. The Seidel representation associates to a loop of Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms φt the quantum cohomology class S(φ) ∈ QH*(X) (the Seidel element) and the corresponding autoequivalence cap(S(φ)). This yields a group homomorphism π1(Ham(X,ω)) → Aut(Fuk(X)). In the monotone case, the quantum cap action can be computed using the pearl complex of Biran–Cornea, where the interior marked point is replaced by a Morse critical point of a Hamiltonian. The action controls the deformation theory of the Fukaya category: the obstruction to extending a deformation of the A∞‑structure lies in the image of the quantum cap action. Moreover, the generation criterion of Abouzaid states that a set of Lagrangians split‑generates Fuk(X) if and only if the quantum cap action of the unit 1 ∈ QH*(X) factors through the subcategory generated by those Lagrangians.

  • Assuming every Lagrangian sphere yields a spherical twist without checking the grading and spin structure; a non‑graded or non‑spin sphere does not define a spherical object in the Fukaya category.
  • Overlooking that the Ganatra–Pardon–Shende equivalence requires a Weinstein structure; it does not hold for arbitrary Liouville manifolds without a Morse‑Bott skeleton.
  • Neglecting the cyclic symmetry condition in the definition of a Calabi–Yau A∞‑category; the inner product must satisfy the cyclic symmetry for all A∞‑operations, not just m2.
  • Treating the quantum cap action as a strict ring homomorphism to the endomorphism ring of the identity functor; it lands in Hochschild cohomology, which is only a ring up to homotopy, and the Maurer–Cartan equation must be satisfied.
  • Believing the Seidel representation factors through the fundamental group of the symplectomorphism group; it is defined on π1(Ham(X,ω)), which can be a proper quotient of π1(Symp(X,ω)).
  • Ignoring the role of the pearl complex in computing the quantum cap action for monotone manifolds; the pearl differential includes both Morse and Floer contributions, and omitting either gives an incorrect action.

Fukaya Categories and the Gross–Siebert Program

The Gross–Siebert program provides a purely algebro‑geometric construction of mirror pairs using tropical geometry and logarithmic structures. Starting from a maximally degenerate Calabi–Yau variety — a union of toric varieties glued along toric strata — one builds a scattering diagram on the associated integral affine manifold with singularities. The walls of the diagram are decorated by counts of tropical curves, and the wall‑crossing automorphisms generate a formal deformation of the central fiber. The resulting formal scheme is the mirror family; its generic fiber is a smooth Calabi–Yau variety whose derived category of coherent sheaves is conjecturally equivalent to the Fukaya category of the original symplectic manifold.

On the symplectic side, the affine manifold arises as the base of an SYZ fibration on a toric Calabi–Yau manifold. The tropical curves correspond to pseudoholomorphic disks with boundary on the Lagrangian torus fibers, and the scattering diagram encodes the wall‑crossing of the open Gromov–Witten invariants that define the A∞‑operations in the Fukaya category. Gross, Hacking, and Keel showed that for a log Calabi–Yau pair (X,D) with D a normal‑crossing anticanonical divisor, the mirror constructed by the Gross–Siebert algorithm carries a canonical theta‑function basis whose multiplication rules match the structure constants of the Fukaya category of XD. This identification uses the wrapped Fukaya category of the complement and the equivalence of Ganatra–Pardon–Shende with constructible sheaves on the skeleton.

Key technical points:

  • The scattering diagram lives on the tropicalization of (X,D) and its walls are rational polyhedral cones decorated by formal power series in the Novikov variable.
  • Consistency of the diagram (the product of wall‑crossing automorphisms around any loop is the identity) is equivalent to the A∞‑relations in the Fukaya category.
  • The mirror family is constructed over the formal spectrum of the completed group ring of the tropical vertex group; the central fiber is the union of toric strata.
  • The theta functions provide a basis of the mirror coordinate ring, and their product expansions reproduce the counts of tropical disks, which by the correspondence theorem equal the counts of pseudoholomorphic disks in the symplectic manifold.

Homological Mirror Symmetry for Hypersurfaces in Projective Space

Seidel’s pioneering work established homological mirror symmetry for the quartic surface in ℂℙ³ and, more generally, for smooth hypersurfaces of degree d in ℂℙⁿ. The mirror of a degree d hypersurface X ⊂ ℂℙⁿ is a Landau–Ginzburg model ( (ℂ*)ⁿ, W ) with superpotential W = x₁ + … + xₙ₊₁ + q/(x₁⋯xₙ₊₁) where q is the complexified Kähler parameter. The Fukaya category Fuk(X) is generated by the vanishing cycles of a Lefschetz fibration obtained by projecting X from a generic point. Seidel proved that the directed Fukaya category of this Lefschetz fibration is quasi‑equivalent to the category of matrix factorizations MF(W), which by Orlov’s theorem is equivalent to the derived category of coherent sheaves on the mirror hypersurface.

The proof proceeds in several stages:

  1. Construct a Lefschetz fibration π : X → ℂ with generic fiber a symplectic manifold of dimension 2n−2 and vanishing cycles L₁,…,L_μ.
  2. Show that the vanishing cycles split‑generate Fuk(X) using the generation criterion of Abouzaid (the quantum cap action of the unit factors through the subcategory generated by the L_i).
  3. Identify the directed A∞‑category of the vanishing cycles with the category of matrix factorizations of W. This uses the Seidel functor, which associates to each vanishing cycle a matrix factorization built from the Floer cohomology of the cycle with a distinguished Lagrangian section.
  4. Extend the equivalence to the full Fukaya category by passing to the wrapped Fukaya category of the complement of the anticanonical divisor and invoking the Ganatra–Pardon–Shende equivalence with constructible sheaves.

For higher‑dimensional hypersurfaces (n ≥ 3) the same strategy works, but the Lefschetz fibration has higher‑dimensional fibers and the vanishing cycles are Lagrangian spheres. Sheridan extended the result to all smooth Fano hypersurfaces, showing that the wrapped Fukaya category of the complement of the anticanonical divisor is equivalent to the derived category of the mirror Landau–Ginzburg model. The quantum cap action of the Seidel element provides the autoequivalence corresponding to the monodromy of the Landau–Ginzburg superpotential.

Worked Example: Generation by Vanishing Cycles
Let X ⊂ ℂℙ³ be a smooth quartic surface. Consider the Lefschetz fibration π : X → ℂ obtained by projecting from a point p ∉ X. The fibration has 24 nodal fibers, each giving a vanishing cycle L_i ≅ S¹. Explain why the collection {L_i} split‑generates Fuk(X).
The quantum cap action of the unit 1 ∈ QH*(X) is a natural transformation of the identity functor on Fuk(X). By Abouzaid’s generation criterion, a set of objects split‑generates if and only if the component of cap(1) on each object lies in the subcategory generated by that set. For the quartic surface, the Seidel element S(φ) associated to the monodromy of π equals the unit in quantum cohomology. The quantum cap action cap(1) is computed by counting pseudoholomorphic disks with one interior marked point constrained to a point class; these disks are exactly the thimbles of the vanishing cycles. Hence cap(1)_L factors through the subcategory generated by the vanishing cycles for every Lagrangian L. Therefore the vanishing cycles split‑generate Fuk(X).
Exam Strategy
When a question asks for the generation criterion, state Abouzaid’s theorem precisely: a collection of Lagrangians split‑generates Fuk(X) iff the quantum cap action of the unit factors through the subcategory they generate. Do not confuse this with the older criterion using the closed‑open map; the quantum cap action incorporates the full A∞‑structure and is the correct tool for non‑exact settings.

Fukaya Categories over the Universal Novikov Field and Convergence of the A∞‑Operations

The universal Novikov field Λ = { ∑i=0^∞ a_i Tλ_i | a_i ∈ ℂ, λ_i ∈ ℝ, λ_i → +∞ } provides the coefficient ring for Floer theory on non‑exact symplectic manifolds. Each pseudoholomorphic polygon contributing to the A∞‑operation m_k carries a symplectic area A ≥ 0; its weight is T^A. Because Gromov compactness guarantees that for any fixed energy bound there are only finitely many homology classes of polygons, the sum defining m_k converges in the T‑adic topology of Λ. The completed tensor product Λ̂ ⊗ … ⊗ Λ̂ ensures that the A∞‑relations hold as identities of continuous multilinear maps.

For exact Lagrangians in an exact symplectic manifold, all disk areas are zero and the Novikov variable can be set to 1, reducing the coefficient ring to ℂ. In the monotone case, the area is proportional to the Maslov index, so the Novikov ring collapses to a polynomial ring in a single variable Tλ₀. For general non‑exact Lagrangians, one must introduce bounding cochains b ∈ CF¹(L,L) ⊗ Λ₊ to cancel the curvature term m₀(1). The deformed operations m_k^b satisfy the A∞‑relations and converge because the energy of a polygon with boundary on L is bounded below by the valuation of the bounding cochain. The construction of Fukaya–Oh–Ohta–Ono (FOOO) shows that the set of weak Maurer–Cartan solutions modulo gauge equivalence forms the object set of the Fukaya category over Λ.

Convergence issues also appear in wrapped Fukaya categories, where the Hamiltonian grows quadratically at infinity. There one uses the completed group ring of the fundamental group of the Liouville manifold, and the A∞‑operations are defined by counting polygons with asymptotically cylindrical ends. The energy filtration ensures that for each output chord the sum over input chords is finite in the completed tensor product. This analytic foundation is essential for the proof of the Ganatra–Pardon–Shende equivalence, where the wrapped Fukaya category is identified with the category of constructible sheaves on the skeleton.

Fukaya Categories of Symplectic Manifolds with a Normal Crossing Divisor and the Log Fukaya Category

A log symplectic manifold is a pair (X,D) where X is a symplectic manifold and D ⊂ X is a normal‑crossing symplectic divisor. The log Fukaya category Fuk(X,D) has as objects Lagrangian submanifolds L that are either disjoint from D or intersect D transversely in a prescribed manner: near each stratum D_I = ∩i∈I D_i the intersection L ∩ D_I is a Lagrangian submanifold of D_I. Morphism spaces are Floer cohomology groups CF*(L₀,L₁) defined using Hamiltonians that are adapted to the log structure — they are required to be tangent to the strata of D.

The A∞‑operations m_k count pseudoholomorphic polygons with boundary on the Lagrangians and interior marked points mapping to the strata of D. Each interior marked point carries a contact order with the divisor components; the total contact order determines the power of the log parameter q in the Novikov coefficient. When D is anticanonical (c₁(X) = [D]), the log Fukaya category is a genuine A∞‑category (curvature vanishes). If D is not anticanonical, the category is curved: m₀(1) = ∑β n_β Tω(β) qD·β where the sum runs over disks intersecting D. This curvature is precisely the obstruction to extending the Fukaya category of the complement XD across the divisor.

The log Fukaya category is central to the Gross–Siebert program: the mirror of (X,D) is a log Calabi–Yau variety (X̌, Ď) whose structure sheaf carries a canonical log structure. Abouzaid–Auroux–Katzarkov conjectured an equivalence D^π Fuk(X,D) ≃ D^b Coh(X̌, Ď) of Calabi–Yau A∞‑categories. In the case where D is a smooth anticanonical divisor, this reduces to the usual homological mirror symmetry for the complement XD, which is a Liouville manifold. The Ganatra–Pardon–Shende equivalence identifies the wrapped Fukaya category of XD with constructible sheaves on the skeleton; the log Fukaya category extends this by adding objects supported on the divisor strata, corresponding to sheaves with singular support along Ď.

Key references: Gross–Siebert “Logarithmic Gromov–Witten invariants” (2013); Abouzaid–Auroux–Katzarkov “Lagrangian fibrations on blow‑ups of toric varieties and mirror symmetry for hypersurfaces” (2016); Ganatra–Pardon–Shende “Covariantly functorial wrapped Floer theory on Liouville sectors” (2020).

  • Assuming the A∞‑operations converge without invoking the energy filtration; convergence fails for non‑exact Lagrangians unless bounding cochains are introduced.
  • Treating the log Fukaya category as an ordinary A∞‑category when the divisor is not anticanonical; the curvature term m₀(1) is non‑zero and must be accounted for in the Maurer–Cartan equation.
  • Overlooking the role of the scattering diagram in the Gross–Siebert program; the diagram encodes the wall‑crossing of open Gromov–Witten invariants and its consistency is equivalent to the A∞‑relations.
  • Believing that the Seidel functor for hypersurfaces works without the generation criterion; the vanishing cycles must be shown to split‑generate, which relies on the quantum cap action of the unit.
  • Neglecting the distinction between the wrapped Fukaya category of the complement and the log Fukaya category; the latter includes additional objects and morphisms supported on the divisor strata.
  • Assuming the mirror of a log Calabi–Yau pair is always a smooth variety; the mirror is typically a log scheme with a non‑trivial log structure, and the equivalence is of categories of log coherent sheaves.

Fukaya Categories with B‑Field Twists and Gerbes

A B‑field in symplectic geometry is a closed 2‑form B representing a class in H²(X; ℝ/ℤ). When a Lagrangian L is equipped with a flat unitary line bundle ∇ whose holonomy around a loop γ ⊂ L is exp(2πi ∫γ B), the Floer differential on CF*(L₀,L₁) is twisted by the parallel transport of these bundles along pseudoholomorphic strips. Concretely, each rigid strip u contributes a term multiplied by the holonomy of the tensor product of the bundles on the boundary components. This yields a twisted Fukaya category Fuk(X, B) whose objects are pairs (L, ∇) with ∇ a flat connection whose curvature equals the restriction of B to L. The A∞‑operations mk are defined by counting polygons with boundary on the Lagrangians and weighting each polygon by the product of holonomies along its edges.

When B is not integral, the twist cannot be absorbed into a line bundle; one must use a gerbe with connection. A gerbe is a higher analogue of a line bundle, classified by a class in H³(X; ℤ). The Fukaya category twisted by a gerbe, denoted Fuk(X, 𝒢), has objects that are Lagrangians equipped with a trivialization of the gerbe restriction. Morphism spaces are defined using a choice of trivialization on each polygon, and the A∞‑relations hold up to the gerbe’s 3‑cocycle. This construction appears in the work of Kapustin–Rozansky on topological strings and in the mathematical formulation by Block–Da Silva. The twisted category is essential for mirror symmetry on Calabi–Yau manifolds with non‑trivial B‑field, where the mirror is a complex manifold with a non‑trivial gerbe on the B‑model side.

Key technical points: the Novikov ring must be enlarged to keep track of the B‑field periods; the energy filtration still guarantees convergence because the B‑field contributes only a phase factor. The twisted Fukaya category is quasi‑equivalent to the untwisted one when B is exact, but for non‑exact B it can change the derived category. In the toric setting, the B‑field corresponds to a choice of complexified Kähler parameters on the mirror, and the equivalence of categories matches the variation of GIT stability conditions.

Fukaya Categories of Symplectic Manifolds with Contact Type Boundary

Let (X, ω) be a compact symplectic manifold with boundary ∂X of contact type, i.e. there exists a Liouville vector field Z defined near ∂X pointing outward and satisfying ℒZω = ω. The contact form α = ιZω on ∂X defines a contact manifold (Y, ξ = ker α). The Fukaya category of X with contact type boundary, often called the relative Fukaya category Fuk(X, ∂X), has as objects exact Lagrangian submanifolds L ⊂ X that are either disjoint from ∂X or meet ∂X transversely along Legendrian submanifolds Λ = L ∩ ∂X ⊂ Y. Morphism spaces are Floer cohomology groups CF*(L₀,L₁) defined using Hamiltonians that are linear in the Liouville coordinate near the boundary, so that chords correspond to Reeb chords on the Legendrian ends.

The A∞‑operations count pseudoholomorphic polygons with boundary on the Lagrangians and with interior marked points allowed to map to the boundary divisor. The Legendrian boundary conditions introduce additional algebraic structure: the Chekanov–Eliashberg differential graded algebra of the Legendrian Λ appears as the endomorphism algebra of the corresponding object in the wrapped Fukaya category of the completion X̂. There is a localization exact triangle relating the compact Fukaya category of X, the wrapped Fukaya category of the interior X̂, and the category of modules over the Legendrian contact homology algebra. This triangle is a categorical shadow of the Viterbo transfer map in symplectic cohomology.

Applications include the computation of Fukaya categories of cotangent bundles with boundary (where the Legendrians are conormal bundles) and the proof of the Ganatra–Pardon–Shende equivalence for Liouville sectors. The contact type boundary condition also allows one to define a partially wrapped Fukaya category by choosing a stop on the contact boundary; varying the stop yields a categorical action of the contact mapping class group. The theory requires a careful treatment of the asymptotic behavior of pseudoholomorphic curves near the boundary, using the SFT compactness theorem of Bourgeois–Eliashberg–Hofer–Wysocki–Zehnder.

Technique
Explain how a non‑exact B‑field modifies the Floer differential on CF*(L₀,L₁) and why the resulting twisted Fukaya category is not quasi‑equivalent to the untwisted one in general.
A non‑exact B‑field B ∈ Ω²(X) with [B] ∉ H²(X;ℤ) cannot be written as the curvature of a line bundle. One equips each Lagrangian L with a flat unitary connection ∇ whose holonomy around a loop γ ⊂ L is exp(2πi ∫γ B|L). For a rigid pseudoholomorphic strip u contributing to the differential ∂: CF*(L₀,L₁) → CF*(L₀,L₁), the boundary of u consists of two paths on L₀ and L₁. The contribution is multiplied by the parallel transport of ∇₀ along the L₀‑edge and ∇₁ along the L₁‑edge. Because B is not exact, these holonomies cannot be simultaneously gauged away; they define a non‑trivial twist of the A∞‑structure. The twisted category Fuk(X,B) has the same objects but different morphism complexes. If B were exact, a gauge transformation would give a quasi‑equivalence to the untwisted category. For non‑exact B, the twist changes the derived category: for instance, on a torus T² with a constant B‑field of irrational slope, the twisted Fukaya category is equivalent to the category of coherent sheaves on a non‑commutative torus, which is not equivalent to the usual derived category of the commutative torus.
Strategy
When a question asks about B‑field twists, first identify whether the class is integral or not. Integral twists are absorbed by line bundles and do not change the derived category; non‑integral twists require gerbes and genuinely alter the category. Remember that the Novikov ring must record the B‑field periods, and the energy filtration still controls convergence because the twist contributes only a unit‑modulus phase.

Categorical Actions of Symplectic Mapping Class Groups on Fukaya Categories

The symplectic mapping class group π₀(Symp(X, ω)) acts on the Fukaya category by sending a Lagrangian L to φ(L) for a symplectomorphism φ. This action lifts to an action of the group of A∞‑autoequivalences of Fuk(X). For Dehn twists τL along an exact Lagrangian sphere L, Seidel proved that the induced autoequivalence is the spherical twist TL defined on the derived category by the exact triangle Hom*(L, –) ⊗ L → Id → TL. The collection of spherical twists for a basis of vanishing cycles generates a braid group action, which in many cases (e.g. An Milnor fibers) yields a faithful representation of the mapping class group.

Beyond spherical twists, general symplectomorphisms that are not Dehn twists induce autoequivalences that can be described using Lagrangian correspondences. Given φ ∈ Symp(X), its graph Γφ ⊂ X⁻ × X is a Lagrangian correspondence. The associated functor ΦΓφ: Fuk(X) → Fuk(X) is given by convolution with the correspondence. When φ is Hamiltonian isotopic to the identity, ΦΓφ is quasi‑isomorphic to the identity functor. The map π₀(Symp(X)) → π₀(Auteq(Fuk(X))) is known to be injective for many Liouville manifolds (e.g. cotangent bundles of spheres) by work of Abouzaid–Seidel and Ganatra–Pardon–Shende.

Recent developments include the construction of categorical actions of the full mapping class group on the wrapped Fukaya category of a punctured surface, where the action is generated by Dehn twists and the “point‑pushing” maps. The latter correspond to autoequivalences given by wrapping around the puncture. The interplay between the categorical mapping class group and the symplectic mapping class group is a central theme in the study of stability conditions on Fukaya categories and in the homological mirror symmetry for surfaces.

Fukaya Categories and the Factorization Algebra Approach of Costello and Gwilliam

Costello and Gwilliam reformulated the Fukaya category as a factorization algebra on the Ran space of a symplectic manifold. In this framework, the A∞‑structure arises from the factorization product of observables supported on disjoint subsets of the source curve. The key idea is to consider the topological string B‑model as a factorization algebra of observables on the moduli space of pseudoholomorphic curves. The Fukaya category appears as the category of modules over the factorization algebra of boundary observables.

Concretely, one assigns to each open set U ⊂ X a cochain complex of observables supported in U. The factorization product corresponds to gluing of curves along boundary components. The A∞‑operations mk are recovered as the structure maps of the factorization algebra when one restricts to configurations of k boundary marked points. This perspective makes the homotopical nature of the A∞‑relations manifest: they are the coherence conditions for a factorization algebra. Moreover, the factorization algebra framework naturally incorporates bulk deformations (closed string insertions) as modules over the factorization algebra of bulk observables.

The approach has been used to prove the equivalence between the Fukaya category of a Weinstein manifold and the category of constructible sheaves on its skeleton (the GPS equivalence) by showing that both sides arise as categories of modules over the same factorization algebra. It also provides a systematic way to define Fukaya categories for symplectic manifolds with singularities, where the factorization algebra is defined on a stratified space. The main technical input is the construction of a factorization algebra from the SFT compactification of the moduli space of pseudoholomorphic curves, using the language of derived geometry and the BV formalism.

Fukaya Categories of Symplectic Orbifolds and Global Quotients

When X is a symplectic orbifold, the Fukaya category must account for twisted sectors coming from the inertia orbifold. Objects are Lagrangian suborbifolds L ⊂ X equipped with a flat orbibundle (a representation of the isotropy groups). Morphism spaces CF*(L₀,L₁) are defined using the Chen–Ruan orbifold Floer theory: generators are tuples consisting of an intersection point x ∈ L₀ ∩ L₁ together with a choice of sector (an element of the isotropy group at x). The differential counts pseudoholomorphic strips with orbifold marked points mapping to the twisted sectors, and the A∞‑operations count polygons with similar data.

For a global quotient X = M/G with G a finite group acting symplectically on a manifold M, the Fukaya category Fuk(X) is equivalent to the G‑equivariant Fukaya category of M. Objects are G‑invariant Lagrangians in M equipped with a G‑equivariant flat bundle. Morphisms are the G‑invariant part of the Floer complex on M. The A∞‑operations are induced by the G‑equivariant counts of polygons. This equivalence is a theorem of Seidel and of Fukaya–Oh–Ohta–Ono in the orbifold setting.

Applications include the computation of Fukaya categories of weighted projective spaces and of toric orbifolds, where mirror symmetry predicts an equivalence with the derived category of coherent sheaves on the mirror stack. The orbifold Fukaya category also appears in the study of symplectic resolutions: if X admits a crepant resolution Ỹ → X, the Fukaya category of Ỹ is expected to be a categorical crepant resolution of Fuk(X) in the sense of Kuznetsov. The orbifold theory requires a careful treatment of the Novikov ring with coefficients in the representation ring of the isotropy groups, and the grading is shifted by the age (degree shifting number) of each twisted sector.

  • Assuming that a contact type boundary automatically makes the Fukaya category wrapped; one must specify a stop or a Legendrian boundary condition to obtain a wrapped or partially wrapped category.
  • Thinking that every symplectomorphism induces a spherical twist; only Dehn twists along exact Lagrangian spheres give spherical twists, general symplectomorphisms give more complicated autoequivalences.
  • Overlooking the role of the factorization algebra’s locality axiom; the A∞‑relations are precisely the factorization product associativity, not an ad hoc combinatorial identity.
  • Treating orbifold twisted sectors as ordinary intersection points; each sector carries an isotropy representation that affects both the grading and the composition maps.
  • Believing that the equivariant Fukaya category of a global quotient is simply the invariant subcategory; the equivariant structure on the bundles is essential for the correct A∞‑structure.
  • Neglecting the age shift in orbifold Floer theory; the degree of a generator from a twisted sector is shifted by the age, which is crucial for the Calabi–Yau property.

The Arnold Conjecture and Hochschild Homology of the Fukaya Category

The Arnold conjecture, formulated by Vladimir Arnold in 1965, asserts that a Hamiltonian symplectomorphism of a closed symplectic manifold has at least as many fixed points as the sum of the Betti numbers of the manifold. Andreas Floer proved this for monotone symplectic manifolds in the late 1980s by constructing Floer homology HF*(φH) for a Hamiltonian diffeomorphism φH and showing it is isomorphic to the singular homology of the manifold. The categorical reformulation identifies the fixed-point Floer homology with the Hochschild homology of the Fukaya category. For the identity symplectomorphism, the diagonal bimodule Δ in Fuk(X) × Fuk(X)op represents the identity functor, and its endomorphism complex computes HH*(Fuk(X)). The closed-open string map CO: QH*(X) → HH*(Fuk(X)) and the open-closed map OC: HH*(Fuk(X)) → QH*(X) satisfy the Cardy relation OC ∘ CO = quantum cap action. When CO is an isomorphism — as proved by Ganatra for Weinstein manifolds and by Abouzaid for many compact cases — the Hochschild homology is non-degenerate and recovers quantum homology. The Arnold conjecture then follows from the non-vanishing of HH*(Fuk(X)) and the fact that the rank of HF*(φH) equals the rank of HH*(Fuk(X)) for any Hamiltonian symplectomorphism, because φH induces an autoequivalence of Fuk(X) acting on Hochschild homology. This categorical perspective, developed by Fukaya, Seidel, Kontsevich, and others, transforms a fixed-point problem into a statement about the categorical trace of an autoequivalence.

The Seidel–Smith Braid Group Representation on Fuk(Symn(Σ)) and Khovanov Homology

In a series of papers beginning in 2006, Paul Seidel and Ivan Smith constructed a faithful action of the braid group Bn on the Fukaya category of the n-fold symmetric product Symn(Σ) of a Riemann surface Σ of genus g ≥ 1. The symmetric product Symn(Σ) carries a natural symplectic structure as the Hilbert scheme of n points on Σ. The Seidel–Smith representation uses Lagrangian submanifolds Lγ ⊂ Symn(Σ) associated to collections of disjoint arcs γ in Σ; these Lagrangians are products of the arcs in the symmetric product. The braid group generators σi act by generalized Dehn twists along Lagrangian spheres Si ⊂ Symn(Σ) which are the vanishing cycles of the Hilbert–Chow morphism Symn(Σ) → Symn(Σ)/Sn. The resulting autoequivalences satisfy the braid relations because the Lagrangian spheres intersect pairwise in a single point, and the composition of the corresponding spherical twists reproduces the Artin presentation of Bn. The categorical trace of this action on the Hochschild homology of Fuk(Symn(Σ)) recovers the Jones polynomial, and the full Floer-theoretic invariant defines a link homology theory — symplectic Khovanov homology — which is conjecturally isomorphic to the original Khovanov homology. This construction is distinct from the spherical twist braid group actions on the Fukaya category of a general symplectic manifold (covered elsewhere) because it exploits the specific geometry of symmetric products and provides a symplectic categorification of the Jones polynomial. The work of Manolescu, Woodward, and others has extended this to a full TQFT framework.

Worked Example: Braid Group Action on Fuk(Sym2(T2))
Let Σ = T2 be the two-torus. Describe the Lagrangian spheres in Sym2(T2) whose spherical twists generate the Seidel–Smith braid group action for n = 2, and compute the intersection number of the two standard Lagrangian spheres S1 and S2 corresponding to the braid generator σ1.
For n = 2, Sym2(T2) is a symplectic 4-manifold diffeomorphic to T2 × S2 blown up at four points (the Hilbert scheme of 2 points on T2). The braid group B2 ≅ Z is generated by σ1. The Lagrangian sphere S1 ⊂ Sym2(T2) is the vanishing cycle of the Hilbert–Chow morphism; it can be described as the set of unordered pairs {x, y} where x and y lie on a fixed simple closed curve γ ⊂ T2 and collide. Topologically, S1 ≅ S2. Since B2 has only one generator, there is only one Lagrangian sphere S1 up to Hamiltonian isotopy. The self-intersection of S1 in Sym2(T2) is −2 (as for any Lagrangian sphere in a 4-manifold). The spherical twist τS1 acts on Fuk(Sym2(T2)) and generates a Z-action. The intersection number of S1 with itself is not defined as a transverse intersection; the relevant intersection for the braid relation is between distinct spheres, but for n = 2 there is only one sphere. The braid relation is vacuous, and the action is simply the infinite cyclic group generated by τS1.
Exam Strategy: Distinguishing Braid Group Actions
Questions often conflate the Seidel–Smith representation on symmetric products with the spherical twist braid group action on a general Fukaya category. Remember: Seidel–Smith acts on Fuk(Symn(Σ)) using Lagrangian spheres coming from the Hilbert–Chow morphism, and categorifies the Jones polynomial. The general spherical twist action uses Dehn twists along exact Lagrangian spheres in any symplectic manifold and generates a subgroup of the symplectic mapping class group. The former is a specific geometric construction; the latter is a general categorical mechanism. If a question mentions symmetric products, Hilbert schemes, or Khovanov homology, it is Seidel–Smith. If it mentions Dehn twists, exact Lagrangian spheres, or the mapping class group, it is the general spherical twist action.

Relative Fukaya Categories for Smooth Divisors and Orlov-Type Equivalences

Let (X, ω) be a closed symplectic manifold and D ⊂ X a smooth symplectic divisor (a codimension-2 symplectic submanifold Poincaré dual to a positive multiple of [ω]). The relative Fukaya category Fuk(X, D) has as objects exact, graded, spin Lagrangian submanifolds L ⊂ X that intersect D transversely. Morphism spaces CF*(L0, L1) are generated by intersection points in X D, and the differential counts pseudoholomorphic strips that do not intersect D. The A∞-operations count polygons with boundary on the Lagrangians and no interior marked points mapping to D. There is a localization exact triangle relating the Fukaya category of X, the Fukaya category of D, and the relative category: Fuk(D) → Fuk(X) → Fuk(X, D). When X is the total space of a line bundle over D, this recovers the Orlov equivalence between the derived category of coherent sheaves on the base and the category of matrix factorizations on the total space. In the symplectic setting, Auroux, Katzarkov, and Orlov showed that for a del Pezzo surface X with an anticanonical divisor D, the wrapped Fukaya category of the complement X D is equivalent to the derived category of coherent sheaves on the mirror Landau–Ginzburg model. The relative category Fuk(X, D) interpolates between the compact Fukaya category of X and the wrapped category of X D; the stop for the wrapped category is precisely the divisor D. This framework generalizes to normal crossing divisors (the log Fukaya category) but the smooth divisor case already exhibits the key feature: the relative category is a module category over Fuk(X) and a comodule category over Fuk(D), and the Orlov-type equivalence identifies it with a category of singularities or matrix factorizations on the mirror side.

Homological Mirror Symmetry for Elliptic Curves: The Fukaya Category of the Two-Torus

The first complete proof of homological mirror symmetry was given by Polishchuk and Zaslow in 1998 for the two-torus T2. The symplectic manifold is T2 = R2/Z2 with the standard area form. Its mirror is the elliptic curve Eτ = C/(Z + τZ) with τ in the upper half-plane. Objects of Fuk(T2) are graded, spin Lagrangian circles (simple closed curves) equipped with a rank-1 local system (a flat U(1)-connection). A Lagrangian circle of slope p/q ∈ Q ∪ {∞} (in lowest terms) corresponds under mirror symmetry to a degree-p line bundle on Eτ if q = 1, or more generally to a semi-stable vector bundle of rank q and degree p. The morphism space CF*(L0, L1) for two transverse circles is the vector space generated by their intersection points; the differential vanishes because there are no non-constant holomorphic strips on T2 with boundary on two distinct circles (any such strip would lift to a holomorphic map from the strip to R2 with boundary on parallel lines, which must be constant by the maximum principle). The A∞-operations mk count holomorphic k-gons; these are also constant for degree reasons, so the A∞-structure is formal. The category is generated by two Lagrangians: the zero-section L0 (slope 0) and a fiber L∞ (slope ∞). Their endomorphism algebra is the path algebra of the Kronecker quiver with two vertices and two arrows, which is derived equivalent to the category of coherent sheaves on Eτ. The grading on Lagrangians corresponds to the phase of the central charge Z(L) = ∫L (ω + iB) in the stability condition on Fuk(T2), matching the slope stability on Coh(Eτ). This example is the local model for all Lagrangian torus fibrations in the SYZ programme.

Fukaya Categories and the Categorical Formulation of Homological Mirror Symmetry for K3 Surfaces

A K3 surface X is a simply connected compact complex surface with trivial canonical bundle; it admits a hyperkähler metric and a 20-dimensional family of complex structures. The homological mirror symmetry conjecture for K3 predicts that for a generic K3 surface X with a symplectic form ω, there exists a mirror K3 surface X∨ such that Fuk(X) ≅ DbCoh(X∨) as Calabi–Yau A∞-categories of dimension 2. The Fukaya category of a K3 surface has as objects graded, exact Lagrangian spheres (diffeomorphic to S2) and more generally Lagrangians with b1 = 0. The spherical twists along Lagrangian spheres generate a subgroup of the autoequivalence group Autoeq(Fuk(X)) isomorphic to the braid group on the set of spherical objects. On the mirror side, DbCoh(X∨) carries the action of spherical twists along spherical sheaves (objects E with Ext*(E, E) ≅ H*(S2)), and the numerical Grothendieck group is the Mukai lattice H*(X∨, Z) with the Mukai pairing (v, w) = ∫ ch(v)∨ ch(w) td(X∨). The categorical HMS equivalence must identify the Mukai lattice with the numerical Grothendieck group of Fuk(X), which is the lattice of Lagrangian spheres modulo the Euler pairing χ(L0, L1) = rank HF*(L0, L1). The Hochschild homology HH*(Fuk(X)) is isomorphic to the quantum homology QH*(X) ≅ H*(X) because K3 surfaces have no quantum corrections in degree 2 (the Gromov–Witten invariants of genus 0 with 3 insertions vanish for degree reasons). The closed-open map CO: H*(X) → HH*(Fuk(X)) is an isomorphism, and the Calabi–Yau structure on Fuk(X) corresponds to the Serre functor on DbCoh(X∨). The conjecture is known for Kummer surfaces (Seidel, 2015) and for certain elliptic K3 surfaces via the work of Abouzaid, Smith, and others using the SYZ fibration. The categorical formulation also predicts that the autoequivalence group of Fuk(X) contains the symplectic mapping class group of X, and that the action on the numerical Grothendieck group preserves the Mukai pairing, giving a symplectic realization of the global Torelli theorem for K3 surfaces.

For a complex abelian variety A of dimension g equipped with a principal polarization, the mirror is a dual abelian variety A∨ with a symplectic form determined by the polarization. The Fukaya category Fuk(A) is generated by Lagrangian tori equipped with unitary local systems; these correspond under homological mirror symmetry to skyscraper sheaves on A∨. The A∞‑structure on Fuk(A) is formal because all holomorphic polygons have Maslov index zero, so higher products vanish for degree reasons. The endomorphism algebra of a single Lagrangian torus with a generic local system is the group algebra of π₁(T²g) twisted by the local system, which is isomorphic to the exterior algebra on g generators. This matches the Ext‑algebra of a skyscraper sheaf on A∨. Fukaya Categories and the Categorical Mirror Symmetry for Calabi–Yau Threefolds

Let X be a compact Calabi–Yau threefold with a symplectic form ω and a nowhere‑vanishing holomorphic volume form Ω. Objects are graded, exact Lagrangian submanifolds L ⊂ X with b₁(L)=0 (typically L ≅ S³) equipped with spin structures and local systems. The A∞‑operations mₖ count rigid pseudoholomorphic (k+1)‑gons with boundary on the given Lagrangians; the Calabi–Yau condition forces the cyclic symmetry ⟨mₖ(x₁,…,xₖ), xₖ₊₁⟩ = (−1)‖x₁‖(‖x₂‖+…+‖xₖ₊₁‖) ⟨mₖ(x₂,…,xₖ₊₁), x₁⟩ where the pairing is the Poincaré duality pairing on Floer cohomology. The HMS conjecture predicts an A∞‑equivalence Fuk(X) ≃ DᵇCoh(X∨) that identifies the numerical Grothendieck group K₀(Fuk(X)) with the Mukai lattice Heven(X∨,ℤ) and sends the Euler pairing χ(L₀,L₁)=rank HF*(L₀,L₁) to the Mukai pairing. The Hochschild homology HH*(Fuk(X)) is isomorphic to the quantum cohomology QH*(X); the closed‑open map CO: QH*(X) → HH*(Fuk(X)) is conjecturally an isomorphism for all Calabi–Yau threefolds, but this is known only in special cases (e.g. the quintic threefold via work of Sheridan and others). The open‑closed map OC: HH*(Fuk(X)) → QH*(X) recovers the genus‑zero Gromov–Witten invariants with one interior marked point. The categorical formulation also predicts that the autoequivalence group of Fuk(X) contains the symplectic mapping class group of X, and that spherical twists along Lagrangian 3‑spheres correspond to spherical twists along spherical objects in DᵇCoh(X∨).

Worked Example: Computing the Euler Pairing on a Pair of Lagrangian 3‑Spheres
Let L₀ and L₁ be two transverse graded Lagrangian 3‑spheres in a Calabi–Yau threefold X intersecting in a single point p. Assume the Maslov index of the unique holomorphic strip from p to p is 0. Compute the Euler pairing χ(L₀,L₁) = rank HF*(L₀,L₁).
Because L₀ and L₁ intersect transversely at one point, the Floer complex CF*(L₀,L₁) is generated by the single intersection point p. The grading of p is determined by the phase difference of the graded Lagrangians; with Maslov index 0 the differential m₁ vanishes for degree reasons. Hence HF*(L₀,L₁) ≅ Λ in a single degree, so its rank over the Novikov field is 1. Therefore χ(L₀,L₁) = 1. This matches the Mukai pairing of the corresponding spherical objects in DᵇCoh(X∨), which is also 1 for a pair of spherical objects with a single Ext¹.

Remember that the Novikov field coefficients do not affect the rank; they only keep track of symplectic area.

Fukaya Categories and the Mirror Symmetry for Toric Deligne–Mumford Stacks

Toric Deligne–Mumford stacks 𝔛 are presented by a stacky fan (N,Σ,β) where β: ℤᵐ → N is a map from the lattice of one‑parameter subgroups to the cocharacter lattice. The mirror of 𝔛 is a Landau–Ginzburg model (Y,W) where Y = (ℂ*)ⁿ × ℂᵏ and the superpotential W is a sum of monomials determined by the stacky fan data. The A∞‑operations count orbifold pseudoholomorphic polygons with boundary on the branes; the orbifold structure introduces twisted sectors that contribute to the Floer differential. The wrapped Fukaya category W(𝔛) is generated by Lagrangian thimbles associated to the critical points of W. The homological mirror symmetry theorem of Fukaya–Oh–Ohta–Ono and later refined by Abouzaid–Fukaya–Oh–Ohta–Ono states that there is an A∞‑equivalence W(𝔛) ≃ MF(Y,W) between the wrapped Fukaya category and the category of matrix factorizations of W. The compact Fukaya category Fuk(𝔛) corresponds to the subcategory of MF(Y,W) generated by matrix factorizations with proper support. The closed‑open map CO: QH*orb(𝔛) → HH*(Fuk(𝔛)) identifies the orbifold quantum cohomology with the Hochschild homology of the Fukaya category; this map is an isomorphism for toric Deligne–Mumford stacks because the orbifold quantum product is semisimple. The categorical formulation also yields a description of the autoequivalence group: the symplectic mapping class group of 𝔛 acts by spherical twists on Fuk(𝔛), and under mirror symmetry this matches the action of the braid group on the exceptional collection of line bundles on the mirror stack.

Fukaya Categories and the Non‑Commutative Geometry of the Mirror via Deformation Quantization

In the framework of Kontsevich’s deformation quantization, the mirror of a symplectic manifold X can be described as a non‑commutative space whose structure sheaf is a sheaf of A∞‑algebras obtained by quantizing the Poisson structure on the mirror complex manifold X∨. The Fukaya category Fuk(X) is expected to be equivalent to the derived category of modules over this quantized structure sheaf. Concretely, one constructs a sheaf of curved A∞‑algebras 𝒜 on X∨ whose fiber at a point is the endomorphism algebra of the corresponding Lagrangian torus fiber in the SYZ fibration. The A∞‑operations on 𝒜 are given by counts of holomorphic polygons with boundary on the torus fibers, which are precisely the structure constants of the quantum corrected multiplication on the mirror. The Hochschild cohomology HH*(𝒜) computes the quantum cohomology QH*(X), and the closed‑open map CO: QH*(X) → HH*(Fuk(X)) corresponds to the natural map from the center of 𝒜 to its Hochschild cohomology. This perspective explains why the Fukaya category of a symplectic manifold with a Lagrangian torus fibration carries a natural action of the quantum cohomology ring: the quantum product acts by convolution with the structure constants of 𝒜. The non‑commutative mirror also provides a conceptual home for the B‑field twist: a B‑field on X corresponds to a gerbe on X∨, and twisting the Fukaya category by a flat B‑field is equivalent to twisting the module category over 𝒜 by the corresponding gerbe module. This approach has been made rigorous for toric varieties and for certain log Calabi–Yau surfaces using the technology of factorization algebras.

Fukaya Categories and the Categorical Formulation of the Homological Mirror Symmetry for Fano Varieties

Let X be a Fano variety of dimension n with an ample anticanonical divisor. The mirror of X is a Landau–Ginzburg model (Y,W) where Y is a non‑compact Calabi–Yau manifold and W: Y → ℂ is a superpotential with isolated critical points. The Fukaya category Fuk(X) is a monotone A∞‑category; its objects are monotone Lagrangian submanifolds with minimal Maslov number at least 2. The wrapped Fukaya category W(Y,W) is generated by Lefschetz thimbles associated to the critical points of W. The categorical formulation identifies the quantum cohomology QH*(X) with the Hochschild homology HH*(MF(Y,W)), and the closed‑open map CO: QH*(X) → HH*(Fuk(X)) is an isomorphism. The quantum product on QH*(X) is recovered from the A∞‑structure on MF(Y,W) via the open‑closed map. The categorical HMS also predicts that the quantum differential equation (the Dubrovin connection) on QH*(X) coincides with the Gauss–Manin connection on the cohomology of the fibers of W, providing a bridge between enumerative geometry and singularity theory.

Fukaya Categories and the Strominger–Yau–Zaslow Conjecture with Singular Fibers

The SYZ conjecture posits that mirror symmetry for a Calabi–Yau manifold X is realized by a special Lagrangian torus fibration π: X → B whose generic fibers are Lagrangian tori. The mirror X∨ is obtained by dualizing the smooth fibers, but the singular fibers of π encode the quantum corrections to the complex structure of X∨. In the Fukaya category Fuk(X), the singular fibers correspond to Lagrangians with non‑trivial Maslov index or non‑vanishing obstruction classes m0. The Gross–Siebert program constructs the mirror by degenerating X to a union of toric varieties glued along toric strata; the singular fibers of the SYZ fibration become the toric boundary divisors in the central fiber. The Fukaya category of the smooth fiber is related to the category of coherent sheaves on the mirror via the homological mirror symmetry equivalence, and the wall‑crossing formulas for the A∞‑operations on Fuk(X) are precisely the scattering diagrams of Gross–Siebert. For a K3 surface with an elliptic fibration, the singular fibers are classified by Kodaira type; each type contributes a specific set of vanishing cycles whose Floer cohomology computes the monodromy of the fibration. The A∞‑structure on the Fukaya category of the K3 surface encodes the Seiberg–Witten prepotential of the associated N=2 gauge theory, and the singular fibers correspond to massless BPS states. The work of Abouzaid–Auroux–Katzarkov on the SYZ conjecture for hypersurfaces in toric varieties shows that the Fukaya category of the hypersurface is generated by Lagrangian sections of the SYZ fibration, and the mirror is constructed by counting holomorphic disks with boundary on these sections. The autoequivalence group of Fuk(S) contains the symplectic mapping class group, which is isomorphic to the orthogonal group of the Mukai lattice preserving the Hodge structure; under mirror symmetry this corresponds to the group of derived autoequivalences of S∨ generated by spherical twists and tensoring with line bundles. The categorical HMS for K3 surfaces implies the classical Torelli theorem: the period map from the moduli space of K3 surfaces to the period domain is injective up to the action of the mapping class group.

Fukaya Categories and the Non‑Commutative Geometry of the Mirror via Deformation Quantization

When the mirror of a symplectic manifold X is not a geometric space but a non‑commutative space, the Fukaya category Fuk(X) is equivalent to the category of modules over a non‑commutative algebra 𝒜 obtained by deformation quantization of the mirror. Let X be a symplectic manifold with a Lagrangian torus fibration π: X → B with singular fibers. The mirror is the non‑commutative space whose algebra of functions is the deformation quantization of the Poisson algebra of functions on the base B with the Poisson structure induced by the symplectic form on X. The algebra 𝒜 is generated by the holonomy variables of the torus fibers, with relations given by the counts of holomorphic disks with boundary on the fibers. The Fukaya category Fuk(X) is A∞‑equivalent to the category of perfect modules over 𝒜. The objects of Fuk(X) are Lagrangian sections of the fibration, and the morphism space between two sections is the Floer cohomology, which is isomorphic to the space of sections of a line bundle on B twisted by the non‑commutative algebra 𝒜. The A∞‑operations on Fuk(X) correspond to the A∞‑structure on the module category over 𝒜 given by the deformation quantization product. The Hochschild cohomology HH*(𝒜) computes the quantum cohomology QH*(X), and the closed‑open map CO: QH*(X) → HH*(Fuk(X)) corresponds to the natural map from the center of 𝒜 to its Hochschild cohomology. This perspective explains why the Fukaya category of a symplectic manifold with a Lagrangian torus fibration carries a natural action of the quantum cohomology ring: the quantum product acts by convolution with the structure constants of 𝒜. The non‑commutative mirror also provides a conceptual home for the B‑field twist: a B‑field on X corresponds to a gerbe on X∨, and twisting the Fukaya category by a flat B‑field is equivalent to twisting the module category over 𝒜 by the corresponding gerbe module. This approach has been made rigorous for toric varieties and for certain log Calabi–Yau surfaces using the technology of factorization algebras.
