A satellite of mass m is in a circular orbit around Earth (mass M, radius R) at a height h = R above the surface. The ratio of the kinetic energy of the satellite to the magnitude of its gravitational potential energy is:
A. 1/4
B. 1/2
C. 1
D. 2
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: For any bound circular orbit, the Virial Theorem gives K = -E_total and |U| = 2K, so K/|U| = 1/2 directly. Full Solution: Orbital radius r = 2R. Velocity v² = GM/r. A. 120
B. 116
C. 110
D. 106
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: Total triangles from 10 points = ¹⁰C₃ = 120. Triangles lost due to 4 collinear points = ⁴C₃ = 4. Required triangles = 120 - 4 = 116. Full Solution: Select any 3 points out of 10 to form a triangle in ¹⁰C₃ ways. However, any 3 points chosen from the 4 collinear points do not form a triangle. Subtract these ⁴C₃ invalid cases. ¹⁰C₃ - ⁴C₃ = 120 - 4 = 116.

Q9. Mathematicsmodel-generated · 2026-11-27
The sum of the infinite series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n(n+1)(n+2)}$ is:
A. 1/2
B. 1/3
C. 1/4
D. 1/6
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut: Write the general term as $\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{1}{n(n+1)}-\frac{1}{(n+1)(n+2)}\right]$; the series telescopes to $\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{1\cdot2}=\frac{1}{4}$. Full solution: Using partial fractions, $\frac{1}{n(n+1)(n+2)}=\frac{1}{2n}-\frac{1}{n+1}+\frac{1}{2(n+2)}$; summing from $n=1$ to $N$ and taking $N\to\infty$ gives $\frac{1}{4}$.

A. m1/m2
B. √(m1/m2)

Correct Answer: C

The standard Gibbs free energy change for a reaction at 298 K is –11.4 kJ mol⁻¹. The equilibrium constant K at this temperature is closest to:
A. 10
B. 100
C. 1000
D. 1
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: At 298 K, RT ≈ 2.48 kJ mol⁻¹; –ΔG°/RT = 11.4/2.48 ≈ 4.6, so ln K ≈ 4.6 → K ≈ e⁴·⁶ ≈ 100. Full solution: ΔG° = –RT ln K → ln K = –ΔG°/(RT) = 11400 J / (8.314×298) ≈ 4.60 → K = e⁴·⁶⁰ ≈ 99.5 ≈ 100.
Q15. Mathematicsmodel-generated · 2026-11-25
The value of the definite integral ∫₀^{π/2} (sin³x)/(sin³x + cos³x) dx is:
A. π/2
B. π/4
C. π/6
D. π/3
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: Use King's Property: I = ∫₀^{π/2} f(sin x, cos x) dx = ∫₀^{π/2} f(cos x, sin x) dx. Let I be the given integral. Replacing x by π/2 - x gives I = ∫₀^{π/2} (cos³x)/(cos³x + sin³x) dx. Adding the two expressions: 2I = ∫₀^{π/2} 1 dx = π/2 ⇒ I = π/4. Full Solution: Let I = ∫₀^{π/2} sin³x/(sin³x+cos³x) dx. Substitute x → π/2 - x: I = ∫₀^{π/2} cos³x/(sin³x+cos³x) dx. Adding: 2I = ∫₀^{π/2} (sin³x+cos³x)/(sin³x+cos³x) dx = ∫₀^{π/2} dx = π/2. Therefore, I = π/4.
