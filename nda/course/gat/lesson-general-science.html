Key Concepts

Physics — Basics

  • Motion: Distance (scalar) vs displacement (vector); speed vs velocity; acceleration (rate of change of velocity); Newton's laws of motion — 1st (inertia), 2nd (F = ma), 3rd (action-reaction); momentum (p = mv); impulse.
  • Gravitation: Universal law of gravitation (F = Gm₁m₂/r²); acceleration due to gravity (g ≈ 9.8 m/s²); weight vs mass; satellites — geostationary, polar; escape velocity (11.2 km/s), orbital velocity.
  • Work, Energy, Power: Work = force × displacement; kinetic energy (½mv²), potential energy (mgh); conservation of energy; power = work/time (watt).
  • Heat and Thermodynamics: Temperature scales (Celsius, Fahrenheit, Kelvin); specific heat; latent heat; modes of heat transfer — conduction, convection, radiation; laws of thermodynamics.
  • Sound: Longitudinal wave; speed of sound (~343 m/s in air); reflection — echo; audible range (20 Hz–20 kHz), infrasonic (<20 Hz), ultrasonic (>20 kHz); SONAR.
  • Light: Reflection (laws of reflection), refraction (Snell's law), dispersion (spectrum VIBGYOR), total internal reflection; lenses (convex/concave), mirrors (plane, concave, convex); human eye defects — myopia, hypermetropia, presbyopia, cataracts.
  • Electricity: Current (I = Q/t), voltage, resistance (Ohm's law V = IR), series/parallel circuits, heating effect (H = I²Rt), electric power (P = VI), fuse, earthing, domestic circuits.
  • Magnetism: Magnetic field, electromagnetism, electromagnetic induction (Faraday's law), electric motor, generator, transformer.
  • Units: SI units — metre (length), kilogram (mass), second (time), ampere (current), kelvin (temperature), mole (amount), candela (luminous intensity); derived units — newton (force), joule (energy), watt (power), volt, ohm, ampere, coulomb.

Chemistry — Basics

  • Matter: States — solid, liquid, gas, plasma; changes of state; purity (pure substance vs mixture); elements, compounds, mixtures (homogeneous/heterogeneous).
  • Atomic structure: Dalton's atomic theory; Rutherford, Bohr models; electron (negative), proton (positive), neutron (neutral); atomic number (Z = protons), mass number (A = protons + neutrons), isotopes (same Z, different A), isobars (same A, different Z).
  • Periodic classification: Mendeleev's periodic table (atomic mass), modern periodic table (atomic number); groups (vertical) and periods (horizontal); metals, non-metals, metalloids; valency.
  • Chemical reactions: Types — combination, decomposition, displacement, double displacement, oxidation-reduction (redox); exothermic (heat released) and endothermic (heat absorbed); chemical equation balancing; catalysts.
  • Acids, bases, salts: Acids (turn blue litmus red, sour taste), bases (turn red litmus blue, bitter, soapy), salts (neutralisation: acid + base → salt + water); pH scale (0–14, 7 neutral); acids pH < 7, bases pH > 7; indicators (litmus, phenolphthalein, methyl orange).
  • Carbon and its compounds: Catenation, tetravalency; homologous series; functional groups; ethanol, ethanoic acid; soaps and detergents (cleansing action, micelle).
  • Metals and non-metals: Physical properties (malleable, ductile, sonorous, good conductors — metals); reactivity series; extraction of metals; corrosion and prevention (galvanisation, painting, alloying — steel, brass, bronze).
  • Common compounds: Water (H₂O), common salt (NaCl), washing soda (Na₂CO₃·10H₂O), baking soda (NaHCO₃), bleaching powder (CaOCl₂), plaster of Paris (CaSO₄·½H₂O), quicklime (CaO), slaked lime (Ca(OH)₂).

Biology — Basics

  • Cell: Basic unit of life; prokaryotic (bacteria, no nucleus) vs eukaryotic (plants/animals, nucleus); cell organelles — cell wall (plants), cell membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus, mitochondria (powerhouse), chloroplast (plants, photosynthesis), ribosomes (protein synthesis), Golgi apparatus, lysosomes (suicide bags), vacuole.
  • Tissues: Plant — meristematic (dividing), permanent (parenchyma, collenchyma, sclerenchyma, xylem, phloem); Animal — epithelial, connective, muscular, nervous.
  • Nutrition: Autotrophic (plants, photosynthesis), heterotrophic (animals); modes — holozoic (humans), saprophytic (fungi), parasitic (tapeworm); human digestive system — enzymes, absorption, assimilation.
  • Respiration: Aerobic (with oxygen, releases more energy) vs anaerobic (without oxygen, e.g., yeast, muscles during exercise); human respiratory system — breathing, gaseous exchange in alveoli.
  • Transportation: Plants — xylem (water/minerals up), phloem (food down); transpiration; Humans — blood (plasma, RBC, WBC, platelets), heart (4 chambers), arteries/veins/capillaries, double circulation, lymph.
  • Excretion: Removal of metabolic wastes; human — kidneys (nephrons — filtration unit), ureters, urinary bladder, urethra; sweat, lungs (CO₂).
  • Control and coordination: Plants — tropic movements (phototropism, geotropism), hormones (auxin, gibberellin, cytokinin, abscisic acid, ethylene); Animals — nervous system (brain, spinal cord, nerves, reflex arc), endocrine glands (pituitary, thyroid, adrenal, pancreas, gonads) — hormones (insulin, thyroxine, adrenaline).
  • Reproduction: Asexual (fission, budding, fragmentation, spore formation, vegetative propagation), sexual (plants — pollination, fertilisation; humans — male/female reproductive systems, menstrual cycle, fertilization, embryo, placenta).
  • Heredity and Evolution: Mendel's laws (dominance, segregation, independent assortment); genes, DNA, chromosomes; evolution — Darwin (natural selection), Lamarck (use/disuse), human evolution stages.
  • Environment: Ecosystem (biotic + abiotic), food chains/food webs, energy flow (10% law), ozone layer depletion, waste management (biodegradable/non-biodegradable), sustainable development.
  • Diseases: Communicable (bacteria — TB, cholera; virus — flu, COVID, dengue; protozoa — malaria) vs non-communicable (diabetes, cancer, hypertension); vaccines, antibiotics (penicillin), immunisation.

Human Body — Key Facts

  • Bones: Adult human — 206 bones; longest bone — femur; smallest bone — stapes (ear).
  • Blood: Groups — A, B, AB, O; universal donor — O negative; universal recipient — AB positive; RBC lifespan ~120 days; WBC — immunity; platelets — clotting.
  • Heart: ~72 beats/min, 4 chambers (2 auricles/atria, 2 ventricles), pacemaker — SA node, blood pressure normal ~120/80 mmHg.
  • Brain: Cerebrum (thinking/memory), cerebellum (balance/coordination), medulla oblongata (involuntary — heartbeat, breathing).
  • Senses: Eye — retina (rods/cones), cornea, lens; Ear — cochlea (hearing), semicircular canals (balance); Nose — olfactory; Tongue — taste (sweet, sour, salty, bitter, umami); Skin — touch, temperature.

Space Technology

  • ISRO (Indian Space Research Organisation): Founded 1969, headquarters Bengaluru; first Indian satellite Aryabhata (1975); SLV-3 (1980); PSLV (workhorse), GSLV (heavy lifts, cryogenic), SSLV.
  • Moon missions: Chandrayaan-1 (2008 — water on Moon), Chandrayaan-2 (2019 — orbiter + lander Vikram attempt), Chandrayaan-3 (2023 — soft landing near south pole — first country).
  • Mars: Mangalyaan / Mars Orbiter Mission (2013 — first Asian nation, first attempt success).
  • Sun: Aditya-L1 (2023 — solar mission at L1 point).
  • Human spaceflight: Gaganyaan (planned), Vyommitra (humanoid).
  • Navigation: NavIC (Indian Regional Navigation Satellite System).
  • Rockets/satellites: GSAT (communication), IRS (remote sensing), INSAT; spaceports — Sriharikota (SHAR).
  • International: NASA (USA), Roscosmos (Russia), ESA (Europe), CNSA (China), JAXA (Japan); ISS; Hubble, James Webb telescopes.
Technique: Newton's laws
Question: Which law explains why a passenger falls forward when a moving bus stops suddenly?
Answer: Newton's First Law of Motion (law of inertia) — the passenger's body tends to remain in motion while the bus stops.
Technique: pH identification
Question: A solution turns blue litmus red. It is
Answer: Acidic (acids turn blue litmus red; bases turn red litmus blue).

Common Traps

  • Confusing mass (constant) with weight (varies with gravity).
  • Mixing up convex lens (converging) and concave lens (diverging).
  • Assuming all isotopes are radioactive — some are stable.
  • Forgetting that aerobic respiration releases more energy than anaerobic.
  • Mixing up xylem (water upward) and phloem (food downward) in plants.

Memory Tricks

  • Newton's laws: 1st = inertia, 2nd = F=ma, 3rd = action-reaction.
  • Cell organelles: Mitochondria = powerhouse; Ribosomes = protein synthesis; Lysosomes = suicide bags.
  • Blood groups: O− = universal donor; AB+ = universal recipient.
  • SI units: Newton (force), Joule (energy), Watt (power).
NDA GAT Science strategy
NDA science questions are NCERT-level factual. Focus on definitions, SI units, human body facts, ISRO missions, and common chemical compounds. Diagram-based questions are rare; most are direct recall. Elimination works well.
