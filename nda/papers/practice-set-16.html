Q1. General Knowledge
Under the Indian Constitution, the power to legislate on 'Inter-State Trade and Commerce' resides with:
A. The State Legislature exclusively
B. The Union Parliament exclusively
C. Both Parliament and State Legislature (Concurrent List)
D. Neither; it is a residuary subject
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: Article 301-307 (Part XIII) deal with Trade/Commerce. 'Inter-State' implies movement across borders, a classic Union subject (Entry 42, Union List) to ensure economic unity. 'Intra-State' trade is State subject (Entry 26, State List). Option C (Concurrent) applies to subjects like Education, Forests. Detailed: Article 246 read with Seventh Schedule. Union List Entry 42: 'Inter-State trade and commerce'. Parliament has exclusive power (Article 246(1)). State List Entry 26 covers 'Trade and commerce within the State'. The Constitution (Articles 301-304) guarantees freedom of inter-state trade subject to Parliament's regulatory power.

Correct Answer: C

Q11. General Knowledge
The phenomenon of 'Ocean Acidification' is primarily caused by the absorption of which gas by seawater?
A. Methane (CH4)
B. Nitrous Oxide (N2O)
C. Carbon Dioxide (CO2)
D. Sulfur Dioxide (SO2)
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut: Link 'Acidification' to 'Carbonic Acid'. CO2 + H2O -> H2CO3 (Carbonic Acid). This lowers pH. Methane/N2O are potent GHGs but don't dissolve to form strong acids in ocean volumes. SO2 causes acid rain (atmospheric), not primary driver of ocean acidification. Detailed: Oceans absorb ~30% of anthropogenic CO2 emissions. Dissolved CO2 reacts with water (H2O) to form carbonic acid (H2CO3), which dissociates into bicarbonate (HCO3-) and hydrogen ions (H+). Increased H+ concentration lowers pH (acidification) and reduces carbonate ion (CO3 2-) availability, harming calcifying organisms (corals, shellfish).

In part (B), the verb 'has submitted' is singular. However, the nearest subject is 'his subordinates', which is plural. Therefore, the correct verb should be 'have submitted'. Q15. General Knowledge
Which of the following phenomena explains the formation of the 'Thar Desert' in Rajasthan?
A. Rain shadow effect of the Western Ghats
B. Cold ocean current along the western coast
C. Divergence of the Arabian Sea branch of the Monsoon and the Aravalli range running parallel to it
D. High pressure belt permanently situated over North-West India
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut: Eliminate geographical mismatches. Western Ghats (Option A) affect the Deccan plateau, not Rajasthan. Cold currents (Option B) affect coastal deserts (Atacama/Namib), not inland Thar. Permanent High Pressure (Option D) is a general subtropical feature, not the specific dynamic cause for Thar's aridity. The specific mechanism is the Aravalli range running SW-NE, parallel to the SW Monsoon winds (Arabian Sea branch), preventing orographic lift and causing rain shadow/divergence. Full Solution: The Aravalli Hills run parallel to the direction of the Arabian Sea branch of the South-West Monsoon (South-West to North-East). They do not act as a barrier to intercept the moisture-laden winds. Consequently, the winds pass over Rajasthan without significant orographic rainfall, creating the Thar Desert. This is a classic case of 'Parallel Range Effect' rather than a standard Rain Shadow (which requires perpendicular ranges).
