Affine Grassmannians — the Loop Group, the Affine Flag Variety & the Geometric Satake

The affine Grassmannian Gr_G = G(K)/G(O) for a reductive group G over a field k, with K = k((t)) and O = k[[t]], is the central geometric object of the geometric Langlands program. It is an ind-scheme — an infinite union of projective schemes — that parametrizes G-bundles on the formal disk trivialized on the punctured disk. The loop group LG = G(K) is the group of Laurent-series-valued points of G, and the positive loop group L⁺G = G(O) is the subgroup of Taylor-series-valued points. The Cartan decomposition Gr_G = ∐_{λ∈X_*(T)_+} G(O)·t^λ decomposes the affine Grassmannian into Schubert cells indexed by dominant coweights. The geometric Satake equivalence (Mirković-Vilonen, Beilinson-Drinfeld, Ginzburg) is a categorical equivalence between G(O)-equivariant perverse sheaves on Gr_G and representations of the Langlands dual group Ĝ — a deep geometric realization of the classical Satake isomorphism. Mirković-Vilonen cycles and MV polytopes give a geometric basis for tensor product representations, with applications to the local Langlands correspondence, the theory of automorphic forms, and integrable systems via the Hitchin system and the quantum Knizhnik-Zamolodchikov equations.

Part 1

📏 The Loop Group and the Affine Grassmannian

Foundations
Loop Groups, the Affine Grassmannian, and the Cartan Decomposition
Let G be a connected reductive group over a field k. Define the field of formal Laurent series K = k((t)) and the ring of formal Taylor series O = k[[t]]. The loop group LG = G(K) is the group of K-points of G — its elements are formal Laurent loops γ: Spec K → G. The positive loop group L⁺G = G(O) is the subgroup of O-points — Taylor loops that extend regularly over the formal disk. The affine Grassmannian is the quotient Gr_G = LG / L⁺G = G(K) / G(O). It is an ind-scheme: an ind-projective ind-scheme, meaning it is a direct limit Gr_G = lim⟶ Gr_G^(≤λ) of projective schemes Gr_G^(≤λ) = G(O)·t^λ. For a maximal torus T ⊂ G, let X_*(T) be the coweight lattice and X_*(T)_+ the set of dominant coweights. The Cartan decomposition (or Iwasawa decomposition) gives Gr_G = ∐_{λ∈X_*(T)_+} G(O)·t^λ. Each Schubert cell Gr_G^λ = G(O)·t^λ is a finite-dimensional locally closed subscheme of dimension ⟨2ρ, λ⟩ where ρ is the half-sum of positive roots. The affine Schubert variety Gr_G^λ is the closure Gr_G^λ = ∪_{μ≤λ} Gr_G^μ (under the Bruhat order). The topology of Gr_G is governed by the affine Weyl group W_aff = W ⋉ X_*(T). The L⁺G-orbits on Gr_G are in bijection with dominant coweights. The affine flag variety Fl_G = G(K)/I where I ⊂ G(O) is the Iwahori subgroup — the preimage of a Borel B ⊂ G under evaluation at t=0 — refines the Schubert stratification of Gr_G.
The affine Grassmannian Gr_G = G(K)/G(O) is an infinite-dimensional projective ind-scheme whose Schubert cells are indexed by dominant coweights; it parametrizes G-bundles on the formal disk with a trivialization on the punctured disk.
Part 2

🌍 The Geometric Satake Equivalence

Satake
The Classical Satake Isomorphism and the Geometric Satake Equivalence
The classical Satake isomorphism (Satake, 1963): the spherical Hecke algebra H(G(O)\G(K)/G(O)) of compactly supported G(O)-bi-invariant functions on G(K) under convolution is isomorphic to the representation ring Rep(Ĝ) of the Langlands dual group Ĝ over ℂ. Explicitly, for each dominant coweight λ, the characteristic function f_λ of the double coset G(O)·t^λ·G(O) corresponds to the character of the irreducible representation V_λ of Ĝ. The geometric Satake equivalence (Lusztig, Ginzburg, Mirković-Vilonen, 1995–2007) lifts this to a categorical level: the category Perv_{G(O)}(Gr_G) of G(O)-equivariant perverse sheaves on Gr_G (with coefficients in ℂ or ℚ̄_ℓ), equipped with a convolution product ⋆, is equivalent as a tensor category to the category Rep(Ĝ) of finite-dimensional algebraic representations of Ĝ. The key correspondence: the intersection cohomology complex IC_λ = IC(Gr_G^λ) — the intermediate extension of the constant sheaf on the Schubert cell Gr_G^λ — corresponds under the Satake functor S: Perv_{G(O)}(Gr_G) → Rep(Ĝ) to the irreducible representation V_λ of Ĝ with highest weight λ. The convolution product F ⋆ G of two perverse sheaves is defined via the convolution diagram Gr_G ×̃ Gr_G → Gr_G, where Gr_G ×̃ Gr_G = LG ×^{L⁺G} Gr_G is the twisted product. The fusion product (Beilinson-Drinfeld) realizes the convolution via the Beilinson-Drinfeld Grassmannian Gr_{G, X^2} over the square of the curve — giving the factorization structure. The Mirković-Vilonen theorem (2007): the geometric Satake functor is an equivalence of tensor categories, with the commutativity constraint on Rep(Ĝ) corresponding to the fusion product commutativity on the geometric side.
The geometric Satake equivalence identifies G(O)-equivariant perverse sheaves on the affine Grassmannian with representations of the Langlands dual group — a geometric Langlands correspondence for the formal disk.
Part 3

🧮 Mirković-Vilonen Cycles and MV Polytopes

MV Cycles
Mirković-Vilonen Cycles, MV Polytopes, and Canonical Bases
For a dominant coweight λ and a generic point in the (affine) building, the intersection of the Schubert cell Gr_G^λ with a (co)minuscule cell yields an algebraic cycle — the Mirković-Vilonen cycle (MV cycle). More precisely, for a generic cocharacter η: 𝔾_m → T such that ⟨α, η⟩ ≫ 0 for all positive roots α, the fixed-point set (Gr_G^λ)^{η(𝔾_m)} is finite. The MV cycles define a basis {m_λ,γ} of each irreducible representation V_λ of Ĝ (the MV basis), indexed by the components of the fixed-point set. The MV polytope P_λ associated to a tensor product basis vector in V_λ ⊗ V_μ is a convex polytope in the coweight space X_*(T) ⊗ ℝ — it encodes the eigenvalues of the torus action on the corresponding MV cycle. The Anderson-Mirković conjecture (2002) states that the set of MV polytopes coincides with the set of Lusztig's canonical basis polytopes (the string polytopes arising from the canonical basis of quantum groups). This was proved by Baumann–Kamnitzer–Tingley (2018) using the theory of crystal bases and the geometric Satake equivalence. The MV polytopes satisfy the Mirković-Vilonen conjecture (now theorem): the number of MV cycles for the tensor product V_λ ⊗ V_μ equals the dimension of the space of invariants (Hom_{Ĝ}(V_ν, V_λ ⊗ V_μ)). The Beilinson-Drinfeld Grassmannian Gr_{G,X^n} over X^n provides the relative (global) setting: it parametrizes G-bundles on X with trivializations on the complement of n points. The factorization property of Gr_{G,X^n} — the fiber over (x_1,…,x_n) is the product of Gr_G at each x_i when the points are distinct — is crucial for the construction of the fusion product and for the global affine Grassmannian (the Beilinson-Drinfeld lattice model).
MV cycles give a geometric realization of the canonical basis of Ġ-representations; MV polytopes are convex polytopes encoding the torus weights of the geometric Satake correspondence.
Part 4

🔥 Applications: Langlands Program and Integrable Systems

Langlands
The Local Langlands Correspondence, the Geometric Langlands, and Integrable Systems
The local Langlands correspondence for unramified representations — the classification of irreducible admissible representations of G(K) that are G(O)-spherical (contain a G(O)-fixed vector) — follows from the classical Satake isomorphism: spherical representations are parametrized by semisimple conjugacy classes in Ĝ. The geometric Satake equivalence provides the categorical foundation for the geometric Langlands program: the spectral decomposition of the category of sheaves on Bun_G (the moduli stack of G-bundles on a smooth projective curve X) via the Langlands dual group Ĝ. The Kazhdan-Lusztig theorem: the representation theory of the affine Lie algebra ĝ at the critical level is equivalent to the geometry of the affine Grassmannian — specifically, the center of the critical level enveloping algebra is isomorphic to the algebra of functions on the space of Ĝ-opers, via the Satake isomorphism for the affine Grassmannian. In the theory of integrable systems, the Beilinson-Drinfeld construction of the classical r-matrix for the Gaudin model arises from the geometry of the affine Grassmannian. The quantum Knizhnik-Zamolodchikov equations (qKZ) — difference equations satisfied by correlation functions in the XXZ spin chain — are solved via the geometric Satake: the qKZ solutions are given by the Baxter Q-operators constructed from the geometric Satake correspondence (the Q-operators are traces of the universal R-matrix on the MV basis). The Hitchin integrable system — the moduli space of Higgs bundles on X with the Hitchin map — is dual under Langlands duality: the spectral curve of a G-Higgs bundle is a Ĝ-Higgs bundle on the curve, and the affine Grassmannian governs the Lax pairs for the Hitchin system. Maffei's theorem: the representation theory of quivers (Nakajima quiver varieties) is realized via the affine Grassmannian of GL_n — quiver varieties appear as moduli spaces of framed torsion-free sheaves on the projective plane, which are related to the affine Grassmannian by the ADHM construction.
The affine Grassmannian and the geometric Satake equivalence are the geometric engine behind the local Langlands correspondence, the geometric Langlands program, and the theory of integrable systems — from the Hitchin system to the quantum KZ equations.
