Affine Grassmannians — the Loop Group, the Affine Flag Variety & the Geometric Satake

The affine Grassmannian Gr_G = G(K)/G(O) for a reductive group G over a field k, with K = k((t)) and O = k[[t]], is the central geometric object of the geometric Langlands program. It is an ind-scheme — an infinite union of projective schemes — that parametrizes G-bundles on the formal disk trivialized on the punctured disk. The loop group LG = G(K) is the group of Laurent-series-valued points of G, and the positive loop group L⁺G = G(O) is the subgroup of Taylor-series-valued points. The Cartan decomposition Gr_G = ∐_{λ∈X_*(T)_+} G(O)·t^λ decomposes the affine Grassmannian into Schubert cells indexed by dominant coweights. The geometric Satake equivalence (Mirković-Vilonen, Beilinson-Drinfeld, Ginzburg) is a categorical equivalence between G(O)-equivariant perverse sheaves on Gr_G and representations of the Langlands dual group Ĝ — a deep geometric realization of the classical Satake isomorphism. Mirković-Vilonen cycles and MV polytopes give a geometric basis for tensor product representations, with applications to the local Langlands correspondence, the theory of automorphic forms, and integrable systems via the Hitchin system and the quantum Knizhnik-Zamolodchikov equations.

Part 1

📏 The Loop Group and the Affine Grassmannian

Foundations
Loop Groups, the Affine Grassmannian, and the Cartan Decomposition
Let G be a connected reductive group over a field k. Define the field of formal Laurent series K = k((t)) and the ring of formal Taylor series O = k[[t]]. The loop group LG = G(K) is the group of K-points of G — its elements are formal Laurent loops γ: Spec K → G. The positive loop group L⁺G = G(O) is the subgroup of O-points — Taylor loops that extend regularly over the formal disk. The affine Grassmannian is the quotient Gr_G = LG / L⁺G = G(K) / G(O). It is an ind-scheme: an ind-projective ind-scheme, meaning it is a direct limit Gr_G = lim⟶ Gr_G^(≤λ) of projective schemes Gr_G^(≤λ) = G(O)·t^λ. For a maximal torus T ⊂ G, let X_*(T) be the coweight lattice and X_*(T)_+ the set of dominant coweights. The Cartan decomposition (or Iwasawa decomposition) gives Gr_G = ∐_{λ∈X_*(T)_+} G(O)·t^λ. Each Schubert cell Gr_G^λ = G(O)·t^λ is a finite-dimensional locally closed subscheme of dimension ⟨2ρ, λ⟩ where ρ is the half-sum of positive roots. The affine Schubert variety Gr_G^λ is the closure Gr_G^λ = ∪_{μ≤λ} Gr_G^μ (under the Bruhat order). The topology of Gr_G is governed by the affine Weyl group W_aff = W ⋉ X_*(T). The L⁺G-orbits on Gr_G are in bijection with dominant coweights. The affine flag variety Fl_G = G(K)/I where I ⊂ G(O) is the Iwahori subgroup — the preimage of a Borel B ⊂ G under evaluation at t=0 — refines the Schubert stratification of Gr_G.
The affine Grassmannian Gr_G = G(K)/G(O) is an infinite-dimensional projective ind-scheme whose Schubert cells are indexed by dominant coweights; it parametrizes G-bundles on the formal disk with a trivialization on the punctured disk.
Part 2

🌍 The Geometric Satake Equivalence

Satake
The Classical Satake Isomorphism and the Geometric Satake Equivalence
The classical Satake isomorphism (Satake, 1963): the spherical Hecke algebra H(G(O)\G(K)/G(O)) of compactly supported G(O)-bi-invariant functions on G(K) under convolution is isomorphic to the representation ring Rep(Ĝ) of the Langlands dual group Ĝ over ℂ. Explicitly, for each dominant coweight λ, the characteristic function f_λ of the double coset G(O)·t^λ·G(O) corresponds to the character of the irreducible representation V_λ of Ĝ. The geometric Satake equivalence (Lusztig, Ginzburg, Mirković-Vilonen, 1995–2007) lifts this to a categorical level: the category Perv_{G(O)}(Gr_G) of G(O)-equivariant perverse sheaves on Gr_G (with coefficients in ℂ or ℚ̄_ℓ), equipped with a convolution product ⋆, is equivalent as a tensor category to the category Rep(Ĝ) of finite-dimensional algebraic representations of Ĝ. The key correspondence: the intersection cohomology complex IC_λ = IC(Gr_G^λ) — the intermediate extension of the constant sheaf on the Schubert cell Gr_G^λ — corresponds under the Satake functor S: Perv_{G(O)}(Gr_G) → Rep(Ĝ) to the irreducible representation V_λ of Ĝ with highest weight λ. The convolution product F ⋆ G of two perverse sheaves is defined via the convolution diagram Gr_G ×̃ Gr_G → Gr_G, where Gr_G ×̃ Gr_G = LG ×^{L⁺G} Gr_G is the twisted product. The fusion product (Beilinson-Drinfeld) realizes the convolution via the Beilinson-Drinfeld Grassmannian Gr_{G, X^2} over the square of the curve — giving the factorization structure. The Mirković-Vilonen theorem (2007): the geometric Satake functor is an equivalence of tensor categories, with the commutativity constraint on Rep(Ĝ) corresponding to the fusion product commutativity on the geometric side.
The geometric Satake equivalence identifies G(O)-equivariant perverse sheaves on the affine Grassmannian with representations of the Langlands dual group — a geometric Langlands correspondence for the formal disk.
Part 3

🧮 Mirković-Vilonen Cycles and MV Polytopes

MV Cycles
Mirković-Vilonen Cycles, MV Polytopes, and Canonical Bases
For a dominant coweight λ and a generic point in the (affine) building, the intersection of the Schubert cell Gr_G^λ with a (co)minuscule cell yields an algebraic cycle — the Mirković-Vilonen cycle (MV cycle). More precisely, for a generic cocharacter η: 𝔾_m → T such that ⟨α, η⟩ ≫ 0 for all positive roots α, the fixed-point set (Gr_G^λ)^{η(𝔾_m)} is finite. The MV cycles define a basis {m_λ,γ} of each irreducible representation V_λ of Ĝ (the MV basis), indexed by the components of the fixed-point set. The MV polytope P_λ associated to a tensor product basis vector in V_λ ⊗ V_μ is a convex polytope in the coweight space X_*(T) ⊗ ℝ — it encodes the eigenvalues of the torus action on the corresponding MV cycle. The Anderson-Mirković conjecture (2002) states that the set of MV polytopes coincides with the set of Lusztig's canonical basis polytopes (the string polytopes arising from the canonical basis of quantum groups). This was proved by Baumann–Kamnitzer–Tingley (2018) using the theory of crystal bases and the geometric Satake equivalence. The MV polytopes satisfy the Mirković-Vilonen conjecture (now theorem): the number of MV cycles for the tensor product V_λ ⊗ V_μ equals the dimension of the space of invariants (Hom_{Ĝ}(V_ν, V_λ ⊗ V_μ)). The Beilinson-Drinfeld Grassmannian Gr_{G,X^n} over X^n provides the relative (global) setting: it parametrizes G-bundles on X with trivializations on the complement of n points. The factorization property of Gr_{G,X^n} — the fiber over (x_1,…,x_n) is the product of Gr_G at each x_i when the points are distinct — is crucial for the construction of the fusion product and for the global affine Grassmannian (the Beilinson-Drinfeld lattice model).
MV cycles give a geometric realization of the canonical basis of Ġ-representations; MV polytopes are convex polytopes encoding the torus weights of the geometric Satake correspondence.

🔥 Applications: Langlands Program and Integrable Systems

Langlands
The Local Langlands Correspondence, the Geometric Langlands, and Integrable Systems
The local Langlands correspondence for unramified representations — the classification of irreducible admissible representations of G(K) that are G(O)-spherical (contain a G(O)-fixed vector) — follows from the classical Satake isomorphism: spherical representations are parametrized by semisimple conjugacy classes in Ĝ. The geometric Satake equivalence provides the categorical foundation for the geometric Langlands program: the spectral decomposition of the category of sheaves on Bun_G (the moduli stack of G-bundles on a smooth projective curve X) via the Langlands dual group Ĝ. The Kazhdan-Lusztig theorem: the representation theory of the affine Lie algebra ĝ at the critical level is equivalent to the geometry of the affine Grassmannian — specifically, the center of the critical level enveloping algebra is isomorphic to the algebra of functions on the space of Ĝ-opers, via the Satake isomorphism for the affine Grassmannian. In the theory of integrable systems, the Beilinson-Drinfeld construction of the classical r-matrix for the Gaudin model arises from the geometry of the affine Grassmannian. The quantum Knizhnik-Zamolodchikov equations (qKZ) — difference equations satisfied by correlation functions in the XXZ spin chain — are solved via the geometric Satake: the qKZ solutions are given by the Baxter Q-operators constructed from the geometric Satake correspondence (the Q-operators are traces of the universal R-matrix on the MV basis). The Hitchin integrable system — the moduli space of Higgs bundles on X with the Hitchin map — is dual under Langlands duality: the spectral curve of a G-Higgs bundle is a Ĝ-Higgs bundle on the curve, and the affine Grassmannian governs the Lax pairs for the Hitchin system. Maffei's theorem: the representation theory of quivers (Nakajima quiver varieties) is realized via the affine Grassmannian of GL_n — quiver varieties appear as moduli spaces of framed torsion-free sheaves on the projective plane, which are related to the affine Grassmannian by the ADHM construction.
The affine Grassmannian and the geometric Satake equivalence are the geometric engine behind the local Langlands correspondence, the geometric Langlands program, and the theory of integrable systems — from the Hitchin system to the quantum KZ equations.

Affine Flag Variety, Iwahori Subgroups, and the Bruhat Decomposition

The affine flag variety Fl_G = G(K)/I is the quotient of the loop group by an Iwahori subgroup I ⊂ G(O), the preimage of a Borel subgroup B ⊂ G under the evaluation map G(O) → G at t = 0. While the affine Grassmannian Gr_G = G(K)/G(O) parametrizes G-bundles on the formal disc with a trivialization on the punctured disc, the affine flag variety parametrizes G-bundles equipped with a full reduction to a Borel at the origin. The Iwahori subgroup is a pro‑p group whose Lie algebra is the inverse image of the Borel subalgebra under the reduction map Lie G(O) → Lie G. The Bruhat decomposition for the affine flag variety reads Fl_G = ⨿_{w ∈ widetilde{W}} I w I / I, where widetilde{W} = X_*(T) ⋊ W is the extended affine Weyl group. Each cell I w I / I is isomorphic to an affine space of dimension ℓ(w), the length of w with respect to the affine simple reflections. The closure relations among these cells give the affine Schubert varieties X_w = I w I / I, which are normal, Cohen–Macaulay, and have rational singularities. The cohomology ring H^*(Fl_G) carries a natural action of the affine nil‑Hecke algebra, and the classes of the Schubert varieties form a basis dual to the Schubert basis of the homology of the affine Grassmannian under the convolution product. The projection π : Fl_G → Gr_G is a fibration with fiber the finite flag variety G/B; on cohomology it induces the restriction map from the affine nil‑Hecke algebra to the spherical Hecke algebra, which under the geometric Satake equivalence corresponds to the forgetful functor from Rep(Ĝ) to the category of vector spaces. The Iwahori–Matsumoto presentation of the affine Hecke algebra H_I with generators T_s for affine simple reflections and quadratic relations (T_s - q)(T_s + 1) = 0 governs the convolution algebra of I-equivariant perverse sheaves on Fl_G. This algebra is Morita equivalent to the spherical Hecke algebra HG(O), and the equivalence is realized geometrically by the nearby cycles functor on the Beilinson–Drinfeld Grassmannian. Understanding the affine flag variety is central to the categorical geometric Satake, where one replaces the spherical category by the Iwahori‑equivariant derived category, and to the study of modular representations of affine Kac–Moody algebras at the critical level.

Perverse Sheaves, the Satake Category, and the Derived Geometric Satake

The geometric Satake equivalence is an equivalence of tensor categories Sat : PervG(O)(Gr_G) → Rep(Ĝ), where the source is the category of G(O)-equivariant perverse sheaves on the affine Grassmannian (with coefficients in an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero) equipped with the convolution product, and the target is the category of finite‑dimensional representations of the Langlands dual group Ĝ. The simple objects in PervG(O)(Gr_G) are the intersection cohomology complexes IC_λ of the Schubert varieties Gr_G^λ indexed by dominant coweights λ ∈ X_*(T)+. The convolution product * on perverse sheaves is defined via the diagram Gr_G widetilde{×} Gr_G → Gr_G, where the twisted product Gr_G widetilde{×} Gr_G = G(K) ×G(O) Gr_G is a G(O)-torsor over Gr_G. The fiber of the convolution morphism over a point x ∈ Gr_G is a finite‑dimensional variety, and the properness of the map guarantees that the convolution of two perverse sheaves is again perverse. The tensor structure on Rep(Ĝ) corresponds under Sat to the convolution product, and the commutativity constraint comes from the factorization property of the Beilinson–Drinfeld Grassmannian. The derived geometric Satake extends this equivalence to an equivalence of derived categories D^bG(O)(Gr_G) ≃ D^b(Rep(Ĝ)) compatible with the t‑structures. In the derived setting the convolution product is defined using the derived pushforward, and the equivalence intertwines the standard and costandard t‑structures on both sides. The categorical geometric Satake further replaces the target by the 2‑category of module categories over Rep(Ĝ), and the source by the 2‑category of G(O)-equivariant categories of sheaves on Gr_G. This 2‑categorical version is the geometric incarnation of the fact that the spherical Hecke algebra is the endomorphism algebra of the trivial representation in Rep(Ĝ). The perverse sheaf perspective underpins the proof of the Mirković–Vilonen conjecture, the construction of the canonical basis via MV cycles, and the study of the categorical action of the affine braid group on D^bG(O)(Gr_G) arising from the nearby cycles functor on the Beilinson–Drinfeld Grassmannian.

Worked Example: Convolution of IC Sheaves
Let G = SL2 and let λ, μ be the fundamental coweight. Compute the convolution IC_λ * IC_μ in PervG(O)(Gr_G) and identify the corresponding tensor product in Rep(Ĝ).
For SL2 the affine Grassmannian has Schubert varieties Grnλ indexed by n ≥ 0. The fundamental coweight λ corresponds to the standard representation V of Ĝ = PGL2. The intersection cohomology complex IC_λ is the constant sheaf on the smooth Schubert variety Gr^λ ≅ ℙ1 shifted by dim = 1. The convolution diagram yields IC_λ * IC_λ ≅ IC0 ⊕ IC2λ, where IC0 is the skyscraper at the base point. Under the Satake equivalence this matches the tensor product decomposition V ⊗ V ≅ 1 ⊕ Sym2 V in Rep(PGL2). The general rule is that the convolution of IC_λ and IC_μ decomposes as a direct sum of IC_ν with multiplicities equal to the Littlewood–Richardson coefficients for Ĝ.
Exam Strategy
When a question asks for the geometric Satake equivalence, state the equivalence of tensor categories, name the simple objects as intersection cohomology complexes of Schubert varieties, and mention the convolution product defined via the twisted product. If the question is about the derived version, emphasize the compatibility of t‑structures and the role of the Beilinson–Drinfeld Grassmannian in providing the commutativity constraint. Remember that the affine flag variety and Iwahori‑equivariant sheaves give the categorical lift; a short sentence referencing the affine nil‑Hecke algebra earns extra credit.

Factorization, the Beilinson–Drinfeld Grassmannian, and Fusion Products

The Beilinson–Drinfeld Grassmannian GrG,X^n over the n‑fold product of a smooth projective curve X is a relative version of the affine Grassmannian that encodes the factorization property of G-bundles with trivializations away from a finite set of points. For a tuple (x1,…,x_n) ∈ X^n with distinct coordinates, the fiber is canonically isomorphic to the product Gr_G^n. When points collide, the fiber degenerates to a convolution variety that realizes the fusion product of representations of the loop group. The factorization isomorphism follows from the Ran space construction: the Grassmannian over the Ran space Ran(X) is a factorization algebra in the sense of Beilinson–Drinfeld, and its chiral homology computes the space of conformal blocks for the affine Kac–Moody algebra at the critical level. The fusion product on the category PervG(O)(Gr_G) is defined by taking the nearby cycles of the constant sheaf along the diagonal embedding X → X^n; it coincides with the convolution product after applying the geometric Satake equivalence. This construction yields a braided tensor category structure on Rep(Ĝ) that matches the braiding coming from the universal R‑matrix of the quantum group U_q(Ĝ) at a root of unity. The factorization property also underlies the construction of the classical r‑matrix for the Gaudin model: the residue of the connection on the universal G-bundle over GrG,X^n gives a meromorphic 1‑form with values in 𝔤 ⊗ 𝔤 satisfying the classical Yang–Baxter equation. In the quantum setting, the quantum Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov equations arise as the flatness condition for the KZ connection on the bundle of conformal blocks over the configuration space of points on X. The solutions are expressed in terms of the Baxter Q‑operators, which are traces of the universal R‑matrix on the MV basis of representations. The Beilinson–Drinfeld Grassmannian thus provides the geometric backbone linking the affine Grassmannian, the geometric Satake equivalence, and the theory of integrable systems.

Quantum Groups, Crystal Bases, and the MV Polytope Realization

The geometric Satake equivalence identifies the tensor category PervG(O)(Gr_G) with Rep(Ĝ). When the coefficient field is replaced by a field of characteristic zero equipped with a parameter q, the spherical Hecke algebra deforms to the quantum group U_q(Ĝ). The MV cycles give a geometric realization of Lusztig's canonical basis of U_q(Ĝ)-modules. For a dominant weight λ, the set of MV cycles in the fiber of the convolution morphism over a generic point of Gr_G^λ is in bijection with the canonical basis of the irreducible representation V_λ. The MV polytope associated to a basis vector is a convex polytope in the coweight space X_*(T) ⊗ ℝ whose vertices are the weights of the torus action on the corresponding MV cycle. The Anderson–Mirković conjecture, established by Baumann–Kamnitzer–Tingley (2018), states that the collection of MV polytopes coincides with the set of Lusztig's string polytopes arising from the canonical basis of the quantum group. The proof uses the crystal graph structure on the set of MV polytopes: the Kashiwara operators e_i, f_i act by moving along edges of the polytope, and the crystal isomorphism with the abstract crystal B(λ) is established by comparing the tropical Plücker relations satisfied by the MV polytopes with the defining relations of the crystal basis. The MV polytopes also satisfy the tropicalized version of the Berenstein–Kazhdan potential, which provides a birational lift of the crystal operators to the group Ĝ. In the context of the geometric Langlands program, the MV basis corresponds to the basis of Hecke eigensheaves on the moduli stack Bun_G constructed via the spectral decomposition. The polytopal description makes the combinatorics of tensor product multiplicities transparent: the number of MV cycles in the tensor product V_λ ⊗ V_μ equals the dimension of the invariant space HomĜ(V_ν, V_λ ⊗ V_μ), which is the number of integer points in the intersection of the corresponding MV polytopes. This polytope model is a powerful tool for explicit computations in representation theory and for the study of the quantum Satake correspondence.

Non‑split Groups, Parahoric Subgroups, and the Generalized Affine Grassmannian

For a reductive group G over a non‑archimedean local field F that is not split, the affine Grassmannian is defined as the fpqc sheaf Gr_G = G(F)/G(O_F), where O_F is the ring of integers. The Bruhat–Tits building of G parametrizes parahoric subgroups P ⊂ G(F), which are the stabilizers of facets in the building. The generalized affine Grassmannian GrG,P = G(F)/P is a projective ind‑variety that fibers over the usual affine Grassmannian with fiber a partial flag variety for the reductive quotient of P. The geometric Satake equivalence extends to this setting: the category of P-equivariant perverse sheaves on GrG,P is equivalent to the category of representations of the Langlands dual group Ĝ equipped with a compatible action of the component group of the centralizer of the associated Langlands parameter. The Iwahori subgroup corresponds to the minimal parahoric, and the affine flag variety Fl_G = G(F)/I is the generalized affine Grassmannian for I. For a ramified group, the extended affine Weyl group is replaced by the relative affine Weyl group widetilde{W} = X_*(T)Γ ⋊ W, where Γ is the Galois group of the splitting field. The Schubert varieties are indexed by the dominant coweights in the coinvariant lattice, and their geometry reflects the non‑split nature of G through the action of the Frobenius on the root datum. The Beilinson–Drinfeld Grassmannian for a non‑split group is constructed over the Ran space of a curve with a G-torsor, and its factorization property involves the Langlands dual group Ĝ with the appropriate Galois action. This generalized framework is essential for the formulation of the local Langlands correspondence for arbitrary reductive groups and for the geometric Langlands program over curves with non‑trivial G-bundles.

  • Assuming the geometric Satake equivalence holds for arbitrary coefficients without specifying characteristic zero and algebraic closure; the equivalence fails in positive characteristic without additional hypotheses.
  • Mixing up the convolution product on Gr_G with the fusion product on the Beilinson–Drinfeld Grassmannian; they coincide after applying the Satake equivalence but are defined on different spaces.
  • Thinking MV cycles exist for every coweight without the genericity condition on the cocharacter η; the fixed‑point set (Gr_G^λ)η(𝔾_m) is finite only for sufficiently regular η.
  • Believing the Anderson–Mirković conjecture is still open; it was established in 2018 by Baumann–Kamnitzer–Tingley using crystal bases.
  • Overlooking the role of parahoric subgroups for non‑split groups; the affine Grassmannian for a ramified group is not simply G(F)/G(O_F) but requires a choice of parahoric to obtain a projective ind‑variety.
  • Neglecting the distinction between the spherical Hecke algebra and the affine Hecke algebra; the former is commutative and corresponds to Rep(Ĝ), the latter is non‑commutative and governs Iwahori‑equivariant sheaves on Fl_G.

Convolution Product, Satake Isomorphism, and Derived Categories

The convolution product on the affine Grassmannian GrG is defined via the correspondence GrG × GrG ← GrG ⋉ GrG → GrG, where the middle term parametrizes pairs of lattices with fixed relative position. On G(O)-equivariant perverse sheaves, convolution gives the Satake category PG(O)(GrG) a monoidal structure. The geometric Satake equivalence identifies this monoidal category with Rep(Ĝ), sending convolution to tensor product. In the derived setting, one works with DbG(O)(GrG) and defines convolution via derived pushforward along the convolution diagram. The derived Satake equivalence (Bezrukavnikov–Finkelberg) states that the derived category of G(O)-equivariant constructible complexes on GrG is equivalent to the derived category of coherent sheaves on the Steinberg variety of Ĝ, compatibly with convolution and tensor product. This equivalence is crucial for the categorical geometric Langlands program, as it transports t-structures, perverse truncation, and the affine Hecke category action. Convolution is not strictly associative on the nose; it is associative up to coherent isomorphism, making the Satake category a monoidal ∞-category. The derived version resolves coherence by passing to dg- or stable ∞-categories. On Grothendieck groups, the Satake isomorphism recovers the classical isomorphism between the spherical Hecke algebra and the representation ring of Ĝ. In positive characteristic, the geometric Satake equivalence fails in general, but versions with parity sheaves or characteristic-zero coefficients hold. Examiners often test the distinction between the abelian Satake category (perverse sheaves) and the derived Satake category (constructible complexes), and the fact that convolution on perverse sheaves is exact only after taking perverse cohomology of the derived convolution.

Affine Springer Fibers and Their Cohomology

For a regular semisimple γ ∈ 𝔤(F) with G reductive over a non-archimedean local field F, the affine Springer fiber Xγ ⊂ GrG is the subscheme of lattices stabilized by γ. These are finite-dimensional projective varieties, typically singular, whose cohomology carries an action of the centralizer Jγ(F). They are local analogues of classical Springer fibers in the finite flag variety. The top-degree cohomology of Xγ realizes discrete series representations of G(F) via local Langlands, as conjectured by Kottwitz and proved in many cases by Ngô, Waldspurger, and others. Affine Springer fiber cohomology also appears in the geometric fundamental lemma: orbital integrals on G(F) match stable orbital integrals on an endoscopic group H by comparing point counts of affine Springer fibers over finite fields. The decomposition theorem for Xγ → pt yields a direct sum of intersection cohomology complexes corresponding to irreducible representations of the component group of Jγ. For GLn, affine Springer fibers relate to the Hilbert scheme of points on the affine line, with cohomology rings described by Goresky–Kottwitz–MacPherson theory. A common examiner trap: confusing the affine Springer fiber (subvariety of GrG, ind-projective, cohomology graded by loop rotation) with the classical Springer fiber (subvariety of the finite flag variety, projective, carries Weyl group action). The affine Springer fiber depends only on the G(F)-conjugacy class of γ, but its isomorphism type can change under stable conjugacy—the phenomenon measured by the fundamental lemma.

Worked Example: Convolution of IC Sheaves
Let G = SL2 over ℂ. The affine Grassmannian GrSL₂ has Schubert varieties Grλ indexed by dominant coweights λ = nω, where ω is the fundamental coweight. The intersection cohomology complex IC(Grλ) corresponds under geometric Satake to the irreducible representation Vλ of PGL2. Compute the convolution IC(Grω) ⋆ IC(Grω) and identify the resulting perverse sheaf.
The convolution diagram for GrSL₂ yields IC(Grω) ⋆ IC(Grω) ≅ IC(Gr0) ⊕ IC(Gr2ω). Under geometric Satake, Vω is the standard 2-dimensional representation of PGL2. The tensor product Vω ⊗ Vω decomposes as the trivial representation V0 plus the 3-dimensional representation V2ω. Hence convolution matches tensor product, confirming the monoidal equivalence. IC(Gr0) is the constant sheaf on the base point; IC(Gr2ω) is the intersection cohomology complex of the dimension-2 Schubert variety.
Exam Strategy
When asked for the convolution of two IC sheaves on GrG, identify the corresponding representations via geometric Satake, compute their tensor product in Rep(Ĝ), then translate back to the direct sum of IC sheaves indexed by the dominant coweights appearing. This avoids explicit geometry of the convolution diagram.

Global Affine Grassmannian and Factorization Homology

The global affine Grassmannian GrG,𝑋 over a smooth projective curve X is the moduli space of G-bundles on X with trivialization on the complement of a finite set of points. It is an ind-scheme fibering over the Ran space of X with fiber the product of local affine Grassmannians at the chosen points. The factorization property states that over the configuration space of n distinct points, GrG,𝑋 is isomorphic to the n-fold product of the local affine Grassmannian. This factorization structure geometrically incarnates factorization homology of X with coefficients in the factorization algebra of G(O)-equivariant perverse sheaves on the local affine Grassmannian. Beilinson and Drinfeld constructed the factorization category of D-modules on GrG,𝑋 and proved its global sections recover D-modules on the moduli stack BunG of G-bundles on X. Francis and Gaitsgory showed that factorization modules over the factorization algebra PG(O)(GrG) are equivalent to D-modules on BunG, a key step in geometric Langlands: it identifies Hecke eigensheaves on BunG with factorization modules over the Satake category. The global affine Grassmannian carries a natural action of the loop group G(KX) (KX = adèles of X), and the factorization property is compatible with the fusion product on the Beilinson–Drinfeld Grassmannian. A subtle point often tested: the factorization product (on the Ran space) and the convolution product (on the local affine Grassmannian) are defined on different spaces; they coincide only after applying the Satake equivalence. The global affine Grassmannian is an ind-scheme of ind-finite type; its cohomology is computed via factorization homology, reducing global computations to local data.

Categorical Geometric Satake and 2‑Representations

The categorical geometric Satake equivalence upgrades the monoidal equivalence to an equivalence of monoidal 2-categories. On one side, the 2-category of G(O)-equivariant perverse sheaves on GrG has objects = perverse sheaves, 1-morphisms = morphisms of perverse sheaves, 2-morphisms = natural transformations. On the other, the 2-category of representations of Ĝ has objects = finite-dimensional representations, 1-morphisms = intertwining operators, 2-morphisms = linear maps between intertwining spaces. The equivalence (Bezrukavnikov–Finkelberg–Ostrik) uses factorization categories and the geometry of GrG over the Ran space. It implies the Drinfeld center of the Satake category is equivalent to Ĝ-equivariant coherent sheaves on the Steinberg variety, relating to representations of the affine Hecke algebra. The 2-categorical structure is essential for the loop group action on D-modules on BunG and for formulating geometric Langlands as a 2-categorical equivalence. A landmark result: the categorical Satake equivalence intertwines convolution with tensor product at the level of 1-morphisms, and associativity constraints match. Examiners may ask to distinguish the abelian Satake category (monoidal category) from its categorification (monoidal 2-category), and to explain why the 2-categorical structure is necessary (Hecke functors act as 1-morphisms on D-modules). Another trap: assuming the categorical equivalence holds for arbitrary coefficients; it requires characteristic zero and algebraic closure for semisimplicity and the required factorization structure.

p‑adic Uniformization, Rapoport–Zink Spaces, and Local Shimura Varieties

Rapoport–Zink spaces are formal schemes over the ring of integers of a p-adic field parametrizing deformations of p-divisible groups with additional structure. They provide a p-adic uniformization of certain Shimura varieties, analogous to complex uniformization by Hermitian symmetric domains. The affine Grassmannian for a reductive group G over a p-adic field appears as the generic fiber of the Rapoport–Zink space attached to a basic local Shimura datum. The special fiber geometry is described by affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties, locally closed subvarieties of the affine flag variety. Rapoport–Zink space cohomology realizes the local Langlands and Jacquet–Langlands correspondences for GLn and inner forms. Fargues and Fontaine introduced the Fargues–Fontaine curve, whose vector bundles are classified by the affine Grassmannian of G. The stack of G-bundles on this curve is isomorphic to the quotient of the affine Grassmannian by the Frobenius action, and the geometric Satake equivalence for the curve yields a categorical equivalence between G(O)-equivariant sheaves on GrG and representations of Ĝ over the p-adic field. This p-adic geometric Satake equivalence is a cornerstone of the Fargues–Scholze program for local Langlands. A key distinction: the p-adic affine Grassmannian is a diamond (a sheaf on the pro-étale site of perfectoid spaces), not an ind-scheme, and convolution uses the derived category of étale sheaves. Examiners often test the relationship among the affine Grassmannian, Rapoport–Zink space, and Fargues–Fontaine curve: the affine Grassmannian is the generic fiber of the Rapoport–Zink space, while the Fargues–Fontaine curve provides a global object whose bundle moduli recover the affine Grassmannian. Another subtle point: the p-adic geometric Satake equivalence uses ℓ-adic coefficients and requires G unramified; for ramified groups one works with parahoric subgroups and affine flag varieties.

  • Confusing the derived convolution product with the abelian convolution on perverse sheaves; the former lives in the derived category and requires perverse truncation to land in the Satake category.
  • Assuming affine Springer fibers are smooth; they are typically singular and their cohomology requires intersection cohomology complexes.
  • Mixing up the factorization product on the Ran space with the convolution product on the local affine Grassmannian; they are defined on different spaces and coincide only after Satake equivalence.
  • Thinking the categorical geometric Satake equivalence holds for any coefficient field; it requires characteristic zero and algebraic closure for semisimplicity.
  • Believing the p-adic affine Grassmannian is an ind-scheme; it is a diamond (a sheaf on perfectoid spaces) and its geometry uses the pro-étale topology.
  • Overlooking the role of the Frobenius action on the affine Grassmannian in the Fargues–Fontaine curve construction; the stack of bundles is the quotient by Frobenius, not the affine Grassmannian itself.
  • Neglecting the distinction between the spherical Hecke algebra (commutative) and the affine Hecke algebra (non-commutative) when discussing the derived Satake equivalence; the latter governs Iwahori-equivariant sheaves on the affine flag variety.

These advanced topics illustrate the depth and breadth of affine Grassmannian theory and its central role in modern representation theory and the Langlands program.

Central Extensions of the Loop Group and the Affine Kac–Moody Algebra

The loop group LG = G(k((t))) for a reductive group G over a field k admits non‑trivial central extensions classified by invariant bilinear forms on its Lie algebra. For a simply connected simple group, the universal central extension is by a one‑dimensional torus k× and yields the affine Kac–Moody algebra ĝ at a fixed level ℓ ∈ ℤ. The Lie algebra of LG is the loop algebra g ⊗ k((t)), and the central extension is given by the Kac–Moody cocycle ω(X ⊗ f, Y ⊗ g) = ⟨X, Y⟩ Rest=0(f dg), where ⟨·,·⟩ is an invariant form (e.g., the Killing form). This central extension integrates to a group extension 1 → k× → LĜ → LG → 1. The level ℓ appears as the weight of the central element on representations. In the geometric Satake correspondence, the affine Grassmannian GrG carries a natural line bundle ℒℓ whose space of sections realizes the level‑ℓ integrable highest‑weight representations of ĝ. The Borel–Weil–Bott theorem for the affine flag variety identifies these sections with the cohomology of line bundles on the affine flag variety, linking the geometry of GrG to the representation theory of the affine Kac–Moody algebra. Examiners often ask to compute the central charge of the Sugawara construction or to explain why the level must be a positive integer for unitarity in the Wess–Zumino–Witten model.

The Geometric Langlands Correspondence and the Affine Grassmannian

The geometric Langlands program seeks a categorical equivalence between D-modules on the moduli stack BunG of G-bundles on a smooth projective curve X and quasi‑coherent (or ind‑coherent) sheaves on the moduli stack LocSysĜ of Ĝ-local systems. The affine Grassmannian enters as the local model for the Hecke correspondences that generate the Hecke functors acting on D-modules. For a point x ∈ X, the Hecke stack Heckex classifies modifications of a G-bundle at x; its fiber over the trivial bundle is precisely GrG. The geometric Satake equivalence identifies the category of G(O)-equivariant perverse sheaves on GrG with Rep(Ĝ), and this identification is used to define the Hecke eigensheaf property. Beilinson and Drinfeld showed that the category of D-modules on BunG carries an action of the monoidal category of G(O)-equivariant D-modules on GrG, which under geometric Satake corresponds to the tensor category Rep(Ĝ). The global geometric Langlands conjecture predicts that this action extends to an equivalence of categories after a suitable categorification. Recent work of Gaitsgory and Lurie uses the factorization structure of the Beilinson–Drinfeld Grassmannian to construct the required monoidal ∞‑category. Examiners may test the distinction between the local geometric Satake (a monoidal equivalence) and the global geometric Langlands (a categorical equivalence of ∞‑categories), and the role of the affine Grassmannian as the fiber of the Hecke correspondence.

Worked Example: Hecke Functor on the Affine Grassmannian
Let G = SL2 and consider the Hecke functor HV associated to the standard representation V of Ĝ = PGL2. Describe the action of HV on the skyscraper sheaf at the base point of GrSL2.
The base point corresponds to the trivial coset G(O) ∈ GrSL2. The Hecke correspondence for V is the subvariety of GrSL2 × GrSL2 where the relative position is the dominant coweight ω1. The fiber over the base point is the Schubert variety Gr≤ω1 ≅ ℙ1. The Hecke functor sends the skyscraper sheaf to the intersection cohomology complex IC(Gr≤ω1), which under geometric Satake corresponds to the standard representation V. Thus HV(δbase) = IC(Gr≤ω1).
Exam Strategy
When a question asks for the Hecke functor action, first identify the dominant coweight attached to the representation, then recall that the Hecke functor is given by convolution with the corresponding IC sheaf on the affine Grassmannian. The geometric Satake equivalence translates this convolution into tensor product in Rep(Ĝ). Memorize the correspondence for the first few fundamental representations of classical groups.

The Affine Grassmannian for Kac–Moody Groups and Flag Varieties

Beyond reductive groups, one can define the affine Grassmannian for a Kac–Moody group G associated to a generalized Cartan matrix (in the sense of Tits or Kumar). The loop group LG is replaced by the formal loop group of the Kac–Moody group, and the positive loop subgroup G(O) is defined via evaluation at t = 0. The quotient GrG = LG / G(O) is an ind‑scheme whose connected components are indexed by the dominant coweights of the maximal torus. The Schubert stratification is governed by the affine Weyl group, a Coxeter group of infinite type. The geometry of these Schubert varieties is more subtle: they are typically infinite‑dimensional and their singularities are controlled by the Bruhat order on the affine Weyl group. The geometric Satake equivalence does not hold in full generality for non‑finite‑type Kac–Moody groups because the category of perverse sheaves is not semisimple. However, for symmetrizable Kac–Moody groups, a version of the Satake equivalence has been established relating the category of G(O)-equivariant perverse sheaves on GrG to the category of integrable highest‑weight representations of the Langlands dual Kac–Moody algebra at a fixed level. This equivalence uses the theory of factorization algebras and the Beilinson–Drinfeld Grassmannian. Examiners may ask to explain why the finite‑type assumption is essential for the classical geometric Satake, and how the affine Grassmannian for affine type A1(1) relates to the moduli space of bundles on an elliptic curve.

Vertex Algebras, Chiral Algebras, and the Affine Grassmannian

The affine Grassmannian provides a geometric realization of the representation theory of vertex algebras. For a simple Lie algebra g, the universal affine vertex algebra Vk(g) at level k is constructed from the vacuum module of the affine Kac–Moody algebra ĝ. The space of conformal blocks of this vertex algebra on a curve X with marked points is isomorphic to the space of global sections of a determinant line bundle on the moduli stack of G-bundles. Beilinson and Drinfeld introduced chiral algebras as a sheaf‑theoretic version of vertex algebras on a curve; the chiral algebra associated to Vk(g) is the sheaf of D-modules on the Ran space of X obtained by factorization from the affine Grassmannian. The factorization product on the Beilinson–Drinfeld Grassmannian GrG,X encodes the operator product expansion of the vertex algebra. In particular, the fiber of GrG,X over a point x ∈ X is the local affine Grassmannian GrG, and the convolution product of D-modules on this fiber recovers the vertex algebra structure. The geometric Satake equivalence for chiral algebras identifies G(O)-equivariant D-modules on GrG with modules over the chiral algebra; by the Kazhdan–Lusztig equivalence, this category is equivalent to representations of the quantum group Uq(g) at a root of unity when k is a negative integer. Examiners often test the relationship between the level k, the central charge of the vertex algebra, and the parameter q = exp(πi/(k + h∨)), where h∨ is the dual Coxeter number.

The Affine Grassmannian in the Theory of Quantum Groups at Roots of Unity

For certain integer levels k, the representation theory of the affine Kac–Moody algebra is equivalent to that of the quantum group Uq(g) at a root of unity q = exp(πi/(k + h∨)). The small quantum group uq(g) is a finite‑dimensional quotient of Uq(g). For a reductive group G, the geometric Satake equivalence extends to this setting: the category of G(O)-equivariant perverse sheaves on GrG with coefficients in a field of characteristic zero is equivalent to the category of finite‑dimensional representations of Uq(g) at the corresponding root of unity. The convolution product on perverse sheaves matches the tensor product of quantum group representations, and the commutativity constraint corresponds to the braiding given by the universal R-matrix. The affine Grassmannian also appears in the construction of the modular tensor category associated to the Wess–Zumino–Witten model: the simple objects are the intersection cohomology complexes of Schubert varieties labeled by dominant weights at level k, and their fusion rules are given by the Verlinde formula. The factorization homology of the affine Grassmannian over a surface computes the space of conformal blocks, providing a topological field theory interpretation. Examiners may ask to derive the Verlinde formula from the geometry of the affine Grassmannian, or to explain the relationship between the semisimplicity of the perverse sheaf category (at a root of unity) and the non‑semisimplicity of the quantum group at generic q.

  • Confusing the central extension of the loop group with the central extension of the affine Kac–Moody algebra; the former is a group extension, the latter a Lie algebra extension, and they integrate only for simply connected groups.
  • Assuming the geometric Satake equivalence holds for all Kac–Moody groups; it requires finite type or symmetrizable type with additional finiteness conditions.
  • Mixing the factorization product on the Beilinson–Drinfeld Grassmannian with the convolution product on the local affine Grassmannian; they coincide only after applying the Satake equivalence and taking global sections.
  • Overlooking the role of the level k in determining the quantum parameter q; the relation q = exp(πi/(k + h∨)) is essential for the equivalence with quantum groups at roots of unity.
  • Treating the affine Grassmannian for a non‑simply‑connected group as a quotient by G(O) without accounting for the fundamental group; the correct quotient is by the connected component of the identity in G(O).
  • Believing the Verlinde formula follows directly from the geometry of Schubert varieties without using the factorization structure; the fusion rules require the factorization product on the Ran space.

The Affine Hecke Algebra and Its Geometric Realization on the Affine Flag Variety

The affine Hecke algebra H attached to a reductive group G over a local field F is the convolution algebra of compactly supported, Iwahori‑bi‑invariant functions on the loop group G(F((t))). It admits a presentation due to Bernstein: generators Ts for simple affine reflections s and invertible elements θλ for cocharacters λ, subject to the quadratic relation (Ts+1)(Ts−q)=0, the braid relations, and the cross relation Tsθλ−θs(λ)Ts=(q−1)(θλ−θs(λ))/(1−θ−α). Geometrically, H is isomorphic to the Ext‑algebra of the constant perverse sheaf on the affine flag variety Fl=G(F((t)))/I, where I is the Iwahori subgroup. The convolution product of I‑equivariant perverse sheaves on Fl yields a monoidal category equivalent to the category of finite‑dimensional H‑modules. Under this equivalence, the standard sheaves ICw correspond to the Kazhdan–Lusztig basis elements Cw, and the character of an H‑module is computed by the stalk cohomology of the corresponding sheaf. The Satake isomorphism for the affine Hecke algebra identifies the center Z(H) with the ring of symmetric functions in the θ‑variables, matching the spherical Hecke algebra of G(F((t))). This geometric realization plays a central role in the classification of irreducible admissible representations of G(F((t))) via Langlands parameters, because the category of I‑equivariant perverse sheaves on Fl is equivalent to the category of modules over the affine Hecke algebra, which in turn is Morita equivalent to the category of smooth G(F((t)))‑representations generated by their Iwahori‑fixed vectors.

The Double Affine Hecke Algebra and the Affine Grassmannian

Cherednik’s double affine Hecke algebra (DAHA) H̃ is generated by the affine Hecke algebra H, a second copy of the polynomial ring ℂ[Y±] in the cocharacter lattice, and a Fourier‑transform element S satisfying S²=1 and SθλS=Yλ. The DAHA acts on the space of Laurent polynomials ℂ[X±] via the polynomial representation, and its eigenfunctions are the non‑symmetric Macdonald polynomials. Geometrically, Varagnolo and Vasserot constructed H̃ as the convolution algebra of G(O)‑equivariant perverse sheaves on the affine Grassmannian GrG with coefficients in the equivariant K‑theory of a point. The Fourier transform S corresponds to the geometric Fourier–Mukai transform on the cotangent bundle T*GrG, which is isomorphic to the nilpotent cone of the Langlands dual group G∨. The spherical subalgebra of H̃ recovers the spherical DAHA, whose representation theory is governed by the geometry of the Hilbert scheme of points on ℂ². In the geometric Langlands program, the DAHA is closely related to the algebra of global differential operators on the moduli stack of G‑bundles on an elliptic curve; the affine Grassmannian for the loop group of G over the elliptic curve provides a factorization space whose chiral homology is expected to compute DAHA‑modules. The parameter q of the affine Hecke algebra and the parameter t of the second polynomial ring correspond to the two equivariant parameters of the torus action on T*GrG, and the limit q,t→1 recovers the universal enveloping algebra of the affine Kac–Moody algebra.

Technique: Identifying the DAHA Action on Macdonald Polynomials
Let H̃ be the double affine Hecke algebra of type A₁ with parameters q,t. The polynomial representation acts on ℂ[x±1] by T = t¹/² s + (t¹/²−t⁻¹/²)/(1−x²)(s−1), Y = x, and S f(x) = f(x⁻¹). Show that the non‑symmetric Macdonald polynomial E₁(x) = x + (1−t)/(1−q x²) is an eigenfunction of Y and compute its eigenvalue.
The operator Y acts by multiplication by x. Applying Y to E₁ gives x·E₁ = x² + x(1−t)/(1−q x²). For E₁ to be an eigenfunction, we require Y E₁ = λ E₁ for some scalar λ. Using the DAHA relation Y T = T⁻¹ Y and the explicit form of T, one finds that the eigenvalue of Y on the degree‑1 non‑symmetric Macdonald polynomial is q. Direct verification uses the identity x² = q x + (q x² - q x) and the defining property of Macdonald polynomials; the computation confirms Y E₁ = q E₁. Hence the eigenvalue is q.
Exam‑Day Strategy
When a question asks for the geometric origin of a Hecke algebra relation, first state the convolution diagram (e.g., Fl ×I Fl → Fl) and then translate the sheaf‑theoretic convolution into the algebraic generators. For DAHA, remember that the second polynomial ring comes from the equivariant K‑theory of the base point, and the Fourier transform is the geometric Fourier–Mukai on the cotangent bundle. Write down the two equivariant parameters explicitly; examiners often test the specialization q=t (trigonometric limit) versus q,t independent (elliptic limit).

The Local Langlands Correspondence for GLn via the Affine Grassmannian

The local Langlands correspondence (LLC) for GLn(F) over a p‑adic field F matches irreducible smooth representations of GLn(F) with n‑dimensional Frobenius‑semisimple Weil–Deligne representations of the Weil group WF. The geometrization of LLC by Fargues and Scholze replaces the p‑adic group by the moduli stack BunG of G‑bundles on the Fargues–Fontaine curve XFF. For G=GLn, the affine Grassmannian GrGLn classifies modifications of the trivial bundle at a point; its GLn(O)‑orbits are indexed by dominant coweights, i.e., partitions. The Hecke operators acting on the derived category of sheaves on BunGLn are given by convolution with perverse sheaves on GrGLn. The spectral action of the category of representations of the L‑group GLn(ℂ) on D(BunGLn) is constructed via the geometric Satake equivalence. In this framework, the LLC is formulated as the statement that Hecke eigensheaves on BunGLn correspond to Langlands parameters under the spectral action. The cohomology of the Lubin–Tate tower, which realizes the LLC for GLn, is related to the cohomology of certain shtuka spaces mapping to GrGLn. The affine Grassmannian also appears in the p‑adic uniformization of Rapoport–Zink spaces: the generic fiber of the Rapoport–Zink space for GLn is isomorphic to a disjoint union of copies of the Drinfeld upper half‑space, which in turn is a period domain inside the affine Grassmannian. These identifications suggest that the local Langlands correspondence is deeply encoded in the geometry of the affine Grassmannian and its Hecke category.

The Affine Grassmannian in Positive Characteristic and the Geometric Satake Equivalence

When the base field k has characteristic p>0, the category of G(O)‑equivariant perverse sheaves on GrG with coefficients in a field of characteristic ℓ≠p remains semisimple, and the geometric Satake equivalence holds verbatim (Bezrukavnikov–Finkelberg–Mirković). However, for coefficients in characteristic p, the category of perverse sheaves is no longer semisimple; the simple objects are the intersection cohomology complexes ICλ of Schubert varieties, but their Ext groups are non‑trivial. The correct replacement is the category of parity sheaves, which are direct sums of shifts of ICλ and satisfy a parity vanishing condition. The modular geometric Satake equivalence (Juteau–Mautner–Williamson) identifies the category of G(O)‑equivariant parity sheaves on GrG with the category of tilting modules for the quantum group Uq(g) at a root of unity q of order p. The convolution product corresponds to the tensor product of tilting modules, and the Frobenius morphism on GrG induces the Steinberg tensor product on tilting modules. The linkage principle for tilting modules translates into the statement that the support of a parity sheaf is a union of Schubert varieties whose highest weights lie in a single block of the affine Weyl group at level p−h∨. This positive‑characteristic theory is essential for the modular representation theory of reductive groups and for the geometric Langlands program in characteristic p, where the Hecke category is replaced by the category of parity sheaves.

The Affine Grassmannian for Non‑Reductive Groups: Unipotent and Solvable Loop Groups

For a unipotent algebraic group U over a field k, the loop group U(K) is ind‑unipotent and contractible in the ind‑scheme topology; consequently the affine Grassmannian GrU=U(K)/U(O) is a point. The geometric Satake equivalence for U is trivial: the category of U(O)‑equivariant perverse sheaves on a point is the category of vector spaces, which matches the representation category of the trivial group. For a solvable group B=T⋉U (a Borel subgroup of a reductive group), the loop group B(K) fits into an exact sequence 1→U(K)→B(K)→T(K)→1. The affine Grassmannian GrB fibers over the torus affine Grassmannian GrT=X*(T) with fiber GrU (a point), so GrB is a disjoint union of points indexed by the cocharacter lattice. The B(O)‑equivariant perverse sheaves on GrB are therefore direct sums of skyscraper sheaves, and their convolution algebra is the group algebra of the cocharacter lattice, i.e., the polynomial ring ℂ[X*(T)]. This matches the representation category of the torus T. For a general non‑reductive group H with unipotent radical Ru(H) and reductive quotient Hred, the affine Grassmannian GrH fibers over GrHred with fiber the affine Grassmannian of the unipotent radical (a point). The geometric Satake equivalence for H is then expected to be governed by the Tannakian formalism for the category of representations of H, which is a tensor subcategory of representations of Hred closed under the action of the pro‑unipotent radical. The convolution category of H(O)‑equivariant perverse sheaves on GrH is conjecturally equivalent to the category of finite‑dimensional representations of the Langlands dual group H∨, a non‑reductive group whose reductive quotient is (Hred)∨ and whose unipotent radical is the dual of the Lie algebra of Ru(H).

  • Assuming the double affine Hecke algebra is simply the tensor product of two affine Hecke algebras; the Fourier transform S intertwines the two polynomial representations and is essential for the Macdonald theory.
  • Treating the local Langlands correspondence for GLn as a purely representation‑theoretic bijection without reference to the geometry of the Fargues–Fontaine curve and the affine Grassmannian; the geometric realization is now the standard framework.
  • Overlooking the distinction between perverse sheaves and parity sheaves in positive characteristic; the modular geometric Satake uses parity sheaves, and the simple perverse sheaves ICλ are not enough to generate the category.
  • Believing the affine Grassmannian for a non‑reductive group is always a point; for solvable groups it is a disjoint union of points indexed by the cocharacter lattice of the torus part.
  • Mixing the convolution product on the affine Grassmannian (which is commutative up to the braiding) with the convolution on the affine flag variety (which is non‑commutative); they are related by the Satake equivalence but live on different spaces.
  • Neglecting the role of the level k in the quantum group parameter q=exp(πi/(k+h∨)) when moving between the affine Kac–Moody algebra and the double affine Hecke algebra; the DAHA parameters q,t correspond to the two equivariant parameters on the cotangent bundle, not directly to the level.

The Affine Grassmannian and the Geometry of the Hitchin Fibration

The Hitchin fibration gives a completely integrable system on the moduli space of Higgs bundles over a smooth projective curve. The generic fiber is an abelian variety, while singular fibers encode deep representation‑theoretic data. The affine Grassmannian appears as the local model for the Hitchin fiber over a point of the base: the nearby cycles functor on the Hitchin map produces a category of perverse sheaves on the affine Grassmannian equivalent to representations of the Langlands dual group. This equivalence is a local form of the geometric Langlands correspondence and explains the action of the spherical Hecke algebra on the cohomology of Hitchin fibers.

In Ngô Bao Châu's proof of the fundamental lemma, orbital integrals on the Lie algebra are compared with the cohomology of the Hitchin fibration. The affine Grassmannian enters through the local model of the Hitchin fiber at a very singular point, where the fiber is a union of affine Springer fibers. The decomposition theorem for the Hitchin map implies that the intersection cohomology of these fibers is a direct sum of shifted intersection cohomology complexes on the affine Grassmannian, indexed by dominant coweights. Endoscopic transfer factors then appear as weights of the Frobenius action on the stalks of these complexes.

The global Hitchin fibration also yields a factorization structure on perverse sheaves on the affine Grassmannian. The Beilinson–Drinfeld Grassmannian, parametrizing G‑bundles with trivialization away from a finite set of points, provides a family of affine Grassmannians over the configuration space of the curve. The factorization property translates into the fusion product on the spherical Hecke category, which is the convolution product on the affine Grassmannian. This fusion product is the geometric counterpart of the tensor product of representations of the Langlands dual group.

The Affine Grassmannian and the Theory of Affine Schubert Calculus

Affine Schubert calculus studies the intersection theory of Schubert varieties in the affine Grassmannian and affine flag variety. Schubert varieties are indexed by the affine Weyl group; their classes form a basis of equivariant (co)homology. The structure constants for the product in equivariant homology are the affine Littlewood–Richardson coefficients, which count points in triple intersections of generic translates of Schubert varieties. These coefficients are non‑negative integers; a positivity conjecture for them was proved by Lam and Shimozono using MV polytopes.

The affine Grassmannian carries a natural action of T × ℂ×, where ℂ× corresponds to loop rotation. The equivariant cohomology ring H_T×ℂ×^*(Gr_G) is isomorphic to the homology of the affine nil‑Hecke algebra, with Schubert classes corresponding to affine Stanley symmetric functions. The coproduct in this Hopf algebra encodes restriction of Schubert classes to affine Grassmannians of Levi subgroups, giving a geometric shadow of the comultiplication in the quantum group U_q(g) at a root of unity.

Work by Lam, Shimozono, and others has identified affine Schubert calculus with the tropical geometry of the totally non‑negative part of the loop group. MV polytopes give a piecewise‑linear model for affine Schubert varieties, and affine Littlewood–Richardson coefficients become counts of integral points in Minkowski sums of the corresponding polytopes. This tropical interpretation yields explicit combinatorial formulas and connects affine Schubert calculus to cluster algebras and the amplituhedron.

Worked Example: Computing an Affine Littlewood–Richardson Coefficient
Let G = SL3 and consider the affine Grassmannian Gr_G. The dominant coweights are λ = (2,0,-2) and μ = (1,0,-1). Compute the affine Littlewood–Richardson coefficient cλ,μ^ν for ν = (3,0,-3) using the MV polytope model.
The MV polytope P_λ for λ = (2,0,-2) is the convex hull of the Weyl group orbit of λ in the coweight lattice, a hexagon with vertices at the six permutations of (2,0,-2). Similarly P_μ is the hexagon for (1,0,-1). The Minkowski sum P_λ + P_μ has dominant vertex (3,0,-3), corresponding to ν. The coefficient equals the number of integral points in the fiber of the moment map over ν, i.e., the number of ways to write ν as a sum of a point in P_λ and a point in P_μ with both points dominant. Here there is exactly one such decomposition, so cλ,μ^ν = 1.
Exam‑Day Strategy
To compute an affine Littlewood–Richardson coefficient, identify the dominant coweights and construct the corresponding MV polytopes. The coefficient is the number of integral points in the intersection of appropriately translated polytopes (equivalently, the number of dominant decompositions in the Minkowski sum). For simply‑laced groups the polytopes are GGMS polytopes satisfying tropical Plücker relations, not merely permutohedra. Do not assume the coefficient is 1 whenever the sum of highest weights is dominant; coefficients can be larger and must be computed via the polytope model or crystal graphs.

The Affine Grassmannian and the Theory of D‑Modules on the Loop Group

The category of D‑modules on the loop group LG = G(K) is dual to the category of perverse sheaves on the affine Grassmannian. Since Gr_G = LG / G(O), pull‑push along this quotient gives an equivalence between G(O)‑equivariant D‑modules on LG and D‑modules on Gr_G. Under this equivalence, convolution of D‑modules on Gr_G corresponds to the fusion product on LG, defined via the factorization structure of the Beilinson–Drinfeld Grassmannian.

The center of the category of D‑modules on LG is the algebra of functions on ^L G‑opers on the formal disc. This center acts on every object, yielding a spectral decomposition that is the geometric analogue of the Harish‑Chandra isomorphism. The eigen‑D‑modules are the Hecke eigensheaves of the geometric Langlands correspondence. The affine Grassmannian enters because the spherical Hecke category (G(O)‑equivariant perverse sheaves on Gr_G) is equivalent to representations of ^L G; the D‑module version is the geometric Satake equivalence for D‑modules.

Gaitsgory identified the category of D‑modules on the affine Grassmannian with modules over the affine Kac–Moody algebra at the critical level. At this level the Feigin–Frenkel isomorphism identifies the center of the completed universal enveloping algebra with functions on ^L G‑opers. The affine Grassmannian thus geometrically realizes the representation theory of the affine Kac–Moody algebra at the critical level, with convolution corresponding to the tensor product of representations.

The Affine Grassmannian and the Theory of Quantum Cohomology of the Affine Flag Variety

The quantum cohomology ring QH_T^*(Fl_G) of the affine flag variety generalizes the quantum cohomology of finite‑dimensional flag varieties. Fl_G = LG / I (I the Iwahori subgroup) is an ind‑scheme with T‑fixed points indexed by the affine Weyl group. The quantum product counts genus‑zero stable maps from ℙ1 to Fl_G; its structure constants are three‑point Gromov–Witten invariants satisfying the quantum Chevalley formula, expressing the product of a divisor class with a Schubert class as a sum over affine roots.

Peterson proved that the quantum cohomology of the affine flag variety is isomorphic to the homology of the affine Grassmannian with a shifted grading. Under this isomorphism, the quantum product corresponds to the Pontryagin product on H_*(Gr_G) from the convolution diagram. Quantum parameters q_i correspond to simple coroots, and the quantum Chevalley formula becomes the classical Chevalley formula for the affine Grassmannian. This gives a geometric proof of the Lam–Shimozono result that affine Stanley symmetric functions are the Schubert polynomials for the affine flag variety.

The quantum cohomology ring also admits a presentation as a quotient of the polynomial ring in equivariant and quantum parameters by the ideal of quantum Giambelli formulas. The Bethe ansatz equations for quantum cohomology coincide with the eigenvalue equations for the quantum Toda lattice, the integrable system associated to the affine flag variety. The affine Grassmannian appears as the phase space of the Toda lattice, and the quantum cohomology ring is the algebra of quantum integrals of motion.

The Affine Grassmannian and the Theory of the Affine Grassmannian for Symmetric Spaces

For a symmetric space G/H with H the fixed points of an involution θ, define GrG/H = G(K) / H(O). This space parametrizes H‑bundles on the formal disc with a trivialization of the associated G‑bundle. Its geometry relates to the representation theory of the symmetric pair (G,H) and spherical varieties. Schubert varieties in GrG/H are indexed by the θ‑twisted affine Weyl group; their intersection cohomology computes multiplicities in branching rules for the restriction of representations of G to H.

The geometric Satake equivalence for symmetric spaces identifies H(O)‑equivariant perverse sheaves on GrG/H with finite‑dimensional representations of the Langlands dual symmetric pair (^L G, ^L H). Convolution on GrG/H corresponds to the tensor product of representations of ^L H, with the fiber functor given by hypercohomology of IC complexes. This equivalence was established by Nadler and Yun using factorization algebras and the Hitchin fibration for symmetric spaces.

For GL2n / Sp2n, the affine Grassmannian GrGL2n/Sp2n is isomorphic to the space of Lagrangian subspaces in the symplectic vector space K2n. The Affine Grassmannian and the Theory of Affine Demazure Modules

Affine Demazure modules arise as submodules of integrable highest‑weight representations of the affine Kac–Moody algebra widehat{g} attached to a reductive group G. For a dominant weight λ and an element w of the affine Weyl group Waff, the Demazure module D_w(λ) is the B-submodule generated by the extremal weight vector vwλ inside the level‑k representation V_k(λ), where B is the standard Borel subgroup of the loop group G(K). Geometrically, D_w(λ) is identified with the space of global sections H0(X_w,L_λ) of the ample line bundle L_λ on the Schubert variety X_w⊂ Fℓ_G in the affine flag variety. The projection π:Fℓ_G→ operatorname{Gr}_G sends X_w to a Schubert variety operatorname{Gr}_w in the affine Grassmannian, and the pushforward π_*L_λ decomposes into a direct sum of intersection cohomology complexes whose stalks compute the graded multiplicities of weight spaces in D_w(λ).

The Demazure character formula expresses operatorname{ch} D_w(λ) as the action of a Demazure operator π_i on the formal character e^λ. In the language of the affine Grassmannian, this formula is equivalent to the statement that the class [OX_w] in the equivariant K-theory KTtimesC^×(Fℓ_G) maps under the pushforward π_* to the Hall–Littlewood polynomial P_λ (or its affine analogue). Work by Kashiwara, Littelmann, and Joseph relates the crystal basis of D_w(λ) to the set of Mirković–Vilonen cycles contained in operatorname{Gr}_w, providing a combinatorial model for the module. For simply‑laced types, the graded character of D_w(λ) matches the nonsymmetric Macdonald polynomial Ewλ(q,t) at t=0, linking affine Demazure modules to the representation theory of the double affine Hecke algebra.

In the geometric Satake equivalence, the perverse sheaf operatorname{IC}_w on operatorname{Gr}_w corresponds to the simple G^vee-module V(λ); the Demazure module D_w(λ) appears as the stalk cohomology of the nearby cycles functor applied to operatorname{IC}_w along the projection to the affine Grassmannian. This relationship underlies proofs of the positivity of affine Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials and the construction of the affine Schur–Weyl duality. The affine Grassmannian thus serves as the geometric arena where Demazure modules, MV cycles, and Macdonald theory intersect.

The Affine Grassmannian and the Theory of Equivariant K-Theory of the Affine Flag Variety

The equivariant K-theory KTtimesC^×(Fℓ_G) of the affine flag variety carries a rich algebraic structure. Here T is a maximal torus of G and C^× acts by loop rotation. The fixed points of the TtimesC^×-action are indexed by the affine Weyl group Waff; localization yields an injection

KTtimesC^×(Fℓ_G) bigoplusw∈ Waff R(TtimesC^×)

whose image consists of tuples (f_w) satisfying the GKM‑type congruences f_w≡ fws_αpmod{1-e-α} for every affine root α. The ring R(TtimesC^×) is the Laurent polynomial ring Z[X^±,q^±] where X denotes the character lattice of T and q the loop rotation weight.

The pushforward π_*:KTtimesC^×(Fℓ_G)→ KTtimesC^×(operatorname{Gr}_G) induced by the projection π:Fℓ_G→operatorname{Gr}_G is a surjective homomorphism of R(TtimesC^×)-modules. Its kernel is generated by the classes of Schubert varieties that map to lower‑dimensional strata in operatorname{Gr}_G. Under the identification KTtimesC^×(operatorname{Gr}_G)≅ ΛTtimesC^× (the ring of symmetric functions with coefficients in R(TtimesC^×)), the class [OX_w] maps to the (affine) Hall–Littlewood polynomial P_λ. This is the K-theoretic avatar of the Satake isomorphism: the convolution product on KTtimesC^×(operatorname{Gr}_G) corresponds to the tensor product of representations of the Langlands dual group G^vee.

Quantum K-theory of the affine flag variety, developed by Givental, Lee, and others, deforms the ordinary equivariant K-theory by quantum parameters q_i attached to simple coroots. A theorem of Peterson (circulated in unpublished notes) identifies the quantum K-theory ring QKTtimesC^×(Fℓ_G) with the homology H_*(operatorname{Gr}_G) equipped with the Pontryagin product, after a suitable grading shift. The quantum parameters become the simple coroots in the affine Grassmannian, and the quantum Chevalley formula in QK translates into the classical Chevalley formula for the affine Grassmannian. This isomorphism provides a geometric proof of the Lam–Shimozono result that affine Stanley symmetric functions are the Schubert polynomials for Fℓ_G. The affine Grassmannian thus appears as the phase space of the quantum Toda lattice, and its homology ring is the algebra of quantum integrals of motion.

The Affine Grassmannian and the Theory of the Affine Grassmannian for Supergroups

For a Lie supergroup G=GL(m|n) or OSp(r|2s), the loop supergroup G(K) is defined as the functor of points on the category of super‑algebras. The affine Grassmannian operatorname{Gr}_G=G(K)/G(O) becomes an ind‑scheme in the category of super‑schemes; its reduced scheme is the affine Grassmannian of the even part Gev. The Z2-grading introduces odd coordinates on the formal disc, and the stratification by G(O)-orbits is indexed by dominant weights of Gev together with an atypicality datum. Schubert varieties operatorname{Gr}_λ are not normal in general; their singularities are controlled by the odd root system.

The geometric Satake equivalence for supergroups, developed by Cheng–Wang–Zhang and independently by Serganova, identifies the category of G(O)-equivariant perverse sheaves on operatorname{Gr}_G with the category of finite‑dimensional representations of the Langlands dual supergroup G^vee. The convolution product corresponds to the tensor product of G^vee-representations, but the braiding involves the parity shift functor. Mirković–Vilonen cycles are replaced by super MV cycles, which are irreducible components of the intersection of a Schubert variety with a semi‑infinite orbit in the super‑ind‑scheme. Their moment polytopes are super polytopes: convex polytopes in the weight lattice of Gev equipped with a Z2-grading on the vertices, satisfying tropical Plücker relations that involve both even and odd minors.

In type GL(m|n), the affine Grassmannian can be realized as the space of G(O)-lattices in the superspace Km|n. The Schubert cells are indexed by pairs of partitions (λ+,λ-) with λ+ of length ≤ m and λ- of length ≤ n. The intersection cohomology of operatorname{Gr}(λ+,λ-) computes the multiplicities in the tensor product of the corresponding GL(m|n)^vee-representations. The super MV polytopes are in bijection with the crystal basis of the quantum supergroup U_q(gl(m|n)) at generic q. This combinatorial model yields a super analogue of the Littlewood–Richardson rule, where the coefficients count super MV polytopes in a Minkowski sum and satisfy a signed positivity property reflecting the parity of the odd roots.

The Affine Grassmannian and the Theory of the Affine Grassmannian in the Context of the Geometric Langlands Program for Non‑Simply Connected Groups

When G is not simply connected, e.g. G=PGL_n or SO2n, the affine Grassmannian operatorname{Gr}_G has a disconnected Picard group. The line bundles L_λ corresponding to dominant weights λ of the universal cover widetilde{G} descend to operatorname{Gr}_G only when λ lies in the weight lattice of G. The geometric Satake equivalence for the adjoint group Gad involves the category operatorname{Perv}Gad(O)(operatorname{Gr}Gad) of perverse sheaves with a fixed central character of the fundamental group π1(Gad). Equivalently, one works with the derived category of coherent sheaves on the Langlands dual group G^vee, which is simply connected, and the fiber functor is given by global sections on the Steinberg variety.

Bezrukavnikov–Finkelberg–Mirković showed that the affine Grassmannian for the universal cover widetilde{G} maps finite‑to‑one onto operatorname{Gr}Gad with Galois group π1(Gad). The pushforward of the Satake category along this map yields a π1(Gad)-equivariant category on operatorname{Gr}Gad whose π1(Gad)-invariants recover the usual Satake category. The central extensions of the loop group widetilde{G}(K) by C^× are classified by the level kinZ; at the critical level k=-h^vee the Feigin–Frenkel center acts, and the category of representations of the affine Kac–Moody algebra at critical level is equivalent to the category of D-modules on operatorname{Gr}Gad. This equivalence realizes the local geometric Langlands correspondence for non‑simply connected groups.

In the global geometric Langlands program, the moduli stack operatorname{Bun}_G of G-bundles on a curve X has connected components indexed by π1(G). The Hecke operators act on D-modules on operatorname{Bun}_G and are constructed using the affine Grassmannian operatorname{Gr}_G at marked points. For non‑simply connected G, the Hecke eigensheaves correspond to G^vee-local systems with a prescribed monodromy at the components of operatorname{Bun}_G. The affine Grassmannian thus encodes both the local and global aspects of the Langlands correspondence for groups with non‑trivial fundamental group.

The Affine Grassmannian and the Theory of the Affine Grassmannian for Loop Groups of Parabolic Subgroups

Let P⊂ G be a standard parabolic subgroup with Levi factor L. The partial affine flag variety Fℓ_P=G(K)/P(O) interpolates between the affine Grassmannian (P=G) and the full affine flag variety (P=I, the Iwahori). Its G(O)-orbits are indexed by the quotient Waff/W_L where W_L is the finite Weyl group of L. The Schubert varieties Fℓ_P^w are projective and their intersection cohomology computes the multiplicities in the parabolic induction and restriction of representations of the affine Kac–Moody algebra at positive level.

The projection π_P:Fℓ_P→operatorname{Gr}_G is a fibration with fibre the finite flag variety G/P. The pushforward πP* in equivariant K-theory sends the class of a Schubert variety to a symmetric function that is a parabolic Hall–Littlewood polynomial P_λ(L). Under the geometric Satake equivalence, the category operatorname{Perv}P(O)(Fℓ_P) of P(O)-equivariant perverse sheaves is monoidally equivalent to the category of finite‑dimensional representations of the Levi subgroup L^vee of the Langlands dual group. The convolution product on Fℓ_P corresponds to the tensor product over L^vee, and the fiber functor is given by hypercohomology of the intersection cohomology complexes.

Braverman–Finkelberg–Gaitsgory constructed a factorization algebra on the Ran space of a curve whose stalk at a point is Fℓ_P. The global sections of this factorization algebra yield the category of D-modules on the moduli stack of P-bundles, providing a geometric realization of the parabolic induction functor in the geometric Langlands program. The affine Grassmannian for the parabolic subgroup thus serves as the local model for the Hecke operators with parabolic level structure, and its Schubert calculus governs the branching rules for the restriction of G^vee-representations to L^vee.

Borel Subgroups, the Affine Grassmannian, and the Geometric Satake Fiber Functor

Let G be a connected reductive group over an algebraically closed field k and let B ⊂ G be a Borel subgroup. The loop group B(K) with K = k((t)) sits inside G(K) and its intersection with G(O) for O = k[[t]] is the Iwahori subgroup I = B(O) ⋅ G(O)1, where G(O)1 is the first congruence kernel. The quotient G(K)/I is the full affine flag variety FℓG, while the quotient G(K)/G(O) is the affine Grassmannian GrG. The natural projection π : FℓG → GrG is a G(O)-equivariant fibration with fibre the finite flag variety G/B.

The geometric Satake equivalence identifies the category PervG(O)(GrG) of G(O)-equivariant perverse sheaves on GrG with the category Rep(G∨) of finite‑dimensional representations of the Langlands dual group G∨. The fiber functor ω : PervG(O)(GrG) → Vect is given by hypercohomology of the intersection cohomology complexes ICλ attached to the G(O)-orbits Grλ indexed by dominant coweights λ. The Borel subgroup enters this picture in two essential ways. First, the Iwahori subgroup I is the preimage of B under the evaluation map G(O) → G. The I-equivariant derived category DI(FℓG) carries a convolution product that categorifies the affine Hecke algebra Haff. Second, the nearby cycles functor Ψ : DI(FℓG) → PervG(O)(GrG) intertwines the convolution on the affine flag variety with the tensor product on Rep(G∨). Under this functor, the standard sheaves on FℓG corresponding to elements of the affine Weyl group map to the intersection cohomology complexes on GrG.

The stratification of GrG by G(O)-orbits is indexed by the dominant coweight lattice X*(T)+, while the I-orbits on FℓG are indexed by the full affine Weyl group Waff. The projection π sends an I-orbit Fℓw to the G(O)-orbit Grλ where λ is the dominant coweight obtained from w by the usual projection Waff → X*(T)+. The fibres of π over a point in Grλ are isomorphic to the Schubert variety Xw ⊂ G/B for a minimal length representative w of the coset Waff/W0 mapping to λ. This geometric picture underlies the proof that the convolution product on GrG corresponds to the tensor product of representations: the tensor product multiplicities are computed by the dimensions of the stalks of the pushforward π*ICw along the fibres.

In the Whittaker model, the Borel subgroup appears through the mirabolic subgroup P = B ⋉ U where U is the unipotent radical of the opposite Borel. The geometric Casselman–Shalika formula expresses the Whittaker function on G(K) as a sum over the G(O)-orbits on GrG of the trace of Frobenius on the stalks of ICλ. This formula is a direct consequence of the compatibility of the fiber functor with the convolution product and the fact that the Whittaker sheaf on GrG is the image under Ψ of the constant sheaf on the affine flag variety.

Affine Grassmannian and the Lubin–Tate Tower over Non‑Archimedean Local Fields

Let F be a finite extension of Qp with ring of integers OF and uniformizer π. The Lubin–Tate formal group law LT of height n over OF gives a tower of rigid analytic spaces LTm parametrizing πm-division points of LT. The inverse limit LT∞ = limm LTm carries a continuous action of GLn(F) × D× × WF, where D is the central division algebra of invariant 1/n and WF is the Weil group of F. The work of Fargues–Fontaine and Scholze identifies the generic fibre of the Rapoport–Zink space for LT with the affine Grassmannian GrGLn over F. Concretely, there is a GLn(OF)-equivariant period map πHT : LT∞ → GrGLn which is a pro‑étale cover with Galois group GLn(F). The stratification of GrGLn by GLn(OF)-orbits (indexed by dominant coweights λ = (λ1 ≥ … ≥ λn)) pulls back to the Newton stratification on LT∞. The cohomology of the Lubin–Tate tower realizes the local Langlands correspondence for GLn: the ℓ-adic cohomology groups H*(LT∞, Qℓ) decompose as a direct sum of irreducible representations of GLn(F) ⊗ D× ⊗ WF matching the local Langlands parameter.

The affine Grassmannian enters the picture through the Hecke operators on the Lubin–Tate tower. For a dominant coweight λ, the Hecke correspondence on LT∞ is defined by the fibre product LT∞ ×Gr Grλ where Grλ is the GLn(OF)-orbit closure in GrGLn. The action of the spherical Hecke algebra on the cohomology of LT∞ factors through the geometric Satake equivalence for GLn over F. In particular, the Satake isomorphism identifies the spherical Hecke algebra with the representation ring of the dual group GLn(C), and the Hecke eigenvalues on the Lubin–Tate cohomology correspond to the Satake parameters of the associated Galois representation.

The period map πHT is constructed using the Hodge–Tate filtration on the Tate module of the universal formal group over LT∞. At a point x ∈ LT∞, the Hodge–Tate sequence 0 → Lie(LT) ⊗ C → Tp(LT) ⊗ C → LT[p∞] ⊗ C → 0 yields a lattice in the n-dimensional F-vector space Tp(LT) ⊗ F C, i.e. a point of GrGLn. This map is GLn(OF)-equivariant and its fibres are isomorphic to the affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties for GLn. The geometry of these fibres controls the supercuspidal part of the local Langlands correspondence.

Affine Grassmannian over Global Function Fields and the Geometric Langlands Correspondence

Let C be a smooth projective curve over a finite field Fq and let G be a reductive group over Fq. The moduli stack BunG of G-bundles on C is an algebraic stack locally of finite type. For a finite set of points S ⊂ C, the global affine Grassmannian GrG,C is the ind‑scheme over C whose R-points for an Fq-algebra R are pairs (x, ℱ, τ) where x ∈ C(R), ℱ is a G-bundle on C × Spec R, and τ is a trivialization of ℱ on the complement of the graph of x. The fibre over a point x ∈ C is the usual affine Grassmannian GrG for the completed local field Fq((tx)). The Beilinson–Drinfeld Grassmannian GrG,CI over the Ran space of C encodes the factorization property: for disjoint finite subsets I = I1 ⊔ I2 there is a canonical isomorphism GrG,CI ≅ GrG,CI1 × GrG,CI2.

The geometric Langlands correspondence predicts an equivalence of categories D-mod(BunG) ≅ QCoh(LocSysG∨) where LocSysG∨ is the stack of G∨-local systems on C. The Hecke operators Hλ : D-mod(BunG) → D-mod(BunG) are defined by the correspondence BunG ← Heckeλ → BunG where Heckeλ classifies modifications of G-bundles of type λ at a point. The fibre of Heckeλ over a point of BunG is the Schubert variety Grλ ⊂ GrG. The convolution product on the category of G(O)-equivariant perverse sheaves on GrG (the Satake category) matches the tensor product of Hecke operators via the geometric Satake equivalence.

Lafforgue’s proof of the global Langlands correspondence for GLn over function fields uses the cohomology of the moduli stack of shtukas. A shtuka is a G-bundle with a Frobenius‑twisted modification at a finite set of points; its moduli space is a finite‑type algebraic stack that maps to the product of affine Grassmannians at the modification points. The trace of Frobenius on the ℓ‑adic cohomology of the shtuka space yields automorphic forms whose Hecke eigenvalues are the Frobenius eigenvalues on the associated Galois representation. The factorization property of the affine Grassmannian ensures that the Hecke operators at different points commute, which is essential for the construction of the global Langlands parameter.

Elliptic Quantum Groups and the Elliptic Hall Algebra via the Affine Grassmannian

Let E be an elliptic curve over C and let G = GLn. The elliptic quantum group Eτ,η(gln) is a Hopf algebra depending on the modular parameter τ of E and a deformation parameter η. Burban and Schiffmann constructed the elliptic Hall algebra EHA as the Drinfeld double of the spherical Hall algebra of the category of coherent sheaves on E. The equivariant K‑theory KT×C×(Hilbd(E)) of the Hilbert scheme of d points on E carries a natural action of EHA. Schiffmann and Vasserot showed that this action is isomorphic to the action of the spherical double affine Hecke algebra (DAHA) of type An−1 at critical level.

The affine Grassmannian GrGLn over the elliptic curve E (i.e. the ind‑scheme of GLn-bundles on E with a trivialization on the formal neighbourhood of a point) provides a geometric realization of EHA. The convolution product on the category of GLn(OE)-equivariant perverse sheaves on this affine Grassmannian corresponds to the multiplication in EHA. The fixed points of the torus T × C× action on GrGLn are indexed by n-tuples of partitions, and the classes of the structure sheaves of the attracting cells give a basis identified with the elliptic Macdonald polynomials. The elliptic Hall algebra contains the DAHA as the subalgebra generated by the Hecke operators at a single point; the full EHA is generated by Hecke operators at all points of E, reflecting the factorization structure of the affine Grassmannian over E.

The representation theory of EHA is governed by the geometry of the affine Grassmannian over E. In particular, the spherical subalgebra of EHA acts on the equivariant K‑theory of the affine Grassmannian, and its joint eigenvalues are given by the elliptic Macdonald polynomials. The modular tensor category arising from the quantum group at a root of unity appears as the category of perverse sheaves on the quantum affine Grassmannian (see next section). The elliptic Hall algebra thus interpolates between the rational DAHA (degeneration τ → i∞) and the trigonometric DAHA (degeneration q → 1), with the affine Grassmannian over E providing the unifying geometric framework.

The Quantum Affine Grassmannian and the Quantum Geometric Satake Equivalence

Let G be a simply connected simple algebraic group over C and let q be a primitive ℓ-th root of unity with ℓ greater than the Coxeter number. The quantum loop group Uq(Lg) is a Hopf algebra deformation of the universal enveloping algebra of the affine Lie algebra Lg. The quantum positive loop group Uq(Lg)≥0 is the subalgebra generated by the positive root vectors and the Cartan part. The quantum affine Grassmannian GrG,q is defined as the quotient Uq(Lg) / Uq(Lg)≥0 in the category of ind‑schemes over the quantum plane. Its T-fixed points are indexed by dominant weights λ modulo the level ℓ − h∨. The quantum Schubert cells Grλ,q are quantum analogues of the classical Schubert cells; their closures are the quantum Schubert varieties.

The quantum geometric Satake equivalence, conjectured by Bezrukavnikov and Finkelberg, asserts that the category PervG(O)q(GrG,q) of perverse sheaves on the quantum affine Grassmannian (with an appropriate notion of equivariance) is tensor equivalent to the category Tilting(Uq(g)) of tilting modules for the quantum group Uq(g) at the root of unity. The convolution product on the quantum affine Grassmannian corresponds to the tensor product of tilting modules, and the fiber functor is given by the quantum hypercohomology of the intersection cohomology complexes ICλ,q. The quantum intersection cohomology computes the fusion rules of the modular tensor category C(g, ℓ) arising from the representation theory of Uq(g) at level k = ℓ − h∨.

The quantum affine Grassmannian also appears in the quantum geometric Langlands program of Gaitsgory and Lurie. There, the category of D‑modules on the moduli stack of G-bundles on a curve is replaced by a category of modules over a quantum version of the chiral algebra. The Hecke operators are constructed using the quantum affine Grassmannian, and the quantum geometric Satake equivalence provides the local input for the quantum Langlands correspondence. The quantum parameter q corresponds to the Planck constant in the associated topological quantum field theory, and the modular tensor category C(g, ℓ) governs the braiding of the Hecke operators.

The Affine Grassmannian and the Fargues–Fontaine Curve in p‑adic Geometric Langlands

The Fargues–Fontaine curve XC attached to a perfectoid field C gives a geometric incarnation of the local Langlands correspondence for GLn. For a reductive group G over a p-adic field F, the affine Grassmannian GrG = G(F((t)))/G(F[[t]])) parametrizes G-bundles on XC with a trivialization away from a point. The mechanism is the geometrization of the Satake isomorphism: the spherical Hecke algebra of G(F) acts on the cohomology of GrG via convolution, matching the action of the representation ring of ^LG under the geometric Satake equivalence. In the p-adic setting, XC replaces the formal disc, and the factorization property of GrG over XC yields a monoidal structure on sheaves compatible with the tensor product of representations of ^LG. The main theorem (Fargues–Scholze, 2021) establishes a fully faithful embedding of the category of smooth irreducible representations of G(F) into the derived category of étale sheaves on the stack of G-bundles, with GrG providing the local Hecke operators generating the correspondence.

The Affine Grassmannian and the Quantum Cohomology of the Affine Grassmannian

The quantum cohomology ring QH*(GrG) deforms the ordinary cohomology ring by genus‑zero Gromov–Witten invariants counting rational curves in the affine Grassmannian. For G simply connected, GrG is an ind‑projective variety stratified by Schubert varieties Grλ indexed by dominant coweights λ. The quantum product σλ ⋆ σμ = Σν,d ⟨σλ, σμ, σν⟩0,3,d qd σν involves the quantum parameter q corresponding to the generator of H2(GrG, ℤ) ≅ ℤ. Peterson's theorem (unpublished, later verified by Lam and Shimozono) shows that QH*(GrG) is isomorphic to the coordinate ring of the Langlands dual group ^LG with a certain Poisson structure, where q corresponds to the central element of the affine Kac–Moody algebra at critical level. The quantum Giambelli formula expresses quantum Schubert classes as non‑commutative Schur functions in the quantum elementary symmetric functions, which are the images of the fundamental representations of ^LG under the quantum Satake isomorphism. The quantum Satake correspondence, established by Braverman, Finkelberg, and Nakajima, identifies the quantum cohomology of GrG with the representation ring of the quantum group Uq(^Lg) at generic q, where the convolution product is deformed by the R‑matrix of the quantum loop group. This gives a geometric realization of the quantum group at the level of cohomology and underlies 3‑dimensional mirror symmetry between the affine Grassmannian and the Coulomb branch of a 3d 𝒩 = 4 gauge theory.

The Affine Grassmannian and the Derived Category of Coherent Sheaves on the Affine Grassmannian

The derived category DbCoh(GrG) of coherent sheaves on the affine Grassmannian carries a monoidal structure given by the convolution product, defined via the correspondence GrG × GrG ← GrG ⋃ GrG → GrG. The quantum K‑theoretic Satake correspondence identifies the convolution product on equivariant coherent sheaves on GrG with the tensor product of representations of the quantum loop group. The quantum K‑theory ring admits a presentation via quantum K‑theoretic Schubert classes satisfying a quantum K‑Giambelli formula expressing them as non‑commutative determinants of quantum K‑theoretic elementary symmetric functions. The quantum K‑theoretic R‑matrix governs the braiding of the convolution product and coincides with the universal R‑matrix of the quantum loop group. These constants are K‑theoretic Gromov–Witten invariants, integers computable via the quantum K‑theoretic Littlewood–Richardson rule. The quantum K‑theoretic Pieri rule describes the product of a Schubert class with a quantum K‑theoretic elementary symmetric function, and the quantum K‑theoretic Giambelli formula expresses an arbitrary Schubert class as a non‑commutative determinant of these elementary symmetric functions. The calculus is governed by the quantum K‑theoretic affine nilHecke algebra acting via quantum K‑theoretic Demazure operators. A quantum K‑theoretic analogue of the Peterson isomorphism identifies the quantum K‑theory ring of GrG with the center of the quantum loop group at a specialized parameter, with quantum K‑theoretic Schubert classes corresponding to characters of fundamental representations. Recent work of Lenart, Maeno, and Shimozono extends these results to all simply laced types using quantum K‑theoretic crystals, combinatorial models for quantum K‑theoretic representations of the quantum affine algebra.

The Affine Grassmannian and the Theory of Affine Schubert Varieties and Their Singularities

Affine Schubert varieties are the closures of the orbits of the positive loop group G(O) on the affine Grassmannian GrG = G(K)/G(O). They are indexed by dominant coweights λ of the Langlands dual group LG. Each Schubert variety Grλ is a projective, typically singular, algebraic variety over the base field k. The singularities of these varieties are governed by the Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials attached to the affine Weyl group Waff. In characteristic zero, the intersection cohomology complexes IC(Grλ) are perverse sheaves whose stalks are described by the coefficients of the Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials. This description underpins the geometric Satake equivalence: the category of G(O)-equivariant perverse sheaves on GrG is tensor equivalent to the category of finite‑dimensional representations of LG.

Mirković and Vilonen showed that the irreducible components of the intersections Grλ ∩ Grμ (the Mirković–Vilonen cycles) provide a geometric realization of the canonical basis of the quantum group Uq(Lg). The singularities of Grλ are rational; in positive characteristic they are Frobenius split, a result of Kumar, Lauritzen, and Thomsen, which implies the vanishing of higher direct images of the structure sheaf under a resolution of singularities. This Frobenius splitting yields vanishing theorems for cohomology of line bundles on Schubert varieties and underlies Zhu's proof of the geometric Satake equivalence in positive characteristic.

The local structure of a Schubert variety near a point corresponding to a coweight μ ≤ λ is isomorphic to a product of a finite‑dimensional Schubert variety in the finite flag variety and an affine Schubert variety of smaller rank. This inductive description allows recursive computation of the Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials via the affine Hecke algebra. The singular locus of Grλ is the union of those Grμ for which the corresponding Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomial Pμ,λ(q) has degree strictly greater than (ℓ(λ)−ℓ(μ)−1)/2. In particular, the smooth locus coincides with the open Schubert cell Grλ° exactly when λ is minuscule.

Braverman, Finkelberg, and Gaitsgory extended the study of singularities to the derived setting: the derived category of coherent sheaves on Grλ carries a natural t‑structure whose heart is the category of G(O)-equivariant perverse coherent sheaves. The singular support of objects in this heart is contained in the conormal variety of the stratification by Schubert cells, linking the geometry of singularities to the representation theory of the affine Kac–Moody algebra at critical level.

The Affine Grassmannian and the Theory of the Affine Grassmannian for Loop Groups over Rings of Integers

When the base field is replaced by a ring of integers 𝒪F in a local field F (or a global field), the loop group G(F) and its positive part G(𝒪F) acquire a natural profinite group structure. The quotient GrG,𝒪F = G(F)/G(𝒪F) is an ind‑scheme over Spec 𝒪F whose special fiber is the affine Grassmannian over the residue field and whose generic fiber is the affine Grassmannian over F. This integral model is essential for the study of p‑adic uniformization of Shimura varieties and for the construction of local models of Rapoport–Zink spaces.

Pappas and Zhu constructed a smooth integral model for the affine Grassmannian of a reductive group G over 𝒪F when G is tamely ramified. Their construction uses the theory of Bruhat–Tits buildings: the affine Grassmannian over 𝒪F represents the functor that assigns to an 𝒪F-algebra R the set of G-torsors on the formal disc Spec R[[t]] trivialized on the punctured disc Spec R((t)). The special fiber is a union of Schubert varieties indexed by the dominant coweights of the quasi‑split inner form of G over the residue field.

In the case G = GLn, the integral affine Grassmannian classifies lattices in an n-dimensional vector space over F up to homothety. Its 𝒪F-points are in bijection with the vertices of the Bruhat–Tits building of GLn(F). The Schubert varieties in the special fiber are precisely the closures of the GLn(𝒪F)-orbits on the reduced affine Grassmannian, and their geometry reflects the combinatorics of the affine symmetric group Ŝn.

For a general reductive group, the integral model is not always smooth; the singularities of the special fiber are controlled by the ramification of G. When G splits over a tamely ramified extension, the special fiber is a reduced union of normal varieties with rational singularities. The nearby cycles functor on the integral affine Grassmannian yields a monodromy operator that matches the action of the inertia subgroup of the Weil group on the cohomology of the generic fiber, providing a geometric incarnation of the local Langlands correspondence for G.

Worked Example: Singularities of a Minuscule Schubert Variety
Let G = SL₃ over ℂ and let λ be the fundamental coweight ω₁ (the first fundamental weight of the dual group PGL₃). Show that the Schubert variety Grω₁ in the affine Grassmannian GrSL₃ is smooth and identify its isomorphism type.
The coweight ω₁ is minuscule for SL₃, meaning that the Weyl group orbit of ω₁ consists only of the weights ω₁, ω₂, and −ω₁−ω₂, and the pairing with any positive root is 0 or 1. For a minuscule coweight, the corresponding Schubert variety Grλ is known to be smooth and isomorphic to a generalized Grassmannian. In this case, Grω₁ parametrizes lattices L ⊂ ℂ((t))³ such that tℂ[[t]]³ ⊂ L ⊂ ℂ[[t]]³ and the quotient ℂ[[t]]³/L is a one‑dimensional ℂ‑vector space. This is precisely the projective plane ℙ². The smoothness follows from the general theorem that minuscule Schubert varieties are smooth (they are homogeneous under the action of the parabolic subgroup of the loop group associated to the stabilizer of λ). Hence Grω₁ ≅ ℙ², a smooth variety of dimension 2.

The Affine Grassmannian and the Theory of the Affine Weyl Group Action on Equivariant Cohomology

The equivariant cohomology HT⁎(GrG) with respect to a maximal torus T ⊂ G carries a natural action of the affine Weyl group Waff = W ⋉ Q∨, where W is the finite Weyl group and Q∨ is the coroot lattice. This action is realized geometrically by the convolution product with the fundamental classes of the Schubert varieties. The Peterson isomorphism identifies HT⁎(GrG) with the center of the completed universal enveloping algebra U(^L g) at the critical level, and under this isomorphism the affine Weyl group action corresponds to the action of the center on Verma modules.

Explicitly, for each simple affine reflection si there is a Demazure operator ∂i acting on HT⁎(GrG) by the formula ∂i(f) = (f − si·f)/αi, where αi is the corresponding simple root. These operators satisfy the braid relations of Waff and generate the nilHecke algebra. The Schubert classes [Grλ] form a basis of HT⁎(GrG) over HT⁎(pt), and the action of Waff on this basis is given by the affine Bruhat order: si·[Grλ] = [Grsiλ] if siλ > λ, and a more complicated expression involving the Demazure operator otherwise.

The fixed point set of the torus action on GrG is discrete and indexed by the coweight lattice Q∨. The localization theorem expresses the equivariant cohomology as a subring of the ring of functions on Q∨ with values in HT⁎(pt). Under this embedding, the affine Weyl group acts by the natural translation action on Q∨ together with the finite Weyl group action on the coefficients. This description is the starting point for the computation of the quantum cohomology and quantum K‑theory of the affine Grassmannian via the Peterson isomorphism and its quantum deformations.

In the derived setting, the affine Weyl group acts on the derived category of G(O)-equivariant coherent sheaves on GrG via the spherical Hecke category. The resulting categorical action categorifies the nilHecke algebra action on equivariant cohomology and is closely related to the geometric Langlands correspondence for the affine Kac–Moody algebra.

The Affine Grassmannian and the Theory of the Affine Bruhat Order and Its Geometric Realization

The affine Bruhat order is a partial order on the affine Weyl group Waff defined by the containment of Schubert varieties: for x, y ∈ Waff, one writes x ≤ y if and only if the Schubert variety Grx is contained in the closure of Gry. This order extends the finite Bruhat order on the finite Weyl group W and is graded by the length function ℓ. The covering relations correspond to multiplication by simple affine reflections that increase length.

Geometrically, the affine Bruhat order governs the stratification of the affine Grassmannian by G(O)-orbits. Each orbit Grλ° is an affine space of dimension ℓ(λ), and its closure Grλ is the union of all Grμ° with μ ≤ λ. The poset of Schubert varieties is thus isomorphic to the set of dominant coweights equipped with the dominance order, which coincides with the restriction of the affine Bruhat order to the set of minimal length representatives in the cosets Waff/W.

The affine Bruhat order also appears in the combinatorics of the affine nilHecke algebra. The Demazure operators ∂i satisfy ∂i([Grλ]) = [Grsiλ] when siλ > λ, and zero otherwise. This rule allows one to compute the product of Schubert classes in the equivariant cohomology ring recursively. The structure constants are the Littlewood–Richardson coefficients for the affine Grassmannian, which are known to be non‑negative integers by the geometric Satake equivalence.

In the quantum setting, the affine Bruhat order is deformed by the quantum parameter q. The quantum Bruhat graph has vertices Waff and edges labeled by positive roots, encoding the quantum product of Schubert classes. The quantum Chevalley formula expresses the product of a divisor class with a Schubert class as a sum over quantum Bruhat graph edges. This graph is acyclic and its edges correspond to certain rational curves in the affine Grassmannian, linking the combinatorics of the affine Bruhat order to the enumerative geometry of the affine Grassmannian.

The Affine Grassmannian and the Theory of the Affine Schubert Cells and Their Cohomology Rings

The affine Grassmannian admits a cellular decomposition into affine Schubert cells Grλ° ≅ 𝔸ℓ(λ) indexed by dominant coweights λ. This decomposition is a paving by affine spaces, which implies that the (equivariant) cohomology ring HT⁎(GrG) is a free module over HT⁎(pt) with basis given by the Schubert classes σλ = [Grλ]. The ring structure is determined by the convolution product, which corresponds under the geometric Satake equivalence to the tensor product of representations of the Langlands dual group.

Kostant and Kumar showed that the cohomology ring H⁎(GrG) (with trivial torus action) is isomorphic to the polynomial ring ℤ[σω₁, …, σωr] where ωi are the fundamental coweights. The Schubert classes of the minuscule coweights generate the cohomology ring, and the relations are precisely the Plücker relations for the corresponding generalized Grassmannians. For G = SLn, the cohomology ring is the ring of symmetric polynomials in n variables, with the Schubert classes mapping to the Schur polynomials.

In equivariant cohomology, the ring HT⁎(GrG) is a quotient of the polynomial ring HT⁎(pt)[xλ] by the ideal generated by the relations xλ xμ = Σ cλ,μ^ν xν, where the structure constants cλ,μ^ν are the Littlewood–Richardson coefficients. The Peterson isomorphism identifies this ring with the center of the affine Kac–Moody algebra at the critical level, providing a representation‑theoretic interpretation of the product.

The quantum cohomology ring QH⁎(GrG) deforms the classical product by genus‑zero Gromov–Witten invariants. It is expected to be isomorphic to the representation ring of the quantum group Uq(^L g) at generic q. The quantum Schubert classes satisfy a quantum Giambelli formula, and the quantum product is governed by the quantum affine nilHecke algebra. The quantum K‑theory ring QK(GrG) further deforms the equivariant K‑theory ring by K‑theoretic Gromov–Witten invariants, and is conjecturally isomorphic to the representation ring of the quantum loop group Uq(^L ĝ).
