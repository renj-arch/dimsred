Knot Theory — From Trefoil to Khovanov Homology

Knot theory studies embeddings of S¹ in S³ (or ℝ³) up to ambient isotopy — deformations without cutting. It's a subfield of low-dimensional topology with deep connections to algebra (braid groups, Yang-Baxter equation), quantum invariants (Jones polynomial), and categorical structures (Khovanov homology). The classification of knots remains fundamentally open.

"Knot theory is the study of closed curves in 3-space — the simplest nontrivial objects in topology."
— Dale Rolfsen
Part 1

🪢 Knot Invariants — Distinguishing the Inseparable

Coloring and Crossing
Reidemeister Moves, Tricolorability, and the Alexander Polynomial
A knot is an embedding S¹ → ℝ³; two knots are isotopic if one can be deformed into the other without passing through itself. The three Reidemeister moves (twist, poke, slide) generate all isotopies between knot diagrams. A knot invariant is a quantity unchanged by Reidemeister moves. Tricolorability: a knot is tricolorable if each crossing can be colored with 3 colors such that at each crossing, either all three or only one color meet. The trefoil is tricolorable; the figure-8 is not. The Alexander polynomial Δ_K(t) is computed from a knot diagram via the skein relation: Δ_{L+} − Δ_{L-} = (t^{1/2} − t^{-1/2})Δ_{L0}. For the trefoil, Δ(t) = t − 1 + t^{-1}. The knot group π₁(ℝ³\K) is the fundamental group of the complement — it's a powerful invariant computed via the Wirtinger presentation from crossings.
Knot invariants separate knots into equivalence classes — tricolorability is the simplest; the Alexander polynomial is the oldest algebraic invariant.
Part 2

🧶 Braid Groups and the Jones Polynomial

Braiding and Quantum Trace
Braid Groups, the Jones Polynomial, and the Yang-Baxter Equation
A braid on n strands weaves strands without backtracking. Artin's braid group B_n has generators σ_i (cross strand i over i+1) with relations σ_iσ_{i+1}σ_i = σ_{i+1}σ_iσ_{i+1} (braid relation) and σ_iσ_j = σ_jσ_i for |i-j|≥2. Every knot is the closure of some braid (Alexander's theorem). The Jones polynomial V_K(t) ∈ ℤ[t^{±1/2}] satisfies V_{L+} − tV_{L-} = (1 − t^{1/2})V_{L0} with V_{unknot} = 1. It detects the chirality of the trefoil (V(3₁) = t + t³ − t⁴, V(mirror) = t^{-1} + t^{-3} − t^{-4}). The Jones polynomial arises from von Neumann algebras or the Yang-Baxter equation R∈End(V⊗V) satisfying (R⊗I)(I⊗R)(R⊗I) = (I⊗R)(R⊗I)(I⊗R). Solutions to YBE give braid group representations and knot invariants via the trace of the braid word.
The Jones polynomial links knot theory to statistical mechanics, quantum groups, and operator algebras — a revolutionary connection.
Part 3

🔬 Khovanov Homology — Categorifying the Jones Polynomial

From Polynomial to Homology
Khovanov Homology — When Knot Invariants Become Bigraded Vector Spaces
Khovanov homology (2000) categorifies the Jones polynomial: instead of a polynomial V_K(t), we get a bigraded homology theory Kh^{i,j}(K) whose graded Euler characteristic Σ (−1)^i t^j dim Kh^{i,j} = V_K(t). It assigns a chain complex to a knot diagram whose homology is an invariant. The complex is built from Kauffman states (resolutions of crossings). Khovanov homology is strictly stronger than the Jones polynomial — it distinguishes knots that the Jones polynomial cannot. The Lee-Rasmussen spectral sequence relates Khovanov homology to the s-invariant, a concordance invariant that gives lower bounds on slice genus and detects exotic smooth structures on ℝ⁴. The s-invariant of a positive torus knot T(p,q) is (p-1)(q-1). Heegaard Floer homology (Ozsváth-Szabó) provides a different categorification for 3-manifolds, with knot Floer homology giving the genus of a knot.
Khovanov homology turns the Jones polynomial into a richer algebraic structure — the homology is a knot invariant, not just its Euler characteristic.
Part 4

🌊 Concordance and 4-Dimensional Topology

When Knots Bound Surfaces
Slice Knots, Concordance Groups, and Exotic ℝ⁴
A knot K is slice if it bounds a smooth properly embedded disk in B⁴ — it's the boundary of a surface in 4D. The concordance group C has knots modulo slice knots — the operation is connected sum. The trefoil is not slice (it has nonzero Arf invariant). The Levine-Tristram signature σ_ω(K) is the signature of the symmetrized Alexander matrix evaluated on S¹. For alternating knots, the signature is computed from the knot diagram. The Rasmussen s-invariant from Khovanov homology gives a homomorphism s: C → 2ℤ — it's a powerful concordance obstruction. The topological slice genus is the minimal genus of a locally flat surface in B⁴ bounding K. Freedman's theorem (1982) classified topological 4-manifolds using Casson handles — showing that every knot with trivial Alexander polynomial is topologically slice. This led to the existence of exotic ℝ⁴: smooth structures on ℝ⁴ that are homeomorphic but not diffeomorphic to standard ℝ⁴ — a phenomenon unique to dimension 4.
Knot concordance is the gateway to 4-dimensional topology — exotic ℝ⁴ and the classification of 4-manifolds hinge on slice knots.
