Extended Topological Field Theory — The Cobordism Hypothesis, Higher Categories & the Turaev-Viro Construction

Deep dive into Extended Topological Field Theory — Atiyah's axiomatic definition of a TQFT as a symmetric monoidal functor from the cobordism category to the category of vector spaces, the Baez-Dolan cobordism hypothesis (a fully extended n-dimensional TFT is determined entirely by its value on a single point, which must be a fully dualizable object in an (∞,n)-category), (∞,n)-categories and the homotopy hypothesis, the Lurie proof of the cobordism hypothesis using Morse theory and the classification of framed manifolds, the Turaev-Viro-Barrett-Westbury state sum TQFT (3d TFT from spherical fusion categories using the 6j-symbols of the category), the Reshetikhin-Turaev invariants of 3-manifolds constructed from modular tensor categories (skein theory and the Jones-Witten invariants), the Witten-Reshetikhin-Turaev TQFT (the 3-dimensional Chern-Simons TQFT), the Crane-Yetter invariant in 4 dimensions (a state sum for 4-manifolds from a braided fusion category), the Dijkgraaf-Witten TQFT (finite group gauge theory in arbitrary dimension), and the application to topological quantum computing (anyons: particles with braid statistics in 2+1 dimensions, and the Fibonacci anyon model for universal quantum computation).

"A topological quantum field theory is a symmetric monoidal functor from the cobordism category to the category of vector spaces."

Part 1

📏 Axiomatic TQFT and the Cobordism Category

Axioms
Atiyah's Axioms, the Cobordism Category, and the Pachner Moves
The cobordism category Cob_d has objects: closed (d−1)-dimensional manifolds, and morphisms: d-dimensional cobordisms (manifolds M with boundary ∂M = Σ₀ ⊔ Σ₁, where Σ₀ is the incoming and Σ₁ the outgoing boundary). Composition is gluing of cobordisms along a common boundary component. The monoidal structure is disjoint union, and the unit is the empty manifold. A (d-dimensional) topological quantum field theory (TQFT) is a symmetric monoidal functor Z: Cob_d → Vect_𝕜 (the category of vector spaces over a field 𝕜). Atiyah's axioms: Z(∅) = 𝕜, Z(Σ ⊔ Σ') = Z(Σ) ⊗ Z(Σ'), Z(∂_in M) → Z(∂_out M) is a linear map, and Z is diffeomorphism invariant (depends only on the diffeomorphism type of the cobordism). The Pachner moves (bistellar moves) relate any two triangulations of a PL manifold by a finite sequence of local moves — a TQFT must be invariant under these moves. The Gluing axiom: Z(M₁ ∪_Σ M₂) = Z(M₂) ∘ Z(M₁). The TQFT is unitary if Z(Σ^op) = Z(Σ)^* (Hermitian adjoint). The Frobenius algebra: Z(S^{d−1}) is a finite-dimensional commutative Frobenius algebra with trace given by Z(M) where M is the d-ball with boundary S^{d−1}.
Atiyah's axioms formalize TQFT as a functor — the cobordism category encodes the topology of manifolds and their gluings, and the TQFT assigns algebraic invariants that respect gluing.
Part 2

🌍 The Cobordism Hypothesis

Hypothesis
Baez-Dolan Cobordism Hypothesis, (∞,n)-Categories, and the Lurie Proof
The cobordism hypothesis (Baez-Dolan, 1995): a fully extended n-dimensional TQFT is completely determined by the value it assigns to a single point. To state this precisely, we need higher category theory. An (∞,n)-category is a weak n-category where all k-morphisms for k > n are invertible (up to homotopy). The bordism (∞,n)-category Bord_n^fr has: objects = 0-manifolds (points), 1-morphisms = 1-bordisms, ..., n-morphisms = n-bordisms, and (n+1)-morphisms = diffeomorphisms, ..., all up to isotopy. The cobordism hypothesis (proved by Lurie, 2009) states: for a symmetric monoidal (∞,n)-category C, evaluation on a point gives an equivalence between the ∞-groupoid of fully extended TQFTs Z: Bord_n^fr → C and the ∞-groupoid of fully dualizable objects in C. A fully dualizable object is one for which all higher adjoints exist (the object has a dual, the dual has its own dual, etc., up to level n). The framing is necessary because the cobordism hypothesis works with framed manifolds; for oriented or unoriented manifolds, we need to factor through the homotopy quotient of the orthogonal group. The Serre functor and Nakayama automorphism appear in the classification of TQFTs with non-trivial anomalies. The Morita equivalence of fully dualizable objects corresponds to equivalence of TQFTs.
The cobordism hypothesis is a classification theorem: fully extended TQFTs are classified by fully dualizable objects in higher categories — a point contains all the information.
Part 3

🧮 State Sum TQFTs and Quantum Invariants

Invariants
Turaev-Viro State Sum, Reshetikhin-Turaev Invariants, and Modular Tensor Categories
The Turaev-Viro TQFT (Turaev-Viro, 1992) is a 3-dimensional state sum TQFT constructed from a spherical fusion category C. Given a triangulated 3-manifold, the invariant Z(M) = Σ_{labelings of edges} Π_{tetrahedra} {6j-symbol} where the sum is over labelings of the edges by simple objects of C, and each tetrahedron contributes the 6j-symbol of C. The Barrett-Westbury theorem: the Turaev-Viro invariant is well-defined (independent of triangulation) if and only if C is a spherical fusion category. The Reshetikhin-Turaev TQFT (Reshetikhin-Turaev, 1991) constructs 3-manifold invariants from a modular tensor category (MTC) — a braided fusion category with a non-degenerate S-matrix. The S-matrix S_{ab} = Tr(σ_{b,a} ∘ σ_{a,b}) (the braiding eigenvalues) is invertible. The T-matrix T_{ab} = δ_{ab} θ_a (the twist factors). The triple (S, T, c) gives a projective representation of SL(2,ℤ). The Witten-Reshetikhin-Turaev invariant for Chern-Simons theory with gauge group G at level k is constructed from the modular tensor category Rep(U_q(g)) at q = exp(2πi/(k + h^∨)). The Jones polynomial of a knot K is recovered as the expectation value of the Wilson loop in SU(2) Chern-Simons theory: J_K(q) = ⟨W_K⟩_{CS}. The Crane-Yetter invariant (1993) generalizes Turaev-Viro to 4 dimensions using a braided fusion category — it is a state sum for 4-manifolds.
State sum TQFTs produce invariants of manifolds by summing over combinatorial data — the 6j-symbols of fusion categories encode the topology of triangulations.
Part 4

🔥 Topological Quantum Computing and Anyons

Computing
Anyons, Braid Statistics, the Fibonacci Anyon Model, and Topological Quantum Computation
Anyons are quasiparticles in 2-dimensional systems whose exchange statistics are neither bosonic nor fermionic — they are governed by the braid group B_n (the group of braids on n strands) rather than the permutation group. In a TQFT, anyon types correspond to the simple objects of a modular tensor category. The fusion rules describe how two anyons combine: a × b = Σ_c N_{ab}^c c, where N_{ab}^c are the fusion coefficients. The braiding is an isomorphism σ_{a,b}: a ⊗ b → b ⊗ a satisfying the Yang-Baxter equation. The topological quantum computation (Kitaev, Freedman) uses the braiding of anyons to encode and process quantum information. The Fibonacci anyon model: there is one non-trivial anyon τ with fusion rule τ × τ = 1 + τ. The dimension of the Hilbert space for n anyons is the n-th Fibonacci number F_{n+1} — hence the name. Braiding of Fibonacci anyons is universal for quantum computation (the braid group B_n is dense in the unitary group U(F_{n+1})). The quantum dimension d_a = Tr(Id_a) of an anyon a is the asymptotic growth rate of the Hilbert space dimension. The S-matrix S_{ab} = (1/D) Σ_c N_{ab}^c d_c θ_c (where D = Σ_a d_a² is the total quantum dimension) encodes the mutual statistics of anyon types. The Ising anyon model (from the SU(2)₂ MTC) has three anyon types: 1, σ, ψ with σ × σ = 1 + ψ and σ × ψ = σ — this model supports Clifford gate operations but is not universal. The Freedman-Kitaev conjecture: the TQFT from SU(2) at level k can be simulated by a quantum computer, and for k ≥ 3 (Fibonacci anyons), the simulation is universal.
Topological quantum computation uses the braiding of anyons — the worldlines of anyons in 2+1 dimensions are braids, and the TQFT assigns unitary matrices to these braids, enabling fault-tolerant quantum gates.
