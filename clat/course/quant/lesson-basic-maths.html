The Number System

The number system defines the rules governing integers, decimals, and fractions, acting as the vocabulary for all mathematical operations.

Definition: A real number is either rational (can be expressed as p/q where q is not zero) or irrational (cannot be expressed as such). The set of natural numbers starts from 1, while whole numbers include 0. Integers encompass both positive and negative values along with zero.

The Core Rule: When multiplying two integers, the sign of the result depends strictly on the signs of the operands. If you multiply a positive by a negative, the result is negative. If you multiply two negatives, they cancel out to produce a positive. This works because multiplication represents repeated addition; subtracting a negative quantity is mathematically equivalent to adding a positive quantity.

The Division Rule: When dividing fractions, you must invert the second fraction (the divisor) and change the operation to multiplication. This is necessary because dividing by a number is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal. For instance, dividing by 1/2 is the same as multiplying by 2.

Examiner Pattern: Questions often ask you to identify if a decimal terminates or repeats. If the denominator of a simplified fraction contains only prime factors of 2 and/or 5, the decimal terminates. If it contains any other prime factor (like 3, 7, or 11), the decimal will repeat infinitely.

LCM and HCF

Least Common Multiple (LCM) and Highest Common Factor (HCF) are essential for solving problems involving cycles, ratios, and divisibility.

Definition: The HCF of two or more numbers is the largest number that divides each of them exactly. The LCM is the smallest number that is divisible by each of the given numbers.

The Fundamental Formula: For any two positive integers a and b, the product of their numbers equals the product of their HCF and LCM. Mathematically, this is written as: a × b = HCF(a,b) × LCM(a,b). This relationship holds true because the prime factors common to both numbers form the HCF, while all unique and common factors combined form the LCM.

The Pattern for Solving: To find these values quickly, you must use prime factorization. Break every number down into its prime building blocks (2, 3, 5, 7, etc.). For HCF, take only the common prime factors with the lowest power. For LCM, take all prime factors present in any of the numbers, using the highest power found.

Examiner Pattern: The examiner frequently asks for the time when two events repeat together (like traffic lights changing) or the largest container size that can measure exact volumes of liquids. These require finding the LCM. Conversely, questions about dividing items into equal groups with no remainder require finding the HCF.

Mensuration

Mensuration deals with the measurement of geometric shapes, calculating their area, perimeter, and volume.

Definition: Area measures the space enclosed within a 2D shape, while Volume measures the space occupied by a 3D object. Perimeter is the total length of the boundary.

The Exact Formulas:

  • Circle: Area = πr²; Circumference = 2πr or πd. Here, r is radius and d is diameter (d=2r).
  • Square: Area = side²; Perimeter = 4 × side.
  • Rectangle: Area = length × breadth.
  • Cylinder: Curved Surface Area = 2πrh; Total Surface Area = 2πr(h+r); Volume = πr²h.

The Logic Behind Formulas: The area of a circle is derived by cutting the circle into sectors and rearranging them into a shape resembling a rectangle with height r and length πr. The volume of a cylinder is simply the area of the circular base multiplied by the height, essentially stacking circles on top of each other.

Examiner Pattern: You will often see questions involving "squares inscribed in circles" or "circles inscribed in squares." Remember that if a circle fits perfectly inside a square, the diameter equals the side length. If a square fits inside a circle, the diagonal of the square equals the diameter of the circle.

Probability

Probability quantifies the likelihood of an event occurring, ranging from impossible (0) to certain (1).

Definition: The probability of an event E is defined as the ratio of the number of favorable outcomes to the total number of possible outcomes. It is expressed as P(E) = Favorable Outcomes / Total Outcomes.

The Fundamental Rule: The sum of probabilities of all possible mutually exclusive events in a sample space must always equal 1. This is because one of the outcomes must happen. If you know the probability of an event happening, the probability of it NOT happening is simply 1 minus that probability.

The Pattern for Dice and Cards: When rolling two dice, the total number of outcomes is 6 × 6 = 36. For a standard deck of cards, there are 52 cards, divided into 4 suits (Hearts, Diamonds, Clubs, Spades) and 13 ranks per suit.

Examiner Pattern: Questions often involve "at least one" scenarios. To solve these easily, calculate the probability of the complement (none happening) and subtract it from 1. For example, to find the chance of getting at least one head in two tosses, calculate the chance of getting no heads (TT) and subtract from 1.

Technique: Finding LCM without Prime Factorization
Question: Find the LCM of 12, 15, and 20 using a method other than full prime factorization.
Solution:
Instead of breaking every number down immediately, look for relationships.
Step 1: Write the numbers: 12, 15, 20.
Step 2: Find LCM of first two (12 and 15). Multiples of 12 are 12, 24, 36, 48, 60. Multiples of 15 are 15, 30, 45, 60. The common multiple is 60.
Step 3: Now find LCM of 60 and the remaining number (20). Since 60 is a multiple of 20 (20 × 3 = 60), the LCM is simply 60.
Final Answer: The LCM of 12, 15, and 20 is 60.
Technique: Area Conversion in Mensuration
Question: A rectangular field has a length of 50 meters and a breadth of 40 meters. If the cost of fencing is ₹15 per meter, what is the total cost? (Note: Do not calculate area for this specific question).
Solution:
The question asks for fencing cost, which depends on Perimeter, not Area.
Step 1: Identify shape = Rectangle.
Step 2: Formula for Perimeter = 2 × (Length + Breadth).
Step 3: Substitute values: 2 × (50 + 40) = 2 × 90 = 180 meters.
Step 4: Calculate Cost = Perimeter × Rate per meter.
Step 5: Cost = 180 × 15.
Calculation: 180 × 10 = 1800; 180 × 5 = 900. Total = 2700.
Final Answer: ₹2700.

Common Traps

Avoid these frequent errors that cost valuable marks:

  • Confusing LCM and HCF formulas: Students often mix up which formula to use for area vs. perimeter or volume vs. surface area.
  • Neglecting Units: If length is in cm and breadth is in m, you cannot multiply them directly. Always convert to the same unit first.
  • Ignoring the "At Least" wording in Probability: Failing to use the complement rule (1 - P(not happening)) leads to undercounting outcomes.
  • Misinterpreting Fractions: Thinking that 1/2 of a number is larger than 1/3 of the same number, forgetting that smaller denominators mean larger fractions.

Memory Tricks

Use these shortcuts to recall rules instantly during the exam:

  • The "Zero" Rule for HCF/LCM: If any number in a set is 0, the LCM is undefined (or infinite) because you cannot divide by zero. However, if one number is a multiple of another, the LCM is simply the larger number.
  • The "Pi" Approximation: Memorize π ≈ 22/7 for quick calculations in circles. If the diameter ends in 0 or 5, use 3.14; otherwise, 22/7 is usually faster.
  • Square Numbers Pattern: The last digit of a square number depends only on the last digit of the root. For example, numbers ending in 3 or 7 will always produce squares ending in 9 (since 3²=9 and 7²=49).
Exam-Day Strategy
Start with the Simplification and Number System questions as they are quick wins. Leave Mensuration involving complex shapes (like spheres or cones) for last if you are running out of time. Always write down the given units before starting calculations to avoid conversion errors. If a probability question seems too long, immediately check if the complement method is faster.
