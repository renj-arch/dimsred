A. Denying the antecedent: it assumes that because the act was not intended to kill, it cannot be voluntary.
B. Affirming the consequent: it infers the presence of the required mental element (knowledge) solely from the occurrence of death.
C. Circular reasoning: it uses the conclusion (liability) as the premise for establishing the act was voluntary.
D. False dilemma: it presents only two extremes — either full liability or no liability — ignoring partial defences.
Correct Answer: B
Correct answer: Affirming the consequent: it infers the presence of the required mental element (knowledge) solely from the occurrence of death..
