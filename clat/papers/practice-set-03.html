Q4. Logical Reasoningmodel-generated · 2026-11-08
In a logical reasoning test involving sets, consider the following premises: 1) All cats are mammals. 2) Some mammals are pets. What can be logically inferred from these two statements alone?
A. A) All cats are pets.
B. B) Some cats are pets.
C. C) No cats are pets.
D. D) It cannot be determined whether any cats are pets based on the given information.
Correct Answer: D
From premise 1, we know cats belong to the set of mammals. From premise 2, we know there is an overlap between mammals and pets, but we do not know if that overlap includes cats. The intersection of 'cats' and 'pets' could be empty, partial, or total; the premises do not provide enough information to confirm any specific relationship between cats and pets. Therefore, options A, B, and C make definitive claims that are not supported by the logic. Only option D correctly reflects the logical limitation.

Q10. Legal Reasoningmodel-generated · 2026-11-08
A contract between Party A and Party B stipulates that Party A must deliver goods by Friday. However, an unforeseen flood destroys the factory where the goods were to be manufactured on Thursday. Under the doctrine of frustration of contract, what is the legal consequence regarding Party A's liability for non-delivery?
A. Party A is liable for breach of contract because the duty was absolute.
B. Party A is not liable because the impossibility of performance was caused by an act of God (force majeure).
C. Party A must pay damages to Party B but can claim reimbursement from the insurance company.
D. Party A is automatically discharged from liability only if they had taken reasonable steps to mitigate the loss before the flood occurred.
Correct Answer: B
The doctrine of frustration discharges parties from their obligations when a supervening event, unforeseen and without fault (like an act of God), makes performance impossible. Since the flood destroyed the goods before the performance date, Party A is not liable for breach. Option 0 is incorrect because absolute duty does not override impossibility due to force majeure. Options 2 and 3 introduce mitigation or insurance concepts that do not negate the primary effect of frustration in this specific scenario.

Q12. Quantitative Techniquesmodel-generated · 2026-11-07
A train travels at an average speed of 60 km/hr without stoppages and 40 km/hr with stoppages. How many minutes does the train stop per hour?
A. A. 15 minutes
B. B. 20 minutes
C. C. 25 minutes
D. D. 30 minutes
Correct Answer: B
The difference in speed due to stoppages is 60 - 40 = 20 km/hr. This means the train covers 20 km less per hour because of stops. At a running speed of 60 km/hr, covering 20 km takes (20/60) * 60 minutes = 20 minutes. Therefore, the train stops for 20 minutes per hour. Option A is incorrect as it underestimates the time lost; Option C and D are incorrect as they overestimate the stoppage time.

A. RZPZ

Correct Answer: B
