Q1. Legal Reasoning
In a criminal trial, the prosecution presents a witness who testifies that they saw the accused commit the crime. However, it is later revealed that the witness had a motive to lie against the accused due to a personal grudge. How does this affect the admissibility of the testimony?
A. The testimony becomes inadmissible as hearsay because it relies on a biased source.
B. The testimony remains admissible but carries less weight; the judge or jury may consider the bias during evaluation.
C. The court must automatically dismiss the entire prosecution case due to the witness's credibility issue.
D. The testimony is inadmissible unless corroborated by independent physical evidence.
Correct Answer: B
Witness credibility and bias are matters for the fact-finder (judge/jury) to evaluate; the testimony itself remains admissible in court. The presence of a motive to lie does not render the statement hearsay or automatically inadmissible under the law, though it affects its probative value. Option 0 is incorrect as hearsay refers to out-of-court statements offered for their truth, not live testimony with credibility issues. Options 2 and 3 are too absolute; courts do not automatically dismiss cases solely due to bias without a full trial on credibility.
Q2. Legal Reasoning
In a dispute over a disputed will, the plaintiff argues that the testator was of sound mind when signing the document. The defendant counters that the testator had recently suffered from severe depression. Which legal principle does the defendant's argument invoke to challenge the validity of the will?
A. Mental Capacity at the time of execution
B. Formalities of Execution
C. Witness Testimony
D. Statute of Frauds
Correct Answer: A
The core issue in challenging a will based on mental health status is whether the testator possessed 'testamentary capacity'—the ability to understand the nature of the act and its consequences—at the specific moment of signing. The defendant's mention of recent severe depression directly targets this requirement of sound mind (Mental Capacity). Options B, C, and D relate to procedural requirements or evidence types but do not address the substantive mental state argument presented.

  2. This directly invokes mental capacity at execution
  3. Choose A

Q4. Legal Reasoning
Which of the following scenarios best illustrates the legal concept of 'Laches' as a defense against a claim for specific performance?
A. A plaintiff files a suit three years after the breach occurred, during which time the defendant purchased and renovated the property in good faith.
B. A plaintiff sues immediately after discovering a breach of contract.
C. A defendant argues that the statute of limitations has expired based on the date of the breach.
D. A plaintiff fails to produce documentary evidence despite having access to it for over a decade.
Correct Answer: A
Laches is an equitable defense based on the delay in bringing a suit, which prejudices the defendant (e.g., by changing their position or spending money). The first option shows significant delay causing prejudice. Option 1 describes prompt action, not laches. Option 2 refers to the Statute of Limitations, a distinct legal principle. Option 4 relates to failure to prove evidence (res ipsa loquitur or burden of proof issues), not necessarily delay-based equity.
Research Team · ~3s · Prejudice Spotlight
  1. Laches requires defendant changed position due to delay.
  2. Only A shows defendant bought/renovated property relying on delay.
  3. Select A.
Q5. Quantitative Techniques
The difference between Compound Interest (compounded annually) and Simple Interest on a certain principal at 10% per annum for 2 years is ₹50. What is the principal amount?
A. ₹4,000
B. ₹5,000
C. ₹6,000
D. ₹5,500
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: For a 2-year period, the difference between CI and SI is given by P × (R/100)². Substituting values: 50 = P × (10/100)² = P/100, so P = ₹5,000. Step-by-step: SI for 2 years = P × 10 × 2/100 = 0.2P. CI for 2 years = P(1 + 10/100)² - P = 1.21P - P = 0.21P. Difference = 0.21P - 0.2P = 0.01P = 50, therefore P = 5,000.
Q6. English Language
The panel of experts, along with their research assistants, ______ scheduled to present the findings at the conference tomorrow.
A. are
B. is
C. were
D. have been
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: Mentally delete the phrase enclosed by commas starting with 'along with' (additive phrase). The true subject is the singular noun 'The panel', requiring the singular verb 'is'. Full Solution: The subject of the sentence is 'The panel', a collective noun treated as singular here. The phrase 'along with their research assistants' is a parenthetical additive phrase that does not change the number of the subject. Therefore, the verb must be singular. Option B 'is' is the only singular present tense verb matching the singular subject 'panel'. Options A, C, and D are plural or plural-form verbs.
Q7. English Language
Rearrange the following sentences to form a coherent paragraph: P: This phenomenon is exacerbated by the algorithmic amplification of sensational content. Q: The erosion of public trust in traditional media institutions has accelerated in the digital age. R: Consequently, users often retreat into echo chambers that reinforce pre-existing biases. S: As a result, the shared factual baseline necessary for democratic discourse is fracturing. Which is the most logical sequence?
A. Q-P-R-S
B. Q-R-P-S
C. P-Q-R-S
D. Q-S-P-R
Correct Answer: A
Shortcut: Identify the opening sentence (Q - general topic statement). Follow logical connectors: 'This phenomenon' (P) refers to Q. 'Consequently' (R) follows the cause in P. 'As a result' (S) is the final consequence. Full Solution: Sentence Q introduces the main topic: erosion of trust. Sentence P uses 'This phenomenon' to refer directly to the erosion mentioned in Q and adds a cause (algorithmic amplification). Sentence R uses 'Consequently' to show the effect of P (users retreating). Sentence S uses 'As a result' to summarize the ultimate consequence of the chain (fracturing baseline). The logical flow is Cause (Q) -> Mechanism (P) -> Effect (R) -> Ultimate Consequence (S).
Q8. Current Affairs & GK

A. Fukushima nuclear disaster (2011)
B. Minamata disease incident (1950s)
C. Battle of Okinawa (1945)
D. Atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki (1945)
Correct Answer: D
Shortcut: 'Hidankyo' and 'Hibakusha' are specific Japanese terms for atomic bomb survivors. Eliminate Fukushima (nuclear plant meltdown), Minamata (mercury poisoning), Okinawa (ground battle). Step-by-step: The Norwegian Nobel Committee awarded the 2024 prize to Nihon Hidankyo for its efforts to achieve a world free of nuclear weapons and for demonstrating through witness testimony that nuclear weapons must never be used again. Q9. Logical Reasoning
If 'All roses are flowers' is true, which of the following statements MUST also be true?
A. All flowers are roses.
B. Some flowers are roses.
C. No roses are flowers.
D. Some roses are not flowers.
Correct Answer: B
The statement 'All roses are flowers' implies that the set of roses is entirely contained within the set of flowers. Therefore, there must be an overlap where some flowers (specifically those that are roses) exist. Option A is incorrect because it reverses the relationship; not all flowers need to be roses. Options C and D are logically false as they contradict the premise that all roses belong to the category of flowers.
Q10. Legal Reasoning
A property owner claims that a neighboring factory's noise levels violate statutory limits. The factory argues that the noise is a 'reasonable' use of its land given its industrial nature. Which legal concept, often invoked to balance private rights with public utility or community welfare, does the factory's argument rely upon?
A. Strict Liability
B. Easement by Prescription
C. Reasonable Expectation / Nuisance Defense based on Reasonableness
D. Doctrine of Laches
Correct Answer: C
The factory's argument relies on the concept of 'Reasonableness' often applied in nuisance cases to determine if an activity is a lawful use of property, balancing private harm against public utility. Option A (Strict Liability) ignores fault entirely and would hold the factory liable regardless of reasonableness. Option B refers to acquiring rights through long-term usage, not defending against noise claims. Option D relates to unreasonable delay in filing a suit, which is irrelevant to the nature of the noise itself.
Q11. English Language
Read the passage and answer the question: "The proposed amendment claims to protect consumer privacy, yet it grants broad exemptions to state agencies for 'national security' purposes without defining the term. This loophole effectively allows unchecked surveillance under the guise of legislation. The bill is less a shield for the citizen and more a charter for the state." The author's tone towards the proposed amendment is best described as:
A. Apprehensive
B. Cynical
C. Objective
D. Supportive
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: Spot negative value judgments ('loophole', 'guise', 'charter for the state'). 'Cynical' implies distrust of motives. Eliminate D (positive), C (neutral/factual), A (fearful future). 'Cynical' fits the exposure of bad faith. Full Solution: The author uses strongly critical language: 'loophole', 'unchecked surveillance', 'guise of legislation', and the contrast 'less a shield... more a charter'. This reveals a belief that the amendment's stated purpose (privacy) is a facade for its real purpose (state power). This distrust of stated motives defines a 'Cynical' tone. 'Apprehensive' suggests anxiety; 'Objective' implies neutrality; 'Supportive' is opposite.

Q14. Logical Reasoning
In a certain code language, 'LOGIC' is written as 'MNHJD'. How will 'REASON' be written in that code?
A. SDBRPM
B. SFCTQN
C. SDBRQN
D. SECTPM
Correct Answer: A
Shortcut: Check the first two letters: L→M (+1), O→N (-1) reveals an alternating +1, -1 pattern. Step-by-step: The pattern alternates between adding 1 and subtracting 1 from the alphabetical position. L(+1)=M, O(-1)=N, G(+1)=H, I(-1)=J, C(+1)=D. Applying to REASON: R(+1)=S, E(-1)=D, A(+1)=B, S(-1)=R, O(+1)=P, N(-1)=M, giving SDBRPM.
Q15. English Language
The village council was divided on whether to accept the government's offer for free solar panels or invest their own limited funds in installing a larger wind turbine system. Despite the uncertainty of future energy needs, the elders decided to reject the immediate aid, fearing that accepting it would lead to a situation where ______.
A. they would be painting the town red
B. they would be throwing good money after bad
C. they were counting their chickens before they hatch
D. they were folding their tents
Correct Answer: C
The idiom 'count your chickens before they hatch' means to anticipate a favorable outcome before it has actually occurred. In the context of the story, the elders fear that accepting the free panels based on current needs might lead to over-reliance or financial trouble later if those specific needs change or if maintenance costs arise, effectively celebrating a gain (free energy) before securing its long-term viability. 'Throwing good money after bad' implies continuing to spend money on a failing investment, which is not the primary concern here regarding future uncertainty. 'Painting the town red' refers to celebration, and 'folding their tents' means abandoning an endeavor.
