Q1. Legal Reasoning
In a contract for the supply of 500 tonnes of steel, Party A agreed to deliver the steel by 1st June. Due to a sudden strike by steelworkers, the supply was delayed until 15th June. Party B sued for damages for delay. Which doctrine will most likely relieve Party A from liability?
A. Doctrine of frustration of contract
B. Doctrine of impossibility of performance
C. Doctrine of force majeure
D. Doctrine of anticipatory breach
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut: Identify the clause that specifically deals with external events beyond control. - Frustration applies when the contract becomes impossible, not merely delayed. - Impossibility also requires total impossibility. - Anticipatory breach involves a clear intention not to perform. - Force majeure covers temporary impediments like strikes. Hence option C is correct. Full solution: A strike is a recognized force majeure event that temporarily prevents performance but does not render the contract impossible. Under standard force majeure clauses, Party A is excused from liability for the delay caused by the strike.

Q3. Quantitative Techniques
What is the unit digit of the product (7^95 × 3^58)?
A. 1
B. 3
C. 7
D. 9
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut: Use cyclicity of unit digits. For 7: cycle is 7, 9, 3, 1 (length 4). 95 ÷ 4 leaves remainder 3 → 3rd digit = 3. For 3: cycle is 3, 9, 7, 1 (length 4). 58 ÷ 4 leaves remainder 2 → 2nd digit = 9. Multiply unit digits: 3 × 9 = 27 → unit digit 7. Step-by-step: 7^95 unit digit = 7^(4×23+3) → (7^4)^23 × 7^3 → 1^23 × 343 → 3. 3^58 unit digit = 3^(4×14+2) → (3^4)^14 × 3^2 → 1^14 × 9 → 9. Product unit digit = 3 × 9 = 27 → 7.
Research Team · ~5s · Cyclicity of 7 and 3
  1. 7's cycle: 7,9,3,1 repeats every 4. 95 mod 4 = 3 → unit digit 3.
  2. 3's cycle: 3,9,7,1 repeats every 4. 58 mod 4 = 2 → unit digit 9.
  3. Multiply unit digits: 3 × 9 = 27 → unit digit 7.

Q6. Logical Reasoning
Premises: (1) Every claim that is verified is reliable. (2) Some reliable statements are unverified. (3) No unverified claim is false. Which of the following must be true?
A. All verified claims are true.
B. Some verified claims are unverified.
C. Some reliable statements are false.
D. No verified claim is false.
Correct Answer: D
Shortcut: Combine the universal statements to see the relationship between verified and false. From (1) verified → reliable. From (3) unverified → ¬false (i.e., unverified claims are not false). Since every verified claim is reliable, and any claim that is not false must be true, we can infer that no verified claim is false. Full solution: (1) ∀x (Verified(x) → Reliable(x)). (3) ∀x (Unverified(x) → ¬False(x)). A verified claim cannot be unverified, so it is not covered by (3). However, if a claim were false, it would contradict (3) because false claims are necessarily verified (otherwise they'd be unverified and not false). Hence a verified claim cannot be false, establishing that no verified claim is false. Options A, B, and C either overstate or contradict the premises.
Research Team · ~5s · Negation Test
  1. 1. Suppose a verified claim is false.
  2. 2. False claim is unverified (premise3 contrapositive).
  3. 3. But verified ⇒ reliable (premise1) and reliable can be unverified (premise2).
  4. 4. This yields a verified claim that is unverified and false, contradicting premise3.
  5. 5. Hence assumption false; verified claims cannot be false.
  6. 6. Option D holds.

Q8. Legal Reasoning

StatuteSubjectYear Enacted
ARegulation of water supply within a municipality2019
BGrant of licences for mining of minerals in a district2020
CImposition of a tax on inter‑state sale of goods2021
A. Statute A
B. Statute B
C. Statute C
D. All of the above
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: Identify the entry list – water supply and mining are in State List, inter‑state tax is Union List. Hence only B (mining) is clearly within State competence. Q12. Legal Reasoning
A citizen files a petition under Article 32 of the Constitution challenging the constitutional validity of a State law that imposes a tax on the sale of agricultural produce within the State. The petition claims that the tax violates the fundamental right to trade and commerce under Article 19(1)(g). Which of the following is the most appropriate ground for the Court to reject the petition?
A. The tax is a reasonable restriction in the public interest and therefore permissible under Article 19(6).
B. The petition is not maintainable as the aggrieved party has not suffered a direct injury.
C. Article 32 does not entertain challenges to State legislation, only to Central legislation.
D. The tax is a fiscal measure and cannot be challenged on the ground of violation of fundamental rights.
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: Eliminate options that are factually incorrect. 1. Article 19(6) allows reasonable restrictions, but the petition is about validity, not restriction. 2. Under Article 32, any person whose rights are infringed can file; direct injury is required – the petitioner must show personal impact. 3. Article 32 can be invoked against any law violating fundamental rights, irrespective of its origin. 4. Fiscal measures can be challenged if they infringe rights. Hence option B is the correct ground for rejection. Full solution: The petitioner must demonstrate that he is directly affected by the tax; mere public interest standing is insufficient. Since the petition lacks a specific personal grievance, the Court can dismiss it on the basis of lack of locus standi.

Q14. Legal Reasoning
A driver was charged under Section 304A IPC for causing death by negligence. The prosecution's case rests solely on the testimony of a surviving passenger who states that the driver was texting while driving. The defense moves to exclude this testimony on the ground that it is hearsay. How should the court rule?
A. Exclude the testimony as it is hearsay and not within any exception.
B. Admit the testimony because the passenger is a party to the case.
C. Admit the testimony as a dying declaration.
D. Admit the testimony as a statement against interest.
Correct Answer: A
Shortcut: Recall the exceptions to hearsay. - Party to case exception applies only to parties, not witnesses. - Dying declaration requires the declarant to be on the brink of death. - Statement against interest requires the statement to be against the declarant's own interest. None apply. Therefore the testimony is hearsay and must be excluded. Full solution: The passenger is a third‑person witness; his statement does not fall under any statutory exception to the hearsay rule. Consequently, the court must exclude the testimony.
Q15. English Language
The new government policy is a __________ for small businesses, as it provides essential financial support to keep them from collapsing during the economic downturn. A) A lifeline B) A tightrope C) A stepping-stone D) A dead-end
A. A lifeline
B. A tightrope
C. A stepping-stone
D. A dead-end
Correct Answer: A
The phrase 'lifeline' refers to something that saves a person or organization from disaster, which perfectly matches the context of financial support preventing collapse. 'Tightrope' implies walking a dangerous path with little margin for error, not receiving help. 'Stepping-stone' suggests a means to progress to a higher goal, not immediate survival. 'Dead-end' indicates a situation with no future or success, which contradicts the supportive nature of the policy.
