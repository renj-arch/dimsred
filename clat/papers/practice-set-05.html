Raj Narain* and subsequent jurisprudence.

The price of an article is increased by 20% and then decreased by 20%. What is the net percentage change in the price?
A. 0%
B. 4% decrease
C. 4% increase
D. 2% decrease
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: For successive increase and decrease by the same percentage x%, the net percentage change is -x²/100%. Here x = 20, so net change = -400/100 = -4% (a 4% decrease). Full solution: Assume original price = 100. After 20% increase, price = 120. After 20% decrease, price = 120 × 0.8 = 96. The final price is 96, which is a 4% decrease from the original 100.

A. The hospital is not liable because Dr. Rao is an independent contractor exercising exclusive professional judgment, and the hospital lacked control over his surgical technique.
B. The hospital is liable under the doctrine of vicarious liability as Dr. Rao was integrated into the hospital's functioning for patient care, and the hospital owes a non-delegable duty of care to its patients.
C. The hospital is liable only if it is proven that the hospital administration was negligent in verifying Dr. Rao's medical credentials before granting him visiting privileges.
D. The hospital is not liable because the negligence occurred during the exercise of Dr. Rao's specialized professional skill, which falls outside the hospital's right of control.
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: Modern vicarious liability for hospitals uses the 'integration/organization test' and 'non-delegable duty of care' — hospitals cannot escape liability for negligence of doctors integrated into their patient care system by labeling them independent contractors. Step-by-step: 1. Identify the relationship: Hospital provides infrastructure/team; Doctor performs core function (surgery). 2. Apply principle: Courts hold hospitals liable for visiting consultants under 'ostensible agency' or 'non-delegable duty' (e.g., *M/s. Spring Meadows Hospital v. Harjol Ahluwalia*). 3. Eliminate A & D (rely on outdated 'control test' for independent contractors). 4. Eliminate C (negligent selection is a separate/direct liability, not the sole basis for vicarious liability). 5. Option B correctly states the current legal position.

  2. Independent contractor status irrelevant for non-delegable duties.

Shortcut: Apply the 'Res Ipsa Loquitur' (The thing speaks for itself) checklist: (1) Instrumentality under defendant's exclusive control, (2) Accident does not ordinarily occur without negligence, (3) No voluntary contribution by plaintiff. Q15. Current Affairs & GKmodel-generated · 2026-11-07
The Supreme Court of India delivered a landmark judgment in November 2024, holding that the right to privacy is a fundamental right protected under Article 21 of the Constitution. In which specific context did the Court affirm this principle most recently, distinguishing it from a mere privilege granted by the State?
A. The validity of biometric data collection for voting in local body elections.
B. The mandatory installation of surveillance cameras in all private educational institutions.
C. The legality of facial recognition technology usage by law enforcement agencies without individual consent.
D. The requirement for citizens to disclose their social media passwords to tax authorities.
Correct Answer: C
The correct option is C because the 2024 judgment specifically addressed the boundaries of state surveillance and the use of advanced biometric technologies like facial recognition, reinforcing that such intrusion requires strict procedural safeguards and cannot be arbitrary. Option A is incorrect as voting eligibility is governed by the Representation of the People Act rather than a fundamental right to privacy in this specific context. Option B is wrong because private educational institutions do not have the same level of state interference regarding surveillance mandates without due process, and the judgment did not focus on them. Option D is incorrect because while privacy protects against arbitrary disclosure, the tax laws operate under distinct statutory frameworks that may compel disclosure for verification purposes, which was not the primary subject of the 2024 ruling.
