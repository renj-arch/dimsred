Quantitative Aptitude Speed Tricks and Shortcuts

12 min read · Updated June 2026

Quantitative Aptitude is often the deciding section in competitive exams. It tests not just your mathematical ability but also your speed and accuracy under time pressure. In SSC CGL, you have an average of 54 seconds per question. In Banking PO exams, it is even tighter at 48 seconds per question. This guide covers time-tested speed tricks, calculation shortcuts, and mental math techniques used by top rankers to solve Quant questions in half the average time.

1. Mental Math Foundation

Before learning advanced tricks, build a strong mental math foundation. Spend two weeks practicing these basics until they become automatic:

  • Tables up to 30: Memorize multiplication tables from 1 to 30. This alone saves 3-5 seconds per multiplication question.
  • Squares up to 100: Know 1² to 100² by heart. Most Data Interpretation questions require quick square root estimates.
  • Cubes up to 30: 1³ to 30³ for approximation and number series questions.
  • Fraction-to-Percentage conversions: 1/2 = 50%, 1/3 = 33.33%, 1/4 = 25%, 1/5 = 20%, 1/6 = 16.67%, 1/7 = 14.28%, 1/8 = 12.5%, 1/9 = 11.11%, 1/10 = 10%. These are essential for profit-loss, SI/CI, and DI questions.
Example: What is 12.5% of 640?
Traditional method: (12.5/100) × 640 = 0.125 × 640 = 80
Shortcut: 12.5% = 1/8. So 640/8 = 80. Done in 2 seconds.

2. Calculation Shortcuts for Common Operations

Multiplication by 11

To multiply any two-digit number by 11, add the digits and place the sum between them. For 23 × 11: 2 + 3 = 5, so answer is 253. If the sum exceeds 9, carry the extra digit. For 78 × 11: 7 + 8 = 15, so answer is 858 (7, 15, 8 → carry 1 → 858).

Squaring Numbers Ending in 5

Any number ending in 5 can be squared instantly: multiply the first digit(s) by (first digit(s) + 1), then append 25. For 35²: 3 × 4 = 12, append 25 → 1225. For 125²: 12 × 13 = 156, append 25 → 15625.

Percentage Quick Calculations

To find a percentage of a number, reverse the operation: x% of y = y% of x. For example, 16% of 50 = 50% of 16 = 8. This is much faster when the reversed calculation is simpler.

3. Data Interpretation: The Art of Approximation

Data Interpretation questions are time-consuming if you calculate exact values. Most DI questions in competitive exams do not require exact computation — the answer choices are far enough apart that approximation works perfectly.

  • Round to nearest 10 or 100: Instead of calculating 17.3% of 48,923, compute 17% of 49,000 ≈ 8,330. The correct answer will be close to this value.
  • Compare ratios visually: When comparing two pie charts or bar graphs, use visual estimation rather than calculating exact percentages. Is sector A clearly larger than sector B? Eliminate options that contradict the visual evidence.
  • Use the elimination method: For "which year had the highest growth?" questions, scan the graph visually and identify the steepest slope. Verify with one quick calculation on that year only, rather than calculating all years.

4. Simplification and Approximation

Simplification questions test your ability to apply the BODMAS rule correctly under time pressure. Key strategies:

  • Round decimal numbers: Replace 48.76 with 49, 12.34 with 12. The answer will be close enough to identify the correct option.
  • Cancel common factors: In fractions like (36 × 25 × 12) / (18 × 5 × 6), cancel 36/18 = 2, 25/5 = 5, 12/6 = 2. Answer = 2 × 5 × 2 = 20.
  • Use unit digits: Many simplification questions can be solved by checking the unit digit of the result. For example, if the unit digit of the expression is 7 and only one option ends with 7, that is your answer.

5. Number Series: Pattern Recognition

Number series questions test your ability to identify patterns quickly. Common patterns to check (in order of frequency):

  1. Arithmetic progression: Add/subtract a constant difference. Check: 3, 7, 11, 15, ? → difference of 4 → answer 19.
  2. Geometric progression: Multiply/divide by a constant ratio. Check: 2, 6, 18, 54, ? → multiplied by 3 → answer 162.
  3. Square/Cube series: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25 → squares of 1, 2, 3, 4, 5.
  4. Alternating series: Two interleaved patterns. Check positions 1, 3, 5 for one pattern and 2, 4, 6 for another.
  5. Prime number series: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19.

Practice number series daily in the Mental Training module. Pattern recognition improves dramatically with consistent practice — your brain learns to identify patterns faster.

Example: Find the missing number: 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, ?
Pattern: 1×2=2, 2×3=6, 3×4=12, 4×5=20, 5×6=30, 6×7=42
Answer: 42

6. Time-Saving Strategies for the Exam Hall

Beyond mathematical tricks, strategic question selection can save 10-15 minutes in a Quant section:

  • Scan and sort: In the first 30 seconds, quickly scan all Quant questions. Mark easy ones (direct formula application), medium ones (2-step problems), and hard ones (multi-step or tricky). Solve in that order.
  • Skip after 90 seconds: If you cannot solve a question in 90 seconds, mark it for review and move on. Do not get stuck on one question — the opportunity cost is too high.
  • Use option substitution: For equations and algebra questions, substitute the answer options back into the equation. The correct option will satisfy the equation. This is often faster than solving algebraically.
  • Verify with reverse calculation: After solving, do a quick reverse check. If a question asks "what is the profit percentage?" and you calculated 25%, verify by computing the selling price from the cost price using 25% profit. If it matches the given data, your answer is correct.
