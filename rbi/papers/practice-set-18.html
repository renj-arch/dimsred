What is the unit digit of the expression \((347)^{89} \times (562)^{103} \times (789)^{56}\)?
A. 2
B. 4
C. 6
D. 8
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut: Use cyclicity of unit digits. Unit digit of 7^89: cycle 7,9,3,1 (length 4), 89%4=1 → 7. Unit digit of 2^103: cycle 2,4,8,6 (length 4), 103%4=3 → 8. Unit digit of 9^56: cycle 9,1 (length 2), 56%2=0 → 1. Multiply unit digits: 7×8×1 = 56 → unit digit 6. Step-by-step: 347^89 ends in 7^89. 7^4=2401 (ends in 1), so 7^88 ends in 1, 7^89 ends in 7. 562^103 ends in 2^103. 2^4=16 (ends in 6), 2^100 ends in 6, 2^103=2^100×8 ends in 8. 789^56 ends in 9^56. 9^2=81 (ends in 1), so 9^56 ends in 1. Final product unit digit = 7×8×1 = 56 → 6.
Research Team · ~5s · Cyclicity Shortcut
  1. Find unit digit of each base: 7, 2, 9.
  2. Reduce exponents modulo cycle length: 89 mod 4 = 1, 103 mod 4 = 3, 56 mod 2 = 0.
  3. Compute unit digits: 7^1 = 7, 2^3 = 8, 9^0 = 1.
  4. Multiply unit digits: 7 × 8 = 56 → 6, 6 × 1 = 6.

The following sentence is divided into four parts labelled (A), (B), (C), and (D). One of these parts contains a grammatical error. Identify the erroneous part. "The number of loan applications received by the bank (A) / have increased significantly (B) / since the announcement of the new interest subvention scheme (C) / for the MSME sector. (D)"
A. The number of loan applications received by the bank
B. have increased significantly
C. since the announcement of the new interest subvention scheme
D. for the MSME sector.
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: Apply the 'The number of vs A number of' rule. 'The number of' takes a singular verb (has); 'A number of' takes a plural verb (have). Here, the subject is 'The number' (singular), so the verb must be 'has increased'. Full Solution: The subject of the verb is the singular noun phrase 'The number'. The prepositional phrase 'of loan applications...' modifies the subject but does not change its number. Therefore, the plural verb 'have increased' in part (B) is incorrect; it should be the singular 'has increased'. Parts (A), (C), and (D) are grammatically correct.
