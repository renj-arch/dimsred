Q1. General Awareness
With reference to the Reserve Bank of India's Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF) framework, which of the following statements regarding the Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) is correct?
A. It was introduced in April 2020 as a replacement for the Marginal Standing Facility (MSF).
B. It requires banks to provide government securities as collateral to absorb surplus liquidity.
C. It serves as the floor of the LAF interest rate corridor and does not require collateral for operations.
D. The interest rate on SDF is fixed at 25 basis points above the Policy Repo Rate.
Correct Answer: C
**Shortcut (Elimination by Key Features):** The SDF is defined by two unique features: (1) **No Collateral Required** (unlike Reverse Repo) and (2) **Floor of the LAF Corridor**. Scan options for these keywords. Option B says 'requires collateral' -> Eliminate. Option D says 'above Repo Rate' (Ceiling/MSF territory) -> Eliminate. Option A has wrong date (2020) and wrong replaced facility (MSF) -> Eliminate. Option C matches both key features. **Step-by-Step Solution:** The Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) was introduced by the RBI on **April 8, 2022**, replacing the Fixed Rate Reverse Repo (FRRR) as the **floor** of the LAF corridor. Its most distinct operational feature is that it **does not require collateral** (government securities) for banks to park surplus liquidity, unlike the erstwhile Reverse Repo window. The Marginal Standing Facility (MSF) remains the **ceiling** (penal rate, usually Repo + 25 bps). The SDF rate is set **below** the Policy Repo Rate (currently 25 bps below), not above it. Therefore, only statement C is factually accurate.
Q2. Reasoning
In the following question, the symbols @, #, $, % and & are used with the following meanings: 'P @ Q' means 'P is greater than Q'; 'P # Q' means 'P is smaller than Q'; 'P $ Q' means 'P is greater than or equal to Q'; 'P % Q' means 'P is smaller than or equal to Q'; 'P & Q' means 'P is equal to Q'. Statement: A @ B # C $ D % E Conclusions: I. A @ D II. E $ C
A. Only Conclusion I follows
B. Only Conclusion II follows
C. Both Conclusions I and II follow
D. Neither Conclusion I nor II follows
Correct Answer: D
Shortcut: Decode symbols to standard signs: A > B < C >= D <= E. Chain breaks at B < C (no link A to C/D) and D <= E (no link E to C/D). Check I: A > B < C >= D -> Relation between A and D cannot be established. Check II: E >= D <= C -> Relation between E and C cannot be established. Step-by-step: 1. Translate: A > B, B < C, C >= D, D <= E. 2. For I (A > D): Path A > B < C >= D. Signs reverse at B (<) and C (>=), so no definite relation. 3. For II (E >= C): Path E >= D <= C. Signs reverse at D (<=), so no definite relation. 4. Neither follows.

The current standard limit fixed since May 2015 (revised from 125k) is USD 250,000. Eliminate lower (125k - old limit) and higher (500k, 1M - never been the LRS limit for individuals). Step-by-step: 1. LRS was introduced in 2004 with a limit of USD 25,000. 2. The limit was progressively raised. 3. In May 2015, the limit was raised from USD 125,000 to USD 250,000 per financial year. 4. This limit has remained unchanged since then. Q5. English Language
Select the most appropriate option to replace the underlined segment in the given sentence. If no improvement is required, select 'No improvement'. The regulatory framework mandates that every NBFC *maintains* a minimum Capital Risk Weighted Assets Ratio (CRAR) of 15%.
A. maintain
B. maintained
C. should maintain
D. No improvement
Correct Answer: A
**Shortcut (Elimination & Grammar Rule):** Identify the structure: 'mandates that' triggers the **Mandative Subjunctive** mood. In this mood, the verb *must* be in its **base form (V1)**, regardless of the subject (singular/plural) or tense of the main verb. 1. **Eliminate Option B (‘maintained’):** Past tense (V2) is never used in the subjunctive ‘that’-clause after mandates/recommends/demands. 2. **Eliminate Option C (‘should maintain’):** While ‘should + V1’ is grammatically possible in British English, the **preferred, concise, and standard form for competitive exams (RBI/SBI/IBPS) is the bare infinitive (V1) without ‘should’**. 3. **Eliminate Option D (‘No improvement’):** The original sentence uses ‘maintains’ (V1+s), which is indicative mood, incorrect for a mandate. 4. **Select Option A (‘maintain’):** Base form of the verb. Correct Subjunctive usage. **Step-by-Step:** 1. Spot the trigger verb: ‘mandates’ (others: recommend, suggest, demand, insist, order, require). 2. Spot the connector: ‘that’. 3. Apply Rule: Subject + Trigger Verb + That + Subject + **Base Verb (V1)**. 4. Subject is ‘every NBFC’ (singular), but verb remains ‘maintain’ (not ‘maintains’).
Q6. Reasoning
Statements: 1. Only a few Pens are Pencils. 2. All Pencils are Erasers. 3. No Eraser is a Sharpener. Conclusions: I. Some Pens are not Erasers. II. All Pencils can never be Sharpeners.
A. Only Conclusion I follows
B. Only Conclusion II follows
C. Both Conclusions I and II follow
D. Neither Conclusion I nor II follows
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: 'Only a few A are B' = Some A are B + Some A are not B. 'All B are C' + 'No C is D' -> 'No B is D' (Definite). Conclusion I: Pens -> Pencils (Some) -> Erasers (All). 'Some Pens are Erasers' is definite. 'Some Pens are not Erasers' is possible but not definite. Conclusion II: 'All Pencils can never be Sharpeners' matches 'No Pencil is Sharpener' (Definite). Step-by-step: 1. Decode Stmt 1: Some Pens are Pencils; Some Pens are not Pencils. 2. Chain: Some Pens -> Pencils -> Erasers. So Some Pens are Erasers (Definite). Conclusion I claims Some Pens are NOT Erasers -> Not definite (False). 3. Chain: All Pencils -> Erasers -> No Sharpener. So No Pencil is Sharpener (Definite). Conclusion II 'Can never be' = Definite No -> Follows (True).
Q7. General Awareness
As per the RBI's implementation of the Basel III Capital Regulations in India, what is the minimum Capital Conservation Buffer (CCB) that scheduled commercial banks are required to maintain in the form of Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) capital, expressed as a percentage of Risk Weighted Assets (RWAs)?
A. 1.25%
B. 1.875%
C. 2.50%
D. 3.50%
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut/Trick: Basel III CCB global standard is 2.5%. India followed a phased implementation (0.625% per year starting 2016). Full implementation (2.5%) was achieved from October 1, 2021. Eliminate 1.25% and 1.875% (interim phased levels). 3.5% is the Countercyclical Buffer max range, not CCB. Step-by-step: 1. The Capital Conservation Buffer (CCB) is a mandatory buffer of 2.5% of RWAs under Basel III, to be met entirely with CET1 capital. 2. RBI implemented this in a phased manner: 0.625% (Mar 2016), 1.25% (Mar 2017), 1.875% (Mar 2018), 2.5% (Oct 2021 - deferred due to COVID). 3. Currently, the fully phased-in CCB requirement is 2.50%. This is over and above the minimum CET1 requirement of 5.5% (plus CCB makes 8% CET1 minimum effectively for distribution constraints).
Q8. General Awareness
Under the Reserve Bank of India's 'Scale Based Regulation (SBR)' framework for Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs), introduced in October 2021, which layer is intended to remain empty under normal circumstances and will only be populated if supervisors perceive a substantial increase in systemic risk from specific NBFCs in the Upper Layer?
A. Base Layer (NBFC-BL)
B. Middle Layer (NBFC-ML)
C. Upper Layer (NBFC-UL)
D. Top Layer (NBFC-TL)
Correct Answer: D
Shortcut/Trick: Remember the hierarchy 'Base -> Middle -> Upper -> Top'. The framework is pyramidal. The 'Top' layer is a supervisory construct designed to be empty initially; it acts as a deterrent. If an Upper Layer NBFC is seen as posing extreme risk, it moves to Top Layer. Step-by-step: 1. The SBR framework classifies NBFCs into four layers based on size, activity, and riskiness. 2. Base Layer (lowest regulation), Middle Layer (stricter), Upper Layer (enhanced regulation for top 25-30 NBFCs by scoring). 3. The Top Layer (NBFC-TL) is ideally vacant. It is populated only if the RBI decides that specific NBFCs in the Upper Layer require significantly higher supervisory intervention due to systemic risk concerns.

Q10. Quantitative Aptitude
Two trains start simultaneously from stations A and B towards each other. Train X travels at 60 km/h and Train Y at 90 km/h. The distance between A and B is 450 km. At what distance from station A will they meet?
A. 180 km
B. 200 km
C. 270 km
D. 300 km
Correct Answer: A
Shortcut: In the same time, distances covered are in the ratio of speeds. Ratio of speeds = 60:90 = 2:3, so Train X covers 2/(2+3) of the total distance = 2/5 × 450 = 180 km. Full method: Relative speed = 60+90 = 150 km/h. Time to meet = 450/150 = 3 h. Distance travelled by Train X = 60 × 3 = 180 km.

Q13. Quantitative Aptitude
What is the unit digit of (7^95 + 3^58)?
A. 0
B. 2
C. 4
D. 6
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: Use cyclicity of unit digits. 7 has a cycle of 4 (7,9,3,1). 95 mod 4 = 3 → unit digit of 7^95 is 3. 3 has a cycle of 4 (3,9,7,1). 58 mod 4 = 2 → unit digit of 3^58 is 9. Sum of unit digits = 3+9 = 12 → unit digit 2. Full method: Write the cycles, find remainders, pick corresponding unit digits, add and take the last digit.
Q14. Reasoning
In a row of students, Rohan is 12th from the left end and 18th from the right end. If 5 students leave the row from the right end, what is the new position of Rohan from the right end?
A. 12
B. 14
C. 13
D. 15
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut: Since students leave from the right end (behind Rohan who is 18th from right), his rank from the right simply improves by the number of students leaving: 18 - 5 = 13. Full Solution: Total students initially = 12 + 18 - 1 = 29. After 5 students leave from right, total = 24. Rohan's position from left remains 12. New position from right = Total - Left Position + 1 = 24 - 12 + 1 = 13.
Q15. General Awareness
According to the Reserve Bank of India's Master Direction on 'Priority Sector Lending (PSL) - Targets and Classification' (updated as of 2023), what is the prescribed lending target for Small Finance Banks (SFBs) as a percentage of their Adjusted Net Bank Credit (ANBC) or Credit Equivalent Amount of Off-Balance Sheet Exposure (CEOBE), whichever is higher?
A. 40%
B. 50%
C. 60%
D. 75%
Correct Answer: D
Shortcut/Trick: Remember the hierarchy of PSL targets. Domestic Scheduled Commercial Banks = 40%. Regional Rural Banks = 75%. Small Finance Banks = 75%. Foreign Banks (with <20 branches) = 40%. SFBs and RRBs share the higher 75% target due to their financial inclusion mandate. Step-by-step: 1. The RBI mandates different PSL targets for different bank categories. 2. For Domestic Scheduled Commercial Banks (excluding RRBs and SFBs), the target is 40% of ANBC/CEOBE. 3. For Small Finance Banks (SFBs), the Master Direction explicitly prescribes a higher target of 75% of ANBC/CEOBE to ensure credit flows to the unserved/underserved sections. 4. This 75% target applies to the total priority sector, with sub-targets for agriculture, micro enterprises, etc.
