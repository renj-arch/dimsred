Data Interpretation

Tables, bar graphs, line graphs, pie charts, caselet DI.
Tables · Bar Graphs · Line Graphs · Pie Charts

Learning Objectives

  • Understand the fundamental concepts of Data Interpretation
  • Apply key formulas and techniques to solve problems
  • Practice with exam-level questions to build speed and accuracy

Key Concepts

What is Data Interpretation?

Data Interpretation (DI) involves analyzing numerical data presented in various formats — tables, bar graphs, line graphs, pie charts, and mixed diagrams. You must extract relevant information, perform calculations, and answer questions. DI is a major component in IBPS PO, SBI Clerk, SSC GD, and RBI Grade B exams, typically carrying 10-15 marks.

Types of Data Interpretation

1. Table-Based DI: Data is organized in rows and columns. You need to read specific values, calculate totals, averages, percentages, ratios, and differences. Tables may have multiple rows and columns with different categories.

2. Bar Graph DI: Data is shown as bars of varying heights. Questions involve comparing values, finding percentage changes, and calculating averages. Stacked bar graphs show multiple components in each bar.

3. Line Graph DI: Data points connected by lines show trends over time. Questions involve growth rates, percentage increase/decrease, and comparing multiple lines.

4. Pie Chart DI: Data is shown as sectors of a circle, each sector representing a percentage or value. Questions involve finding angles, percentages, and comparing sectors.

5. Mixed DI: Combination of two or more types — e.g., a table with a bar graph, or a pie chart with a line graph. These require integrating data from multiple sources.

Key Formulas for DI

1. Percentage Change: ((New - Old) / Old) × 100

2. Ratio: Value A : Value B (simplify by dividing by HCF)

3. Average: Sum of values / Number of values

4. Angle in Pie Chart: (Component / Total) × 360°

5. Growth Rate (CAGR): ((Final/Initial)^(1/n) - 1) × 100

Solved Examples

Example 1 (Table DI)
A table shows the production (in tonnes) of 5 companies over 3 years:

Years | A | B | C | D | E
2018 | 200 | 150 | 300 | 250 | 180
2019 | 220 | 180 | 320 | 240 | 200
2020 | 250 | 200 | 350 | 260 | 210

What is the average production of company C over the 3 years?

a) 320 b) 323.33 c) 330 d) 340
Solution: b) 323.33. Average of C = (300 + 320 + 350) / 3 = 970 / 3 = 323.33 tonnes.
Example 2 (Pie Chart DI)
A pie chart shows the expenditure of a family: Food 30%, Rent 20%, Education 15%, Transport 10%, Healthcare 10%, Savings 15%. If total monthly income is ₹60,000, what is the amount spent on Rent and Education together?

a) ₹21,000 b) ₹18,000 c) ₹24,000 d) ₹20,000
Solution: a) ₹21,000. Rent + Education = 20% + 15% = 35%. Amount = 35% of 60,000 = 0.35 × 60,000 = ₹21,000.
Example 3 (Bar Graph DI)
A bar graph shows the number of students in three schools over two years. School X: 2019=400, 2020=480; School Y: 2019=350, 2020=420; School Z: 2019=500, 2020=450. What was the percentage increase in students of School Y from 2019 to 2020?

a) 20% b) 15% c) 25% d) 10%
Solution: a) 20%. Increase = 420 - 350 = 70. Percentage increase = (70/350) × 100 = 20%.

Shortcut Techniques

  • Approximate calculations when answer choices are far apart — you don't always need exact values.
  • Read the units carefully — values may be in thousands, lakhs, or crores.
  • In pie charts, use percentages directly — convert to fractions (e.g., 25% = 1/4, 12.5% = 1/8) for faster calculation.
  • For tables, identify the column/row you need before starting calculations to avoid reading unnecessary data.
  • Use the 'difference' method for percentage change: (Change / Base) × 100.
Pro Tip
In IBPS PO mains, DI questions are calculation-heavy. Master Vedic maths techniques for fast multiplication and division. A typical DI set of 5 questions should take 6-8 minutes — practice with strict time limits.
