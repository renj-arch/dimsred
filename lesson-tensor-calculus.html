Tensor Calculus & Differential Forms — Tensors, Exterior Algebra & Cartan Calculus

Deep dive into Tensor Calculus & Differential Forms — the tensor product of vector spaces (the universal property: T: V×W → U bilinear factors through V⊗W), symmetric and exterior algebras (S(V) = T(V)/⟨v⊗w−w⊗v⟩, Λ(V) = T(V)/⟨v⊗v⟩), tensor fields on manifolds (sections of tensor bundles T^r_s M), differential forms (alternating covariant tensors, the de Rham complex Ω^*(M) with exterior derivative d: Ω^k → Ω^{k+1} satisfying d² = 0, the de Rham cohomology H^*_{dR}(M)), the Cartan calculus (the Lie derivative £_X, interior product ι_X, and the Cartan formula £_X = dι_X + ι_X d), integration of forms over chains (Stokes' theorem ∫_∂c ω = ∫_c dω), the Riemann curvature tensor R(X,Y)Z = ∇_X∇_Y Z − ∇_Y∇_X Z − ∇_{[X,Y]}Z and the Bianchi identities, Hodge theory (the Hodge star ⋆, the codifferential δ = ⋆ d ⋆, the Hodge decomposition theorem), and applications to gauge theory (the Yang–Mills curvature F = dA + A∧A).

Part 1

🧮 Tensors and Multilinear Algebra

Linear Algebra
Tensor Products, Universal Properties, and Tensor Fields on Manifolds
The tensor product V ⊗ W of vector spaces satisfies the universal property: for every bilinear map φ: V×W → U, there exists a unique linear map φ̃: V⊗W → U such that φ(v,w) = φ̃(v⊗w). The tensor product of a (r,s)-tensor and a (p,q)-tensor is a (r+p, s+q)-tensor. The contraction C: T^{r}_{s} → T^{r-1}_{s-1} pairs a covariant index with a contravariant index. A tensor field of type (r,s) on a manifold M is a smooth section of the bundle T^r_s M = (TM)^{⊗r} ⊗ (T^*M)^{⊗s}. In coordinates (x^i) with basis ∂_i for TM and dx^i for T^*M, a tensor writes as T = T^{i_1…i_r}_{j_1…j_s} ∂_{i_1}⊗…⊗∂_{i_r}⊗dx^{j_1}⊗…⊗dx^{j_s}. The transformation law under coordinate change x → y: T'^{i}_{j} = (∂y^i/∂x^k)(∂x^ℓ/∂y^j) T^k_ℓ — contravariant indices transform with the Jacobian, covariant with its inverse. The metric tensor g = g_{ij} dx^i ⊗ dx^j is a symmetric (0,2)-tensor field that defines the Riemannian structure. The Kronecker delta δ^i_j and the Levi-Civita symbol ε_{i_1…i_n} are the canonical invariant tensors.
Tensors are the multilinear generalization of vectors and covectors — their coordinate transformation laws encode the geometry of the manifold.
Part 2

📐 Differential Forms and the de Rham Complex

Exterior Algebra
Differential Forms, the Exterior Derivative, and de Rham Cohomology
A differential k-form ω is a smooth section of Λ^k T^*M — a totally antisymmetric covariant k-tensor field. In coordinates: ω = (1/k!) ω_{i_1…i_k} dx^{i_1} ∧ … ∧ dx^{i_k}. The wedge product (ω∧η)(X_1,…,X_{p+q}) = (1/p!q!) Σ_σ sgn(σ) ω(X_{σ(1)},…,X_{σ(p)}) η(X_{σ(p+1)},…,X_{σ(p+q)}) makes Ω^*(M) = ⊕ Ω^k(M) into a graded-commutative algebra: ω∧η = (−1)^{|ω||η|} η∧ω. The exterior derivative d: Ω^k → Ω^{k+1} is the unique antiderivation satisfying d(f) = df (the differential of f), d² = 0, and d(ω∧η) = dω∧η + (−1)^{|ω|} ω∧dη. In coordinates: d(ω_{i_1…i_k} dx^{i_1}∧…∧dx^{i_k}) = (∂ω_{i_1…i_k}/∂x^j) dx^j∧dx^{i_1}∧…∧dx^{i_k}. The de Rham cohomology H^k_{dR}(M) = ker d_k / im d_{k-1} measures closed forms modulo exact forms. For compact oriented M, Poincaré duality: H^k_{dR}(M) ≅ (H^{n-k}_{dR}(M))^*. The Mayer–Vietoris sequence computes de Rham cohomology from a cover. The de Rham theorem states H^k_{dR}(M) ≅ H^k(M; ℝ) — de Rham cohomology equals singular cohomology with real coefficients.
Differential forms with the exterior derivative form the de Rham complex, whose cohomology encodes the global topology of the manifold.
Part 3

⚙️ Cartan Calculus: Lie Derivatives and Interior Products

Calculus
The Lie Derivative, the Interior Product, Cartan's Magic Formula, and Stokes' Theorem
The Lie derivative £_X: Ω^k → Ω^k measures the change of a form along the flow φ_t of a vector field X: £_X ω = d/dt|_{t=0} φ_t^* ω. The interior product ι_X: Ω^k → Ω^{k-1} contracts a form with X: (ι_X ω)(X_1,…,X_{k-1}) = ω(X, X_1,…,X_{k-1}). Cartan's magic formula: £_X = d ι_X + ι_X d — the Lie derivative is the graded commutator of d and ι_X. This implies £_X is a derivation commuting with d. The Poincaré lemma: every closed form is locally exact — if dω = 0, then locally ω = dη. For a chain c (a formal sum of smooth simplices) and a k-form ω, integration ∫_c ω is defined by pulling back to the simplex. Stokes' theorem: ∫_{∂c} ω = ∫_c dω — the cornerstone connecting differential calculus to topology. The divergence theorem, Green's theorem, and the classical Stokes' theorem are special cases. The integration of forms over oriented manifolds uses a partition of unity subordinate to an oriented atlas. The change of variables formula ∫_M f^* ω = deg(f) ∫_N ω for a smooth map f: M → N of compact oriented manifolds, where deg(f) is the Brouwer degree.
Cartan's magic formula unifies the Lie derivative, exterior derivative, and interior product — Stokes' theorem is the fundamental theorem of calculus on manifolds.
Part 4

🔥 Hodge Theory, Curvature, and Gauge Theory

Riemannian
The Hodge Star, the Codifferential, the Ricci and Einstein Tensors, and Yang–Mills
On an oriented Riemannian manifold (M^n, g), define the Hodge star ⋆: Ω^k → Ω^{n-k} by ⋆(dx^{i_1}∧…∧dx^{i_k}) = √det g/(n−k)! ε^{i_1…i_k}_{i_{k+1}…i_n} dx^{i_{k+1}}∧…∧dx^{i_n}. It satisfies ⋆⋆ = (−1)^{k(n−k)} id. The codifferential δ = (−1)^{n(k+1)+1} ⋆ d ⋆: Ω^k → Ω^{k-1} is the formal adjoint of d with respect to the L² inner product ⟨ω,η⟩ = ∫ ω∧⋆η. The Hodge Laplacian Δ = dδ + δd. The Hodge decomposition theorem: every k-form ω decomposes uniquely as ω = dα + δβ + γ where γ is harmonic (Δγ = 0). Thus H^k_{dR}(M) ≅ ℋ^k(M) — harmonic forms represent de Rham cohomology classes. The Riemann curvature tensor R(X,Y)Z = ∇_X∇_Y Z − ∇_Y∇_X Z − ∇_{[X,Y]}Z. In coordinates: R^ℓ_{ijk} = ∂_i Γ^ℓ_{jk} − ∂_j Γ^ℓ_{ik} + Γ^m_{jk} Γ^ℓ_{im} − Γ^m_{ik} Γ^ℓ_{jm}. The Bianchi identities: R_{ijkl} + R_{iklj} + R_{iljk} = 0 (first) and ∇_i R_{jklm} + ∇_j R_{kilm} + ∇_k R_{ijlm} = 0 (second). The Ricci tensor R_{ij} = R^k_{ikj} and the scalar curvature R = g^{ij} R_{ij}. In gauge theory, the curvature of a connection A on a principal bundle is F = dA + A∧A (with the Lie algebra-valued exterior derivative). The Yang–Mills equations d_A ⋆ F = 0 are the Euler–Lagrange equations of the Yang–Mills action S_YM = ∫ Tr(F∧⋆F). The Maxwell equations in differential form: dF = 0, d⋆F = J.
Hodge theory establishes a deep link between analysis (harmonic forms) and topology (de Rham cohomology) — the curvature tensor is the central object in Riemannian geometry and gauge theory.
