Key Concepts

Mastering General Science for SSC GD & Agniveer

General science is the foundation of the competitive exam syllabus, covering fundamental concepts from physics, chemistry, and biology. For SSC GD and Agniveer exams, questions are typically framed around high school level knowledge, focusing on everyday scientific phenomena and essential facts. This lesson will systematically break down these topics into manageable sections to ensure you understand not just the answers, but the underlying principles.

Physics: The Science of Matter and Motion

Physics in this context deals with how matter moves and interacts with energy. The cornerstone is Newton's Second Law, which states that Force equals Mass times Acceleration (F = ma). This formula explains why a heavier object requires more force to move at the same speed as a lighter one. Another critical concept is Gravitation, where the acceleration due to gravity on Earth is approximately 9.8 meters per second squared (g = 9.8 m/s²). Light behaves according to laws of reflection and refraction, which govern how mirrors and lenses form images. Sound travels through air at a standard speed of 340 meters per second, creating echoes when it bounces off surfaces. Finally, electricity is governed by Ohm's Law (V = IR), linking Voltage, Current, and Resistance in circuits.

Chemistry: The Study of Substances

Chemistry explains the composition of matter and how substances change. At the atomic level, an atom consists of protons, neutrons, and electrons. Matter exists as elements (pure substances), compounds (chemically bonded elements like H₂O or CO₂), and mixtures (physically combined substances). Acids are defined by a pH value less than 7, while bases have a pH greater than 7; neutral solutions sit at exactly 7. Oxidation-Reduction reactions involve the transfer of electrons between molecules, a process vital for combustion and respiration.

Biology: Life and the Human Body

Biology focuses on living organisms, starting with the cell as the basic unit of life. The human body operates through complex systems like the digestive, respiratory, circulatory, and nervous systems. A crucial part of biology for exams is understanding Vitamins; these are essential organic compounds required in small quantities. For instance, Vitamin A is vital for vision, while Vitamin C prevents scurvy. Deficiencies lead to specific diseases, such as Rickets caused by a lack of Vitamin D or Calcium.

Solved Examples

Example 1: Applying Ohm's Law
A circuit has a voltage of 12 Volts and a resistance of 4 Ohms. Calculate the current flowing through it.

a) 3 Amps
b) 48 Amps
c) 0.33 Amps
d) 16 Amps
Solution: a) 3 Amps. We use Ohm's Law: V = I × R. To find Current (I), we rearrange the formula to I = V / R. Substituting the values: I = 12V / 4Ω = 3A. This confirms that 3 Amps of current flows through the circuit.
Example 2: Vitamin Deficiency
Which vitamin deficiency leads to night blindness?

a) Vitamin B1
b) Vitamin C
c) Vitamin A
d) Vitamin D
Solution: c) Vitamin A. The retina of the eye requires Vitamin A to convert light into signals for the brain. Without sufficient Vitamin A, the eye cannot see properly in low-light conditions, leading to night blindness. Vitamin B1 is for nerve function, C for immunity/scurvy, and D for bone health.

Common Traps

Students often fall into specific traps that lead to incorrect answers during the exam:

  • Confusing Units: Mixing up SI units like confusing Watts (Power) with Volts (Voltage) or Amps (Current). Always check if the question asks for force, energy, or power.
  • pH Scale Misconception: Thinking that a higher number means more acidic. Remember, high pH is basic/alkaline, low pH is acidic.
  • Speed of Sound vs Light: Assuming sound travels faster than light. In reality, light is instantaneous for practical distances, while sound takes time (hence thunder after lightning).
  • Vitamin Sources: Confusing sources, such as thinking Vitamin A comes from citrus fruits (it does not; that is Vitamin C). Vitamin A is found in carrots and liver.

Memory Tricks

To retain these facts quickly for the exam, use these mnemonics and comparisons:

  • Vitamin Mnemonic: "A" for Antioxidant (Carrots), "B" for Bread (Grains/Nerves), "C" for Citrus (Oranges/Immunity), "D" for Dairy (Milk/Bones).
  • Periodic Table Trend: Remember that reactivity increases down Group 1 (Alkali metals) because the outer electron is further from the nucleus and easier to lose.
  • Newton's Laws Order: First Law is Inertia (objects keep doing what they are doing). Second Law is Force (F=ma). Third Law is Action-Reaction (every push has an equal opposite push).
  • Acid-Base Trick: Think of "A"cid as "A"ttacking (low pH, sour taste) and "B"ase as "B"lue litmus paper turning red.
Exam-Day Strategy
When facing a General Science question, first identify the branch: Is it Physics (motion/energy), Chemistry (substances/reactions), or Biology (life/cells)? If you are unsure of a specific fact, eliminate options based on units. For example, if a question asks for energy and one option is in Watts, discard it immediately because Watts measure power, not energy (Joules). This process of elimination saves valuable time.
