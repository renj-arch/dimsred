Key Concepts

Indian Economy — Basics for CDS

Indian Economy for CDS covers economic development, national income, inflation, banking, budgeting, fiscal policy, and economic reforms. Questions are factual and NCERT-level.

  • National income: GDP (gross domestic product — value of all final goods/services produced within a country in a year), GNP, NNP, per capita income. GDP = C + I + G + (X − M). Real GDP (constant prices) vs Nominal GDP (current prices). GVA = GDP − product taxes + subsidies.
  • Economic growth vs development: Growth = rise in GDP/output; Development = growth + improvements in health, education, living standards, equity (HDI). India ranks ~134/193 in HDI (2022).
  • Sectors of economy: Primary (agriculture, mining), secondary (manufacturing, industry), tertiary (services). India's economy is services-led — services contribute ~50%+ of GDP, agriculture ~18%, industry ~25%.
  • Inflation: General rise in price levels over time. Measured by CPI (Consumer Price Index — retail) and WPI (Wholesale Price Index). Demand-pull (excess demand) and cost-push (rising input costs) inflation. RBI targets CPI inflation at 4% ± 2%.
  • Banking and financial system: RBI (central bank, est. 1935, nationalised 1949) — monetary authority, banker to banks/government, currency issuer, regulator. Repo rate (policy rate), CRR, SLR. Commercial banks, RRBs, cooperative banks, payment banks, small finance banks. NPA (non-performing assets/bad loans). Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code 2016.
  • Union Budget (Article 112): Annual Financial Statement presented by Finance Minister (traditionally Feb 1). Revenue account (income/expenditure) and capital account (assets/liabilities). Fiscal deficit = total expenditure − total receipts (excluding borrowings). FRBM Act 2003 targets fiscal discipline.
  • Taxes: Direct taxes (income tax, corporate tax — levied on income/profit) and indirect taxes (GST — levied on goods/services, passed to consumer). GST launched July 1, 2017 (101st Amendment) — one nation, one tax; CGST/SGST/IGST; GST Council.
  • Economic reforms (1991): Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation (LPG) — dismantling licence raj, reducing import tariffs, encouraging FDI/FPI, disinvestment. Since then, India's growth accelerated.
  • External sector: Exports and imports; BoP (balance of payments — current + capital account); FDI (long-term, controlling) vs FPI (portfolio, short-term); forex reserves (RBI-mantained); exchange rate (managed float).
  • Poverty and employment: Poverty lines (Tendulkar, Rangarajan), NITI Aayog MPI (multidimensional poverty), MGNREGA (100 days rural employment guarantee), PLFS (unemployment data), informal sector ~90% of workforce.
  • Key institutions: NITI Aayog (replaced Planning Commission 2015), Finance Commission (Centre-state revenue sharing, 5-yearly), SEBI (securities market regulator), PFRDA (pension), IRDAI (insurance), CCI (competition).
  • Major schemes: PMJDY (financial inclusion, zero-balance accounts), PMJAY (health insurance ₹5 lakh/family), PMUY (LPG to BPL women), PMAY (housing), MUDRA (small business loans), Make in India, Startup India, Digital India.
Technique: GDP vs GNP
Question: GDP measures production
Answer: GDP measures production within a country's geographical boundaries (domestic), by both residents and non-residents. GNP includes production by a country's nationals anywhere (including abroad) and excludes foreign nationals' production domestically.
Technique: Direct vs indirect tax
Question: GST is which type of tax?
Answer: GST (Goods and Services Tax) is an indirect tax — levied on supply of goods/services and collected from the end consumer by the seller. Income tax is a direct tax (paid directly by the earner).

Common Traps

  • Confusing fiscal deficit (total borrowing) with revenue deficit (borrowing for consumption).
  • Assuming RBI directly controls inflation — it influences via interest rates; supply-side inflation (food/oil) is harder to control.
  • Thinking FDI and FPI are the same — FDI is stable long-term investment; FPI is volatile portfolio investment.
  • Missing that GST is a destination-based consumption tax.
  • Overlooking that agriculture is ~18% of GDP but employs ~45% of workforce.

Memory Tricks

  • Deficits: Revenue < Fiscal < Primary (narrowest to widest).
  • RBI rate: Repo = RBI lends to banks; Reverse repo = RBI borrows from banks.
  • GST: One nation, one tax, one market (July 1, 2017).
  • Sectors: Primary = farm/mine; Secondary = factory; Secondary = services.
CDS GK Economy strategy
CDS economy questions are direct and factual — article numbers, institution heads, scheme names, recent Budget/Economic Survey figures. Elimination is effective. Revise RBI rates, GST facts, and major schemes regularly.
