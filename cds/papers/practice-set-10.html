Select the most appropriate meaning of the idiom: "To blow one's own trumpet."
A. To criticize others
B. To play a musical instrument
C. To announce arrival
D. To praise oneself
Correct Answer: D
Shortcut: Visualize the literal imagery — blowing a trumpet creates loud noise to draw attention to oneself. Worked Solution: The idiom metaphorically means to boast or praise one's own abilities/achievements. Option A is opposite, B is literal, C is unrelated. Option D captures the figurative meaning of self-praise.
Q2. English
Identify the segment containing the grammatical error: "No sooner did the teacher enter the class when the students stood up."
A. No sooner did the teacher
B. enter the class
C. when the students stood up
D. No error
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut: Correlative Conjunction Rule: 'No sooner' always pairs with 'than' (never 'when'). 'Hardly/Scarcely' pairs with 'when/before'. Inversion (Did + Subject) after 'No sooner' is correct. Step-by-step: 1. Sentence starts with negative adverbial 'No sooner' -> Inversion 'did the teacher enter' is correct. 2. Error is conjunction 'when'. 3. Standard pair is 'No sooner... than'. 4. Correction: 'No sooner did the teacher enter the class than the students stood up.'
Q3. English
Improve the bracketed part of the sentence: 'He (not only came late but) forgot to bring his books.'
A. not only did he come late but also forgot
B. came not only late but also forgot
C. not only he came late but also forgot
D. not only came late but also forgot
Correct Answer: D
Elimination Trick: Test Parallelism. The structure is 'Not only A but also B'. A and B must be grammatically similar (both verbs/verb phrases sharing the subject 'He'). Option A inverts subject-verb unnecessarily ('did he come'). Option B splits the verb 'came' from 'not only'. Option C inserts 'he' breaking the flow. Option D maintains perfect parallelism: 'He [not only came late] [but also forgot]'. Full Solution: Correlative conjunctions 'not only... but also' require parallel structure. Since the subject 'He' is common, the verbs 'came' and 'forgot' should follow 'not only' and 'but also' respectively. The original sentence misses 'also', making Option D the correct improvement.
Q4. English
Change the following sentence into Passive Voice: "You must finish the work today."
A. The work must be finished by you today.
B. The work must finished by you today.
C. The work must be finish by you today.
D. The work is must finished by you today.
Correct Answer: A
Shortcut: For Modal verbs (must, should, ought to) in Passive Voice, use the formula: Object + Modal + be + V3 + by + Subject. Step-by-step: 1. Identify Object ('the work'). 2. Keep Modal ('must'). 3. Add 'be' + Past Participle of main verb ('be finished'). 4. Add 'by' + Subject ('by you'). 5. Retain adverbial ('today'). Result: 'The work must be finished by you today.'
Q5. English
Choose the word which best expresses the meaning: "A place where birds are kept."
A. Aviary
B. Apiary
C. Aquarium
D. Sanctuary
Correct Answer: A
Shortcut: Use root-word elimination. 'Avi-' (Latin 'avis') = bird; 'Api-' = bee; 'Aqua-' = water. Worked Solution: 'Apiary' is for bees, 'Aquarium' for aquatic life, 'Sanctuary' is a general refuge. 'Aviary' specifically denotes a large enclosure for keeping birds.
Q6. English
Improve the bracketed part of the sentence: "If he (would have studied) harder, he would have passed the exam."
A. had studied
B. studied
C. has studied
D. No improvement
Correct Answer: A
Shortcut: Third Conditional (Unreal Past) Rule: 'If' clause never uses 'would have'. Structure: If + Past Perfect (had + V3), ... would have + V3. Step-by-step: 1. Sentence describes hypothetical past result. 2. 'Would have studied' is grammatically incorrect inside 'If' clause. 3. Correct tense for 'If' clause is Past Perfect: 'had studied'. 4. Main clause 'would have passed' is already correct. Result: 'If he had studied harder...'

Q8. English
Identify the segment in the sentence which contains the grammatical error: 'The economics of the proposed project are very complicated for a layman to understand.'
A. The economics
B. of the proposed project
C. are
D. very complicated
Correct Answer: C
Elimination Trick: Identify the subject ('Economics') – it is a noun plural in form but singular in meaning (denoting a field of study), like Mathematics, Physics, or News. Such subjects always take a singular verb. Option C ('are') is a plural verb, creating a Subject-Verb Agreement error. The correct verb is 'is'. Full Solution: The subject 'Economics' refers to the academic discipline/science, which is singular. Therefore, the verb must be singular ('is'). Segment C contains the error.
Q9. English
Change the narration: He said to me, "Please don't open the door."
A. He ordered me to not open the door.
B. He requested me not to open the door.
C. He asked me that I should not open the door.
D. He told me please don't open the door.
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: Imperative sentences with 'Please' change Reporting Verb to 'requested/advised'. Negative 'don't' becomes 'not to'. Remove inverted commas. Step-by-step: 1. 'Said to' becomes 'requested' (due to 'Please'). 2. 'Me' remains object. 3. 'Don't open' converts to 'not to open' (Infinitive negative). 4. Pronouns unchanged. Result: 'He requested me not to open the door.'
Q10. English
Fill in the blank with the most appropriate preposition: 'The manager did not agree ______ my proposal for the new marketing strategy.'
A. with
B. on
C. to
D. for
Correct Answer: C
Elimination Trick: Apply the 'Agree' rule shortcut. 'Agree WITH' takes a person/entity (I agree with you). 'Agree TO' takes a proposal, plan, suggestion, or request (I agree to the plan). 'Agree ON' takes a topic/issue (We agree on the date). Here the object is 'my proposal' (a plan/suggestion). Options A, B, D are incorrect collocations for 'proposal'. Full Solution: The verb 'agree' collocates differently based on the object. When the object is a proposal, suggestion, or plan, the correct preposition is 'to'. Since 'proposal' is a plan put forward for consideration, 'agree to' is the standard usage.

Q12. English
Choose the word which best expresses the meaning of the given phrase: 'A person who hates mankind.'
A. Philanthropist
B. Misanthrope
C. Misogynist
D. Ascetic
Correct Answer: B
Elimination Trick: Break down the roots. 'Anthropos' = mankind/human. 'Miso' = hatred. 'Gyne' = woman. 'Phil' = love. Option A (Philanthropist) loves mankind. Option C (Misogynist) hates women. Option D (Ascetic) practices self-denial. Option B (Misanthrope) combines Miso + Anthropos = hatred of mankind. Full Solution: 'Misanthrope' is the precise one-word substitution for a person who dislikes and avoids human society. The root 'anthropos' refers to humans/humanity, and 'mis-' indicates hatred.
Q13. English
Identify the segment which contains the grammatical error: "Neither the captain nor the players was happy with the referee's decision."
A. Neither the captain
B. nor the players
C. was happy
D. with the referee's decision
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut: Apply the Proximity Rule — with 'Neither...nor' / 'Either...or', the verb agrees with the noun/pronoun nearest to it (here, 'players', plural). Worked Solution: The subjects are 'captain' (singular) and 'players' (plural) joined by 'nor'. The verb must agree with the closer subject 'players', requiring plural 'were'. Segment C uses singular 'was', hence the error.
Q14. English
Choose the word nearest in meaning to 'Ubiquitous'.
A. Rare
B. Confusing
C. Omnipresent
D. Ambiguous
Correct Answer: C
Shortcut: Root 'Ubi-' (Latin: everywhere). Eliminate antonyms (Rare) and unrelated distractors (Confusing, Ambiguous). Step-by-step: 1. 'Ubiquitous' means existing everywhere simultaneously. 2. 'Omnipresent' (Omni=all, present) is the direct synonym. 3. 'Rare' is antonym. 4. 'Confusing/Ambiguous' relate to clarity, not presence. Correct: Omnipresent.
Q15. English
Improve the bracketed part of the sentence: "It is high time that we (leave) this place immediately."
A. leave
B. left
C. have left
D. had left
Correct Answer: B
Shortcut: The structure 'It is high time / It is time' is always followed by Simple Past (V2) to indicate present/future necessity (Subjunctive mood). Worked Solution: 'Leave' is base form (V1), 'left' is Simple Past (V2). The sentence expresses current urgency, so 'left' is grammatically correct. 'Have left' (Present Perfect) and 'had left' (Past Perfect) are structurally incorrect here.
