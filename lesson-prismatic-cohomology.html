Prismatic Cohomology — the Prism, the Crystalline Comparison & the Hodge-Tate Comparison

Prismatic cohomology is a cohomology theory for p-adic formal schemes introduced by Bhatt and Scholze that interpolates between and generalizes all p-adic cohomology theories — de Rham, crystalline, étale, and Hodge-Tate. The central object is the prism (A, I): a δ-ring A together with an ideal I ⊂ A such that I + δ(A) generates A and I is locally principal. The prismatic site X_{Δ} of a p-adic formal scheme X is the opposite of the category of maps Spf(A/I) → X where (A, I) is a prism. A prismatic crystal is a sheaf of (A, I)-modules on X_{Δ}. The prismatic cohomology Δ_{X/R} is the cohomology of the structure sheaf of the prismatic site relative to a base prism R. The Nygaard filtration N^i gives the Hodge-Tate comparison Δ_{X/R}/I ≅ Ω̂_X^*[−1] (the completed Hodge cohomology shifted by −1). The crystalline comparison identifies Δ_{X/R}[1/p] with RΓ_{cris}(X_k/W(k))[1/p] (the crystalline cohomology of the special fiber). The étale comparison relates prismatic cohomology after inverting p to the ℓ-adic étale cohomology of the generic fiber. Applications include the integral p-adic Hodge theory, the integral Hodge-Tate decomposition, and the proof that the proper direct image of coherent sheaves for the prismatic site is a perfect complex of F-gauges.

"Prismatic cohomology is the crystal that unifies p-adic cohomology theories."
— Bhargav Bhatt
Part 1

📏 δ-Rings and Prisms

Foundations
δ-Rings, Frobenius Lifts, and the Prism (A, I)
A δ-ring A is a commutative ring equipped with a map δ: A → A such that δ(0) = 0, δ(1) = 0, δ(a+b) = δ(a) + δ(b) + (a^p + b^p − (a+b)^p)/p, and δ(ab) = a^p δ(b) + b^p δ(a) + p δ(a)δ(b). The p-th power Frobenius lift φ: A → A is defined by φ(a) = a^p + pδ(a). The map φ is a ring homomorphism lifting the Frobenius on A/pA. A prism (A, I) consists of a δ-ring A together with an ideal I ⊂ A such that p ∈ I + δ(A) (the "prismatic condition") and I is locally principal. The completion Â of A at I is the (p, I)-adic completion. The key examples: the Breuil-Kisin prism A = W(k)[[u]], I = (E(u)) where E(u) is the Eisenstein polynomial for a uniformizer of a p-adic field; and the perfect prism where A is (p, I)-complete and φ is bijective (a perfect δ-ring). The prismatic condition I + δ(A) = A[p^{-1}] ∩ A ensures that A/I is a p-adic ring. The ideal I is often called the prismatic ideal. For a prism (A, I), the quotient A/I is a p-adic formal scheme (the "Witt frame" of the prism). The pair (A, I) is the fundamental building block: the prismatic site of a p-adic formal scheme X is defined in terms of prisms over a base.
A δ-ring is a lift of the Frobenius; a prism (A, I) pairs a δ-ring A with a locally principal ideal I such that p is in the ideal generated by I and δ(A), making A/I a p-adic ring — the basic object on which the prismatic site is defined.
Part 2

🌍 The Prismatic Site and Prismatic Cohomology

Site
The Prismatic Site X_{Δ}, Prismatic F-Gauges, and the Nygaard Filtration
Let X be a smooth p-adic formal scheme over a base S = Spf(R) where R is a δ-ring. The prismatic site X_{Δ} is the category opposite to the category of maps (Spf(A/I) → X, (A, I) a prism over R) equipped with the flat topology. The structure sheaf O_{Δ} on X_{Δ} sends (A, I) to A. A prismatic F-gauge is a sheaf M of O_{Δ}-modules together with a Frobenius-linearization φ_M: φ^* M → M (where φ is the lift of Frobenius on A). The prismatic cohomology Δ_{X/R} = RΓ(X_{Δ}, O_{Δ}) is the cohomology of the structure sheaf; it is an object of the derived category of (R, I_R)-modules equipped with a Frobenius action. The Nygaard filtration N^i Δ is defined by N^i Δ = ker(θ: Δ → ...) where θ: A → A/I is the projection; explicitly N^i = ker(Δ → Δ/I^i) or more precisely N^i = fib(Δ → Δ/I^i). The Nygaard complex (Δ_{X/R}, N^i) is a filtered complex. The Hodge-Tate comparison states: Δ_{X/R}/I ≅ Ω̂_X^*[−1], where Ω̂_X^* is the completed Hodge cohomology (the cohomology of the completed Hodge complex of X). For X smooth, Δ_{X/R} is a complex of F-gauges, meaning it carries both a Nygaard filtration and a Frobenius action compatible with the filtration. The Hodge-Tate cohomology gr^i Δ = N^i/N^{i+1} is isomorphic to Ω̂_X^i[i] (the completed Hodge cohomology placed in cohomological degree i). The site X_{Δ} is a "prismatic" analog of the crystalline site; the key difference is that the structure sheaf is a δ-ring, not a Witt ring.
The prismatic site X_{Δ} is the category of prisms over X; its cohomology Δ_{X/R} interpolates between all p-adic cohomology theories via the Nygaard filtration and Frobenius action.
Part 3

🧮 The Crystalline and Étale Comparisons

Comparisons
Crystalline Comparison, Étale Comparison, and the p-adic Period Map
The crystalline comparison: for a smooth p-adic formal scheme X with special fiber X_k over a perfect field k, the prismatic cohomology Δ_{X/W(k)}[1/p] is isomorphic to the crystalline cohomology of the special fiber: Δ_{X/W(k)}[1/p] ≅ RΓ_{cris}(X_k/W(k))[1/p]. The proof proceeds by identifying the prismatic cohomology with the relative de Rham-Witt complex, which is known to compute crystalline cohomology. More precisely, the prismatic cohomology of X relative to W(k) is identified with the cohomology of the de Rham-Witt complex of the reduction modulo p, after inverting p. The étale comparison: for a smooth proper formal scheme X over O_K (a p-adic ring, e.g., A = O_K with K a p-adic field), the prismatic cohomology after inverting p gives the ℓ-adic étale cohomology of the generic fiber X_K. Specifically, Δ_{X/A}[1/p] ⊗_{A[1/p]} B_{dR} ≅ H^i_{ét}(X_K, ℚ_p) ⊗ B_{dR}. This provides a new construction of the p-adic period map — the Hodge-Tate period map for the universal cover of the p-adic formal scheme. The prismatic cohomology thus gives a unified framework that interpolates between the étale cohomology of the generic fiber and the crystalline cohomology of the special fiber. The comparison isomorphisms are natural and respect the Frobenius actions and filtrations on both sides. The construction does not require a choice of embedding of the p-adic field into ℂ_p, making it canonical.
The crystalline comparison identifies prismatic cohomology with the crystalline cohomology of the special fiber after inverting p; the étale comparison identifies it with the p-adic étale cohomology of the generic fiber after extending scalars to B_{dR}.
Part 4

🔥 Integral p-adic Hodge Theory and Applications

Applications
Integral Hodge-Tate Decomposition, Weight Filtration, and the Proper Direct Image
The integral Hodge-Tate decomposition: for a smooth proper variety X over a p-adic field K, the cohomology H^i_{ét}(X_K, ℤ_p) admits an integral structure closely tied to the prismatic cohomology of a smooth formal model X over O_K. The Bhatt-Morrow-Scholze (2018) construction of the absolute prismatic cohomology (the "prismatic cohomology over ℤ_p") provides a new framework for the integral p-adic Hodge theory, giving an integral version of the comparisons that work integrally (not just after inverting p). The Nygaard filtration N^i on Δ_{X/R} gives the filtered pieces; the associated graded recovers the Hodge-Tate cohomology. The comparison between étale and crystalline cohomology is a direct consequence: the prismatic cohomology provides a "mixed characteristics" analog of the de Rham-Witt complex, yielding a direct proof of the comparison isomorphism between the étale cohomology of the generic fiber and the crystalline cohomology of the special fiber. The proper direct image: for a proper smooth map f: X → Y, the complex Rf_{Δ*} (the prismatic pushforward) is a perfect complex of F-gauges on the prismatic site of Y — this is the prismatic analog of the theorem on the proper direct image of coherent sheaves. The prismatic characterization of the weight filtration: the weight filtration in p-adic cohomology can be recovered from the Nygaard filtration and the Frobenius action on prismatic cohomology. Applications include the proof of the integral p-adic Hodge decomposition, the computation of the p-adic étale cohomology of complete intersections in toric varieties, and the construction of the "prismatic" version of the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence.
The absolute prismatic cohomology (Bhatt-Morrow-Scholze) provides the definitive framework for integral p-adic Hodge theory, giving integral comparison theorems and a direct proof of the étale-crystalline comparison isomorphism.
