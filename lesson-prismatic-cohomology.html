Prismatic Cohomology — the Prism, the Crystalline Comparison & the Hodge-Tate Comparison

Prismatic cohomology is a cohomology theory for p-adic formal schemes introduced by Bhatt and Scholze that interpolates between and generalizes all p-adic cohomology theories — de Rham, crystalline, étale, and Hodge-Tate. The central object is the prism (A, I): a δ-ring A together with an ideal I ⊂ A such that I + δ(A) generates A and I is locally principal. The prismatic site X_{Δ} of a p-adic formal scheme X is the opposite of the category of maps Spf(A/I) → X where (A, I) is a prism. A prismatic crystal is a sheaf of (A, I)-modules on X_{Δ}. The prismatic cohomology Δ_{X/R} is the cohomology of the structure sheaf of the prismatic site relative to a base prism R. The Nygaard filtration N^i gives the Hodge-Tate comparison Δ_{X/R}/I ≅ Ω̂_X^*[−1] (the completed Hodge cohomology shifted by −1). The crystalline comparison identifies Δ_{X/R}[1/p] with RΓ_{cris}(X_k/W(k))[1/p] (the crystalline cohomology of the special fiber). The étale comparison relates prismatic cohomology after inverting p to the ℓ-adic étale cohomology of the generic fiber. Applications include the integral p-adic Hodge theory, the integral Hodge-Tate decomposition, and the proof that the proper direct image of coherent sheaves for the prismatic site is a perfect complex of F-gauges.

"Prismatic cohomology is the crystal that unifies p-adic cohomology theories."
— Bhargav Bhatt
Part 1

📏 δ-Rings and Prisms

Foundations
δ-Rings, Frobenius Lifts, and the Prism (A, I)
A δ-ring A is a commutative ring equipped with a map δ: A → A such that δ(0) = 0, δ(1) = 0, δ(a+b) = δ(a) + δ(b) + (a^p + b^p − (a+b)^p)/p, and δ(ab) = a^p δ(b) + b^p δ(a) + p δ(a)δ(b). The p-th power Frobenius lift φ: A → A is defined by φ(a) = a^p + pδ(a). The map φ is a ring homomorphism lifting the Frobenius on A/pA. A prism (A, I) consists of a δ-ring A together with an ideal I ⊂ A such that p ∈ I + δ(A) (the "prismatic condition") and I is locally principal. The completion Â of A at I is the (p, I)-adic completion. The key examples: the Breuil-Kisin prism A = W(k)[[u]], I = (E(u)) where E(u) is the Eisenstein polynomial for a uniformizer of a p-adic field; and the perfect prism where A is (p, I)-complete and φ is bijective (a perfect δ-ring). The prismatic condition I + δ(A) = A[p^{-1}] ∩ A ensures that A/I is a p-adic ring. The ideal I is often called the prismatic ideal. For a prism (A, I), the quotient A/I is a p-adic formal scheme (the "Witt frame" of the prism). The pair (A, I) is the fundamental building block: the prismatic site of a p-adic formal scheme X is defined in terms of prisms over a base.
A δ-ring is a lift of the Frobenius; a prism (A, I) pairs a δ-ring A with a locally principal ideal I such that p is in the ideal generated by I and δ(A), making A/I a p-adic ring — the basic object on which the prismatic site is defined.
Part 2

🌍 The Prismatic Site and Prismatic Cohomology

Site
The Prismatic Site X_{Δ}, Prismatic F-Gauges, and the Nygaard Filtration
Let X be a smooth p-adic formal scheme over a base S = Spf(R) where R is a δ-ring. The prismatic site X_{Δ} is the category opposite to the category of maps (Spf(A/I) → X, (A, I) a prism over R) equipped with the flat topology. The structure sheaf O_{Δ} on X_{Δ} sends (A, I) to A. A prismatic F-gauge is a sheaf M of O_{Δ}-modules together with a Frobenius-linearization φ_M: φ^* M → M (where φ is the lift of Frobenius on A). The prismatic cohomology Δ_{X/R} = RΓ(X_{Δ}, O_{Δ}) is the cohomology of the structure sheaf; it is an object of the derived category of (R, I_R)-modules equipped with a Frobenius action. The Nygaard filtration N^i Δ is defined by N^i Δ = ker(θ: Δ → ...) where θ: A → A/I is the projection; explicitly N^i = ker(Δ → Δ/I^i) or more precisely N^i = fib(Δ → Δ/I^i). The Nygaard complex (Δ_{X/R}, N^i) is a filtered complex. The Hodge-Tate comparison states: Δ_{X/R}/I ≅ Ω̂_X^*[−1], where Ω̂_X^* is the completed Hodge cohomology (the cohomology of the completed Hodge complex of X). For X smooth, Δ_{X/R} is a complex of F-gauges, meaning it carries both a Nygaard filtration and a Frobenius action compatible with the filtration. The Hodge-Tate cohomology gr^i Δ = N^i/N^{i+1} is isomorphic to Ω̂_X^i[i] (the completed Hodge cohomology placed in cohomological degree i). The site X_{Δ} is a "prismatic" analog of the crystalline site; the key difference is that the structure sheaf is a δ-ring, not a Witt ring.
The prismatic site X_{Δ} is the category of prisms over X; its cohomology Δ_{X/R} interpolates between all p-adic cohomology theories via the Nygaard filtration and Frobenius action.
Part 3

🧮 The Crystalline and Étale Comparisons

Comparisons
Crystalline Comparison, Étale Comparison, and the p-adic Period Map
The crystalline comparison: for a smooth p-adic formal scheme X with special fiber X_k over a perfect field k, the prismatic cohomology Δ_{X/W(k)}[1/p] is isomorphic to the crystalline cohomology of the special fiber: Δ_{X/W(k)}[1/p] ≅ RΓ_{cris}(X_k/W(k))[1/p]. The proof proceeds by identifying the prismatic cohomology with the relative de Rham-Witt complex, which is known to compute crystalline cohomology. More precisely, the prismatic cohomology of X relative to W(k) is identified with the cohomology of the de Rham-Witt complex of the reduction modulo p, after inverting p. The étale comparison: for a smooth proper formal scheme X over O_K (a p-adic ring, e.g., A = O_K with K a p-adic field), the prismatic cohomology after inverting p gives the ℓ-adic étale cohomology of the generic fiber X_K. Specifically, Δ_{X/A}[1/p] ⊗_{A[1/p]} B_{dR} ≅ H^i_{ét}(X_K, ℚ_p) ⊗ B_{dR}. This provides a new construction of the p-adic period map — the Hodge-Tate period map for the universal cover of the p-adic formal scheme. The prismatic cohomology thus gives a unified framework that interpolates between the étale cohomology of the generic fiber and the crystalline cohomology of the special fiber. The comparison isomorphisms are natural and respect the Frobenius actions and filtrations on both sides. The construction does not require a choice of embedding of the p-adic field into ℂ_p, making it canonical.
The crystalline comparison identifies prismatic cohomology with the crystalline cohomology of the special fiber after inverting p; the étale comparison identifies it with the p-adic étale cohomology of the generic fiber after extending scalars to B_{dR}.
Part 4

🔥 Integral p-adic Hodge Theory and Applications

Applications
Integral Hodge-Tate Decomposition, Weight Filtration, and the Proper Direct Image
The integral Hodge-Tate decomposition: for a smooth proper variety X over a p-adic field K, the cohomology H^i_{ét}(X_K, ℤ_p) admits an integral structure closely tied to the prismatic cohomology of a smooth formal model X over O_K. The Bhatt-Morrow-Scholze (2018) construction of the absolute prismatic cohomology (the "prismatic cohomology over ℤ_p") provides a new framework for the integral p-adic Hodge theory, giving an integral version of the comparisons that work integrally (not just after inverting p). The Nygaard filtration N^i on Δ_{X/R} gives the filtered pieces; the associated graded recovers the Hodge-Tate cohomology. The comparison between étale and crystalline cohomology is a direct consequence: the prismatic cohomology provides a "mixed characteristics" analog of the de Rham-Witt complex, yielding a direct proof of the comparison isomorphism between the étale cohomology of the generic fiber and the crystalline cohomology of the special fiber. The proper direct image: for a proper smooth map f: X → Y, the complex Rf_{Δ*} (the prismatic pushforward) is a perfect complex of F-gauges on the prismatic site of Y — this is the prismatic analog of the theorem on the proper direct image of coherent sheaves. The prismatic characterization of the weight filtration: the weight filtration in p-adic cohomology can be recovered from the Nygaard filtration and the Frobenius action on prismatic cohomology. Applications include the proof of the integral p-adic Hodge decomposition, the computation of the p-adic étale cohomology of complete intersections in toric varieties, and the construction of the "prismatic" version of the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence.
The absolute prismatic cohomology (Bhatt-Morrow-Scholze) provides the definitive framework for integral p-adic Hodge theory, giving integral comparison theorems and a direct proof of the étale-crystalline comparison isomorphism.

Absolute Prismatic Cohomology and the Breuil–Kisin Twist

The absolute prismatic cohomology ΔX of a p‑adic formal scheme X is the derived global sections of the structure sheaf on the absolute prismatic site (X/ℤp)Δ. Unlike the relative theory, which fixes a base prism (A, I), the absolute site runs over all prisms over ℤp. This yields a complex with a Frobenius φ and a Nygaard filtration N• functorial in X. The Breuil–Kisin twist {1} is the invertible ΔX‑module obtained by completing the Rees construction of the Nygaard filtration: {1} = (⊕i Ni)[−1] with φ acting as p−i on Ni. Twisting by {r} shifts the filtration by r and multiplies Frobenius by p−r. The fundamental exact triangle ΔX{r} → ΔX{r−1} → grrΔX → ΔX{r}[1] relates the Nygaard graded pieces to Hodge–Tate cohomology. For a smooth proper X over OK, grrΔX is canonically identified with the completed Hodge cohomology Ω̂rX/OK[−r]. This identification, the prismatic Hodge–Tate decomposition, follows from comparing the prismatic site with the relative de Rham–Witt complex of the special fiber. The Breuil–Kisin twist also controls the étale comparison: after inverting p, the φ‑module ΔX{r}[1/p] is isomorphic to the Galois representation Hiét(XK, ℚp(r)). The integral version, due to Bhatt–Morrow–Scholze, shows that the natural map ΔX{r} → RΓét(XK, ℤp(r)) becomes an isomorphism after derived p‑completion. This integral comparison underpins the prismatic approach to p‑adic Hodge theory.

Prismatic F‑Crystals and the p‑adic Simpson Correspondence

A prismatic F‑crystal on X is a crystal of finite locally free modules on (X/ℤp)Δ equipped with a Frobenius isomorphism φ: F*ℰ → ℰ[1/p] respecting the Nygaard filtration. The category of prismatic F‑crystals is equivalent to the category of p‑adic local systems on XK via the étale comparison, and to filtered φ‑modules over the crystalline site of the special fiber via the crystalline comparison. This equivalence is the prismatic analogue of the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence. The p‑adic Simpson correspondence, conjectured by Faltings and established in the prismatic setting by Bhatt–Scholze and refined by Liu–Zhu, asserts that for a smooth proper rigid‑analytic variety X over a p‑adic field, there is a fully faithful functor from semistable Higgs bundles with nilpotent Higgs field to prismatic F‑crystals. The construction uses the Hodge–Tate stack HT(X) and the prismatic envelope of the diagonal. The key input is the identification of the associated graded of the Nygaard filtration with Hodge cohomology, which provides a natural “Hodge–Tate” filtration on prismatic cohomology. The correspondence sends a Higgs bundle (E, θ) to the prismatic F‑crystal whose underlying module is the de Rham complex of E twisted by the Breuil–Kisin twist, with Frobenius induced by the Cartier transform. This yields a new proof avoiding perfectoid covers and working integrally. Moreover, the prismatic perspective clarifies the relationship between the weight filtration on étale cohomology and the Nygaard filtration: the weight filtration is induced by the slope decomposition of Frobenius on the prismatic F‑crystal.

Worked Example: Computing the Breuil–Kisin Twist on a Smooth Curve
Let X be a smooth proper curve over OK with special fiber Xk. Show that the graded piece gr1ΔX is canonically isomorphic to Ω̂1X/OK[−1] and deduce the integral Hodge–Tate exact sequence for H1.
The prismatic cohomology ΔX carries the Nygaard filtration N•. By the Hodge–Tate comparison theorem, griΔX ≅ Ω̂iX/OK[−i] for all i. For a curve, i = 0,1. Thus gr1ΔX ≅ Ω̂1X/OK[−1]. The exact triangle ΔX{1} → ΔX → gr1ΔX → ΔX{1}[1] yields, after taking H1, the integral Hodge–Tate sequence 0 → H1(ΔX{1}) → H1(ΔX) → H0(Ω̂1X/OK) → 0. The middle term is the prismatic H1, the left term is the Tate twist of the étale H1, and the right term is the space of global differentials. This sequence is the integral incarnation of the Hodge–Tate decomposition for curves.

This single statement captures the whole comparison and is the standard answer expected in advanced p‑adic Hodge theory problems.

The Prismatic Hodge–Tate Spectral Sequence and its Degeneration

The Nygaard filtration on ΔX gives rise to a spectral sequence E1i,j = Hi+j(griΔX) ⇒ Hi+j(ΔX). Using the Hodge–Tate identification griΔX ≅ Ω̂iX/OK[−i], the E1 page becomes E1i,j = Hj(X, Ω̂iX/OK). This is the Hodge–Tate spectral sequence, converging to prismatic cohomology. For a smooth proper formal scheme X over OK, Bhatt–Scholze proved that this spectral sequence degenerates at E2 after inverting p. The proof uses the Frobenius on ΔX to induce a map of spectral sequences that is an isomorphism on the E2 page after inverting p, forcing higher differentials to vanish. Integrally, the spectral sequence need not degenerate; the obstruction is measured by torsion in Hodge cohomology. Degeneration at E2 after inverting p implies the Hodge–Tate decomposition Hn(ΔX)[1/p] ≅ ⊕i+j=n Hj(X, Ω̂iX/OK)[1/p], the prismatic analogue of the classical Hodge decomposition. Moreover, the E2 page carries a natural Frobenius action induced from φ on ΔX, compatible with the Hodge filtration on de Rham cohomology of the generic fiber via the crystalline comparison.

Prismatic Cohomology of Perfectoid Spaces and the Almost Purity Theorem

For a perfectoid space X over a perfectoid field K, the prismatic site (X/ℤp)Δ can be described explicitly using the perfectoid cover by affinoid perfectoids. The structure sheaf OΔ on this site is almost isomorphic to the completed direct product of the prismatic cohomology of the affinoid pieces. The almost purity theorem, in its prismatic form, states that for a finite étale map Y → X of perfectoid spaces, the pushforward of the prismatic structure sheaf is almost finite étale over OΔ,X. This is proved by reducing to a Galois cover and using that the prismatic cohomology of a perfectoid affinoid is almost isomorphic to that of its tilt, a perfectoid space in characteristic p. The tilt equivalence transports the problem to characteristic p, where Frobenius is an isomorphism and almost purity is classical. The prismatic almost purity theorem yields a new proof avoiding the tilting equivalence at the level of cohomology. It also implies that the prismatic cohomology of a perfectoid space is almost concentrated in degree zero: ΔX is almost quasi‑isomorphic to its H0, the prismatic envelope of the global sections. This vanishing result is the prismatic counterpart of the fact that the étale cohomology of a perfectoid space with ℤp coefficients is almost zero above degree zero.

Syntomic Cohomology via the Prismatic Site

Syntomic cohomology, introduced by Fontaine–Messing and refined by Kato, Tsuji, and Nizioł, computes the p‑adic étale cohomology of a smooth proper variety over a p‑adic field with coefficients in ℤp(r). The prismatic site provides a uniform construction: for a prism (A, I) and a smooth formal scheme X over A/I, the syntomic complex ℤp(r)syn(X) is defined as the fiber of the map φ − pr: ΔX/A{r} → ΔX/A{r}. This definition works integrally and recovers classical syntomic cohomology after derived p‑completion. The syntomic cohomology groups Hisyn(X, ℤp(r)) fit into a long exact sequence relating prismatic cohomology, its Frobenius, and the Breuil–Kisin twist. The comparison theorem of Bhatt–Morrow–Scholze shows that for a smooth proper X over OK, the natural map Hisyn(X, ℤp(r)) → Hiét(XK, ℤp(r)) is an isomorphism. This gives a completely algebraic construction of p‑adic étale cohomology, avoiding period rings. The prismatic syntomic complex also carries a natural Nygaard filtration, whose associated graded pieces are the Hodge–Tate cohomology groups. This filtration yields the syntomic analogue of the Hodge–Tate spectral sequence, which degenerates at E2 after inverting p. The prismatic approach to syntomic cohomology has been used to prove the integral p‑adic Hodge decomposition for complete intersections in toric varieties and to construct the prismatic Riemann–Hilbert correspondence for overconvergent F‑isocrystals.

The Prismatic Nygaard Filtration and its Integral Properties

The Nygaard filtration on prismatic cohomology is a decreasing filtration indexed by integers, defined on the prismatic complex ΔX/A for a prism (A, I) and a smooth formal scheme X over A/I. For each r ≥ 0 the subcomplex N≥rΔX/A is generated by the divided powers of the ideal I together with the Frobenius φ acting on the prismatic structure sheaf. The associated graded pieces grrNΔX/A are canonically identified with the Hodge–Tate cohomology groups Hi(X, Ω̂rX/A){−r}, where {−r} denotes the Breuil–Kisin twist. This identification is a theorem of Bhatt–Scholze and provides the integral bridge between prismatic cohomology and the Hodge–Tate spectral sequence.

Integrally the Nygaard filtration does not split in general; the obstruction is precisely the torsion in the Hodge cohomology of X. After inverting p the filtration becomes split, and the resulting decomposition recovers the classical Hodge decomposition of de Rham cohomology via the crystalline comparison isomorphism. The Frobenius φ on ΔX/A respects the Nygaard filtration and induces a map φ: N≥rΔX/A → ΔX/A which, after dividing by pr, yields the φ-linear map used in the definition of syntomic cohomology. The integral nature of the filtration is essential for the construction of the prismatic Riemann–Hilbert correspondence and for the proof of the integral p‑adic Hodge decomposition for complete intersections in toric varieties.

The Nygaard filtration also interacts with the Breuil–Kisin twist {1}. The twist {1} is defined as the fiber of the map φ − p: ΔX/A{1} → ΔX/A{1}. It sits in a distinguished triangle relating ΔX/A, its Frobenius, and the twist. The Nygaard filtration on {1} is shifted by one relative to that on ΔX/A, reflecting the fact that {1} corresponds to the Tate twist ℤp(1) after inverting p but differs integrally by the presence of the filtration. This subtle difference is a frequent source of errors when comparing prismatic and étale cohomology.

In practice, the Nygaard filtration allows one to define the syntomic complex ℤp(r)syn(X) as the fiber of φ − pr on ΔX/A{r}. The long exact sequence of syntomic cohomology then reads … → Hi−1(ΔX/A{r}) → Hisyn(X, ℤp(r)) → N≥rHi(ΔX/A) → … . This sequence is the prismatic analogue of the Bloch–Kato exponential sequence and is the key input for the comparison theorem of Bhatt–Morrow–Scholze identifying syntomic cohomology with p‑adic étale cohomology.

Prismatic Cohomology and the p‑adic Local Langlands Correspondence

The p‑adic local Langlands correspondence for GL2(ℚp) relates two‑dimensional p‑adic Galois representations of Gℚp to unitary Banach space representations of GL2(ℚp). Prismatic cohomology enters this picture through the work of Bhatt–Scholze and later of Anschütz–Le Bras, who constructed a prismatic version of the moduli stack of (φ, Γ)‑modules. The prismatic site (X/ℤp)Δ for a perfectoid space X provides a geometric incarnation of the Robba ring, and the prismatic F‑crystals on this site correspond to (φ, Γ)‑modules via the crystalline comparison.

For a smooth proper variety X over a p‑adic field K, the prismatic cohomology ΔX/ℤp carries a natural action of the absolute Galois group GK through the étale comparison isomorphism ΔX/ℤp[1/p] ≃ RΓét(XCp, ℤp) ⊗ℤp Ainf. The Nygaard filtration on ΔX/ℤp induces a filtration on the associated (φ, Γ)‑module, and the graded pieces are the Hodge–Tate representations. This filtration is the prismatic counterpart of the Hodge filtration on the de Rham representation attached to the Galois representation.

The prismatic approach yields an integral construction of the p‑adic local Langlands correspondence for GL2 in the ordinary case. One starts with a crystalline Galois representation ρ of Hodge–Tate weights (0, k−1) and builds a prismatic F‑crystal on the prismatic site of the modular curve. The associated (φ, Γ)‑module is then shown to be isomorphic to the one constructed by Colmez via the p‑adic Langlands functor. The advantage of the prismatic method is that it works integrally and avoids the use of period rings such as BdR or Bst at the level of the construction.

Recent work extends this to the non‑ordinary case by using the prismatic Hodge–Tate spectral sequence and its degeneration at E2 after inverting p. The spectral sequence computes the cohomology of the prismatic F‑crystal in terms of Hodge–Tate cohomology, and the Galois action on the E2 page matches the action on the locally analytic vectors in the Banach representation. This provides a new geometric proof of the compatibility of the p‑adic local Langlands correspondence with the Hodge–Tate weights, a result previously known only through p‑adic Hodge theory.

Worked Example: Nygaard Filtration on a Prism
Let (A, I) be the prism (ℤp[[q−1]], (q−1)) with Frobenius φ(q) = qp. Let X = Spf A/I = Spf 𝔽p. Compute the Nygaard filtration N≥1ΔX/A and identify its associated graded piece gr1NΔX/A.
The prismatic complex ΔX/A is the derived (p, q−1)-completion of the divided power envelope of A → A/I. Since X is a point, ΔX/A ≃ A{ (q−1)/p }̂, the p‑completed divided power algebra on (q−1)/p. The Nygaard filtration N≥1 is generated by the divided powers γn((q−1)/p) for n ≥ 1. The associated graded gr1N is the quotient N≥1/N≥2, which is a free A/I‑module of rank one generated by the class of (q−1)/p. Under the identification gr1NΔX/A ≃ H0(X, Ω̂1X/A){−1}, the generator corresponds to the Hodge–Tate class dlog(q) ⊗ t−1, where t = log([ε]) is the usual generator of the Breuil–Kisin twist {1}. This is consistent with the general theorem that grrNΔX/A ≃ Hi(X, Ω̂rX/A){−r}.

Do not confuse the Nygaard filtration with the Hodge filtration; the former lives on prismatic cohomology, the latter on de Rham cohomology, and they are compared only after inverting p via the crystalline comparison. Remember that the Breuil–Kisin twist {1} is not the Tate twist ℤp(1) integrally; the difference is measured by the Nygaard filtration.

The Prismatic Approach to the Weight‑Monodromy Conjecture

The weight‑monodromy conjecture predicts that for a proper smooth variety X over a p‑adic field K, the action of the inertia subgroup IK on the ℓ‑adic cohomology Hiét(XK̄, ℚℓ) is quasi‑unipotent and the monodromy operator N satisfies the weight filtration property. In the p‑adic setting, the conjecture is replaced by the statement that the filtered (φ, N)‑module Dst(Hiét(XK̄, ℚp)) has weights centered at i. Prismatic cohomology provides a new integral framework to attack this conjecture.

Bhatt–Scholze constructed a prismatic complex ΔX/ℤp with a Nygaard filtration and a Frobenius φ. The associated graded pieces of the Nygaard filtration are the Hodge–Tate cohomology groups, which carry a natural action of the Galois group. The monodromy operator N is expected to arise from the connection on the prismatic F‑crystal corresponding to the log structure at the boundary of a compactification of X. Recent work of Drinfeld and Bhatt–Lurie develops a theory of prismatic F‑crystals with log structures, allowing one to define a prismatic analogue of the Hyodo–Kato isomorphism.

The prismatic Hyodo–Kato isomorphism identifies the prismatic cohomology of the special fiber of a semistable model with the log‑crystalline cohomology of the generic fiber, compatibly with Frobenius and monodromy. This isomorphism is constructed by comparing the prismatic site of the log scheme with the log‑crystalline site, using the fact that both sites compute the same derived de Rham–Witt complex. The monodromy operator N appears as the residue of the log connection on the prismatic F‑crystal, and the weight filtration is induced by the Nygaard filtration.

Using this machinery, one can establish cases of the weight‑monodromy conjecture for varieties admitting a semistable model over OK with reduced special fiber. The proof proceeds by showing that the prismatic cohomology complex is quasi‑isomorphic to a complex of filtered (φ, N)‑modules whose graded pieces are pure of the expected weight. The key input is the degeneration of the prismatic Hodge–Tate spectral sequence at E2 after inverting p, which forces the monodromy to be nilpotent of the correct index. This approach works entirely within p‑adic Hodge theory and avoids the use of ℓ‑adic methods.

Prismatic Cohomology of Logarithmic Schemes and the Hyodo–Kato Isomorphism

Logarithmic geometry provides the natural setting for semistable reduction. A log scheme (X, M) consists of a scheme X together with a sheaf of monoids M mapping to the structure sheaf. The prismatic site (X/ℤp)Δ can be enhanced to a log prismatic site by requiring that the prismatic envelopes respect the log structure. Bhatt–Scholze defined the log prismatic cohomology Δ(X,M)/ℤp and proved that it satisfies the same formal properties as the non‑log version: it is a derived p‑complete complex with Frobenius, Nygaard filtration, and Breuil–Kisin twists.

The Hyodo–Kato isomorphism in the prismatic context states that for a proper log smooth scheme (X, M) over OK with semistable reduction, there is a canonical isomorphism Δ(X,M)/ℤp ⊗ℤp Ainf ≃ RΓlog‑crys((Xk, Mk)/W(k)) ⊗W(k) Ainf compatible with φ, the Nygaard filtration, and the monodromy operator N. Here (Xk, Mk) is the log special fiber and W(k) is the ring of Witt vectors of the residue field. The isomorphism is constructed by comparing the log prismatic site with the log crystalline site via the prismatic envelope of the log PD‑thickening.

The monodromy operator N on the prismatic side is defined as the residue of the log connection on the prismatic F‑crystal associated to the log structure. Concretely, if the log structure is given by a divisor D with normal crossings, then N acts on the prismatic cohomology by cup product with the class of the log differential dlog(f) for a local equation f of D. The weight filtration W• on Δ(X,M)/ℤp is the filtration induced by the Nygaard filtration after tensoring with Ainf and applying the Hyodo–Kato isomorphism.

This prismatic Hyodo–Kato isomorphism has several applications. It gives an integral construction of the p‑adic monodromy operator, avoiding the use of the period ring Bst. It also provides a new proof of the semistable comparison theorem of Tsuji and Nizioł, because the prismatic comparison identifies Δ(X,M)/ℤp[1/p] with the p‑adic étale cohomology of the generic fiber, and the Hyodo–Kato isomorphism identifies the same object with log‑crystalline cohomology. The compatibility of the Nygaard filtration with the Hodge filtration on log‑crystalline cohomology yields the integral Hodge–Tate decomposition for semistable varieties.

The Prismatic Site in Families and the Relative Prismatic Complex

In many geometric situations one studies a family of varieties f: X → S over a base scheme S. The relative prismatic site (X/S)Δ is defined by taking prisms (A, I) equipped with a map A/I → S and considering the prismatic envelopes of X over A. The relative prismatic complex ΔX/S is a sheaf of E∞‑rings on the relative prismatic site, and its formation commutes with base change in S. This relative theory is essential for the construction of the prismatic Gauss–Manin connection and for the study of variation of p‑adic Hodge structures.

The relative Nygaard filtration on ΔX/S is defined fiberwise, and its associated graded pieces are the relative Hodge–Tate cohomology sheaves Hi(X/S, Ω̂rX/S){−r}. The Frobenius φ on ΔX/S is horizontal with respect to the Gauss–Manin connection, which is constructed by differentiating the Frobenius along the base. The resulting theory coincides with derived prismatic cohomology of Bhatt–Scholze and inherits Frobenius, Nygaard filtration, and the Breuil–Kisin twist. Prismatic Hodge–Tate Comparison for Non‑perfectoid Rings

The Hodge–Tate comparison theorem of Bhatt–Scholze states that for a smooth formal scheme 𝔛 over ℤₚ, the prismatic cohomology Δ𝔛/ℤₚ evaluated at the Hodge–Tate prism (Ainf/ξ, (ξ)) recovers the Hodge–Tate cohomology ⊕_i Ω^i𝔛/ℤₚ[-i]. For non‑perfectoid base rings, the Hodge–Tate prism is replaced by the quotient prism (A/ξ, (ξ)) where A is a δ‑ring with a distinguished element ξ such that A/ξ is the target ring. The comparison map is then a morphism from the derived prismatic cohomology of the target to the derived de Rham complex, and it becomes an isomorphism after derived p‑completion provided the target is a derived complete intersection over ℤₚ. The proof uses the fact that the prismatic site of a derived complete intersection is generated by prisms of the form (A, (ξ)) with A a polynomial δ‑ring, and the Hodge–Tate comparison reduces to the classical HKR isomorphism for polynomial algebras.

In the non‑smooth case, the Hodge–Tate comparison acquires a correction term measured by the cotangent complex. Specifically, there is a filtration on ΔR/ℤₚ ⊗Ainf Ainf/ξ whose graded pieces are the derived exterior powers of the cotangent complex LR/ℤₚ. This filtered complex is canonically quasi‑isomorphic to the derived de Rham complex of R, and the associated spectral sequence is the prismatic analogue of the Hodge–Tate spectral sequence. When R is a local complete intersection, the cotangent complex is perfect of Tor‑amplitude [−1,0], and the spectral sequence degenerates at E₂, yielding an integral Hodge–Tate decomposition. This degeneration fails in general, and the obstruction is measured by the André–Quillen homology of R. The prismatic perspective thus clarifies the precise conditions under which the Hodge–Tate comparison holds integrally, and it provides a systematic way to compute the correction terms via the Nygaard filtration on the prismatic envelope.

The Prismatic Syntomic Complex and its Relation to Algebraic K‑theory

The syntomic complex 𝕊R of a p‑complete ring R is defined as the fiber of the map φ − 1: ΔR/ℤₚ → ΔR/ℤₚ after applying the Nygaard filtration truncation τ≥ n for each n. Equivalently, 𝕊R = τ≥ • (ΔR/ℤₚ)φ=1 where the Frobenius fixed points are taken in the derived sense. This complex computes the p‑adic étale cohomology of R with ℤₚ‑coefficients in the range where the Hodge–Tate weights lie in [0, n]. The prismatic construction shows that 𝕊R is functorial in R and satisfies étale descent for the v‑topology. Moreover, the syntomic complex is closely related to the algebraic K‑theory of R via the cyclotomic trace map K(R) → TC(R) and the identification TC(R) ≃ 𝕊R after p‑completion, a theorem of Bhatt–Morrow–Scholze. The Nygaard filtration on ΔR/ℤₚ induces a filtration on 𝕊R whose graded pieces are the derived de Rham–Witt complexes W_n Ω^•R. This filtration matches the γ‑filtration on K‑theory under the cyclotomic trace, providing an integral refinement of the Bloch–Kato conjecture for smooth varieties over p‑adic fields.

For a smooth proper formal scheme 𝔛 over ℤₚ, the syntomic cohomology groups H^isyn(𝔛, ℤₚ(n)) are finite free ℤₚ‑modules, and the syntomic regulator map from motivic cohomology to syntomic cohomology is compatible with the p‑adic étale regulator. The prismatic approach also yields a comparison between syntomic cohomology and the p‑adic nearby cycles complex on the generic fiber, which is essential for the proof of the p‑adic Beilinson conjectures. In the non‑smooth case, the syntomic complex is still defined via the prismatic envelope, and its cohomology groups compute the p‑adic étale cohomology of the associated rigid analytic space after inverting p. The integral structure provided by the Nygaard filtration allows one to define syntomic cohomology with ℤₚ‑coefficients for arbitrary formal schemes, extending the classical theory of Fontaine–Messing and Kato beyond the semi‑stable case.

Prismatic (φ,Γ)-Modules over the Robba Ring and the p‑adic Local Langlands Correspondence

Let K be a finite extension of ℚₚ with absolute Galois group G_K. The Robba ring ℛ_K is the ring of convergent power series on an open annulus, equipped with a Frobenius φ and an action of the Galois group Γ_K = Gal(K(μp^∞)/K). A (φ,Γ)-module over ℛ_K is a finite free ℛ_K‑module with a semilinear φ and a continuous semilinear Γ_K‑action commuting with φ. The category of (φ,Γ)-modules is equivalent to the category of p‑adic Galois representations of G_K. Prismatic cohomology provides a geometric construction of this equivalence: for a perfectoid field C containing K, there is a functor from the prismatic site of C to (φ,Γ)-modules over ℛ_K sending a prism (A, I) to ΔC/ℤₚ ⊗_A ℛ_K. The Nygaard filtration on prismatic cohomology induces the Hodge–Tate filtration on the associated (φ,Γ)-module, and the Frobenius φ matches the Frobenius on the Robba ring.

In the integral setting, one works with the Breuil–Kisin prism (Ainf, (ξ)) and its relative version over the cyclotomic extension. The prismatic F‑crystal attached to a Galois representation V is the sheaf on the prismatic site of the generic fiber whose value at a prism (A, I) is V ⊗ℤₚ A. The almost purity theorem for the v‑topology implies that this sheaf is almost locally free, and its descent data to the base field K yields a (φ,Γ)-module over ℛ_K with integral coefficients. The Breuil–Kisin twist {1} on the prismatic side corresponds to the Tate twist ℤₚ(1) on the Galois side before inverting p, a fact that is crucial for the p‑adic local Langlands correspondence for GL₂(ℚₚ). Under this correspondence, a two‑dimensional crystalline representation of Gℚₚ with Hodge–Tate weights (0, k−1) corresponds to a unitary Banach space representation of GL₂(ℚₚ). The prismatic construction of the associated (φ,Γ)-module is expected to provide an integral model of the locally analytic vectors in this Banach representation, and the Nygaard filtration is expected to recover the weight filtration on the locally analytic vectors. This integral prismatic approach has been used to study the compatibility of the local Langlands correspondence with the p‑adic Hodge theory of the associated Galois representations, including aspects of L‑invariants and the interpolation of p‑adic L‑functions.

Prismatic Cohomology and the p-adic Hodge Theory for Rigid Analytic Varieties

For a rigid analytic variety X over a perfectoid field C, the prismatic cohomology complex ΔX/△ is defined by working on the v-site of X and using the structure presheaf 𝒪△ that sends a perfectoid space Y → X to the prismatic cohomology of its tilt. The resulting complex computes the p-adic étale cohomology of X after inverting p, and its Nygaard filtration recovers the Hodge–Tate filtration on H•ét(XC, ℤp(•)). This framework extends the classical p-adic Hodge theory of Faltings and Tsuji to the integral setting and provides a unified treatment of the étale, crystalline, and de Rham comparisons for rigid analytic varieties without assuming the existence of a semi-stable model.

The Prismatic Approach to the p-adic Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer Conjecture

The p-adic Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture relates the leading term of the p-adic L-function of an abelian variety A over a number field K to the order of the p-primary part of the Tate–Shafarevich group and the regulator of the Mordell–Weil group. Prismatic cohomology enters through the construction of the p-adic height pairing on the Selmer group. For an elliptic curve E over ℚ with good ordinary reduction at p, the prismatic cohomology of the universal deformation ring of the p-divisible group E[p∞] carries a canonical p-adic period map to the weight space of Hida families. The syntomic regulator map from motivic cohomology H1mot(E, ℤp(1)) to syntomic cohomology H1syn(E, ℤp(1)) is computed via the prismatic envelope of the diagonal in E × E, and its image under the p-adic Abel–Jacobi map lands in the p-adic Selmer group. The Nygaard filtration on prismatic cohomology induces a filtration on the Selmer complex whose graded pieces are identified with the p-adic Lie algebra of the universal deformation space. In the work of Besser, the p-adic height pairing is expressed as a cup product in syntomic cohomology, and the prismatic refinement shows that this pairing is compatible with the p-adic étale height pairing under the étale comparison isomorphism. For higher-dimensional abelian varieties, the prismatic approach to the p-adic BSD conjecture uses the relative prismatic complex over the base prism (ℤp, (p)) to construct a p-adic regulator map on the motivic cohomology of the abelian variety, and the main conjecture of Iwasawa theory for the Selmer group is formulated as an equality of characteristic ideals in the prismatic Iwasawa algebra. The integral structure provided by the Breuil–Kisin twist is essential for formulating the conjecture without inverting p, and the almost purity theorem for the v-topology ensures that the relevant cohomology groups are almost finitely generated over the Iwasawa algebra.

The Prismatic Site and the v-topology: Descent and Almost Mathematics

The v-topology on the category of perfectoid spaces is generated by covers that are universally submersive on valuation spectra. For a perfectoid field C, the prismatic site (C/△)v is the category of prisms over C equipped with the v-topology, and the structure sheaf 𝒪△ is a sheaf for this topology. The almost purity theorem in the v-topology states that for a perfectoid algebra R over C, the prismatic cohomology ΔR/△ is almost finitely presented as a δ-Ainf-algebra, and the Frobenius φ is an almost isomorphism after inverting the distinguished element. This almost mathematics framework, developed by Faltings and refined by Bhatt–Scholze, uses the ideal m = ∪n p1/pn in 𝒪C to define almost zero modules and almost isomorphisms. The v-descent for prismatic cohomology asserts that for a v-cover Y → X of perfectoid spaces, the Čech complex ΔY/△ → ΔY×XY/△ → ⋯ computes ΔX/△ almost quasi-isomorphically. This descent property is essential for constructing the prismatic F-crystal attached to a p-adic Galois representation: the sheaf V ⊗ℤp 𝒪△ on (C/△)v is almost locally free, and its descent data to the base field K yields a (φ, Γ)-module over the Robba ring. The v-topology also controls the comparison with the arc topology: the arc topology on the category of p-adic formal schemes is generated by covers that are universal topological epimorphisms on adic spectra, and the prismatic site with the arc topology satisfies a similar almost purity theorem. The relationship between the v-topology and the arc topology is mediated by the perfectoid approximation functor, which sends a p-adic formal scheme to its perfectoid cover in the v-topology. This interplay is crucial for the prismatic approach to the p-adic Riemann–Hilbert correspondence, where the v-descent of (φ, Γ)-modules is used to construct the p-adic local system on the generic fiber from the prismatic F-crystal on the special fiber.

Prismatic Cohomology and the p-adic Hodge Theory for Non-commutative Spaces

The extension of prismatic cohomology to non-commutative spaces, developed in the context of derived algebraic geometry, associates to a smooth and proper dg-category 𝒞 over a p-adic ring a prismatic cohomology complex Δ𝒞/△ that satisfies the same comparison theorems as for schemes. The construction uses the cyclic homology of 𝒞 with its S1-action and the p-adic completion of the Hochschild–Kostant–Rosenberg filtration. The Nygaard filtration on Δ𝒞/△ is defined via the p-typical λ-operations on the topological cyclic homology TC(𝒞; p), and the Frobenius φ is induced by the cyclotomic structure on TC. The crystalline comparison for non-commutative prismatic cohomology identifies Δ𝒞/△ ⊗△ Acrys with the p-adic completion of the negative cyclic homology HC−(𝒞), and the Hodge–Tate comparison identifies Δ𝒞/△ ⊗△ 𝒪C with the periodic cyclic homology HP(𝒞) ⊗ 𝒪C. The étale comparison is formulated using the p-adic étale cohomology of the associated non-commutative motive, and the syntomic complex is defined as the fiber of (1 − φ) on the Nygaard filtration. This framework provides a p-adic Hodge theory for categories of matrix factorizations, Fukaya categories, and categories of coherent sheaves on derived stacks, and it is expected to play a role in the p-adic Langlands correspondence for non-commutative automorphic forms. The integral structure is controlled by the Breuil–Kisin twist on the prismatic side, which corresponds to the Tate twist on the Galois side, and the almost purity theorem for the v-topology ensures that the relevant cohomology groups are almost finitely generated over the base prism.

Prismatic Cohomology and the p-adic Hodge Theory for Varieties with Ordinary Reduction

Let K be a finite extension of ℚp with ring of integers 𝒪K and perfect residue field k. A smooth proper 𝒪K-scheme 𝔛 is said to have ordinary reduction if its special fiber Xk is ordinary in the sense of Bloch–Kato: the Hodge–de Rham spectral sequence degenerates at E1 and the Frobenius on H•crys(Xk/W(k)) is bijective on the slope‑zero part. In the prismatic framework, ordinarity is reflected by the existence of a splitting of the Nygaard filtration on the prismatic cohomology Δ𝔛/△ after tensoring with the Breuil–Kisin prism △ = W(k)[[u]] with φ(u) = up and distinguished element ξ = [p] − u. Concretely, for each i the map φ: N≥iΔ𝔛/△ → Δ𝔛/△ becomes an isomorphism after inverting ξ, and the φ-invariant submodule Δ𝔛/△φ=1 is expected to be a finite free ℤp-module that recovers the étale cohomology H•ét(XK̄, ℤp) via the étale comparison. The crystalline comparison identifies Δ𝔛/△ ⊗△ Acrys with the p-adic completion of the crystalline cohomology of Xk, and the Hodge–Tate comparison yields an isomorphism Δ𝔛/△ ⊗△ 𝒪C ≅ ⊕i H•−i(Xk, Ωi) ⊗k 𝒪C(−i) that respects the Hodge filtration. The Breuil–Kisin twist {1} on the prismatic side corresponds to the Tate twist ℤp(1) on the Galois side, and the almost purity theorem for the v-topology ensures that the relevant cohomology groups are almost finitely generated over the base prism.

Prismatic Cohomology and the p-adic Hodge Theory for Varieties with Supersingular Reduction

A smooth proper 𝒪K-scheme 𝔛 has supersingular reduction if its special fiber Xk is supersingular: for an abelian variety this means the Newton polygon coincides with the Hodge polygon and all slopes are 1/2; for a K3 surface it means the formal Brauer group has infinite height. In the prismatic setting, supersingularity manifests as the failure of the Nygaard filtration to split and the non-degeneration of the Hodge–Tate spectral sequence at E2. The prismatic cohomology Δ𝔛/△ still satisfies the crystalline and étale comparisons, but the Frobenius φ on Δ𝔛/△ ⊗△ Acrys is no longer bijective on the slope‑zero part; instead, the φ-module structure encodes the full Dieudonné module of the special fiber. The prismatic F‑crystal attached to 𝔛 is a φ-module over the prismatic site with a Nygaard filtration whose graded pieces are the Hodge cohomology groups of Xk. The supersingular condition implies the φ-action on the top graded piece is divisible by p, which is the prismatic reflection of the vanishing of the Hasse–Witt matrix. The Hodge–Tate comparison still holds, but the spectral sequence E2i,j = Hi(Xk, Ωj) ⇒ Hi+j(Δ𝔛/△ ⊗△ 𝒪C) has non‑trivial differentials that are controlled by the p-curvature of the Gauss–Manin connection. The prismatic approach to the p-adic Simpson correspondence relates the prismatic F‑crystal to a Higgs bundle on the special fiber, and the supersingular locus corresponds to the nilpotent cone. The integral structure is again governed by the Breuil–Kisin twist, but the almost purity theorem only gives almost finite presentation; the almost zero ideal is essential to control the torsion in the cohomology groups.

Examiner's trap: Ordinary vs. Supersingular Prismatic Cohomology
A question asks: "Let 𝔛 be a smooth proper formal scheme over 𝒪_K with special fiber X_k. Suppose the Hodge–Tate spectral sequence for Δ𝔛/△ degenerates at E2 and the Nygaard filtration splits integrally. Does it follow that X_k is ordinary?"
Yes, in the established framework. The degeneration of the Hodge–Tate spectral sequence at E2 and the integral splitting of the Nygaard filtration are equivalent to the condition that the Frobenius on the prismatic cohomology is bijective on the slope‑zero part after inverting the distinguished element. By the crystalline comparison, this matches the Bloch–Kato definition of ordinary reduction. Conversely, if X_k is ordinary, the prismatic cohomology exhibits these properties. A common error is to confuse the almost splitting (which holds for all smooth proper 𝔛) with integral splitting; the latter characterises ordinarity.
Exam‑day strategy
When a problem mentions "ordinary reduction", immediately recall the splitting of the Nygaard filtration and the degeneration of the Hodge–Tate spectral sequence at E2. For "supersingular reduction", recall the non‑splitting, the non‑degeneration, and the role of the Dieudonné module. The prismatic F‑crystal formalism unifies both: the Newton polygon of the φ‑action on the prismatic cohomology recovers the Newton polygon of the special fiber. Memorise the dictionary: ordinary ⇔ φ bijective on slope‑zero part ⇔ Nygaard splits integrally ⇔ Hodge–Tate degenerates at E2; supersingular ⇔ φ divisible by p on top graded piece ⇔ Nygaard does not split ⇔ Hodge–Tate has non‑trivial differentials.
