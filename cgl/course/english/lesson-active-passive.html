Active-Passive & Direct-Indirect

Transformation of sentences between active/passive voice and direct/indirect speech.
Active to Passive · Direct to Indirect · Tense Change Rules

Learning Objectives

  • Convert active voice to passive and vice versa
  • Change direct speech to indirect speech
  • Apply tense change rules correctly

Key Concepts

What are Active-Passive & Direct-Indirect Speech?

Active-Passive Voice involves converting sentences between active voice (subject performs the action) and passive voice (subject receives the action). Direct-Indirect Speech (also called Narration) involves converting direct quotations into reported speech and vice versa. Both are regularly tested in SSC CGL Tier 2 English.

Key Concepts

1. Active ↔ Passive Voice: Active: Subject + Verb + Object. Passive: Object + be-verb (is/am/are/was/were/been/being) + Past Participle + by + Subject. Only transitive verbs (verbs that take an object) can be converted to passive. The tense of the auxiliary verb must match the original tense.

2. Conversion Rules — Active to Passive: Present Simple (write → is/are written), Past Simple (wrote → was/were written), Future Simple (will write → will be written), Present Continuous (is writing → is being written), Present Perfect (has written → has been written), Modals (can write → can be written).

3. Direct ↔ Indirect Speech: Direct: Quoting exact words (He said, 'I am tired'). Indirect: Reporting without quotes (He said that he was tired). Changes: Tense backshift (present → past, past → past perfect), pronoun changes (I → he/she, we → they), time/place changes (now → then, here → there, today → that day, tomorrow → the next day).

4. Interrogative & Imperative in Indirect: Yes/No questions use 'if/whether'. WH-questions retain the WH-word. Imperatives use 'to + verb' (He said, 'Sit down' → He told me to sit down). Exclamatory sentences use 'exclaimed with joy/sorrow'.

Solved Examples

Example 1 (Active to Passive)
Convert to passive voice:

The chef is preparing a delicious meal.

a) A delicious meal is being prepared by the chef
b) A delicious meal was prepared by the chef
c) A delicious meal is prepared by the chef
d) A delicious meal is being preparing by the chef
Solution: a) 'A delicious meal is being prepared by the chef.' Present Continuous Active → Present Continuous Passive (is/am/are + being + past participle).
Example 2 (Direct to Indirect)
Convert to indirect speech:

She said to me, 'I will call you tomorrow.'

a) She told me that she will call me tomorrow
b) She told me that she would call me the next day
c) She said to me that she will call me tomorrow
d) She told me that she would call me tomorrow
Solution: b) 'She told me that she would call me the next day.' 'Said to' → 'told'. 'Will' → 'would' (backshift). 'Tomorrow' → 'the next day'. Pronoun 'you' → 'me' (from the perspective of the reporter).

Shortcut Techniques

  • For Passive Voice, remember 'be + V3': The passive always has some form of 'be' + past participle. Identify the object first, make it the subject, then find the correct 'be' form based on tense.
  • For Indirect Speech, the golden rule is 'one step back' in tense: Present → Past, Past → Past Perfect, Future → Conditional (will → would, can → could, may → might).
  • For modals in passive: modal + be + V3 (can be done, should be done, must be done).
Pro Tip: The most commonly tested conversions are Present Simple ↔ Passive and Direct Speech with 'said to' → Indirect. Practice the special cases — universal truths (tense does NOT change in indirect: 'The Earth revolves around the Sun'), imperative sentences, and questions. Notice the 'said to' vs 'told' distinction.
