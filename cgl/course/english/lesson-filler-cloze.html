1. Grammar-based Fillers

Grammar is the bedrock of filler questions. In SSC CGL, roughly 40% of filler questions test grammatical concepts. Always determine the part of speech needed for the blank before considering meaning. This alone eliminates 2�3 wrong options instantly.

Articles

Articles (a, an, the) are among the most frequently tested grammar items in SSC CGL fillers. Errors commonly arise with words that begin with a vowel letter but a consonant sound, and vice versa.

Definite vs Indefinite Articles

Use a before consonant sounds, an before vowel sounds. Use the for specific, previously mentioned, or unique references. Superlatives, ordinal numbers, and nouns made definite by context always take the.

Example 1 (Article � Vowel Sound)
Fill in the blank:

She is _____ honest woman.

a) a   b) an   c) the   d) no article
Solution: b) an. Although 'honest' begins with the consonant letter 'h', the 'h' is silent � the word begins with a vowel sound /ɒ/. Hence 'an' is correct.
Example 2 (Article � Superlative)
Fill in the blank:

Mount Everest is _____ highest peak in the world.

a) a   b) an   c) the   d) no article
Solution: c) the. Superlative adjectives ('highest') always take the definite article 'the'.

SSC CGL Tip � Article Exceptions
SSC CGL frequently tests article exceptions. Memorise: 'an honest', 'an hour', 'an MBA', 'a university', 'a European', 'a one-sided view'. Also note that 'the' is used before names of rivers, oceans, mountain ranges, and newspapers.

Omission of Articles

Articles are omitted before proper nouns (names of people, countries, cities), uncountable nouns used in a general sense, plural countable nouns used generally, and in certain fixed expressions. SSC CGL tests omission in phrases like 'by bus', 'at night', 'in bed', 'go to school', 'at home'.

Example � Omission of Article
Fill in the blank (or choose 'no article'):

He goes to _____ school by _____ bus every day.

a) the / the   b) a / a   c) no article / no article   d) the / a
Solution: c) no article / no article. 'Go to school' (for education � not the building) and 'by bus' (mode of transport) are fixed expressions where articles are omitted.
SSC CGL Tip � When to Omit 'The'
Do NOT use 'the' before: (a) names of individual mountains (Mount Everest), (b) names of individual islands (Sri Lanka), (c) names of languages (English, Hindi), (d) names of sports (cricket, football), (e) names of meals (breakfast, lunch, dinner) unless specified.

Prepositions

Preposition-based fillers test your knowledge of relationships between words � time, place, direction, and manner. SSC CGL often pairs prepositions with commonly confused options (e.g., in/at/on, since/for, between/among).

Prepositions of Time and Place

In � months, years, cities, countries; At � specific times, points, addresses; On � days, dates, surfaces. Since marks a starting point; For marks a duration.

Example 3 (Preposition � Time)
Fill in the blank:

He has been working here _____ 2018.

a) since   b) for   c) from   d) in
Solution: a) since. '2018' is a specific starting point in the past, used with the present perfect continuous ('has been working'). 'For' would require a duration ('for five years').
SSC CGL Tip � Phrasal Prepositions
SSC CGL fillers often test phrasal prepositions: 'in spite of', 'because of', 'owing to', 'due to', 'in addition to', 'with regard to'. Learn these as fixed units � they are not interchangeable in formal writing.

Prepositions of Direction and Manner

Direction prepositions (to, towards, into, onto, across, through, along, around) indicate movement. Manner prepositions (with, by, like, as) describe how something is done. SSC CGL frequently tests 'by' for method ('by train'), 'with' for tools ('with a knife'), and 'like' for similarity ('like a professional').

Example � Direction Preposition
Fill in the blank:

The cat jumped _____ the table from the floor.

a) on   b) onto   c) into   d) at
Solution: b) onto. 'Onto' indicates movement from one position to a surface. 'On' would be used for a static position. 'Into' is for entering a space; 'at' is for a point.
Example � Manner Preposition
Fill in the blank:

She solved the problem _____ great skill.

a) by   b) with   c) from   d) through
Solution: b) with. 'With' is used to indicate the means or instrument used. 'By' would refer to the method/agent ('by using skill'). 'With great skill' is the standard collocation.

Conjunctions

Conjunctions connect words, phrases, or clauses. SSC CGL filler questions test both coordinating and subordinating conjunctions, with special emphasis on correlative pairs.

Coordinating Conjunctions

FANBOYS � For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So. These connect elements of equal grammatical rank.

Subordinating Conjunctions

Although, because, since, unless, if, when, while, whereas, though, even though. These introduce dependent clauses.

Correlative Conjunctions

Either...or, Neither...nor, Not only...but also, Both...and, Whether...or. These always appear in pairs.

Example 4 (Correlative Conjunction)
Fill in the blank:

_____ the manager _____ the staff were informed about the new policy.

a) Either / or   b) Neither / nor   c) Both / and   d) Not only / but also
Solution: c) Both / and. The verb 'were' (plural) requires a plural subject. 'Both...and' takes a plural verb. 'Either...or' and 'Neither...nor' take a verb that agrees with the nearer subject.

Example � Subordinating Conjunction
Fill in the blank:

_____ the economy is improving, unemployment remains high.

a) Although   b) Because   c) Unless   d) Since
Solution: a) Although. The sentence shows contrast � improvement in the economy vs. continued high unemployment. 'Although' introduces a contrasting clause. 'Because' and 'since' would indicate cause; 'unless' indicates condition.

Verbs & Tenses

Verb-form fillers test subject-verb agreement, tense consistency, and conditional structures. These are high-weightage topics in SSC CGL English.

Subject-Verb Agreement

Singular subjects take singular verbs; plural subjects take plural verbs. Watch for intervening prepositional phrases, collective nouns, and indefinite pronouns.

Tense Consistency

Unless a time shift is indicated, maintain the same tense throughout a sentence or passage. In conditional sentences, follow the standard patterns (Type 0/1/2/3).

Example 5 (Subject-Verb Agreement)
Fill in the blank:

The team of scientists _____ conducting experiments right now.

a) is   b) are   c) were   d) have been
Solution: a) is. The subject is 'team' (singular collective noun), not 'scientists'. The verb agrees with the head noun. 'Is conducting' is the correct present continuous form.
Example 6 (Tense Consistency � Conditional)
Fill in the blank:

If she _____ harder, she would have passed the exam.

a) studied   b) had studied   c) studies   d) has studied
Solution: b) had studied. This is a Type 3 conditional (past unreal). The structure is: If + past perfect, would have + past participle.
SSC CGL Tip � Subject-Verb Agreement Shortcuts
Key S-V agreement rules tested in SSC CGL: (1) 'Each/every/either/neither' take singular verbs. (2) With 'either...or/neither...nor', the verb agrees with the nearer subject. (3) 'Mathematics, news, physics, politics' are singular despite ending in 's'. (4) 'The number of' is singular; 'A number of' is plural.

Active vs Passive Voice in Fillers

SSC CGL fillers frequently test whether the blank requires an active or passive verb form. The clue is whether the subject performs the action (active) or receives the action (passive). Look for the auxiliary verb � 'is/are/was/were/has been/have been' + past participle signals passive voice.

Example � Active vs Passive
Fill in the blank:

The new bridge _____ last week by the Chief Minister.

a) inaugurates   b) inaugurated   c) was inaugurated   d) has inaugurated
Solution: c) was inaugurated. The bridge receives the action (is inaugurated), and 'last week' indicates past tense. 'Was inaugurated' is the correct passive form. Options (a) and (d) are active; (b) lacks an auxiliary verb.

When a sentence has multiple clauses, the tense in each clause must follow logical sequence rules. The table below shows the most common tense sequence patterns tested in SSC CGL.

Fill in the blank:

I will call you as soon as I _____ home.

a) will reach   b) reach   c) reached   d) have reached
Solution: b) reach. In time clauses with 'as soon as', 'when', 'after', 'before', 'until', use the present tense for future meaning. 'Will reach' is incorrect in this structure.

2. Vocabulary-based Fillers

Vocabulary fillers test your knowledge of word meanings, collocations, and fixed expressions. Unlike grammar fillers, these require familiarity with standard English usage rather than rules.

Contextual Word Choice

The correct word must not only fit grammatically but also match the tone, register, and meaning of the sentence. SSC CGL options often include synonyms where only one fits the specific context.

Example 7 (Contextual Word Choice)
Fill in the blank:

The government has _____ new measures to control inflation.

a) announced   b) declared   c) promulgated   d) pronounced
Solution: a) announced. While all four words relate to making something known, 'announced' collocates best with 'measures' in this administrative context. 'Promulgated' is too formal for routine policy; 'declared' suggests a more dramatic statement; 'pronounced' is usually used for words or opinions.
SSC CGL Tip � Word Choice Strategy
When stuck between two synonyms, ask: (1) Which word is more formal/less formal? Match the passage tone. (2) Which word commonly appears with the surrounding words? Use collocation knowledge. (3) Is the word positive, negative, or neutral? Match the sentence sentiment.

Collocations

Collocations are word pairs that naturally occur together in English. Native speakers use them instinctively; non-native speakers must memorise them. SSC CGL routinely tests adjective-noun and verb-noun collocations.

Example 8 (Collocation � Verb + Noun)
Fill in the blank:

The committee decided to _____ a proposal for the new project.

a) do   b) make   c) take   d) put forward
Solution: d) put forward. 'Put forward a proposal' is the standard collocation. 'Make a proposal' is also possible but less common in formal contexts. 'Do a proposal' and 'take a proposal' are incorrect collocations.

Fixed Expressions

Fixed expressions (also called idiomatic phrases) are multi-word units whose meaning cannot be deduced from individual words. SSC CGL tests these both in fillers and in cloze tests.

Example 9 (Fixed Expression)
Fill in the blank:

The manager said that the new policy would be implemented _____ .

a) in the long run   b) by and large   c) at the earliest   d) for good measure
Solution: c) at the earliest. 'At the earliest' means as soon as possible, fitting the implementation context. 'In the long run' refers to long-term; 'by and large' means generally; 'for good measure' means as an extra.
SSC CGL Tip � High-Frequency Fixed Expressions
Memorise these SSC CGL favourites: 'in the wake of', 'on the verge of', 'with a view to', 'in lieu of', 'by virtue of', 'in accordance with', 'on account of', 'in the event of', 'as per', 'in regard to'. These appear in nearly every exam.

Word Replacement / Phrase Replacement

A variation of fillers tested in SSC CGL is word replacement � where a word or phrase in a sentence is underlined and you must choose the best replacement. This combines filler skills with error detection. The same grammar and vocabulary principles apply, but you first identify what is wrong with the underlined portion.

Example � Word Replacement
Which of the following should replace the underlined part to make the sentence correct?

The manager along with his team were present at the meeting.

a) along with his team was   b) and his team was   c) along with his team were   d) and his team were
Solution: a) along with his team was. The subject is 'The manager' (singular). 'Along with his team' is a prepositional phrase that does not change the number of the subject. Therefore, 'was' (singular) is correct. Option (b) 'and his team' would make the subject plural, requiring 'were'.
SSC CGL Tip � Word Replacement Strategy
For word replacement questions: (1) Identify the error in the underlined part � is it grammar, vocabulary, or collocation? (2) Mentally correct the error before looking at options. (3) Match your correction with the options. (4) If multiple options seem correct, re-read the entire sentence with each option to check overall coherence.

Double Fillers � Advanced Strategy

Double filler questions (two blanks in a single sentence) are a staple of SSC CGL Tier 2. The key is to handle each blank independently while ensuring both words fit together coherently.

The Elimination Grid Method

Draw a mental 2x2 grid. Check blank 1 against all four options � mark which options work for blank 1 (usually 2 out of 4). Then check blank 2 independently. The correct answer is the option where BOTH blanks fit. This method prevents you from being misled by an option that works for one blank but fails the other.

Example � Double Fillers Strategy
Fill in the blanks:

The government _____ a new policy to _____ economic inequality.

a) implemented, reduce   b) revoked, increase   c) proposed, ignore   d) criticised, highlight
Solution: a) implemented, reduce. Blank 1: 'implemented' and 'proposed' could work with 'policy'. Blank 2: 'reduce' fits the goal of addressing inequality. Only option (a) fits both blanks. 'Revoked' (cancelled) and 'criticised' don't match the positive intent signalled by 'a new policy'.
SSC CGL Tip � Double Fillers Shortcut
In double fillers, always check the EASIER blank first. If blank 1 has obvious correct/incorrect options, use it to eliminate choices. Then verify the remaining options with blank 2. This rarely fails and saves significant time.

3. Cloze Test Strategy

The cloze test is a passage (usually 150�300 words from a newspaper editorial or informative article) with blanks at regular intervals � typically every 5th to 7th word. SSC CGL Tier 1 includes 1�2 cloze passages with 5�10 blanks total.

Approach & Method

A systematic approach is essential for cloze tests. Rushing to fill blanks individually leads to errors because context from later sentences is missed.

Step 1 � Read the Passage First

Read the entire passage without filling any blanks. This gives you the theme, tone, and logical flow. Most cloze passages are argumentative or informative � understanding the author's stance is critical.

Step 2 � Determine Part of Speech

For each blank, identify what part of speech is needed (noun, verb, adjective, adverb, preposition, conjunction). This immediately eliminates options that are grammatically wrong.

Step 3 � Eliminate Strategically

Cross out options that are clearly wrong first. Often you can eliminate 2�3 options per blank just from grammar or basic meaning, leaving 1�2 choices. Then use context to select the best fit.

Step 4 � Verify Consistency

After filling all blanks, re-read the passage with your chosen words to verify that the passage is coherent, grammatically sound, and consistent in tone and tense.

SSC CGL Tip � The POS Method
Use the POS (Part of Speech) Method for cloze tests: before looking at options, write down what part of speech should go in each blank. A blank after 'the' is likely a noun; after 'has' could be a past participle; before a noun could be an adjective. This simple technique boosts accuracy by 30%.
SSC CGL Tip � Cloze Test Time Management
Allocate 6�8 minutes per cloze passage in Tier 1. Spend 1 minute reading, 4�5 minutes solving blanks, and 1 minute re-reading. If stuck on a blank, mark your best guess and move on � don't spend more than 1 minute on a single blank.

Grammar Clues

Grammar clues are the most reliable guide in cloze tests. Articles, prepositions, auxiliary verbs, and conjunctions around a blank give strong signals about the required word.

Example 10 (Cloze � Grammar Clue)
Passage: 'The government has _____ a new education policy that aims to improve literacy rates. The policy was _____ after extensive consultations with stakeholders.'

Blank 1: a) launch   b) launched   c) launching   d) launches
Blank 2: a) draft   b) drafted   c) drafting   d) drafts
Solution: Blank 1 � b) launched ('has' + past participle). Blank 2 � b) drafted ('was' + past participle, passive voice). Grammar clues: auxiliary 'has' requires a past participle; auxiliary 'was' in passive requires a past participle.

In SSC CGL cloze tests, 'however', 'therefore', 'furthermore', 'nevertheless', and 'consequently' account for over 60% of transition-word blanks. If you see a blank between two contrasting sentences, 'however' or 'nevertheless' is the likely answer.

Context Clues

Context clues come from surrounding words, sentences, and the overall passage theme. The tone (positive/negative), the time frame (past/present/future), and logical relationships all provide context.

Example 11 (Cloze � Context Clue)
Passage: 'The _____ of the ancient civilisation was discovered by archaeologists last year. The site contained well-preserved artefacts that _____ light on daily life thousands of years ago.'

Blank 1: a) remains   b) disappearance   c) construction   d) destruction
Blank 2: a) shed   b) blocked   c) absorbed   d) reflected
Solution: Blank 1 � a) remains. The phrase 'discovered by archaeologists' points to physical remnants, not disappearance or construction. Blank 2 � a) shed. 'Shed light on' is a fixed expression meaning to clarify or reveal information.
Example 12 (Cloze � Tone Context)
Passage: 'The government's handling of the crisis was widely _____. Critics called it a complete failure that _____ public trust.'

Blank 1: a) praised   b) criticised   c) ignored   d) celebrated
Blank 2: a) restored   b) strengthened   c) eroded   d) built
Solution: Blank 1 � b) criticised. The word 'failure' and 'critics' indicate a negative tone. Blank 2 � c) eroded. 'Eroded public trust' matches the negative context. Positive words like 'restored', 'strengthened', 'built' contradict the critical tone.

Transition Words

Transition words signal the logical relationship between sentences. In cloze tests, a blank between two sentences almost always tests knowledge of transition words. Recognising the relationship (contrast, cause-effect, addition, sequence) is the key.

SSC CGL Tip � Transition Word Shortcut
Draw arrows in the margin to map the passage logic. An upward arrow (↑) means addition/support (furthermore, moreover). A right arrow (→) means cause-effect (therefore, consequently). A left-right arrow (↔) means contrast (however, on the other hand). Match the arrow to the correct transition word.

Cloze Test Sample Walkthrough

Let us walk through a typical SSC CGL cloze question step by step to demonstrate the full strategy in action.

Walkthrough � Step-by-Step Cloze Solution
Passage: 'Water scarcity is a (1) _____ problem in many parts of India. The government has (2) _____ various schemes to address this issue. (3) _____, the success of these initiatives depends on community participation.'

Step 1 (Read): The passage is about water scarcity and government schemes. Tone: serious/concerned.
Step 2 (POS): Blank 1 needs an adjective (describing 'problem'). Blank 2 needs a past participle (after 'has'). Blank 3 needs a transition word (between two sentences with logical connection).
Step 3 (Eliminate):
1. a) growing   b) declining   c) solved   d) minor
- 'Declining problem' contradicts the serious tone. 'Solved problem' is illogical (if solved, no schemes needed). 'Minor' contradicts 'scarcity'. Correct: a) growing.
2. a) launches   b) launched   c) launching   d) launch
- 'Has' + past participle. Only 'launched' works.
3. a) However   b) Therefore   c) Furthermore   d) Meanwhile
- The second sentence adds a condition ('depends on community participation'), contrasting with the idea that schemes alone suffice. 'However' fits best.
Step 4 (Verify): 'Water scarcity is a growing problem... The government has launched various schemes... However, the success depends on community participation.' The passage is coherent and logical.
Final Answers: 1 � a) growing, 2 � b) launched, 3 � a) However

Vocabulary Clues in Cloze Tests

Vocabulary clues involve recognising word families (root words, prefixes, suffixes), synonyms and antonyms, and word form (noun, verb, adjective, adverb). SSC CGL cloze passages are often adapted from editorials that use sophisticated vocabulary. Understanding word roots can help decode unfamiliar words.

Using Word Roots and Affixes

Prefixes like 'un-', 'dis-', 'mis-', 'pre-', 'post-' change the meaning of base words. Suffixes like '-tion', '-ment', '-ity' form nouns; '-ate', '-ify', '-ise' form verbs; '-ous', '-al', '-ive' form adjectives. When you encounter an unfamiliar word in options, break it into prefix + root + suffix to guess its meaning.

Example � Vocabulary Clue (Word Root)
Passage: 'The new regulations were designed to _____ the process of obtaining permits.'

a) complicate   b) simplify   c) amplify   d) replicate
Solution: b) simplify. The context ('designed to' + 'process of obtaining permits') suggests making it easier, not harder. 'Simplify' = make simple. 'Complicate' would have the opposite meaning. 'Amplify' = increase volume; 'replicate' = copy.
SSC CGL Tip � Common Prefixes in Cloze
Memorise these high-frequency prefixes: 'pre-' (before � preview, predict), 'post-' (after � postpone, post-war), 'anti-' (against � anti-government), 'pro-' (in favour � pro-active), 'mis-' (wrongly � misunderstand), 'over-' (too much � overestimate), 'under-' (too little � underestimate). These appear in cloze options regularly.

SSC CGL Tier 1 vs Tier 2 � Strategy Differences

Tier 1 (Preliminary)

Tier 1 has 25 English questions in 15 minutes. Typically 3�4 filler questions and 1 cloze test with 5 blanks. Focus on grammar fillers (articles, prepositions, subject-verb agreement) as they are quick and reliable. For cloze, use the POS method aggressively � Tier 1 options are designed to test basic grammar more than vocabulary.

Tier 2 (Mains)

Tier 2 has more time but higher difficulty. Expect 5�8 filler questions (including double fillers and word replacement) and 2 cloze passages with 5�10 blanks each. Vocabulary, collocations, and advanced grammar are tested more heavily. Tier 2 cloze passages are longer (250�350 words) with more sophisticated vocabulary from economic and political editorials.

SSC CGL Tip � Tier-Specific Preparation
For Tier 1: Master articles, prepositions, subject-verb agreement, and basic tense consistency. Practice 3 cloze tests per week. For Tier 2: Expand to advanced collocations, fixed expressions, double fillers, and word replacement. Practice 2 cloze tests per day from editorial sources. Tier 2 vocabulary requirements are significantly higher.
SSC CGL Tip � Last-Minute Revision Checklist
One week before the exam, revise these 10 areas: (1) Article exceptions (an honest, a university). (2) Preposition pairs (since/for, between/among). (3) Correlative conjunctions (neither...nor, both...and). (4) Collective noun verb agreement. (5) Type 2 and 3 conditionals. (6) Ten high-frequency collocations (draw a conclusion, make an effort). (7) Ten fixed expressions (in accordance with, on account of). (8) Five transition words (however, therefore, furthermore, nevertheless, consequently). (9) Common prefixes (pre-, post-, anti-, pro-). (10) The four-step cloze method.

Previous Year SSC CGL Filler Questions � Solved

Here are filler questions from recent SSC CGL exams with detailed solutions to help you understand the exam pattern.

SSC CGL 2022 � Single Filler
Fill in the blank:

The government's decision to _____ the subsidy has been widely criticised.

a) withdraw   b) withdrawn   c) withdrawing   d) withdrawal
Solution: a) withdraw. The infinitive 'to' + base form of verb ('withdraw') is required. 'To withdraw' is the correct infinitive form. 'Withdrawn' is past participle; 'withdrawing' is gerund; 'withdrawal' is a noun.
SSC CGL 2021 � Double Fillers
Fill in the blanks:

The committee _____ the proposal and decided to _____ it immediately.

a) approved, implement   b) rejected, postpone   c) reviewed, ignore   d) criticised, withdraw
Solution: a) approved, implement. The second clause 'decided to _____ it immediately' suggests positive action. 'Approved' and 'implement' form a logical sequence. The other options suggest delay or rejection, which don't match 'immediately'.
SSC CGL 2022 � Preposition Filler
Fill in the blank:

He was accused _____ stealing company funds.

a) for   b) of   c) with   d) about
Solution: b) of. 'Accused of' is the correct collocation. 'Accused for' and 'accused with' are incorrect. 'Accused about' is also incorrect. This is a fixed verb-preposition combination.
SSC CGL 2021 � Article Filler
Fill in the blank (or choose 'no article'):

He is _____ honour to the profession.

a) a   b) an   c) the   d) no article
Solution: b) an. 'Honour' begins with a vowel sound (silent 'h' � /ɒnə/). Therefore, 'an honour' is correct. Note: this is the same pattern as 'an honest' � the 'h' is silent.
SSC CGL 2023 � Cloze Style Filler
Fill in the blank:

The company's profits have been _____ declining over the past five quarters, prompting concerns among investors.

a) steadily   b) rapidly   c) occasionally   d) abruptly
Solution: a) steadily. 'Over the past five quarters' suggests a continuous trend over time. 'Steadily declining' means consistently going down. 'Rapidly' and 'abruptly' suggest sudden change; 'occasionally' suggests intermittent change � neither fits the continuous implication of 'over five quarters'.

Recommended Resources for Further Practice

To further strengthen your filler and cloze test skills, use these resources: (1) Previous year SSC CGL question papers � analyse the pattern of filler questions asked in the last 5 years. (2) Editorial sections of The Hindu and Indian Express � read daily and try to predict missing words. (3) Standard grammar books like Wren & Martin for rules and SP Bakshi for objective English practice. (4) Online mock tests � take at least one full-length English section test every week to build speed and accuracy.

Key Takeaways

  • For single fillers, predict your own word before looking at options � this prevents you from being misled
  • For double fillers, check the easier blank first and use it to eliminate options
  • For cloze tests, always read the entire passage before attempting any blank � context is king
  • Use the POS method: identify the part of speech needed for each blank to eliminate grammatically wrong options
  • Memorise high-frequency collocations, fixed expressions, and transition words � they are the most tested items
  • Maintain an error log of every wrong answer � categorise errors and focus revision on weak areas
  • Practice 3 cloze tests and 10 filler questions daily in the month before the exam
Final SSC CGL Tip � Exam Day Strategy
On exam day, attempt filler questions first (they are quick), then cloze passages. For cloze, mark your best guess for any blank you are unsure about and move on � do not waste time on a single blank. If you have time at the end, revisit uncertain blanks with a fresh perspective. Most SSC CGL toppers attempt the English section in 12�13 minutes, leaving 2�3 minutes for review.
