1. Synonyms & Antonyms

Synonyms are words that share similar meanings (e.g., abandon / forsake, brief / concise). Antonyms are words with opposite meanings (e.g., abundant / scarce, benevolent / malevolent). In SSC CGL Tier 1 and Tier 2 exams, direct synonym/antonym questions appear frequently. Building a strong repository of word pairs is essential for scoring full marks in this section.

Strategies for SSC CGL

To master synonyms and antonyms for SSC CGL, follow a systematic approach. Learn words in thematic groups, use root-word analysis, and practise with actual exam questions from previous years. Focus on words that appear frequently in SSC CGL papers rather than obscure vocabulary.

Tip 1: Root Word Method
Learn common Latin and Greek roots. Example: 'Bene' (good) → benefit, benevolent, benefactor, benediction. 'Mal' (bad) → malicious, malevolent, malpractice, malfunction. 'Omni' (all) → omnipotent, omniscient, omnipresent, omnivorous. This helps you deduce meanings of unfamiliar words on the exam.
Tip 2: Mnemonic Association
Create vivid mental images. For 'Ephemeral' (short-lived), think: "An EPHant lived for just a day — ephemeral!" For 'Gregarious' (sociable), imagine a "GREG" who loves parties. Silly associations stick better than rote memorisation.
Tip 3: Weekly Revision Schedule
Divide 200 words into 10 groups of 20. Learn one group daily Mon-Fri. Revise all 100 on Saturday. Do 20 new + 80 mixed on Sunday. Use a notebook or an app like Anki for spaced repetition. By week 10, you will have covered 200+ words with solid retention.

Context-based Questions

In SSC CGL, synonym and antonym questions often appear in context. You are given a sentence with an underlined word and asked to choose the closest meaning or opposite. Understanding context is key because many words have multiple meanings.

Example 1 (Synonym in Context) — SSC CGL 2022
Select the most appropriate synonym of the underlined word:

"The judge was known for his equitable decisions that treated all parties fairly."

a) Partial   b) Unbiased   c) Strict   d) Lenient
Solution: b) Unbiased. 'Equitable' means fair and impartial, which is synonymous with 'unbiased'. 'Partial' is the opposite (antonym), while strict and lenient refer to severity, not fairness.
Example 2 (Antonym in Context) — SSC CGL 2021
Select the most appropriate antonym of the underlined word:

"His arrogant behaviour made him unpopular among colleagues."

a) Proud   b) Confident   c) Humble   d) Bold
Solution: c) Humble. 'Arrogant' means showing an exaggerated sense of self-importance. The opposite is 'humble' (modest). 'Proud' is similar (not opposite), and 'confident/bold' are neutral qualities.
Example 3 (Choosing Correct Synonym) — SSC CGL 2023
Select the word that is closest in meaning to 'Prolific':

a) Rare   b) Productive   c) Wasteful   d) Slow
Solution: b) Productive. 'Prolific' means producing much fruit or foliage or many offspring; highly productive. A prolific writer publishes many books; a prolific artist creates many works.

Example 4 (One-Word Substitution) — SSC CGL 2020
Select the most appropriate one-word substitution for the given group of words:

"A person who is unable to pay his debts"

a) Insolvent   b) Bankrupt   c) Indigent   d) Destitute
Solution: a) Insolvent. 'Insolvent' specifically refers to inability to pay debts. 'Bankrupt' is a legal status. 'Indigent' and 'Destitute' refer to extreme poverty, not specifically inability to repay debts.
Example 5 (One-Word Substitution) — SSC CGL 2021
"A place where money is coined" — select the correct one-word:

a) Mint   b) Treasury   c) Vault   d) Bank
Solution: a) Mint. A mint is specifically a place where coins of currency are officially manufactured under government authority. A treasury stores money, a vault stores valuables, and a bank facilitates financial transactions.

SSC CGL Pattern Questions

Below are idiom and phrasal verb questions framed in the exact SSC CGL pattern. These include meaning-based, context-based, and substitution-type questions commonly seen in actual exams.

Example 6 (Idiom Meaning) — SSC CGL 2022
Select the correct meaning of the idiom 'To let the cat out of the bag':

a) To adopt a pet   b) To reveal a secret unintentionally   c) To catch a thief   d) To buy something in a bag
Solution: b) To reveal a secret unintentionally. The idiom originates from an old market trick where sellers would put a cat in a bag pretending it was a piglet. If the cat was let out, the trick was exposed.
Example 7 (Phrasal Verb in Context) — SSC CGL 2021
Select the most appropriate phrasal verb to fill the blank:

"The committee decided to _____ the proposal until further review."

a) put off   b) put on   c) put out   d) put by
Solution: a) put off. 'Put off' means to postpone. The sentence indicates the proposal was delayed. 'Put on' means to wear, 'put out' means to extinguish, and 'put by' means to save for later.
Example 8 (Idiom Substitution) — SSC CGL 2023
Select the option that can substitute the underlined idiom in the sentence:

"The manager hit the nail on the head when he identified the real problem."

a) Made a mistake   b) Was exactly right   c) Hit someone   d) Worked hard
Solution: b) Was exactly right. 'Hit the nail on the head' means to be exactly correct about something. The context confirms the manager was accurate in identifying the problem.
Example 9 (Idiom in Context) — SSC CGL 2020
Choose the correct meaning of the underlined idiom:

"She was green with envy when she saw her friend's new car."

a) Feeling sick   b) Very jealous   c) Extremely happy   d) Very angry
Solution: b) Very jealous. 'Green with envy' means extremely jealous or envious of someone else's possessions, achievements, or advantages. The colour green is traditionally associated with envy.

Example 12 (Word Root Analysis) — SSC CGL 2022
The word 'Omniscient' means:

a) All-powerful   b) All-knowing   c) All-present   d) All-loving
Solution: b) All-knowing. Break it down: Omni (all) + Scient (knowing) = all-knowing. 'Omnipotent' is all-powerful, 'Omnipresent' is all-present. Learning the 'Omni' prefix helps you distinguish these three commonly tested words in SSC CGL.
Example 13 (Prefix Analysis) — SSC CGL 2021
What is the meaning of 'Subterranean'?

a) Above ground   b) Underwater   c) Underground   d) Outer space
Solution: c) Underground. Sub (under) + Terra (earth/land) + Anean (relating to) = relating to under the earth = underground. 'Submarine' (under water), 'Subway' (underway), and 'Subterranean' (underground) all share the 'Sub-' prefix meaning 'under'.

6. Contextual Vocabulary

Contextual vocabulary questions test your ability to understand the meaning of a word based on the surrounding text. In SSC CGL, these typically appear in Reading Comprehension and Cloze Test sections. Instead of memorising isolated word lists, you must learn to infer meaning from sentence context.

Strategies for Contextual Vocabulary

When you encounter an unfamiliar word in the SSC CGL exam, use these context clue strategies:

  • Definition Clues: The author directly defines the word. Look for phrases like 'which means', 'that is', 'i.e.', 'namely'. Example: "The doctor was known for his equanimity, which means mental calmness in difficult situations."
  • Synonym Clues: A nearby word with similar meaning. Example: "The arduous journey was difficult and exhausting."
  • Antonym Clues: A nearby word with opposite meaning, often introduced by 'but', 'however', 'instead'. Example: "Unlike her gregarious brother, she was shy and reserved."
  • Example Clues: Examples that illustrate the word. Example: "He collected ephemera: old tickets, postcards, and newspaper clippings."
  • Cause-and-Effect Clues: The relationship explains the word. Example: "The drought caused desiccation of the soil, leaving deep cracks everywhere."
Contextual Strategy: The Surrounding Sentence
When you see a difficult vocabulary word in RC or Cloze, do not panic. Read the sentence before and after the word. Often, the context provides enough hints to deduce the meaning. Look for cause-effect relationships, contrasts (but, however, although), and examples (such as, for example, like). Practice this skill daily with newspaper editorials.
Daily Practice: Read The Hindu Editorial
Reading the editorial page of The Hindu newspaper daily is one of the most effective ways to build contextual vocabulary for SSC CGL. Underline 5–10 unfamiliar words each day, guess their meaning from context, then look them up. Maintain a vocabulary journal with word, meaning, and the sentence in which you found it. Revise every weekend.
Example 14 (Contextual Vocabulary) — SSC CGL 2023
Select the most appropriate meaning of the word 'Laconic' based on the context:

"The manager was known for his laconic style of communication, often replying in just one or two words."

a) Verbose   b) Brief   c) Aggressive   d) Persuasive
Solution: b) Brief. The context says 'replying in just one or two words', which indicates brevity. 'Laconic' means using very few words, concise. 'Verbose' is the opposite (using too many words).
Example 15 (Contextual Antonym) — SSC CGL 2022
Based on the context, select the most appropriate antonym of 'Obstinate':

"The obstinate child refused to change his mind despite his parents' persuasion, while his sister was quite flexible."

a) Stubborn   b) Flexible   c) Rigid   d) Determined
Solution: b) Flexible. The context sets up a contrast: obstinate child vs flexible sister. 'Obstinate' means stubborn or rigid. The antonym in context is 'flexible'. 'Stubborn', 'Rigid', and 'Determined' are synonyms, not antonyms.
Example 16 (Contextual Clue) — SSC CGL 2021
The word 'Ambivalence' most nearly means:

"He felt ambivalence about the job offer; on one hand it paid well, but on the other hand it required relocating."

a) Enthusiasm   b) Mixed feelings   c) Certainty   d) Indifference
Solution: b) Mixed feelings. The context shows opposing thoughts (good pay vs relocation), indicating conflicted or mixed feelings. 'Ambivalence' means having contradictory attitudes or feelings about something. 'Indifference' means not caring, which is different from having mixed feelings.

Memory Trick for Homophones
Create simple mnemonics. For Stationary vs Stationery: 'Stationary' has an 'a' like 'stand still', while 'Stationery' has an 'e' like 'envelope' and 'letter'.
