1. Reading Strategy & Approach

SSC CGL Tier 2 English includes 2-3 unseen passages of 300-500 words each from diverse domains � science, social issues, history, economics, literature, and philosophy. Each passage carries 4-6 questions. A strategic reading approach can save 3-5 minutes per passage and improve accuracy by 20-30%.

Skimming vs Scanning

Skimming is rapid reading to grasp the gist. Read the first and last sentences of each paragraph, noting transition words (however, therefore, moreover). Spend 30-45 seconds maximum. Scanning is hunting for specific information � numbers, names, dates, keywords. Use scanning only after you have read the questions.

How to Skim Effectively

Focus on the topic sentence (usually the first sentence) of each paragraph. Identify keywords like "causes", "effects", "however", "therefore", "in contrast". Ignore examples and detailed data � return to them only if a question requires it. For SSC CGL, practise on 5-6 passages before the exam to build the skimming habit.

Scanning Techniques

When you need to find a specific detail � a year, a name, a percentage � use the keyword method. Remember the key term from the question, then run your eyes quickly through the passage looking only for that term. Once found, read the surrounding 1-2 sentences carefully. Do not re-read the entire passage.

SSC CGL Tip
In the exam, first skim the passage in 30 seconds. Then read all 4-6 questions. Then re-read the passage deeply, underlining relevant lines for each question. This two-pass method prevents re-reading and saves 2-3 minutes.

Time Management

Allocate 6-8 minutes per passage-question set. Break it down: 30 sec skim, 45 sec read questions, 2 min deep read with marking, 3 min answer with elimination. If a question takes more than 90 seconds, mark it and move on � return in the last 2 minutes if time permits.

Phase Duration Activity
Skim 30 sec Read first & last sentences of each paragraph
Scan Questions 45 sec Read all questions to identify what to look for
Deep Read 2 min Read passage thoroughly, mark relevant sentences
Answer 3 min Solve each question using POE (Process of Elimination)

2. Question Types

Every RC question in SSC CGL belongs to one of six categories. Each requires a distinct approach. The table below summarises them.

Question Type Approach Frequency in SSC CGL
Main Idea / Theme Summarise the passage in one sentence. The correct option covers the whole passage, not just one paragraph. Very High (1-2 per passage)
Inference / Implied Meaning Find what the author suggests but does not state outright. Look for clues like "implies", "suggests", "can be concluded". Very High (1-2 per passage)
Tone & Attitude Identify the author's word choice (adjectives, verbs). Positive? Negative? Neutral? Moderate (0-1 per passage)
Vocabulary in Context Replace the word with each option. The one that fits the context without changing meaning is correct. Moderate (0-1 per passage)
Specific Detail Locate the exact sentence in the passage. The answer is explicitly stated. High (1-2 per passage)
Title Selection The best title captures the central idea. It should be specific but not too narrow. Low (0-1 per passage)

Main Idea / Central Theme

The main idea is the central point the author is trying to communicate. It is the one idea that ties the entire passage together. To find it, ask: "What is the author's primary message?" and "What is the one thing all paragraphs support?"

How to Identify the Main Idea

First, read the entire passage and summarise it in one sentence in your own words. Then evaluate each option: does it cover ALL paragraphs, or only one part? The correct answer must encompass the entire passage. Beware of options that repeat exact phrases from the text � they are often supporting details, not the main idea.

SSC CGL Traps to Avoid

SSC frequently uses trap options that are factually true but too narrow (covering only one paragraph) or too broad (a generic statement that could apply to any passage). The correct main idea is specific enough to be about this passage but broad enough to cover all parts of it. Practice identifying the difference between "a point made in the passage" and "the point of the passage."

Quick Trick
Read the first and last sentences of the passage � they often state the main idea directly. For SSC CGL, the correct answer is neither too specific (a supporting detail) nor too broad (a generic statement).
Example 1 � Main Idea
Passage: "The gig economy has expanded rapidly in India over the past decade. Platforms like Swiggy, Zomato, and Uber have created millions of flexible work opportunities. However, concerns about job security, lack of benefits, and low wages persist. While the government has introduced the Social Security Code to address these issues, implementation remains uneven across states."

What is the main idea of the passage?
a) The gig economy provides flexible work opportunities in India
b) The Social Security Code has been implemented across all states
c) The gig economy in India has grown but faces challenges related to worker welfare and uneven regulation
d) Swiggy and Zomato are the largest gig economy platforms
Solution: c) The passage covers both the growth of the gig economy AND the challenges (job security, low wages, uneven implementation). Option a is too narrow (only the positive side). Option b is factually wrong (implementation is uneven). Option d is a minor detail, not the main idea.

Inference & Implied Meaning

Inference questions ask you to read between the lines. The answer is not explicitly stated but is logically suggested by the text. These are the most common question type in SSC CGL Tier 2. Look for words like: implies, suggests, can be inferred, indicates, most likely.

Example 2 � Inference
Passage: "India's renewable energy capacity has grown by 175% in the last seven years. Solar power contributes the largest share, followed by wind. Despite this growth, coal still accounts for over 70% of the country's electricity generation. The transition away from coal faces hurdles including grid stability and employment in coal-mining regions."

What can be inferred about India's energy sector?
a) India has completely phased out coal-based power
b) Solar power is cheaper than coal power
c) The shift from coal to renewables is ongoing but faces significant obstacles
d) Wind energy contributes more than solar energy
Solution: c) The passage indicates growth in renewables but notes that coal remains dominant and that the transition "faces hurdles." This implies an ongoing, incomplete transition with obstacles. Options a, b, and d are not supported by the passage.

Tone & Attitude

Tone reflects the author's attitude toward the subject. Key indicators: adjectives (troubling, promising), verbs (celebrates, condemns), and overall structure. Memorise the three categories and their clue words.

Tone Category Clue Words SSC CGL Examples
Positive appreciative, optimistic, laudatory, hopeful "remarkable progress", "commendable effort"
Negative critical, pessimistic, sarcastic, alarmed "deeply concerning", "woefully inadequate"
Neutral analytical, objective, informative, explanatory "according to data", "studies indicate"
Elimination Strategy
For tone questions, first eliminate options that are clearly wrong based on the passage's overall valence. If the passage is factual and data-driven, eliminate all positive and negative tone words � correct will be analytical or objective. This narrows 4 options to 2 instantly.
Example 3 � Tone
Passage: "The alarming rate of air pollution in Delhi NCR has reached a critical point. Despite numerous court orders and government schemes, AQI levels continue to breach severe categories every winter. The failure to enforce stubble-burning bans and the unregulated growth of vehicular emissions paint a grim picture of urban environmental governance."

The tone of the passage is:
a) Appreciative & hopeful
b) Critical & concerned
c) Neutral & informative
d) Sarcastic & mocking
Solution: b) Words like "alarming", "critical point", "failure", "grim picture" indicate a critical and concerned tone. The author is not neutral (c) � they are clearly expressing concern. Option a is opposite. Option d might be tempting but there is no mockery in the language.

Vocabulary in Context

These questions test your ability to deduce word meanings from context. Even if you do not know the word, the surrounding sentences provide clues about whether the word is positive/negative, its intensity, and its domain.

Context Clue Types
(1) Definition clue � the passage defines the word nearby. (2) Synonym clue � a word with similar meaning appears in the same sentence. (3) Antonym clue � a contrasting word like "but" or "however" signals the opposite. (4) Example clue � examples illustrate the word's meaning.
Example 4 � Vocabulary in Context
Passage: "The professor was known for his pedantic style of teaching. He would spend entire lectures correcting minor grammatical errors in student papers rather than focusing on the broader concepts of literary analysis."

The word "pedantic" most nearly means:
a) Inspiring & motivational
b) Overly concerned with trivial details
c) Quick & efficient
d) Relaxed & informal
Solution: b) The context explains that he focused on "minor grammatical errors" instead of "broader concepts." This suggests an excessive focus on small, unimportant details. Option a is opposite. Options c and d do not fit the critical description.

Specific Detail Questions

These are the easiest RC questions � the answer is directly stated in the passage. Do not overthink. Locate the relevant sentence and match it to the option. Beware of trap options that use the same vocabulary but change the meaning slightly.

Identifying the Relevant Sentence

Use the keywords from the question to scan the passage. If the question mentions a specific year, person, or concept, find that exact term first. Read the sentence containing that term and the sentence immediately before and after it. The answer is almost always within these three sentences.

Paraphrase Traps

SSC CGL often uses paraphrases to disguise the correct answer � the same idea expressed in different words. At the same time, wrong options may use exact words from the passage but in a different context or with altered meaning. Compare the meaning, not just the vocabulary.

Example 5 � Specific Detail
Passage: "The National Education Policy 2020 recommends a 5+3+3+4 curricular structure, replacing the old 10+2 system. It emphasises foundational literacy and numeracy as an urgent national priority. The policy also mandates that instruction be in the mother tongue until at least Grade 5."

According to the passage, what does NEP 2020 recommend for instruction medium?
a) English until Grade 5
b) Mother tongue until at least Grade 5
c) Hindi for all subjects
d) Bilingual instruction from Grade 1
Solution: b) The passage directly states: "The policy also mandates that instruction be in the mother tongue until at least Grade 5." Options a, c, and d are not mentioned.

Title Selection

A good title is both specific (mentions the topic) and comprehensive (covers the main point). It should be neither too narrow (a single detail) nor too broad (something that could apply to many passages).

Example 6 � Title Selection
Passage: "India's startup ecosystem has witnessed unprecedented growth with over 100 unicorns emerging in the last decade. The government's Startup India initiative, improved access to venture capital, and a large domestic market have been key drivers. However, challenges around regulatory compliance, funding in later stages, and talent retention persist. The next phase of growth will depend on how effectively these bottlenecks are addressed."

The most appropriate title for this passage is:
a) India's Unicorn Companies
b) Growth and Challenges of India's Startup Ecosystem
c) The Startup India Initiative
d) Venture Capital in India
Solution: b) The passage covers both the growth factors AND the challenges. Option a is too narrow (only unicorns). Option c is only one of the drivers. Option d is mentioned but not the main focus.

3. Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

Even well-prepared candidates lose marks in RC due to avoidable errors. Recognising these common pitfalls is the first step to eliminating them. SSC CGL examiners deliberately set traps based on these mistakes � knowing them gives you a significant advantage.

Relying on Outside Knowledge

The most common mistake is answering based on what you already know rather than what is stated in the passage. SSC CGL passages often take positions that may differ from conventional wisdom. Always verify each option against the passage text. If an option sounds correct based on your knowledge but is not mentioned in the passage, it is wrong.

Choosing Extreme Options

Options containing words like "always", "never", "completely", "all", "none", "every", "must", or "impossible" are rarely correct in RC questions. Authors typically use qualifiers like "often", "usually", "some", "may", "might", "can", or "many". Extreme language is difficult to defend in a passage, so SSC CGL examiners use it mostly in wrong options.

Overlooking Qualifying Words

Pay close attention to words like "however", "but", "yet", "although", "despite", "nevertheless". These words signal a shift in the author's argument. A question that asks about the author's view may be answered differently before and after such a transition word. Always read the full context around qualifying words.

Misinterpreting Inference Questions

Many candidates treat inference questions as "guess what the author is thinking" � this leads to wrong answers. Inference must be logically derived from the text, not imagined. The correct inference is the option that MUST be true if the passage is true, not the option that COULD be true. Practise this distinction: "must be true" vs "could be true".

Must Be True vs Could Be True
Example: Passage says "Ravi scored 95% in his exam." A "must be true" inference is "Ravi passed the exam" (if passing is below 95%). A "could be true" but wrong inference would be "Ravi is the top scorer" (we don't know other scores). In SSC CGL, only "must be true" inferences are correct.

Spending Too Much Time on One Question

Each RC question should take about 45-60 seconds. If you spend more than 90 seconds on a single question, mark your best guess and move on. SSC CGL passages have 4-6 questions each. Spending 3 minutes on one question means losing time for 2-3 easier questions later. Use the 90-second rule rigorously.

Time-Saving Elimination
When stuck between two options, compare them directly. Ask: "Which one is directly supported by the passage?" and "Which one contains words or ideas not present in the text?" The option that has direct textual support is correct. The one that introduces new information is wrong. This comparison takes 10 seconds and resolves most ambiguities.

Vocabulary Traps

For vocabulary questions, never choose the most common meaning of a word. SSC CGL tests words in specific contexts where the meaning may be different from everyday usage. For example, "novel" in a passage about scientific research means "new and original", not "a book". Similarly, "table" as a verb means "to present for discussion", not a piece of furniture. Always read the surrounding context before choosing.

Common Vocabulary Traps in SSC CGL
'Qualified' can mean 'limited/modified' (not just 'certified'). 'Given' can mean 'assuming/presuming' (not just 'provided'). 'Exceptional' can mean 'unusual/uncommon' (not just 'excellent'). 'Critical' can mean 'crucial' or 'severe' (not just 'fault-finding'). Always let the passage context decide the meaning, not your prior knowledge of the word.

Common RC Question Stems

Familiarity with question stems helps you instantly identify what type of answer is required. SSC CGL uses standardised phrasing for each question type. Recognising the stem type tells you exactly where to look in the passage and what kind of answer to expect.

Question Stem Type Where to Look
"What is the main idea / central theme?" Main Idea Entire passage, intro & conclusion
"What can be inferred / concluded?" Inference Implied by the text, not directly stated
"The tone of the passage is..." Tone Adjectives, verbs, overall language
"The word 'X' most nearly means..." Vocabulary Sentence containing the word + adjacent sentences
"According to the passage / Which is true?" Detail Exact sentence in the passage
"The most suitable title is..." Title Main idea + scope
Example � Avoiding Outside Knowledge Trap
Passage: "Some historians argue that the Industrial Revolution led to an overall improvement in living standards by the mid-19th century, as real wages finally began to rise after decades of stagnation. However, this improvement was uneven, with significant disparities between skilled and unskilled workers."

Question: What does the passage say about living standards during the Industrial Revolution?

Trap approach: Using outside knowledge � "The Industrial Revolution caused terrible working conditions and child labour" (this is true historically but NOT what the passage says).

Correct approach: The passage says living standards "improved... by the mid-19th century" but the "improvement was uneven."
Lesson: Always answer from the passage, not from your own knowledge of history. SSC CGL deliberately chooses passages that may present an unconventional perspective. What you know is irrelevant � what the passage says is everything.

4. Cloze Test

Cloze Test � also called "Fill in the Blanks in a Passage" � appears in SSC CGL Tier 1 and Tier 2. A passage of 150-250 words has 4-6 blanks. You must choose the best word for each blank from four options. Success depends on a combination of grammar knowledge, vocabulary, and understanding the flow of ideas.

Grammar-based Filler Strategy

First, identify the grammatical requirement of the blank. Is it a noun, verb, adjective, preposition, conjunction, or article? This alone eliminates 1-2 options. Key rules: subject-verb agreement, tense consistency, preposition collocations, and article usage.

Grammar-First Approach
Always check the words immediately before and after the blank. "The ____ of" requires a noun. "Has been ____" requires a past participle (V3) or present participle (V-ing). "More ____ than" requires an adjective. SSC CGL cloze tests frequently test these patterns.
Example 7 � Cloze Test (Grammar-based)
Passage: "The recent floods in the region have caused widespread ____ (1). Thousands of people have been ____ (2) to safer locations. The government has announced a compensation ____ (3) of Rs 10,000 per family."

Blank 1: a) destructive   b) destruction   c) destruct   d) destructively
Blank 2: a) evacuate   b) evacuated   c) evacuating   d) evacuates
Blank 3: a) pack   b) package   c) packed   d) packing
Solution: 1-b (destruction � noun needed after "widespread"), 2-b (evacuated � passive voice "have been + V3"), 3-b (package � "compensation package" is a standard collocation). Grammar rules narrow the options before considering meaning.

Vocabulary-based Filler Strategy

When grammar is not enough, use context to determine the meaning. Read the sentence before and after the blank. Identify whether the blank requires a positive or negative word. Look for transition words (however, moreover, consequently) to understand the logical flow.

Context Clue Categories

Definition clues: The passage defines the word immediately. Look for phrases like "i.e.", "that is", "refers to". Synonym clues: A word with similar meaning appears nearby, often connected by "and" or "as well as". Antonym clues: A contrasting word appears, connected by "but", "however", "yet", or "although". Cause-effect clues: Words like "therefore", "consequently", "because", "leads to" link the blank to a cause or effect.

Elimination by Tone

Read the passage first to determine its overall tone. If the tone is pessimistic, the blank is unlikely to take a positive word. If the passage discusses a problem, the blank for a proposed solution word should be positive. The tone of the surrounding sentences is one of the most reliable context clues for vocabulary-based fillers.

Example 8 � Cloze Test (Vocabulary-based)
Passage: "The new policy was met with widespread ____ from environmental groups, who argued that it would lead to further deforestation. The government, ____, maintained that economic development could not be compromised."

Blank 1: a) approval   b) criticism   c) indifference   d) celebration
Blank 2: a) therefore   b) however   c) moreover   d) consequently
Solution: 1-b (criticism � environmental groups opposing deforestation would criticise the policy, not approve or celebrate it), 2-b (however � the government's position contrasts with the groups' criticism; "however" indicates contrast). Vocabulary + logical flow give the answer.

Common Patterns

SSC CGL cloze tests follow predictable patterns. Mastering these patterns can boost your speed significantly.

Pattern Example Tip
Preposition after verb consist of, rely on, differ from Memorise common collocations
Conjunction logic ___ (cause), therefore (effect) Identify cause-effect vs contrast vs addition
Adjective-Noun pair utmost importance, grave concern Some adjectives are always used with specific nouns
Phrasal verbs carry out, put off, turn down Context determines the correct particle
SSC CGL Cloze Test Strategy
Read the entire passage first (ignore the blanks) to understand the overall meaning. Then go back and fill each blank. This ensures your choices are consistent with the passage's theme and flow. Never choose an option based on the blank's immediate context alone.

5. Practice Passages with Solutions

Apply the strategies above to these SSC CGL-level passages. Each passage is followed by 3-4 questions. Try to solve them within 6-8 minutes before checking the solutions.

Practice Passage 1 � Science & Technology
Passage: "Artificial Intelligence (AI) is transforming the healthcare sector in unprecedented ways. From diagnostic algorithms that detect cancer with greater accuracy than human radiologists to predictive models that forecast disease outbreaks, AI applications are expanding rapidly. However, concerns about data privacy, algorithmic bias, and the displacement of healthcare workers remain significant. Critics argue that AI systems trained on biased data can perpetuate existing inequalities in healthcare access. Proponents counter that with proper regulation, AI can democratise healthcare by making diagnostic expertise available in remote and underserved areas. The key lies in striking a balance between innovation and ethical safeguards."

Q1: What is the central theme of the passage?
a) AI is replacing human doctors
b) AI in healthcare offers benefits but also raises ethical concerns that need balanced regulation
c) AI algorithms are always more accurate than radiologists
d) Healthcare workers will lose their jobs due to AI

Q2: The word "perpetuate" in the passage most nearly means:
a) Eliminate   b) Continue or prolong   c) Reduce   d) Celebrate

Q3: What is the author's tone in this passage?
a) Strongly critical of AI   b) Enthusiastically optimistic   c) Balanced and analytical   d) Sarcastic
Solutions:
Q1: b) The passage covers both the transformative potential AND the concerns, concluding with the need for balance. Options a, c, and d are too narrow or extreme.
Q2: b) "Perpetuate existing inequalities" means to cause them to continue. Context: biased data leads to continued inequality.
Q3: c) The author presents both sides (critics AND proponents) and concludes with a balanced view. The tone is analytical, not emotional.
Practice Passage 2 � Social Issues
Passage: "India's female labour force participation rate (FLFPR) has been declining despite rapid economic growth. According to World Bank data, FLFPR fell from 30% in 1990 to approximately 20% in 2022. Sociologists point to several factors: increasing enrolment of women in higher education (delaying entry into the workforce), lack of safe and affordable transportation, social norms that prioritise household responsibilities, and a shortage of formal-sector jobs that offer flexible working conditions. Addressing this issue requires a multi-pronged approach involving educational reforms, infrastructural improvements, and changes in workplace policies."

Q1: Which of the following is NOT mentioned as a reason for declining FLFPR?
a) Lack of safe transportation   b) Social norms about household roles   c) Gender pay gap   d) Enrolment in higher education

Q2: What can be inferred about the relationship between economic growth and FLFPR in India?
a) Economic growth automatically increases FLFPR
b) Economic growth alone does not guarantee higher female workforce participation
c) FLFPR has increased with economic growth
d) Economic growth has no effect on FLFPR
Solutions:
Q1: c) The passage mentions higher education enrolment, transportation issues, social norms, and lack of flexible jobs. The "gender pay gap" is not listed as a reason.
Q2: b) The passage starts by noting that FLFPR has declined "despite rapid economic growth," implying that growth alone does not solve this issue. Options a and c contradict the passage. Option d is too absolute.
Practice Passage 3 � Cloze Test
Passage: "The concept of sustainable development has gained ____ (1) importance in recent decades. It refers to meeting the needs of the present ____ (2) compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs. While the idea is widely ____ (3), its implementation remains challenging."

Q1: a) little   b) tremendous   c) negligible   d) decreasing
Q2: a) without   b) with   c) while   d) despite
Q3: a) rejected   b) accepted   c) ignored   d) criticised
Solutions:
Q1: b) "Gained ____ importance" in recent decades � the context suggests increasing, not decreasing, importance. "Tremendous" fits.
Q2: a) The definition of sustainable development: meeting present needs "without" compromising future generations. This is the standard definition.
Q3: b) "Widely ____ but implementation is challenging" � contrast structure. If implementation is challenging, the idea must be widely "accepted" (not rejected/ignored, as that would not create a meaningful contrast).
Practice Passage 4 � Economics
Passage: "Monetary policy in India is primarily aimed at maintaining price stability while keeping in mind the objective of growth. The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) uses tools like repo rate, reverse repo rate, and cash reserve ratio (CRR) to control inflation. In 2023, the RBI raised the repo rate multiple times to curb inflationary pressures arising from global supply chain disruptions and domestic demand. Higher interest rates make borrowing more expensive, which can slow down economic activity but also helps in stabilising prices. The challenge for the RBI lies in calibrating these measures so that inflation is controlled without stifling growth."

Q1: What is the primary objective of monetary policy in India?
a) Maximising government revenue
b) Maintaining price stability while considering growth
c) Reducing all interest rates to zero
d) Eliminating inflation completely

Q2: What effect do higher interest rates have according to the passage?
a) They make borrowing cheaper
b) They increase economic activity
c) They make borrowing more expensive and can slow economic activity while stabilising prices
d) They have no effect on inflation

Q3: The word "calibrating" in the passage most nearly means:
a) Ignoring   b) Carefully adjusting   c) Abandoning   d) Accelerating
Solutions:
Q1: b) The first sentence states: "primarily aimed at maintaining price stability while keeping in mind the objective of growth."
Q2: c) The passage notes that higher rates "make borrowing more expensive... can slow down economic activity but also helps in stabilising prices."
Q3: b) "Calibrating" means fine-tuning or carefully adjusting. The RBI must carefully balance inflation control with growth.
Practice Passage 5 � Philosophy & Ethics
Passage: "Utilitarianism, a moral philosophy advanced by Jeremy Bentham and John Stuart Mill, holds that the best action is the one that maximises overall happiness or 'utility'. In the context of public policy, utilitarian thinking often informs cost-benefit analyses. For example, a government might decide to fund a vaccination programme that saves thousands of lives rather than a rare-disease treatment that benefits only a few, because the former produces greater total welfare. Critics, however, argue that utilitarianism can justify unjust actions if they produce a net increase in happiness, thereby overlooking individual rights and minority interests."

Q1: What is the main idea of this passage?
a) Utilitarianism is a perfect moral philosophy
b) Utilitarianism focuses on maximising overall happiness but faces criticism for potentially overlooking individual rights
c) Cost-benefit analysis is used in public policy
d) Jeremy Bentham invented utilitarianism

Q2: According to critics, what is a weakness of utilitarianism?
a) It is too expensive to implement
b) It can justify unjust actions by focusing on net happiness at the expense of minority rights
c) It never works in practice
d) It only applies to healthcare decisions
Solutions:
Q1: b) The passage explains both the core idea of utilitarianism (maximising happiness) and the criticism (overlooking individual rights). Option a is too positive, c is too narrow, d is a minor detail.
Q2: b) Critics argue that "utilitarianism can justify unjust actions if they produce a net increase in happiness, thereby overlooking individual rights and minority interests." Options a, c, and d are not mentioned.
Daily Practice Routine
Solve one RC passage and one Cloze Test passage daily for 30 days before the exam. Keep a log of your accuracy by question type. If you consistently get inference questions wrong, practise 10 more inference questions. Targeted practice yields faster improvement than random practice.
Mock Test Strategy
In the actual SSC CGL Tier 2 exam, attempt the RC section first (not last). Your mind is freshest at the start. If a passage topic is unfamiliar (e.g., philosophy), do it last � save the science/social passages that are easier to grasp. Always attempt all questions; there is no negative marking for unattempted questions.
Last-Minute Revision Checklist
Before your SSC CGL English paper, review these key points: (1) Skim first, then read questions, then deep-read. (2) For inference, choose only what MUST be true from the passage. (3) For tone, classify as positive/negative/neutral first, then narrow down. (4) For vocabulary, use context � never choose the first meaning that comes to mind. (5) Mark relevant lines in the passage for each question as you read. (6) Use POE (Process of Elimination) aggressively � eliminate 2 wrong options first, then choose between the remaining 2.
Expected Question Distribution in SSC CGL Tier 2
Based on the last 5 years of papers: Out of 10-12 RC questions per exam, expect 3-4 on main idea/inference, 2-3 on specific details, 1-2 on vocabulary, 1 on tone, 1 on title, and 2-3 on cloze test blanks. Allocate your practice time proportionally. Inference and main idea together account for nearly 50% of RC questions � focus your preparation there.
Quick Reference � RC Question Type Cheatsheet
Main Idea: Ask "What is the passage about as a whole?" → Look at first & last sentences. Eliminate options that are too narrow or too broad.

Inference: Ask "What must be true if the passage is true?" → Look for implied relationships. Distinguish from "what is stated directly".

Tone: Ask "What is the author's attitude?" → Look at adjectives and verbs. Classify: Positive / Negative / Neutral.

Vocabulary: Ask "Which meaning fits this context?" → Replace word with each option. Choose the one that makes the most sense in context.

Detail: Ask "What does the passage explicitly say?" → Find the exact sentence. Match, don't infer.

Title: Ask "What title best captures the entire passage?" → Should be specific to this passage but cover all paragraphs.
Exam Day Strategy
In the examination hall: (1) Start with the passage that has the shortest or most familiar topic. (2) For each passage, read all questions BEFORE deep reading � this focuses your attention on relevant information. (3) Mark the answer in the OMR sheet only after solving all questions of one passage (saves mental switching cost). (4) If unsure between two options, choose the one that is more conservative and uses language closer to the passage text. (5) Never leave any question unanswered � there is no negative marking for unattempted questions in Tier 2, but guessing gives you a 25% chance.
