Key Concepts

What is Reading Comprehension?

Reading Comprehension (RC) tests your ability to read a passage carefully, understand its main idea, infer meanings, and answer questions based on the text. SSC CGL Tier 2 includes 2-3 passages (typically 300-500 words each) on diverse topics — science, social issues, history, economics, literature, or philosophy. Each passage is followed by 4-6 questions.

Key Concepts

1. Types of RC Questions: Direct/Explicit Questions (fact-based — answers directly in the text), Inferential Questions (implied meaning — read between the lines), Vocabulary in Context (meaning of a word as used in the passage), Main Idea/Theme (the central point the author is making), Tone of the Passage (author's attitude — critical, analytical, neutral, appreciative, sarcastic, informative).

2. Reading Strategies: Skim the passage first (30 seconds — read first and last sentences of each paragraph). Then read the questions. Then read the passage thoroughly while marking relevant sentences. For vocabulary questions, use context clues — the surrounding words give hints about the meaning.

3. Common Pitfalls: Relying on prior knowledge (answer ONLY from the passage), choosing 'extreme' options (words like 'always', 'never', 'completely' are rarely correct), overlooking qualifying words ('usually', 'often', 'some', 'may', 'might'), and getting stuck on difficult words.

Solved Examples

Example 1 (Main Idea)
Passage: 'Climate change poses an existential threat to low-lying island nations. Rising sea levels have already displaced communities in the Maldives and Tuvalu. While global agreements like the Paris Accord aim to limit temperature rise, critics argue that current commitments are insufficient. The urgency of the situation demands immediate and collective action beyond political rhetoric.'

What is the main idea of the passage?

a) The Maldives is sinking
b) Climate change is real
c) Climate change is a serious threat requiring urgent global action beyond existing commitments
d) The Paris Accord is a failure
Solution: c) The passage builds from threat (rising seas) to current response (Paris Accord) to the conclusion that more urgent action is needed. Options a and b are too narrow. Option d is too extreme.
Example 2 (Inference)
Passage: 'Despite decades of economic growth, income inequality in India has widened. The top 10% now hold 77% of the national wealth. While poverty has declined significantly in absolute terms, the relative gap between the rich and the poor continues to expand. Policy interventions have focused more on growth than on redistribution.'

What can be inferred about the author's view?

a) Economic growth is harmful
b) Redistribution policies should be strengthened
c) Poverty has not declined
d) The top 10% should be taxed more
Solution: b) The author notes that policy focused more on growth than redistribution and calls out the widening gap. This implies a need for stronger redistribution. Option a is extreme. Option c contradicts the passage. Option d goes beyond what is stated.

Shortcut Techniques

  • Read the first and last sentence of each paragraph first. This gives you the structure: introduction → supporting points → conclusion.
  • For 'tone' questions: Positive (appreciative, optimistic), Negative (critical, pessimistic, sarcastic), Neutral (analytical, objective, informative). Eliminate two clearly wrong options based on whether the passage leans positive/negative/neutral.
  • For vocabulary questions, even if you don't know the word, plug each option into the passage context and see which fits best grammatically and logically.
  • Time management: Spend 2-3 minutes per passage (1 min reading, 1-2 min answering). Don't re-read the whole passage for each question — mark lines in your question booklet.
Pro Tip: For SSC CGL, practice at least one passage daily. Focus on understanding the author's argument structure, not just facts. The most common question type is 'inference-based' — practice distinguishing between 'what is stated' and 'what can be inferred'.

Advanced Grammar

Conditional sentences, subject-verb agreement rules, and advanced vocabulary building techniques.

Key S-V Agreement Rules

  • Each/every/either/neither take singular verbs
  • With either-or/neither-nor, verb agrees with the nearer subject
  • Collective nouns can be singular or plural based on context
  • Mathematics, news, physics are singular despite ending in s
Pro Tip
For RC, read questions FIRST then scan passage. For error detection, read sentence from right to left.
