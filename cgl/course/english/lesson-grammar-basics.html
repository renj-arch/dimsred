1. Parts of Speech

In SSC CGL English, Parts of Speech form the foundation of every grammar question. The exam tests your ability to identify word functions in sentences, choose correct forms, and spot errors in usage. There are eight parts of speech in English.

Part of SpeechFunctionExamplesSSC CGL Importance
NounNames a person, place, thing, or ideaRam, Delhi, table, honestyHigh — Number & Gender rules
PronounReplaces a nounhe, she, it, they, whoHigh — Case & Agreement errors
VerbShows action or staterun, is, have, writeHigh — Tense & Auxiliary errors
AdjectiveDescribes a nountall, beautiful, fiveMedium — Degrees of Comparison
AdverbModifies verb/adjective/adverbquickly, very, quiteMedium — Position in sentence
PrepositionShows relationshipin, on, at, by, forHigh — Fixed collocations
ConjunctionConnects words/clausesand, but, or, becauseMedium — Paired conjunctions
InterjectionExpresses emotionoh!, alas!, hurray!Low — Rarely tested alone

Noun

Types (Proper, Common, Abstract, Collective)

Proper Noun: Names a specific entity — India, Mahatma Gandhi, The Ganges. Always capitalised.
Common Noun: General name — country, man, river.
Abstract Noun: Idea or quality — honesty, bravery, wisdom. Cannot be perceived by senses.
Collective Noun: Group as one unit — team, committee, flock, jury.

SSC CGL 2020 — Noun Type
Identify the type of the underlined noun:

"Honesty is the best policy."

(a) Proper Noun   (b) Common Noun   (c) Abstract Noun   (d) Collective Noun
Solution: (c) Abstract Noun. 'Honesty' is an idea/quality that cannot be perceived by the five senses.
SSC Trick — Noun Number
Words like scissors, trousers, spectacles, belongings, alms, annals are always plural. Use a plural verb: "These scissors are sharp." Exception: Mathematics, Physics, Economics, News, Innings are singular despite ending in 's'.

Number & Gender Rules

Number: Singular → Plural. Regular: add -s/-es (cat→cats, box→boxes). Irregular: man→men, child→children, foot→feet, mouse→mice, phenomenon→phenomena.
Gender: Masculine (actor, tiger, hero), Feminine (actress, tigress, heroine), Neuter (table, book). SSC CGL often tests pronoun agreement: "The judge delivered his verdict."

SSC Gender Trap
Words ending in -ess are feminine: actress, waitress, hostess, lioness, poetess. But author, doctor, friend, student are gender-neutral. Avoid "authoress" — use "woman author" instead. SSC CGL considers "authoress" outdated.

Pronoun

Types (Personal, Reflexive, Relative, Demonstrative)

Personal: I, we, you, he, she, it, they (subject); me, us, him, her, them (object).
Reflexive: myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, themselves.
Relative: who, whom, which, that, whose — connect clauses.
Demonstrative: this, that, these, those — point to nouns.

SSC CGL 2021 — Pronoun Case
Choose the correct option:

"Neither Rohan nor ___ will attend the meeting."

(a) I   (b) me   (c) myself   (d) mine
Solution: (a) I. After 'neither...nor', the pronoun must be in subject case because it is part of the compound subject.

Case & Agreement

Subjective Case: I, he, she, we, they, who — used as subject.
Objective Case: me, him, her, us, them, whom — used as object.
Possessive Case: my/mine, his, her/hers, our/ours, their/theirs, whose.
Agreement Rule: A pronoun must agree with its antecedent in number, gender, and person. "Each student should bring his/her textbook." (not 'their' in formal SSC English)

SSC Pronoun Trick — Who vs Whom
Who = subject case (I, he, she). Whom = object case (me, him, her). Trick: Replace 'who' with 'he' — if it fits, use WHO. Replace 'whom' with 'him' — if it fits, use WHOM. "___ did you call?" → "I called him" → "Whom did you call?"

Verb

Main vs Auxiliary

Main Verb: Carries the primary meaning — run, eat, write, think.
Auxiliary (Helping) Verb: Helps the main verb — be, have, do, will, shall, can, may, must, should, would, could, might.
Primary Auxiliaries: is, am, are, was, were, been, being; have, has, had; do, does, did.
Modal Auxiliaries: can, could, may, might, shall, should, will, would, must, ought to, need, dare, used to.

SSC CGL 2019 — Auxiliary Verb
Identify the error:

"He can speaks English fluently."

(a) can   (b) speaks   (c) English   (d) No error
Solution: (b) speaks. After modals (can, could, may, might, shall, should, will, would, must), the main verb takes base form. Correct: "He can speak English fluently."

Transitive & Intransitive

Transitive Verb: Requires a direct object — She wrote a letter.
Intransitive Verb: Does not take a direct object — She sleeps.
SSC Note: Some verbs work both ways — He runs a business. (transitive) vs He runs fast. (intransitive)

Adjective

Degrees of Comparison

Positive: tall — "He is tall."
Comparative: taller — "He is taller than me."
Superlative: tallest — "He is the tallest in the class."

Rules: 1-syllable → -er/-est. 2-syllable ending in -y → -ier/-iest. 3+ syllables → more/most. Irregular: good→better→best, bad→worse→worst, little→less→least, far→farther/further→farthest/furthest.

SSC CGL 2022 — Degree of Comparison
Fill in the blank:

"This is the ___ (beautiful) painting I have ever seen."

(a) more beautiful   (b) most beautiful   (c) beautifullest   (d) beautifully
Solution: (b) most beautiful. Three-syllable adjectives use 'most' for the superlative degree, not -est.
SSC Adjective Trap — Double Comparatives
Never use 'more' AND '-er' together. "More better" is WRONG. "More taller" is WRONG. Use either "more beautiful" OR "prettier". Also: Further (figurative/abstract) vs Farther (physical distance) — SSC CGL tests this distinction.

Adverb

Adverbs modify verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs. They answer how, when, where, how often, to what extent.
Types: Manner (quickly, slowly), Time (now, then), Place (here, there), Frequency (always, never, often), Degree (very, quite, almost).
SSC Position Rules: Adverbs of frequency come BEFORE the main verb but AFTER auxiliary verbs. "She often visits" ✓ "She is always late" ✓ "She has never been" ✓.

SSC CGL 2021 — Adverb Position
Choose the correct sentence:

(a) I have always been loyal to my country.
(b) I always have been loyal to my country.
(c) I have been always loyal to my country.
(d) I have been loyal always to my country.
Solution: (a) I have always been loyal. Adverbs of frequency are placed between the auxiliary (have) and the main verb (been).

Preposition

Prepositions show relationships of time, place, direction. SSC CGL frequently tests fixed prepositional collocations.

Conjunction

Coordinating: and, but, or, nor, for, yet, so (FANBOYS).
Subordinating: because, although, while, since, unless, if, when, after, before.
Correlative: either...or, neither...nor, both...and, not only...but also.

SSC Conjunction Rule — Parallel Structure
'Not only...but also' requires parallel structure. "He not only plays cricket but also teaches it" — both verbs in same form. "He not only plays cricket but also teaching it" — WRONG.

Interjection

Express sudden emotion: Oh!, Alas!, Hurray!, Bravo!, What!, Ah!. Rarely tested directly in SSC CGL.

2. Tenses

Tenses indicate the time of an action or state. SSC CGL heavily tests tense usage in error-detection and fill-in-the-blank questions. There are 3 main tenses (Present, Past, Future) × 4 aspects (Simple, Continuous, Perfect, Perfect Continuous) = 12 tense forms.

TenseSimpleContinuousPerfectPerfect Continuous
PresentV₁/V₁+s/es

I am writing		has/have + V₃
I have written		has/have been + V₁+ing
I have been writing
PastV₂

I was writing		had + V₃
I had written		had been + V₁+ing
I had been writing
Futurewill/shall + V₁
I will write		will be + V₁+ing
I will be writing		will have + V₃
I will have written		will have been + V₁+ing
I will have been writing

Present Tense

Simple, Continuous, Perfect, Perfect Continuous

Simple Present: Habits, universal truths, scheduled events. "The sun rises in the east." "I go to office daily." "The train leaves at 6 PM."
Present Continuous: Ongoing action at the time of speaking. "I am studying for SSC CGL."
Present Perfect: Past action with present relevance. "I have completed my syllabus."
Present Perfect Continuous: Started in past, continues to present. "I have been studying since morning."

SSC CGL 2020 — Present Tense
Fill in the blank:

"She ___ (work) in this company for five years."

(a) works   (b) is working   (c) has been working   (d) worked
Solution: (c) has been working. 'For five years' indicates duration from past to present → Present Perfect Continuous.

Past Tense

Simple Past: Completed past action. "I saw that movie yesterday."
Past Continuous: Ongoing past action. "I was sleeping when you called."
Past Perfect: Action completed before another past action. "I had finished dinner before he arrived."
Past Perfect Continuous: Duration before another past action. "I had been waiting for two hours when she came."

SSC Tense Trick — Past Perfect
Two past actions: the earlier action takes Past Perfect (had + V₃), the later action takes Simple Past.
"When we reached the station, the train had left."
Key words: before, after, when, by the time, already, just, never.
SSC CGL 2022 — Sequence of Tenses
Find the error:

"When I reached the hall, the chief guest already arrives."

(a) reached   (b) already arrives   (c) the   (d) No error
Solution: (b) already arrives. The guest's arrival happened BEFORE the reaching → Past Perfect needed: "had already arrived".

Future Tense

Simple Future: Will/Shall + V₁ — prediction, promise, spontaneous decision.
Future Continuous: Will be + V₁+ing — action in progress at a future time.
Future Perfect: Will have + V₃ — action completed before a future time.
Future Perfect Continuous: Will have been + V₁+ing — duration up to a future time.

Sequence of Tenses

Rule 1: If main clause is Present/Future, subordinate clause can be in ANY tense. "I know that he is/was/will be a doctor." (All correct)
Rule 2: If main clause is Past, subordinate clause MUST be in a Past tense. "I knew that he was a doctor." ✓ "I knew that he is a doctor." ✗ (Exception: universal truths — "The teacher said the earth is round.")

SSC CGL 2019 — Sequence of Tenses
Choose the correct option:

"My father told me that honesty ___ the best policy."

(a) is   (b) was   (c) has been   (d) will be
Solution: (a) is. Universal truth — remains in Simple Present even when the main clause is past.

Common Errors in Tenses

Error TypeIncorrectCorrectRule
Present Perfect vs Simple PastI have seen that movie yesterday.I saw that movie yesterday.Simple Past with definite past time markers
Since vs ForI am studying since 2 hours.I have been studying for 2 hours.Since + point; For + duration
Past Perfect missingWhen she came, I ate dinner.When she came, I had eaten dinner.Earlier action needs Past Perfect
Will in time clausesI will call when I will reach.I will call when I reach.Time clauses use Present tense for future
Stative + ContinuousI am knowing the answer.I know the answer.Stative verbs (know, believe, own) no -ing
Have vs Had confusionI had completed my work. (no 2nd action)I have completed my work.No second past action → Present Perfect
SSC Tense Cheat Sheet
Keywords: Every day (Simple Present), Right now (Present Continuous), Since/For (Perfect Continuous), Yesterday/Last/ago (Simple Past), Already/Just/Yet (Present Perfect), By the time/Before (Past Perfect), Tomorrow/Next (Future), By next year (Future Perfect). Memorise these — they directly signal the tense!

3. Subject-Verb Agreement

Subject-Verb Agreement is the most tested grammar topic in SSC CGL. The rule: singular subject → singular verb; plural subject → plural verb. SSC CGL tests this through tricky constructions where the true subject is hidden.

Basic Rules

  • Rule 1: Two singular subjects joined by 'and' take a plural verb. "Ram and Shyam are brothers."
  • Rule 2: Subjects joined by 'or/either...or/neither...nor' take a verb agreeing with the nearest subject. "Neither the teacher nor the students were present."
  • Rule 3: 'Each', 'Every', 'Either', 'Neither', 'Many a' are always singular. "Each of the students has passed." "Many a man has died."
  • Rule 4: Words like 'with', 'together with', 'along with', 'as well as' do NOT make a singular subject plural. "The teacher, along with the students, is going."

Special Cases

Each/Every, Either/Neither, Collective Nouns

Each/Every: Always singular. "Every boy and every girl has been given a book." ✗ "have"
Either/Neither: Singular when used alone. "Neither of the answers is correct."
Collective Nouns: Singular (unit) or plural (individuals). "The committee has decided." / "The committee have disagreed among themselves."

SSC CGL 2021 — Collective Noun
Choose the correct verb:

"The jury ___ divided in their opinions."

(a) is   (b) was   (c) were   (d) has
Solution: (c) were. 'Divided in their opinions' indicates individual disagreement, so plural verb 'were' is correct.
SSC CGL 2020 — Each/Every Error
Find the error:

"Each of the players were given a certificate."

(a) Each   (b) players   (c) were   (d) No error
Solution: (c) were. 'Each of + plural noun' is singular. Correct: "Each of the players was given a certificate."

SSC CGL Error Patterns

PatternIncorrect ExampleCorrection
Prepositional phrase confusionThe box of chocolates are on the table.The box of chocolates is on the table.
Either/Or proximityEither the students or the teacher are wrong.Either the students or the teacher is wrong.
Each/Every + plural verbEvery student have to submit.Every student has to submit.
One of + plural nounOne of the boys are missing.One of the boys is missing.
Collective noun confusionThe team were playing well (as unit).The team was playing well.
Uncountable nounsThe furniture are expensive.The furniture is expensive.
SSC Ultimate S-V Agreement Trick
To find the TRUE subject: Remove all prepositional phrases (of, in, on, at, with, along with, together with, as well as, including, accompanied by). The noun/pronoun before the verb is the TRUE subject.
"The collection of old stamps and coins is valuable." → Remove "of old stamps and coins" → "The collection is valuable."

4. Articles

A/An (indefinite) — non-specific. The (definite) — specific. SSC CGL tests articles in fill-in-the-blank, error-detection, and cloze tests.

ArticleUsageExamplesSSC Exception
ABefore consonant soundsa book, a university, a European, a one-rupee note'A' before 'university' (yu sound) — NOT 'an university'
AnBefore vowel soundsan apple, an hour, an honest man, an MBA'An' before 'hour' (silent h) — NOT 'a hour'
TheSpecific noun, unique entities, superlatives, ordinals, rivers/oceans/mountainsthe sun, the biggest, the first, the Ganges, the HimalayasNo article before proper nouns — "I visited Delhi."
∅ (No Article)Plural/generic uncountable, meals, games, languagesMilk is healthy. I play cricket. She speaks French.Fixed: by bus, at home, in bed, go to school, on foot
SSC CGL 2021 — Articles
Fill in the blank:

"She is ___ MLA from this constituency."

(a) a   (b) an   (c) the   (d) no article
Solution: (b) an. 'MLA' starts with a vowel sound (/ɛm/), so 'an' is used. Choice depends on SOUND, not spelling.
SSC CGL 2022 — Article Error
Find the error:

"He is a honest and an intelligent boy."

(a) a   (b) an   (c) and   (d) No error
Solution: (a) a. 'Honest' has a silent 'h' (vowel sound). Should be "an honest and intelligent boy."
SSC Article Shortcut — The Sound Rule
Always check SOUND, not spelling.
✗ "a hour" → "an hour" (silent h)
✗ "an university" → "a university" (yu sound)
✗ "a honest" → "an honest" (silent h)
✗ "an one-eyed man" → "a one-eyed man" (wun sound)

Fill in the blank:

"He was accused ___ theft."

(a) for   (b) of   (c) with   (d) in
Solution: (b) of. The correct collocation is 'accused of'. Similar but different: 'charged with'.

6. Clauses & Phrases

Phrase: A group of words without a subject-verb combination. "in the morning", "on the table", "having completed his work".
Clause: A group of words with a subject and a verb. "when the sun rises", "because he was ill".

Types of Clauses

Independent Clause: Can stand alone. "She sings beautifully."
Dependent Clause: Cannot stand alone. "When she sings" (needs more).

Noun Clause: Acts as a noun. "What he said is true."
Adjective Clause: Acts as an adjective. "The book that you gave me is excellent."
Adverb Clause: Acts as an adverb. "He came after the meeting ended."

SSC CGL 2021 — Clause Identification
Identify the underlined clause:

"I don't know where he lives."

(a) Noun Clause   (b) Adjective Clause   (c) Adverb Clause   (d) Independent Clause
Solution: (a) Noun Clause. The clause acts as the object of the verb 'know' — functions as a noun.
SSC Clause Trick — Who/Whom/Which/That
'Who' = people (subject). 'Whom' = people (object). 'Which' = animals/things. 'That' = both people and things in restrictive clauses.
✗ "The man which called you" → "The man who called you"
✗ "The book who I bought" → "The book that/which I bought"
✗ "This is the man whom called" → "This is the man who called"

Types of Phrases

Noun Phrase: Acting as a noun. "The tall man in the blue shirt" is my neighbour.
Verb Phrase: Main verb + auxiliaries. "She has been waiting."
Prepositional Phrase: Preposition + object. "He sat on the chair."
Infinitive Phrase: To + verb. "I want to learn English."
Gerund Phrase: V+ing as noun. "Swimming is good exercise."
Participial Phrase: V+ing/V₃ as adjective. "Walking quickly, he reached the station."

SSC Phrase vs Clause — Quick Test
Does the group contain a finite verb (shows tense)?
YES → Clause. NO → Phrase.
Example: "Walking on the road" (no finite verb) = Phrase. "While he was walking" (was = finite verb) = Clause.
