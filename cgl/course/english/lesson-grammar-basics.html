Key Concepts

What are Grammar Basics?

Grammar Basics covers the building blocks of English — Parts of Speech (Noun, Pronoun, Verb, Adjective, Adverb, Preposition, Conjunction, Interjection), Tenses (Present, Past, Future — Simple, Continuous, Perfect, Perfect Continuous), Subject-Verb Agreement rules, Articles (A, An, The), and Prepositions. Mastery of these fundamentals is essential for all SSC CGL English questions.

Key Concepts

1. Parts of Speech: Noun (naming word — Ram, table, love), Pronoun (replaces noun — he, she, it, they, who), Verb (action or state — run, is, have), Adjective (describes noun — tall, beautiful), Adverb (modifies verb/adjective — quickly, very), Preposition (shows relation — in, on, at, by, for), Conjunction (connects — and, but, or), Interjection (expresses emotion — oh!, alas!).

2. Tenses: Present Simple (I write), Present Continuous (I am writing), Present Perfect (I have written), Present Perfect Continuous (I have been writing). Similarly for Past and Future. The key is understanding the time reference and the aspect (completed/ongoing).

3. Subject-Verb Agreement: A singular subject takes a singular verb (He runs). A plural subject takes a plural verb (They run). Special cases: 'Either/or' and 'Neither/nor' take the verb that matches the nearest subject. 'Each'/'Every'/'Neither of'/'Either of' take singular verbs. Collective nouns (team, committee) take singular verbs when acting as one unit.

Solved Examples

Example 1 (Subject-Verb Agreement)
Choose the correct option:

Neither the teacher nor the students ___ present in the class.

a) is b) was c) were d) has
Solution: c) were. With 'Neither...nor', the verb agrees with the subject closest to it. Here 'students' (plural) is closer, so the plural verb 'were' is correct.
Example 2 (Tense)
Fill in the blank:

She ___ (study) for three hours before her friends arrived.

a) studied b) has studied c) had been studying d) was studying
Solution: c) had been studying. The action (studying) was ongoing for a duration (three hours) before another past event (arrival). This requires Past Perfect Continuous Tense.

Shortcut Techniques

  • For Subject-Verb agreement, always identify the true subject by removing prepositional phrases. Example: 'The box of chocolates (is/are) on the table' — 'box' is the subject, so 'is' is correct.
  • Tense shortcut: 'Since/For' with present time → Present Perfect Continuous. 'Since' + point in time (since 2000), 'For' + duration (for 5 years).
  • For articles: Use 'A' before consonant sounds (a university — 'yu' sound is consonant), 'An' before vowel sounds (an hour — silent 'h' makes it start with vowel sound).
Pro Tip: Subject-Verb Agreement and Tenses account for the majority of grammar errors in SSC CGL. Practice identifying the subject in complex sentences. The 'spot the error' questions almost always test S-V agreement, tense sequence, or preposition usage.

Advanced Grammar

Conditional sentences, subject-verb agreement rules, and advanced vocabulary building techniques.

Key S-V Agreement Rules

  • Each/every/either/neither take singular verbs
  • With either-or/neither-nor, verb agrees with the nearer subject
  • Collective nouns can be singular or plural based on context
  • Mathematics, news, physics are singular despite ending in s
