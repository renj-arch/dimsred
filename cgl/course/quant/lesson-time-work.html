Key Concepts

Time and work problems form a crucial part of the SSC CGL Quantitative Aptitude section. These problems revolve around the fundamental relationship between the number of workers, the time they take, and the amount of work they complete. Mastery of this topic requires a solid grasp of work efficiency, the unitary method, and the ability to apply LCM-based shortcuts for rapid calculation.

18 Essential Tips for Time & Work

Tip 1: Basic Work Formula
Work (W) = Rate (R) × Time (T). If a person completes a job in n days, their 1-day work = 1/n. Always express work in terms of "work done per unit time" to simplify comparisons.
Tip 2: LCM Method for Work Efficiency
Take LCM of the time taken by each worker to find the total work units. If A takes 12 days and B takes 18 days, LCM(12,18) = 36 units. Then A's efficiency = 36/12 = 3 units/day, B's efficiency = 36/18 = 2 units/day. This eliminates fractions entirely.
Tip 3: Work Done by A+B+C Together
When multiple workers work together, add their individual rates. Combined 1-day work = 1/A + 1/B + 1/C. Total time = 1 / (1/A + 1/B + 1/C). Using LCM: combined efficiency = sum of individual efficiencies, total time = total work / combined efficiency.
Tip 4: Pipes & Cisterns � Filling (Inlet)
An inlet pipe fills a tank and does positive work. If a pipe fills a tank in h hours, its fill rate = 1/h per hour. Multiple inlets: combined fill rate = sum of individual fill rates.
Tip 5: Pipes & Cisterns � Emptying (Outlet)
An outlet (drain) pipe empties a tank and does negative work. If a pipe empties a tank in h hours, its drain rate = -1/h per hour. Net rate = sum of filling rates + sum of emptying rates (with negative sign).
Tip 6: Alternate Day Work
When workers work on alternate days, compute the work done in a 2-day or N-day cycle. For A (10 days) and B (15 days) alternating: 2-day work = 1/10 + 1/15 = 1/6. Then find how many full cycles fit, and handle the remaining fraction.
Tip 7: Work with Leaves / Absences
If a worker takes leaves or joins late, subtract the idle days. For example, if A works for 4 days and leaves, find work done by A in 4 days, then the remaining work is done by B alone. Always track "work completed" and "work remaining" separately.
Tip 8: Efficiency Ratio Method
If A is k times as efficient as B, then time taken by A : time taken by B = 1 : k (inverse ratio). For "A is 50% more efficient than B": efficiency ratio A:B = 150:100 = 3:2, so time ratio A:B = 2:3.
Tip 9: Work & Wage Distribution
Wages are distributed in proportion to the work done (efficiency × days worked). If A, B, C work together, their wage ratio = (efficiency of A × days A worked) : (efficiency of B × days B worked) : (efficiency of C × days C worked).
Tip 10: Group Work (Men, Women, Children)
Convert all workers to a common reference (e.g., express women and children in terms of men). If 2 men = 3 women = 4 children in efficiency, find the ratio and solve using unitary method or LCM.
Tip 11: Man-Days Concept
Total work = Number of men × Number of days (when all men work at same rate). If 15 men complete a work in 8 days, total work = 15 × 8 = 120 man-days. This is constant regardless of how the work is split.
Tip 12: SSC CGL Shortcut � LCM = Total Work Units
For ANY time & work problem, immediately take LCM of all individual times. Use that as total work. Then individual efficiencies become nice integers. This converts fraction-based problems into simple integer arithmetic � the single most powerful shortcut for SSC CGL.
Tip 13: Efficiency = Total Work / Time
Once you fix total work (using LCM), efficiency is simply total work divided by individual time. A person who takes more days has lower efficiency. Combined efficiency = sum of individual efficiencies.
Tip 14: Negative Work for Emptying Pipes
Always assign negative efficiency to outlet pipes. Net efficiency = (sum of inlet efficiencies) - (sum of outlet efficiencies). If net efficiency is positive, the tank fills; if negative, it empties; if zero, it stays constant.
Tip 15: Combined Work Formula
If A takes x days and B takes y days, time taken together = xy / (x + y). For three workers A, B, C: combined time = 1 / (1/x + 1/y + 1/z). Memorize these for speed.
Tip 16: More Men = Less Days (Inverse Proportion)
Work is constant: M1 × D1 = M2 × D2. If men increase, days decrease proportionally. If men decrease, days increase. This is the basis of the man-days concept.
Tip 17: Fraction of Work Remaining
After some work is done, remaining work = 1 - work done. Time for remaining = remaining work / combined efficiency. Always express remaining work as a fraction for easy calculation.
Tip 18: A+B, B+C, A+C Type Problems
When given pairs of workers' combined times, add all three equations: 2(A+B+C) = 1/TAB + 1/TBC + 1/TAC. Then find individual times by subtracting the pair from the total. This is a common SSC CGL pattern.

15 Solved Examples

Example 1 (Basic � A and B Together)
A can do a job in 12 days and B can do it in 18 days. How long will they take if they work together?

a) 6 days   b) 7.2 days   c) 7.5 days   d) 8 days
Solution: b) 7.2 days. A's 1-day work = 1/12, B's = 1/18. Combined = 1/12 + 1/18 = 3/36 + 2/36 = 5/36. Total days = 36/5 = 7.2 days. LCM method: LCM(12,18) = 36 units. A's efficiency = 3, B's = 2. Combined = 5 units/day. Time = 36/5 = 7.2 days.
Example 2 (LCM Method)
A, B and C can complete a work in 10, 15 and 30 days respectively. How many days will they take working together?

a) 4 days   b) 5 days   c) 6 days   d) 7 days
Solution: b) 5 days. LCM(10,15,30) = 30 units. A = 30/10 = 3, B = 30/15 = 2, C = 30/30 = 1. Combined = 6 units/day. Time = 30/6 = 5 days.
Example 3 (A+B+C Combined Efficiency)
A and B together can do a work in 12 days. B and C together can do it in 15 days. A and C together can do it in 20 days. In how many days can A alone complete the work?

a) 20 days   b) 30 days   c) 24 days   d) 18 days
Solution: b) 30 days. A+B = 1/12, B+C = 1/15, A+C = 1/20. Adding: 2(A+B+C) = 1/12 + 1/15 + 1/20 = (5+4+3)/60 = 12/60 = 1/5. So A+B+C = 1/10. A alone = (A+B+C) - (B+C) = 1/10 - 1/15 = (3-2)/30 = 1/30. A takes 30 days.
Example 4 (Pipes � Two Inlets)
Pipe A can fill a tank in 6 hours and pipe B can fill it in 8 hours. If both pipes are opened together, how long will it take to fill the tank?

a) 24/7 hours   b) 7/24 hours   c) 48/14 hours   d) 14 hours
Solution: a) 24/7 hours. LCM(6,8) = 24 units. A's rate = 4, B's = 3. Combined = 7 units/hour. Time = 24/7 hours. Using formula: (6 × 8)/(6 + 8) = 48/14 = 24/7 hours.
Example 5 (Pipes � Inlet + Outlet)
Pipe A can fill a tank in 12 hours. Pipe B can empty it in 20 hours. If both pipes are opened together, how long will it take to fill the tank?

a) 24 hours   b) 30 hours   c) 36 hours   d) 40 hours
Solution: b) 30 hours. LCM(12,20) = 60 units. A fills 60/12 = 5 units/hr. B empties 60/20 = 3 units/hr. Net = 5 - 3 = 2 units/hr. Time = 60/2 = 30 hours.
Example 6 (Alternate Day Work)
A can do a job in 10 days and B in 15 days. They work on alternate days starting with A. When will the work be completed?

a) 10 days   b) 11 days   c) 12 days   d) 13 days
Solution: c) 12 days. LCM(10,15) = 30 units. A = 3 units/day, B = 2 units/day. In 2-day cycle: A+B = 5 units. In 10 days: 5 cycles = 25 units. Remaining 5 units: day 11 A does 3 units, remaining 2 units done by B on day 12. Total = 12 days.
Example 7 (Work with Leaves)
A can do a work in 10 days, B in 15 days. They work together for 4 days, then A leaves. How many more days will B take to finish the remaining work?

a) 5 days   b) 6 days   c) 4 days   d) 3 days
Solution: a) 5 days. LCM(10,15) = 30 units. A = 3, B = 2. Combined in 4 days = 4 × (3+2) = 20 units. Remaining = 10 units. B alone: 10/2 = 5 days.
Example 8 (Efficiency Ratio)
A can do a work in 12 days. B is 50% more efficient than A. In how many days can B alone do the same work?

a) 6 days   b) 8 days   c) 9 days   d) 10 days
Solution: b) 8 days. Efficiency ratio A:B = 100:150 = 2:3. Time is inversely proportional to efficiency. Time ratio A:B = 3:2. B's time = 12 × 2/3 = 8 days.
Example 9 (Wage Distribution)
A, B and C can do a work in 20, 30 and 60 days respectively. They work together and A gets Rs. 7200 as his share. Find the total wages.

a) Rs. 13200   b) Rs. 14400   c) Rs. 15600   d) Rs. 12000
Solution: b) Rs. 14400. Efficiency ratio A:B:C = 1/20:1/30:1/60 = 3:2:1. Total parts = 6. A's share = 3/6 of total = 7200. So total wages = 7200 × 6/3 = Rs. 14400.
Example 10 (Group Work � Men & Women)
2 men or 3 women can do a work in 24 days. How long will 4 men and 6 women take to do the same work?

a) 4 days   b) 6 days   c) 8 days   d) 10 days
Solution: b) 6 days. 2 men = 3 women in efficiency. So 1 man = 1.5 women. 4 men + 6 women = 4×1.5 + 6 = 6 + 6 = 12 women. 3 women take 24 days, so 12 women take 24 × 3/12 = 6 days.
Example 11 (Man-Days Concept)
15 men complete a work in 8 days. How many days will 12 men take to complete the same work?

a) 8 days   b) 10 days   c) 12 days   d) 6 days
Solution: b) 10 days. Total work = 15 × 8 = 120 man-days. Days for 12 men = 120 / 12 = 10 days.
Example 12 (LCM Shortcut)
A takes 8 days, B takes 12 days, and C takes 24 days to do a work. All three start together but C leaves after 2 days. How many more days will A and B take to finish?

a) 2 days   b) 3 days   c) 4 days   d) 5 days
Solution: b) 3 days. LCM(8,12,24) = 24 units. A = 3, B = 2, C = 1. Total = 6 units/day. In 2 days: 12 units done. Remaining = 12 units. A+B = 5 units/day. Time = 12/5 = 2.4 days. Closest is 3 days.
Example 13 (Multiple Pipes)
Pipe A fills a tank in 8 hours, pipe B fills it in 12 hours, and pipe C drains it in 6 hours. If all three are opened together, how long to fill the tank?

a) 12 hours   b) 18 hours   c) 24 hours   d) 36 hours
Solution: c) 24 hours. LCM(8,12,6) = 24 units. A = 3, B = 2, C = -4 (drains). Net = 3 + 2 - 4 = 1 unit/hour. Time = 24/1 = 24 hours.
Example 14 (Comparison � A is x times as good)
A is thrice as good a workman as B and takes 10 days less to complete a work. In how many days can they complete it together?

a) 3.75 days   b) 4 days   c) 4.5 days   d) 5 days
Solution: a) 3.75 days. Efficiency A:B = 3:1. Time ratio A:B = 1:3. Difference = 2 parts = 10 days. So A = 5 days, B = 15 days. Together: 1/5 + 1/15 = 4/15. Time = 15/4 = 3.75 days.
Example 15 (One Leaves, Others Continue)
A and B together can do a work in 24 days. B and C together in 30 days. A and C together in 40 days. A works alone for 10 days, then B and C join. How many total days to finish?

a) 16 days   b) 18 days   c) 20 days   d) 22 days
Solution: d) 22 days. From Tip 18: A+B+C = 1/10 per day. A alone = 1/10 - 1/30 = 1/15. In 10 days, A does 10/15 = 2/3. Remaining 1/3 done by A+B+C = 1/3 ÷ 1/10 = 10/3 = 3.33 days. Total ≈ 22 days.

8 Advanced Problems

Advanced 1 (3 Workers Alternating)
A, B and C can complete a work in 12, 18 and 36 days respectively. They work in cycles: A on day 1, B on day 2, C on day 3, then repeat. How many days to complete the work?

a) 14 days   b) 15 days   c) 16 days   d) 18 days
Solution: d) 18 days. LCM(12,18,36) = 36 units. A=3, B=2, C=1 per day. In 3-day cycle: 3+2+1 = 6 units. In 15 days (5 cycles): 30 units. Remaining 6 units: day 16 A does 3, day 17 B does 2, day 18 C does 1. Total = 18 days.
Advanced 2 (Pipes with Leakage Detected Late)
A pipe fills a tank in 10 hours. Due to a leak at the bottom, it takes 15 hours to fill. If the leak is detected and repaired after 5 hours, how much total time to fill the tank?

a) 10 hours   b) 11 hours   c) 12 hours   d) 13 hours
Solution: c) 12 hours. Fill rate = 1/10. Leak rate = 1/10 - 1/15 = 1/30. Net with leak = 1/10 - 1/30 = 1/15. In 5 hours: tank filled = 5/15 = 1/3. Remaining = 2/3. After repair, fill rate = 1/10. Time = (2/3)/(1/10) = 20/3 ≈ 6.67 hrs. Total ≈ 11.67 hrs. Closest 12 hours.
Advanced 3 (Wages with Varying Efficiency)
A, B and C undertake a work for Rs. 4800. A and B together can do it in 12 days, B and C in 15 days, A and C in 20 days. They work together for the entire duration. Find each person's share.

a) A=1600, B=2400, C=800   b) A=2000, B=1600, C=1200   c) A=2400, B=1600, C=800   d) A=1800, B=2000, C=1000
Solution: a) A=1600, B=2400, C=800. From Example 3: A+B+C = 1/10. A=1/30, B=1/15=2/30, C=1/30. Efficiency ratio A:B:C = 1:2:1. Share = (1:2:1) of 4800 = 1200:2400:1200.
Advanced 4 (A+B, A+C, B+C � Find Individual)
A and B can do a work in 18 days, B and C in 24 days, A and C in 36 days. In how many days can A, B and C together do it? Also find A alone.

a) 16 days, 36 days   b) 24 days, 48 days   c) 16 days, 48 days   d) 12 days, 30 days
Solution: c) 16 days, 48 days. LCM(18,24,36) = 72 units. A+B = 4, B+C = 3, A+C = 2. Adding: 2(A+B+C) = 9. A+B+C = 4.5. Time together = 72/4.5 = 16 days. A alone = (A+B+C) - (B+C) = 4.5 - 3 = 1.5. Time = 72/1.5 = 48 days.
Advanced 5 (Man-Days with Variable Workforce)
20 men can complete a work in 30 days. After 10 days, 5 men leave. How many more days will the remaining men take to complete the work?

a) 24/7 × 30? Let's calculate: 20 men × 30 days = 600 man-days. After 10 days: work done = 20×10 = 200 man-days. Remaining = 400 man-days. Remaining men = 15. Days = 400/15 = 26.67 days.
Solution: Total work = 600 man-days. Work in 10 days = 200. Remaining = 400. With 15 men: 400/15 = 80/3 = 26 ⅔ days.
Advanced 6 (Efficiency Ratio with Combined Work)
A is twice as efficient as B. B takes 12 days more than A to complete a work. Find the number of days A and B take working together.

a) 6 days   b) 7 days   c) 8 days   d) 9 days
Solution: c) 8 days. Efficiency ratio A:B = 2:1. Time ratio A:B = 1:2. Difference = 1 part = 12 days. So A = 12 days, B = 24 days. Together: LCM(12,24) = 24 units. A=2, B=1. Combined=3. Time=24/3=8 days.
Advanced 7 (Pipes � Partial Opening)
Tank has 3 pipes. A fills in 6 hours, B fills in 8 hours, C empties in 12 hours. First A and B open for 2 hours, then C is opened along with A and B. How much total time to fill the tank?

a) 3.6 hours   b) 4 hours   c) 4.5 hours   d) 5 hours
Solution: b) 4 hours. LCM(6,8,12) = 24 units. A=4, B=3, C=-2. First 2h: (4+3)×2 = 14 units done. Remaining = 10 units. All three net = 4+3-2 = 5 units/hr. Additional time = 10/5 = 2 hrs. Total = 2+2 = 4 hours.
Advanced 8 (Work Completion with Variable Attendance)
A can do a work in 20 days. B is 25% more efficient than A. They work together. After 4 days, B leaves and C joins who is 50% less efficient than A. How many more days to complete the work?

a) 8 days   b) 9 days   c) 10 days   d) 12 days
Solution: a) 8 days. LCM(20) = 20 units. A=1, B=1.25, C=0.5 units/day. A+B in 4 days = (1+1.25)×4 = 9 units. Remaining = 11 units. A+C = 1.5 units/day. Days = 11/1.5 ≈ 7.33 days. Closest option: 8 days.
