Key Concepts

What are Calendar & Clock Problems?

Calendar and clock problems test your ability to calculate dates, weekdays, angles, and time-related phenomena using modular arithmetic and basic spatial reasoning. SSC CGL typically asks 1-2 questions from this topic in Tier 1.

Calendar Concepts

1. Odd Days: The number of days beyond a complete week (7 days) is called odd days. For example, 365 days = 52 weeks + 1 odd day. 366 days (leap year) = 52 weeks + 2 odd days.

2. Leap Year Rules: A year divisible by 4 is a leap year. Exception: century years must be divisible by 400 to be leap years (e.g., 1900 is not a leap year, but 2000 is).

3. Weekday Calculation: If you know the weekday of a reference date, you can calculate any other date by adding odd days and getting modulo 7 (0=Sunday, 1=Monday, ..., 6=Saturday).

2. Clock Gains/Losses: A watch that gains or loses time does so at a constant rate. Calculate the net gain/loss per hour and multiply by elapsed time.

Shortcut Techniques

  • For calendar problems, remember odd days: normal year = 1 odd day, leap year = 2 odd days. Months: Jan=3, Feb=0/1, Mar=3, Apr=2, May=3, Jun=2, Jul=3, Aug=3, Sep=2, Oct=3, Nov=2, Dec=3 (odd days in non-leap year).
  • For clock angle, memorize the formula: |30H - 5.5M|. If the result exceeds 180Â°, subtract from 360Â°.
  • For mirror time: subtract actual time from 11:60.
