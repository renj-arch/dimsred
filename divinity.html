🕉️ Divinity & Mythology — the world's religions in one place

Texts, deities, symbols, mantras, festivals and the ideas behind them — across India and the world, written to be studied, not preached.

Hindu traditions

The oldest living complex of traditions, with no single founder and no single book. Authority is layered: Śruti (revealed: the Vedas) and Smṛti (remembered: epics, Purāṇas, Dharmaśāstra).

Texts

  • Four Vedas — Ṛg (hymns), Sāma (melody), Yajur (ritual formulas), Atharva (spells, medicine). Each has Saṃhitā, Brāhmaṇa, Āraṇyaka, Upaniṣad layers.
  • Upaniṣads — the philosophical core: Brahman (ultimate reality) and Ātman (self); "Tat tvam asi" (that thou art).
  • Bhagavad Gītā — 700 verses of the Mahābhārata; karma, jñāna and bhakti yoga; niṣkāma karma (action without attachment).
  • Epics — Rāmāyaṇa (Vālmīki) and Mahābhārata (Vyāsa); the Harivaṃśa and Purāṇas carry the deity lore.
  • Six darśanas — Nyāya, Vaiśeṣika, Sāṃkhya, Yoga, Mīmāṃsā, Vedānta.

Deities and ideas

  • Trimūrti — Brahmā (creator), Viṣṇu (preserver), Śiva (dissolver). Devī in her many forms (Durgā, Kālī, Lakṣmī, Sarasvatī).
  • Daśāvatāra — the ten avatars of Viṣṇu: Matsya, Kūrma, Varāha, Narasiṃha, Vāmana, Paraśurāma, Rāma, Kṛṣṇa, Balarāma (or Buddha), Kalkī.
  • Karma, saṃsāra, mokṣa — action, rebirth, liberation; dharma as duty and order.

Symbols

Oṃ (ॐ) — the primordial sound; swastika — auspiciousness (an ancient benign symbol, distinct from its 20th-century appropriation); tilaka, liṅga, lotus, conch, sudarśana cakra.

Festivals

Dīpāvalī, Holī, Navarātri/Durgā Pūjā, Mahā Śivarātri, Janmāṣṭamī, Gaṇeśa Caturthī, Rath Yātrā, Onam, Pongal, Bihu, Kumbh Melā — regional calendars differ.

Buddhism

  • Founder: Siddhārtha Gautama, the Buddha (c. 5th–4th century BCE), awakened at Bodh Gayā.
  • Four Noble Truths: suffering (dukkha) exists; it has a cause (taṇhā, craving); it can cease; the Eightfold Path is the way.
  • Eightfold Path: right view, intention, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness, concentration.
  • Three Jewels: Buddha, Dhamma, Saṅgha. Three marks: anicca (impermanence), dukkha, anattā (non-self).
  • Schools: Theravāda (Pāli canon), Mahāyāna (śūnyatā, bodhisattva), Vajrayāna (Tibetan tantra).
  • Symbols: dharmacakra (eight-spoked wheel), lotus, Bodhi tree, stūpa. Festival: Vesākha.

Jainism

  • Tīrthaṅkaras: 24 teachers; the 24th is Mahāvīra (c. 6th century BCE), a contemporary of the Buddha.
  • Core vows: ahiṃsā (non-violence), satya, asteya, brahmacarya, aparigraha; anekāntavāda (many-sidedness) and syādvāda.
  • Texts: Āgamas; Tattvārtha Sūtra. Symbol: the hand with the wheel (ahiṃsā). Festival: Paryuṣaṇa, Mahāvīra Jayantī.

Sikhism

  • Founder: Gurū Nānak (1469–1539); ten Gurus; the Gurū Granth Sāhib is the eternal Guru.
  • Ik Oṃkār — "One Universal Creator"; the Mūl Mantar opens the scripture.
  • Khālsā (1699, Gurū Gobind Siṅgh); the five Ks: kesa, kaṅghā, kaṛā, kacchera, kirpān.
  • Gurdwārā, laṅgar (community kitchen), sevā. Festivals: Vaisākhī, Gurpurabs, Bandi Chhor Divas.

Islam

  • Five Pillars: shahāda (declaration), ṣalāh (prayer), zakāh (almsgiving), ṣawm (fasting in Ramaḍān), ḥajj (pilgrimage).
  • Qur'ān — 114 sūrahs, revealed to Prophet Muḥammad; Ḥadīth and sunnah as guidance.
  • Branches: Sunnī and Shīʿa (with further schools); fiqh schools (Ḥanafī, Mālikī, Shāfiʿī, Ḥanbalī).
  • Symbols: crescent and star (a later cultural symbol, not a doctrinal one). Festivals: ʿĪd al-Fiṭr, ʿĪd al-Aḍḥā, Mawlid, ʿĀshūrā.

Christianity

  • Core: Trinity (Father, Son, Holy Spirit); Jesus of Nazareth as Christ; incarnation, crucifixion, resurrection.
  • Bible: Old and New Testaments; Gospels, Acts, Epistles, Revelation.
  • Branches: Catholic, Orthodox, Protestant (Lutheran, Reformed, Anglican, Baptist, Pentecostal and more).
  • Sacraments (varying by tradition): baptism, Eucharist, confirmation, marriage, ordination, reconciliation, anointing.
  • Symbols: cross, ichthys, dove, lamb. Festivals: Christmas, Easter, Pentecost, Lent, Advent.

Judaism

  • Torah (five books), Talmud (Mishnah + Gemara), Tanakh; the Shema ("Hear, O Israel…") is the central declaration.
  • Ten Commandments; covenant; Sabbath (Shabbat) from Friday sunset to Saturday nightfall.
  • Symbols: Star of David, menorah, mezuzah, tefillin. Festivals: Passover (Pesaḥ), Yom Kippur, Rosh Hashanah, Hanukkah, Sukkot.

Zoroastrianism

  • Founder: Zarathustra; Ahura Mazdā as the supreme god; the cosmic struggle of aša (truth/order) and druj (falsehood).
  • Avesta; the Gāthās; fire as a symbol of purity (not worshipped as a god).
  • Ashem Vohu and the Ahuna Vairya prayers; Navroz (New Year); Towers of Silence and the Parsis of India.

Bahá'í Faith

  • Bahá'u'lláh (1817–1892); unity of God, unity of religion, unity of humanity; progressive revelation.
  • Texts: Kitáb-i-Aqdas, Kitáb-i-Íqán; the Nineteen Day Fast; Naw-Rúz; no clergy.

Ancient Egyptian religion

  • Deities: Ra (sun), Osiris (afterlife), Isis (magic, motherhood), Horus (kingship), Anubis (embalming), Thoth (wisdom), Sekhmet.
  • Ma'at — truth, balance, order; the heart weighed against the feather in the afterlife.
  • Texts: Pyramid Texts, Coffin Texts, Book of the Dead. Practice: temples, mummification, the king as Horus on earth.

Mesopotamian mythology

  • Enūma Eliš — the Babylonian creation epic; Marduk defeats Tiamat and orders the cosmos.
  • Inanna/Ishtar (love and war), Enki/Ea (wisdom, water), Gilgamesh (the flood and the search for immortality).
  • Ziggurats as temple-mountains; divination and the calendar.

Greek mythology

  • Olympians: Zeus (sky), Hera, Poseidon, Demeter, Athena, Apollo, Artemis, Ares, Aphrodite, Hephaestus, Hermes, Dionysus (and Hestia/Hades in some lists).
  • Myths: the Titanomachy, Prometheus, the Trojan cycle, the Odyssey, the labours of Heracles.
  • Practice: the Oracle at Delphi, the Eleusinian Mysteries, civic festivals (Panathenaia, Dionysia).

Roman religion

  • Jupiter, Juno, Mars, Minerva, Venus and the numina; heavy syncretism with Greek deities.
  • Vestal Virgins, the Pontifex Maximus, state cult and the imperial cult; later Christianity.
  • Festivals: Saturnalia, Lupercalia, Vestalia.

Norse mythology

  • Æsir and Vanir: Odin (wisdom, war), Thor (thunder), Freyja, Loki, Týr, Baldr.
  • Yggdrasil (world tree), the nine worlds, Ragnarök (the end and renewal); the Eddas (Poetic and Prose) and skaldic verse.
  • Runes as script and magic; burial ships and mounds.

Celtic and Slavic traditions

  • Celtic: druids, the Otherworld, tír na nÓg; Samhain (the origin of Halloween), Beltane, Lughnasadh, Imbolc.
  • Slavic: Perun (thunder), Veles (underworld), Jarilo, Mokoš; the domovoi (house spirit); Kupala night.

Chinese traditions

  • Daoism: Lǎozǐ, Dào Dé Jīng, wú wéi (non-forcing), yīn-yáng, the Five Phases (wǔxíng), qì.
  • Confucianism: Analects, rén (humaneness), lǐ (ritual propriety), xiào (filial piety), the junzi.
  • Deities and festivals: the Jade Emperor, Guānyīn (bodhisattva), Spring Festival, Mid-Autumn, Dragon Boat, Qingming.

Japanese traditions

  • Shintō: kami (sacred spirits), Amaterasu (sun), Izanagi and Izanami, torii gates, purification (misogi, harae).
  • Buddhism blended with Shintō (shinbutsu-shūgō); Zen, Pure Land, Nichiren.
  • Festivals: New Year (Shōgatsu), Obon, Hanami.

African traditional religions

  • Yoruba: Olodumare (supreme being), orishas (Ṣàngó, Ọ̀ṣun, Ọ̀rúnmìlà), Ifá divination; diasporic Candomblé and Santería.
  • Vodun (Benin, Togo, Ghana); Akan Nyame and the abosom; ancestors and libation.
  • Common themes: a remote creator, intermediary spirits, ancestral veneration, community ritual.

Indigenous American traditions

  • Maya: Popol Vuh, Itzamná, Kukulkán, the ballgame, the Long Count calendar.
  • Aztec (Mexica): Huitzilopochtli, Quetzalcóatl, Tlaloc, Tonatiuh; the tonalpohualli calendar.
  • Inca: Inti (sun), Pachamama (earth), Viracocha; capacocha and the ceque system.
  • North America: the Hopi kachina tradition, the Navajo Diné cosmology, the Great Law of Peace of the Haudenosaunee.

Pacific traditions

  • Māori: Māui, Ranginui (sky) and Papatūānuku (earth), tapu and noa, the whakapapa (genealogy).
  • Hawaiian: Pele (volcano), Kanaloa, the kapu system; the hula as sacred narrative.
  • Aboriginal Australian: the Dreaming (Tjukurpa), ancestral beings, songlines and country.

Comparative themes

  • Creation: ex nihilo (Abrahamic), cosmic sacrifice (Vedic Puruṣa; Norse Ymir), separation of sky and earth (Māori, Chinese), emergence (Pueblo, Aztec).
  • Flood: Noah (Genesis), Utnapishtim (Gilgamesh), Manu (Hindu), Deucalion (Greek), Yu the Great (China).
  • Afterlife: judgement and paradise/hell (Abrahamic), rebirth (Hindu, Buddhist, Jain), ancestral realm (many Indigenous, African, East Asian).
  • Ethics: dharma and karma; the Golden Rule across traditions; ahimsa; covenant; ma'at; ren and li.
  • Symbols: the circle, the tree, the mountain, the wheel, the cross, the crescent, the lotus — each with local meaning; never assume a single global one.
